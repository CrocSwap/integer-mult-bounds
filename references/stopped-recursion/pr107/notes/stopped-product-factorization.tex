\subsection{Atom algebra and the global frame convention}
Fix an odd prime $q$ and an atom width $w\ge1$ throughout one invocation.
An atom is a consecutive $w$-digit radix-$q$ field. A field of $f$ atoms
is the product ring $R_f=(\mathbb Z/q^w\mathbb Z)^f$, with rational
unit coefficients acting componentwise. Let $C_f$ reverse the atoms,
preserving their internal digits. The target is
\[
 F_n(H,D)=(C_nD,C_nH).
\]
It acts on two contiguous chunks with arbitrary complete spectators.
The product-ring interpretation preserves the original complete address
set and its lexicographic order. No standalone reversal is assumed free.

At a node with $n=mf$ atoms, conjugate every frame, source gauge and
endpoint of the finite network by the same $\operatorname{diag}(I,C_n)$.
For a projector $P$ the new partial swap is
\[
 D'_P=\begin{pmatrix}
 (I-P)\otimes I_f&(PC_m)\otimes C_f\\
 (C_mP)\otimes C_f&C_m(I-P)C_m\otimes I_f
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
Global conjugation preserves common-frame scalar identities, nested
residuals, copied-center schedules and arbitrary auxiliary restoration.
The full endpoint is now $F_n$. These frame choices define the logical
interpretation of physical inputs; they are not preprocessing passes.

\subsection{Simultaneous generic basis and full involution factorization}
For every proper nonzero residual projector $P_j$ of rank $r_j$, require
all leading principal minors of sizes $1,\ldots,r_j$ to be nonzero in
one common rational basis. For each individual minor, similarity to
$\operatorname{diag}(I_{r_j},0)$ witnesses nonvanishing. Clearing
denominators and taking the finite product on irreducible
$\operatorname{GL}_m$ proves a common rational choice exists; exact
rational enumeration terminates. Include exteriors, data residuals,
internal edges, copied centers and endpoint corrections in this finite
list. The basis is fixed label preparation, not an executed dense map.

For $M=PC_m$, every northeast $i$-by-$j$ rectangle has rank
$\min(i,j,r)$: after reversing its columns it is the leading rectangle
of $P$ and contains the corresponding nonsingular leading square.
Hence physical lower-triangular matrices $L,R$ give
\[
 LMR=\Pi=EF^t,\quad E=(e_1,\ldots,e_r),\quad
 F=(e_m,e_{m-1},\ldots,e_{m-r+1}).
\]
The full involution, not just this corner, must be normalized.
At $f=1$, put $Q=\operatorname{diag}(L,R^{-1})$ and
$g=QD'_PQ^{-1}$. Since $\operatorname{rank}(g-I)=r$ and its upper-right
block is $EF^t$, there are $A\in\mathbb Q^{r\times m}$ and
$B\in\mathbb Q^{m\times r}$ with
\[
 g-I=\binom EB(A,F^t),\qquad AE+F^tB=-2I_r.
\]
The second identity follows from $g^2=I$ and the full column and row
ranks of the factorization. Define
\[
 K=-(B+F)E^t+FA(I-EE^t),\qquad
 S=\begin{pmatrix}I&0\\K&I\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Then $KE=-B-F$, $F^tK=A+E^t$, and direct multiplication yields
\[
 SgS^{-1}=I+\binom E{-F}(-E^t,F^t)
 =\begin{pmatrix}I-EE^t&EF^t\\FE^t&I-FF^t\end{pmatrix}.
\]
This swaps precisely the first $r$ head coordinates and last $r$ tail
coordinates in opposite orders, fixing all others.

For $f$ atoms use $Q_f=\operatorname{diag}(L\otimes I_f,R^{-1}\otimes I_f)$
and $S_f=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}I&0\\K\otimes C_f&I\end{smallmatrix}\right)$.
The rank factorization lifts with $\bar E=E\otimes I_f$,
$\bar F=F\otimes C_f$, $\bar A=A\otimes I_f$ and
$\bar B=B\otimes C_f$. Its middle cross block is $\Pi\otimes C_f$.
Since $C_r\otimes C_f=C_{rf}$ on the selected physical intervals,
it is exactly one child $F_{rf}$ of the same family. There is no
recursive orientation conversion or extra gathering.

Choose $q$ after the finite basis and factorizations, excluding all
needed nonzero numerators, denominators and pivots. The identities hold
on every atom ring. They use only componentwise scalar arithmetic;
reversal commutes with those scalars, without inter-atom carries.
