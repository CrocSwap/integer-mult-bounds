\begin{abstract}
Combining our direct nonadjacent routing audit with the previously audited
variable stopping threshold gives $\kappa=2^{-76}$ in
$O(n(\log n)^{1-\kappa})$, conditional on OpenAI's underlying algorithmic
interfaces. This quadruples our previous supplied exponent saving without
changing the finite network or routing schedule. We give a transferable
parameter recipe and a simple upper bound on further tuning under the
current constraints; this is not a runtime speedup or an optimality claim.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope and parameter recipe}
The source is OpenAI's \emph{Integer multiplication below $n\log n$}, pinned at
\begin{center}\texttt{adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a}.\end{center}
We retain its fixed finite-alphabet, fixed finite number of one-dimensional
tapes model. Our preceding notes establish conditional substitutions for the
$h=46$ network, variable stopping exponent, and direct nonadjacent axis routing.
Their underlying network and machine interfaces remain assumptions here.

\begin{proposition}
Suppose compatible primitives support $\tau=\sigma=1-a$, with rational
$0<a<1/16$, and the guard exponent remains $C_1=20$. Set
\begin{gather*}
 \epsilon=\frac1{21},\quad \beta=\frac9{10},\quad c=\frac{9a}{10},\quad
 \delta=\frac1{16},\\
 \lambda=1-\frac{19a^2}{20},\qquad \lambda'=1-\frac{9a^2}{10}.
\end{gather*}
Under the nonadjacent routing accounting, all recorded parameter constraints
hold, and the smallest of the seven exponent margins is $G=3a^2/70$.
Consequently every fixed rational $0<\kappa<G$ is supported by the conditional
assembly argument.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The packed overhead and leaf inequalities are
\[
 \tau(1+c/\beta)=1-a^2<\lambda<\lambda'<1,
 \qquad \sigma+\beta(1-\sigma)=1-a/10<\lambda'.
\]
The last comparison follows from $a<1/9$. Also
$\lambda>\max\{\tau,\sigma\}$, $\epsilon<1/12$,
$\epsilon C_1=20/21<1$, and $0<\delta<1/8$.
The seven margins are
\begin{align*}
 g_1&=1-\tfrac1{21}(1+9a/10),&
 g_2&=\epsilon ca=\tfrac{3a^2}{70},\\
 g_3&=\epsilon(1-\lambda')=\tfrac{3a^2}{70},&
 g_4&=a(1-\epsilon)=\tfrac{20a}{21},\\
 g_5&=\tfrac14-\delta-\tfrac32\epsilon=\tfrac{13}{112},&
 g_6&=1-\delta-\epsilon=\tfrac{299}{336},\qquad
 g_7=\tfrac1{21}.
\end{align*}
Here $g_1>1/2$, and each margin other than $g_2,g_3$ exceeds $G$.
For $\rho=G-\kappa>0$, every fixed power of $\log p$ is eventually bounded
by $p^\rho$. The same final absorption argument applies.
\end{proof}

For the audited network saving $a=9/500000000000$,
\[
 G=\frac{243}{17500000000000000000000000}
 \approx1.38857\cdot10^{-23}>2^{-76}
 \approx1.32349\cdot10^{-23}.
\]
This is a fourfold increase in the supplied $\kappa$ from $2^{-78}$.
The recipe improves the available minimum margin over $a^2/80$ by exactly
$24/7$. It transfers to stronger primitive exponents if they satisfy the
same interfaces and guard bound; it does not construct such primitives.

\section{Composition and dependency audit}
The earlier variable-threshold proof applies for every fixed rational
$0<\beta<1$. At an internal node $e\ge d^\beta$, the packed overhead is
$O(e^{\tau(1+c/\beta)}+1)$. The geometric sum is bounded by $O(e^\lambda)$.
Leaves contribute $O(e^\sigma d^{\beta(1-\sigma)})$; the logarithmic number
of base-$m$ pieces is absorbed by $\lambda'$.
For the present threshold the exact stopping test is $e^{10}<d^9$.
Depth remains at most $\lceil\log_m d\rceil$ and every leaf has at most
$d$ axes. Thus the previous guard-depth, row-padding and fixed-tape bounds
apply. These arguments do not depend on the assembly's axis-routing schedule.

Conversely, the direct routing proof preserves each resampling line operation,
its order, padding and numerical truncation. Changing the stopping threshold
inside a layer preserves its exact completed tensor operator and final
truncation. The two substitutions therefore preserve each other's interfaces.

The remaining growing quantities, with $p=\Theta(\log n)$, satisfy
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\toprule
Quantity & Bound\\
\midrule
Guard $d^{20}$ & $\Theta(p^{20/21})=o(p)$\\
Resampling $\alpha$ & $\Theta(p^{23/84})=o(p^{1/2})$\\
Resampling $\gamma$ & $O(p^{25/42})=o(p)$\\
Prime-interval ratio & $p^{19/21}\to\infty$\\
Packed chunk $K$ & $\Theta(p^{3a/70})$, hence $K/\log p\to\infty$\\
Axis width $\ell$ & $\Theta(p^{20/21})$, hence $K=o(\ell)$\\
Record length $r$ & $2^{\Theta(p^{20/21})}$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Record length still absorbs every fixed polynomial of descriptor work.
The exceptional repair term is negligible because $K/\log p\to\infty$.
Prime search remains $n^{o(1)}$, and the explicit assembly root computation
becomes $d^{21}\le b$. No analytic $d^2$ factors have been removed.
The source patch changes the layer stopping contract as well as every affected
assembly parameter, headline and root calculation.

\section{A stopping point for this tuning pass}
With $\tau=1-a$ fixed, the current constraints imply
\[
 (1-a)(1+c/\beta)<1,\quad \beta<1,\quad \epsilon<1/20.
\]
Hence $c<\beta a/(1-a)<a/(1-a)$, so every witness under these conditions obeys
\[
 \kappa<g_2=\epsilon ca<\frac{a^2}{20(1-a)}
 \approx1.62000000003\cdot10^{-23}<2^{-75}.
\]
This necessary upper bound is not claimed sharp. It fixes the primitive
exponent and current guard/layer constraints; it is not a lower bound on
integer multiplication or a barrier to improved primitives or accounting.
It explains why another parameter-only search cannot reach the 60s or 50s.
