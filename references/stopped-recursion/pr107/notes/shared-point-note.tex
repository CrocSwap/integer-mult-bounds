\begin{abstract}
Orthogonal complements of forward source spans supply the reversed-stage
frames, allowing equal intermediate sums from different common-point groups
to be shared. The new conditional saving is $\kappa=13\cdot2^{-66}$,
a factor $13/8$ above the preceding $2^{-63}$ witness. This is a small
refinement and a reusable transfer lemma, not an exponent in the 50s or an
optimality claim for general finite networks.
\end{abstract}

\section{Conditional exponent and checks}
With $a=203/10^{11}$ use
\[
 \epsilon=1/21,\quad \beta=9/10,\quad c=9a/10,\quad
 \lambda=1-19a^2/20,\quad\lambda'=1-9a^2/10,
 \quad\delta=1/16,\quad C_1=20.
\]
The existing exact constraint checker gives
\[
 \min_j g_j=\frac{3a^2}{70}
 =\frac{17661}{10^{23}}>\frac{13}{2^{66}}.
\]
This strict margin absorbs the fixed logarithmic factors in the retained
assembly argument. The resulting conditional bound is
$O(n(\log n)^{1-13\cdot2^{-66}})$.

Every retained addition is checked by exact source-support comparison in local
pair coordinates. Equal sums from different groups are decoded by their fixed
pair and variable vertices. Both directions of frame inclusion are checked on
every compiled physical role. Small complete invocations are independently
expanded on every input basis vector, including every dirty-scratch input,
and a full small three-stage model checks bank exchange and scratch sharing.
The written complement-frame proof supplies the rational interpretation of
these checks. They do not formally verify the upstream theorem.

\section{What this does and does not open up}
For this particular finite graph, the retained necessary inequality
$\kappa<a^2/(20(1-a))$ and an exact upper enclosure of
$a=-\log(1-\eta)/\log m$ give a ceiling below $2^{-62}$.
This is not a ceiling on cross-group sharing or on other finite graphs.
With the same $h=46$ rank deficit and downstream recipe, a future graph using
at most 158353 side roles would suffice for $a=6.4\cdot10^{-9}$ and
$\kappa=2^{-59}$. No such graph is constructed here.
