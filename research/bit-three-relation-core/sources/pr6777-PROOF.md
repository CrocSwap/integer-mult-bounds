# Two exponent relations cannot amortize rank at any scalar width

Prepared for **@CubeJerry** with substantial OpenAI Codex assistance.
New work is Apache-2.0. This is a written reduction to a named inherited
three-register theorem, not a proof-assistant formalization. The finite
controls corroborate the new lemmas; they do not establish unbounded
quantifiers by enumeration.

## 1. Main theorem and exact inherited ingredients

Let `H` be a torsion-free abelian group generated by finitely many
exponents `A_1,...,A_k`. Let `L` be the kernel of `Z^k -> H` sending
basis vector `j` to `A_j`. Write `[A][B]=[A+B]` in `R=F_2[H]`.
A scalar call on any finite number `W` of registers is

\[
D_j=I+([A_j]-1)N_j,\qquad N_j=x_jy_j^T,\quad
x_j,y_j\in\mathbb F_2^W,\quad y_j^Tx_j=1.
\]

**Theorem.** If `rank L <= 2` and the exact whole-matrix endpoint is

\[
\prod_{j=1}^kD_j=\operatorname{diag}([C_1],\ldots,[C_W]),          \tag{1}
\]

then there are pairwise disjoint subsets `S_1,...,S_W` of the call
indices such that

\[
C_i=\sum_{j\in S_i}A_j.                                         \tag{2}
\]

The theorem allows arbitrary width and word length, repeated targets,
identity targets, and arbitrary diagonal monomial helper outputs.
Neither a frame-rank hypothesis, target invertibility, nor a fixed
address dimension is needed. For rational matrix exponents it implies

\[
\sum_i\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Q}C_i
\le\sum_j\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Q}A_j.                     \tag{3}
\]

The scalar field is binary; rational address entries are never reduced
modulo two. Constant binary basis changes below are proof changes of
coordinates, not interposed free address transformations or a physical
implementation invoice.

We use these precisely scoped inherited ingredients:

* **Three-register disjoint subsets:** David Govea's CrocSwap/integer-mult-bounds#720, README
  Sections 1--4, proves (2) at `W<=3`, for any torsion-free exponent
  group and relation rank at most two. It permits identity and repeated
  targets and arbitrary address ranks. Pad with identity coordinates
  when `W<3`. We use this universal lemma, not CrocSwap/integer-mult-bounds#720's later finite
  address-dimension-three word search.
* **Off-diagonal fibers:** Nick Petalas's CrocSwap/integer-mult-bounds#5921 proves that a realized
  nonzero off-diagonal pattern has exactly one binary rank-one
  idempotent lift, except a single edge `E_ab`, whose two lifts are
  `E_aa+E_ab` and `E_bb+E_ab`.
* The cube-relation reduction below follows CrocSwap/integer-mult-bounds#720. The common-pattern
  cyclic/acyclic organization follows woklerx's CrocSwap/integer-mult-bounds#1456. Their arguments
  are recalled to make the new composition checkable.

woklerx's CrocSwap/integer-mult-bounds#1846 already classifies all 4,964 four-register pattern
planes as reducible. We independently replay that finite result, but
the analytic scalar classification in Section 4 makes it unnecessary
as a proof dependency of this theorem.

## 2. Saturation, augmentation and the free-coordinate split

The generated group `H` is finitely generated and torsion-free, hence
free abelian. The sequence `0 -> L -> Z^k -> H -> 0` splits, so reduction
modulo two identifies the kernel of `F_2^k -> H/2H` with `L/2L`.
Expand the universal product `prod_j(I+(t_j-1)N_j)` at `t=1`.
Its first-order off-diagonal coefficient vectors belong to `L/2L`
by (1). Consequently the span of the occurring off-diagonal patterns
has dimension at most two.

Only subset sums of frames occur in the universal product. A difference
of subset indicators is a *cube relation*, whose entries belong to
`{-1,0,1}`. If these relations span at most one rational dimension, let
`L_0` be their saturated span in `Z^k`. Then `L_0` is contained in `L`
and has rank at most one. Two subset sums coincide modulo `L` exactly
when they coincide modulo `L_0`, because their difference is itself a
cube relation. Replacing `H` by the torsion-free lift `Z^k/L_0` preserves
every coefficient fiber of the product and its diagonal monomial
endpoint. It suffices to prove (2) in the lift and map back. Its
first-order pattern span is at most one, handled in Section 3.

Otherwise choose independent cube relations `r,s`. If their reductions
modulo two are independent, an odd `2 x 2` minor is `+1` or `-1`: cube
entries bound its absolute value by two. Thus they generate a saturated
rank-two lattice and form a basis of `L`. If their reductions coincide,
replace them by `(r+s)/2,(r-s)/2`. These are integral cube vectors
with disjoint nonempty supports and a unit minor, again a basis of `L`.
For this cube-valued basis there are binary off-diagonal matrices `B,C`
with

\[
\operatorname{offdiag}(N_j)
=(r_j\bmod2)B+(s_j\bmod2)C.                                    \tag{4}
\]

Pattern span at most one is covered by Section 3. If its dimension is
two, `B,C` are independent. Let `S` index noncoordinate projectors and
`T` coordinate projectors. For `j in T`, (4) forces `r_j=s_j=0`
modulo two, hence integrally because they are cube-valued. Every
relation is supported on `S`, and

\[
H\simeq (\mathbb Z^S/L)\oplus\mathbb Z^T=H_S\oplus\mathbb Z^T.   \tag{5}
\]

Thus the coordinate calls have independent free exponent coordinates.
Projecting onto `H_S` removes all of them and preserves a diagonal
endpoint. Section 5 will return them to their original target labels.
This exact lattice split is essential; arbitrary coordinate gates
cannot be added to a noncoordinate pool's invariant-subspace claim.

## 3. A common pattern at arbitrary width and at most two relations

If all patterns vanish, every projector is a coordinate projector and
(2) is immediate. If the common nonzero pattern is acyclic, a common
coordinate order makes every projector, including coordinate gates,
triangular. A rank-one idempotent then has exactly one nonzero diagonal
entry. Product diagonals give disjoint subsets.

In the cyclic case the pattern uniquely determines a projector `N=xy^T`,
as in CrocSwap/integer-mult-bounds#1456 or the fiber lemma. Let `I=supp(x) intersect supp(y)`.
All directed cycles are in `I`, where the graph is complete off the
diagonal. Its size `s` is odd by idempotence, and cyclicity forces
`s>=3`. Order coordinates in `supp(x)\supp(y)`, then `I`, then
`supp(y)\supp(x)`; all other coordinates are isolated. The common
projector and every coordinate projector become block upper triangular.
The only nonzero diagonal block of `N` is the all-ones idempotent `J_s`.
Outside it, singleton targets are sums of their own coordinate-call
frames. The core subword still has relation rank at most two: its
relations embed into the original lattice by zero extension.

It remains to generalize the common-J argument of CrocSwap/integer-mult-bounds#720 Section 3.

**Common-J lemma.** For every odd `s>=3`, a word with projectors in
`{J_s,E_11,...,E_ss}`, relation rank at most two and diagonal monomial
endpoint has the same endpoint after deleting all J factors.

Write augmentation variables `u_j=t_j-1`, and let `L_J` be their sum
over J calls. If none occurs there is nothing to prove. Otherwise
`L_J` is a nonzero universal linear form and first-order off-diagonal
vanishing puts it in the at-most-two-dimensional linear relation space.
For coordinate `i` set

\[
K_i=\operatorname{offdiag}(J_sE_{ii}+E_{ii}J_s),\qquad
q_i=\sum_{j:N_j=E_{ii}}u_j\sum_{a<j:N_a=J_s}u_a.
\]

The stars `K_i` have the sole relation `sum_i K_i=0`, even modulo
`span((J_s)_off)`. Indeed `sum_i a_i K_i=lambda (J_s)_off` says
`a_i+a_j=lambda` for all distinct `i,j`. A triangle gives `lambda=0`,
then all `a_i` are equal.

In this matrix-space quotient the universal first-order term vanishes
**identically before exponent substitution**. Therefore nonlinear
terms of the Laurent substitution cannot repair the quadratic term.
Precisely, the associated graded algebra at augmentation is
`Sym(H/2H)`; the degree-two term of an expression with universally zero
constant and linear terms is mapped by the linear substitution alone.
Mixed J/coordinate pairs reduce, modulo `L_J`, to `sum_i K_i q_i`.
J/J and coordinate/coordinate pairs vanish in the quotient. After
eliminating `L_J`, the quadratic conditions are

\[
q_i+q_s=0\quad(1\le i<s)                                       \tag{6}
\]

modulo at most one further linear relation `M`.

Eliminate the last of the `h` J variables by `L_J=0`. The `h-1`
proper J prefix sums are linearly independent. Each nonzero quadratic
in (6) is bilinear between J and coordinate variables and divisible
by the same possible `M`. A nonzero bilinear quadratic with a linear
divisor has coefficient-matrix rank one: in its factorization into
linear forms, the bidegrees `(2,0)` and `(0,2)` force one factor to use
only J variables and the other only coordinate variables. Its nonzero
columns are proper J prefixes, so all must be the same prefix.
Thus each nonzero quadratic puts its internal coordinate calls in
one gap between J calls.

All these gaps agree. If `q_s!=0`, its coordinate columns occur in
every equation (6), forcing the same gap. If `q_s=0`, nonzero `q_i`
have disjoint coordinate-variable supports. Two cannot share a nonzero
linear divisor in those variables, so their common divisor is the same
J prefix. With only one nonzero quadratic its own rank-one condition
suffices. If all vanish identically after eliminating `L_J` and before
imposing `M`, disjoint variable supports imply every `q_i=0`, so there
are no internal coordinate calls. This includes `h=1`.

Strip exterior invertible diagonal factors and combine consecutive J
factors. The remaining word is

\[
U(b)D U(c),\quad U(z)=I+(z-1)J_s,\quad D=\operatorname{diag}(d_1,\ldots,d_s).
\]

Every letter is a group monomial. Its off-diagonal entry `i,j` is

\[
(b-1)d_j+(c-1)d_i+(b-1)(c-1)\sum_l d_l.                          \tag{7}
\]

If `b=1`, an off-diagonal zero forces `c=1`, and conversely. Otherwise
compare columns `j,k` in a row distinct from both, possible since
`s>=3`. The Laurent domain gives `d_j=d_k`, so `D=dI`. Oddness of `s`
makes `J_s` idempotent, and the word is `d U(bc)`. Diagonality forces
`bc=1`, leaving exactly `D`. Restoring exterior factors proves the
common-J lemma. Together with the triangular tails this establishes
(2) whenever the pattern span has dimension at most one, at any width.

## 4. Joint-image classification for every two-pattern pool

Let `P` be a two-dimensional space of off-diagonal binary matrices in
`M_W(F_2)`. Its *noncoordinate pool* contains every rank-one idempotent
whose nonzero pattern lies in `P`.

**Joint-image lemma.** Either the pool's joint image has dimension at
most **three**, or the pool consists of exactly the four lifts of two
single edges on four distinct coordinates. That exceptional pool is
coordinate triangular and has joint-image dimension four.

We use the fiber lemma of CrocSwap/integer-mult-bounds#5921. A single edge `E_ab` has exactly the
lifts

\[
E_{aa}+E_{ab}=e_a(e_a+e_b)^T,\qquad
E_{bb}+E_{ab}=(e_a+e_b)e_b^T,                                  \tag{8}
\]

whose images span `e_a,e_b`. Every other realized nonzero pattern has
one lift. For completeness, two distinct lifts differ by a nonzero
diagonal matrix of rank at most two and trace zero, hence supported
on exactly two diagonal positions. Both their image and covector
spaces lie in those positions; the six binary `2 x 2` rank-one
idempotents give exactly (8). This fiber theorem is inherited, not
claimed as new.

There are at most three realized nonzero patterns. If all three are
realized and none is a single edge, only three projectors occur, so
the image bound follows. Suppose exactly one is a single edge
`p=E_ab`, with the other two patterns `q,r=q+p` represented uniquely
by `M=xy^T`, `N=uv^T`. Then

\[
K=M+N=E_{ab}+D,\quad D\text{ diagonal},\quad
\operatorname{rank}K\le2,\quad\operatorname{tr}D=0.              \tag{9}
\]

The diagonal support of `D` has size at most three since
`rank D <= rank K+1`. It has even size by trace zero. Therefore
`D=0` or `D=E_cc+E_dd` for distinct `c,d`.

If `D=0`, either `x,u` agree or they are independent. In the latter
case the rank-one sum `K` forces `y=v`; then `(x+u)y^T=E_ab` gives
`x+u=e_a`. In either case adding the edge-lift image span
`span(e_a,e_b)` to `im M+im N` gives dimension at most three.

If `D=E_cc+E_dd`, the sets `{c,d}` and `{a,b}` must intersect.
Otherwise columns `b,c,d` of `K` are the three independent vectors
`e_a,e_c,e_d`. In every allowed case `rank K=2`: the principal minor
on `c,d` is an identity matrix possibly with one off-diagonal entry,
so has determinant one. Thus `im M+im N=im K`. Adding `e_a,e_b`
is contained in the coordinate union `{a,b,c,d}`, now of size at
most three.

If all three patterns are realized and two are single edges, their
sum has exactly two off-diagonal entries. A rank-one idempotent
realizing that sum has support intersection of size one: a cyclic
intersection has odd size at least three and at least six edges.
For the two disjoint tails' sizes `a_0,b_0`, the edge count is
`a_0+b_0+a_0 b_0=2`, forcing `(a_0,b_0)=(2,0)` or `(0,2)`.
The two edges share a row or a column and use three coordinates.
All five possible lifts are supported there, so again the joint
image has dimension at most three. A two-dimensional space cannot
contain three distinct matrix units, since they are linearly
independent.

If fewer than three patterns are realized, there are at most three
projectors unless both realized patterns are single edges. Their four
lifts have joint image equal to the coordinate span of their endpoints.
This is at most three-dimensional unless all four endpoints differ.
In that exceptional case the edges are disjoint, and a coordinate
order making both edges upper triangular triangularizes the entire
pool. This proves the lemma in every ambient dimension.

The sharp example is `P=span(E_01,E_23)`. Its four lifts have independent
joint image; the third pattern is unrealized. The joint-image maximum
is therefore four, improving the cardinality-derived image bound five
in CrocSwap/integer-mult-bounds#5921. That predecessor's sharp pool-cardinality maximum five is
unchanged. The new statement identifies precisely every image-dimension-
four case, rather than merely checking fixed small register counts.

## 5. Restrict the image and reconstruct every original target

In the remaining pattern-span-two case use the split (5), and project
away all coordinate calls `T`. The remaining endpoint is diagonal,
possibly with additional equal or zero target exponents. Apply the
joint-image lemma to its complete pattern pool.

For the exceptional disjoint-edge pool, coordinate triangularity
assigns disjoint frame subsets directly. Otherwise let `V` be its
joint image, of dimension at most three. Every projector preserves
`V` and induces zero on the quotient. The endpoint also preserves
`V tensor R`.

The invariant subspace need not be spanned by original coordinate
vectors. For each distinct projected target exponent `h`, let `E_h`
be the span of original coordinate vectors with that value, and
`pi_h` the coordinate projection. For `v in V`,

\[
Dv=\sum_h[h]\pi_hv\in V\otimes R.
\]

Mapping to `(F_2^W/V) tensor R` and using linear independence of
monomials gives `pi_h v in V` for every `h`. Consequently

\[
V=\bigoplus_h(V\cap E_h).                                      \tag{10}
\]

Choose a binary basis within each `E_h` adapted to this intersection,
and put the basis vectors of `V` first. The endpoint stays diagonal
with its original monomial multiset, while all projectors are block
upper triangular. Each restricted projector on `V` is rank one and
idempotent: its nonzero image vector lies in `V` and is fixed by it.
All quotient projectors vanish, so the quotient endpoint is identity.
On each complementary equal-value block that endpoint is multiplication
by `[h]`; hence every quotient label has `h=0`, since distinct group
elements are distinct basis monomials.

The relation lattice of the restricted word is unchanged; more generally,
removing identity factors embeds its relation lattice by zero extension
and cannot increase its rank above two. Apply the inherited
three-register disjoint-subset lemma to `V`, padding with identity
coordinates if needed. Match its target monomials, with multiplicity,
to original projected target labels. The quotient labels all have
zero exponent and receive empty subsets. Repeated target values pose
no problem: choose any matching inside each equal-value class.

Finally project (1) onto a free generator `t in T`. Its only surviving
call is its original coordinate projector `E_ii`; thus its exponent
belongs to exactly original target `i`. Append that index to target
`i`'s assigned subset. The direct sum (5) makes the reconstructed
exponent equal to the full original target. The assignments remain
pairwise disjoint. This completes the theorem for every `W`.

## 6. Rank consequence and historical boundary

Apply rank subadditivity to (2) to obtain (3). With rational rank-one
paid frames, any saving below total target rank therefore requires
**at least three independent rational frame relations**, regardless
of register count, address dimension or diagonal helper outputs.

For eight calls and three invertible rational `3 x 3` data targets,
frame span at least six gives relation rank at most two but total
target rank at least nine. Such a word is impossible even with
arbitrarily many dirty auxiliary inputs and arbitrary diagonal
monomial auxiliary outputs.

The four-register span-six case was already covered by CrocSwap/integer-mult-bounds#4119, CrocSwap/integer-mult-bounds#5075,
CrocSwap/integer-mult-bounds#5231 and CrocSwap/integer-mult-bounds#5552. Spans at least seven were already excluded at arbitrary
width by the one-relation work. The new span-six consequence extends
the helper width, and follows from a theorem at every word length.
JoyboyBrian's CrocSwap/integer-mult-bounds#5481 already excludes the five-call `2 x 2` block with
arbitrary diagonal auxiliaries and closes eight-call frame spans at
most four. Combining those earlier results with the present theorem
leaves frame span five as the remaining eight-call case in this formal
alphabet. This is not a claim that a span-five construction exists.

The six-call span-three fixture in the verifier is a known sharpness
and scope control, not a new construction or closure of CrocSwap/integer-mult-bounds#5481. This
package supplies no new supplier, physical implementation, recursive
invoice or multiplication exponent. **No new κ.**
