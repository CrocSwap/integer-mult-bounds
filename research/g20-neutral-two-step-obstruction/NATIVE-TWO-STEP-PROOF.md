## Why arbitrary native paths admit an exact finite floor

`NATIVE-PROOF.md` and `NATIVE-GEOMETRY-REVIEW.md` prove the route relaxation.
The current exact rational admission predicate yields 52,624 directed
proper nested edges, with ranks 1 through 7, on all 645 frames. Both
orientations of each proper nested pair are included. The current 2,369
retained directed routes are a strict subset. All 415,380 distinct endpoint
pairs are reachable.

Let `d(u,v)` be the minimum **sum of ranks**, not the minimum concave tariff,
over native paths. Heap shortest paths are certified by edge-potential
inequalities and strictly descending tight parents; a separately authored
integer-bucket implementation independently checks the same distance
matrix. For any finite path with positive ranks `r_1,...,r_m`,

\[
\sum_i r_i^t \geq \left(\sum_i r_i\right)^t \geq d(u,v)^t,
\qquad 0<t<1.
\]

The first inequality is strict when the path has two or more edges. Thus a
single artificial term `H_d` is an optimistic lower bill. In particular
`d=8` does **not** admit an illegal rank-eight child. A zero normalized row
still needs a route for a distinct actual frame, but contributes no paid
query. A zero source omits both coframe demands, precisely as in the
frozen emitter. All public final-frame returns are charged; owned roles
must end at the zero row. These rules are included in every invoice.

For a zero lower bill, the checker additionally verifies that every paid
demand is a direct admitted native edge. The edge's rank equals its metric
distance, so that demand attains the lower bill. Zero-row demands can use
any finite native path. This proves existence of a paid-moment attainer;
it does not prove a stronger complete ordinary-cost supplier or an outer
recurrence. For a strict lower loss, no attainment claim is needed.

## Exact signs over the whole exponent interval

Subtract the complete source histogram
`{1:1281,2:1338,3:58,4:57,5:102,6:10}` from each lower bill.
Each negative occurrence of rank `q` receives a disjoint nonempty group of
positive rank occurrences whose integer sum is at least `q`.
Subadditivity and monotonicity give
`sum_group r^t >= q^t`. The certificate accounts for every positive
occurrence exactly once, either in one group or as a leftover.
A group with two positive ranks, a group whose sum exceeds its target,
or any leftover proves strictness. Nonzero signed bills are accepted only
with such strictness; an empty signed bill is exactly zero.

`native_floor.partition` searches for these finite witnesses.
`native_floor.check_partition` verifies the witness independently of that
search strategy. A separately authored multiset checker verifies the same
coverage, strictness and all reported outcome multiplicities. No floating
point, sampled exponent, or numerical optimization is used in this proof.

## Limitations and next obligations

The finite result rejects this specified neutral-first, two-rewrite route
mechanism as a source of paid-query savings. It does not prove an
all-construction or all-depth lower bound. The literal and emitter-normalized first intermediates coincide on this
checked 22-word domain; no such normalization claim is made at arbitrary
depth. A nonneutral first move followed by a compensating move remains open.

The rational native interpretation, admissible modular realizations and
full ordinary-cost/restoration framework are inherited. The enlarged
route graph is used favorably for a lower-bound obstruction; this package
does not claim a new complete admitted supplier, a new selected frontier,
or a discharge of the five current framework assumptions. A positive
construction using new routes would still require every native, ordinary,
recursive, owned-return and caller cost to be justified.
