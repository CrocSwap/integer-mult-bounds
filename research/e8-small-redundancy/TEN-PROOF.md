# A ten-centre copy-rank obstruction for the E8 bit matrix

Let `Q` be the 120 E8 root lines in the pinned bit word. Use the listed vectors,
which have squared length 4. Put

\[
 K_{qr}=1+\frac{q\cdot r}{2}\pmod 2,
 \qquad C=\operatorname{rowspan}_{\mathbb F_2}K.
\]

For a binary vector `w` on the 120 lines, let `rho(w)` be the rational dimension
of its support. Thus `rho(0)=0`. The exact check gives `dim C=9` and
`rho(c)=8` for every nonzero `c` in `C`.

## Result

Every coset `v+C` other than `C` satisfies

\[
 \sum_{w\in v+C}(8-\rho(w))\leq 8.                 \tag{1}
\]

Consequently, if ten independent vectors span a space that contains `C`, their
support ranks sum to at least 72. More generally, for each binary factorization
`K=A B^T`, define the pairing-rank objective by

`sum_j rank_Q ((q dot r)_(q in supp(a_j), r in supp(b_j)))`.

Here `a_j` and `b_j` are the columns of A and B. Every ten-term factorization
has objective at least 72. This is an algebraic statement. Its interpretation
as a physical drop-cost floor uses the separate scalar copy-tree model and
Theorem C of PR #446. A different delivery model need not have that price.

## Finite geometric facts

A support of rank at most seven lies in a rank-seven flat of the E8 root
matroid. Every such flat is Weyl-conjugate to the roots generated by seven
simple roots. To see this, move a normal vector into a closed dominant Weyl
chamber. A positive root is perpendicular to the normal precisely when its
simple-root expansion uses only simple roots perpendicular to the normal.
A root flat of rank seven therefore omits exactly one simple root.

The check derives a simple system from the 240 signed roots, then checks all
eight deletions. This covers every rational root hyperplane, not only eight
sampled hyperplanes. Weyl transformations preserve `C` and support rank, since
they preserve the Gram matrix modulo 2, including a change in root signs.

For a root hyperplane `H`, set

\[
 D_H=\{c\in C:\rho(\operatorname{supp}(c)\setminus H)\leq7\}.
\]

Write `k_q` for row `q` of `K`. The following facts are checked by exact linear
algebra in `parabolic_check.py`.

1. The eight hyperplanes have 14, 16, 22, 23, 28, 37, 42, and 63 root lines.
2. If `H` is not the 63-line E7 hyperplane, then
   `D_H = {0} union {k_q: q in H}`. For each `q in H`, the support of `k_q`
   outside `H` spans exactly `q^perp`, of dimension seven.
3. If `H=n^perp` is the E7 hyperplane, put `t=k_n`. Its support is exactly
   `H union {n}`. In this case
   `D_H = {0,t} union {k_q,k_q+t: q in H}`. The outside support of either
   `k_q` or `k_q+t` spans exactly `q^perp`.
4. Within E7, the lines other than `q` that are not perpendicular to `q` span
   all seven dimensions. Each pair of distinct lines has exactly 16 common
   nonperpendicular neighbors, distinct from the pair.

There are only 512 words in `C`, so the check of each `D_H` is exhaustive.
Ranks below eight are checked over the rational numbers. A rank-eight result
modulo 101 certifies rational rank eight. This use of reduction modulo a prime
can only certify full rank; it never treats a smaller modular rank as a
rational rank.

## Proof of the coset bound

If the coset has no word of rank at most seven, (1) is immediate. Otherwise
choose such a word `v`, and choose a root hyperplane `H` that contains its
support. Let `d=rho(v)`.

If `w=v+c` has rank at most seven, its support outside `H` equals that of `c`.
Thus `c` belongs to `D_H`.

First suppose `H` is not E7. Every low-rank word other than `v` has the form
`v+k_q`, for `q in H`. Its outside support already spans `q^perp`, so the
whole support must lie in `q^perp`. This requires:

- `v(q)=1`;
- `v(r)=0` for every other `r in H` that is not perpendicular to `q`.

Conversely, these conditions suffice. Each such word has rank seven.
The admissible lines `q` are pairwise perpendicular and belong to the support
of `v`. There are at most `d` of them. The sum in (1) is therefore at most
`(8-d)+d=8`.

Now let `H=n^perp` be E7. Put

\[
 d=\rho(\operatorname{supp}v),\qquad
 e=\rho(H\setminus\operatorname{supp}v).
\]

Then `d,e<=7` and `d+e>=7`. The word `v+t` has rank `1+e`, since its support
is `{n}` together with the complement of the support of `v` in `H`.
All other possible low-rank words have rank exactly seven. They are:

- `v+k_q`, where `q` is in the support of `v` and is perpendicular to every
  other line in that support; call these indices `A`.
- `v+t+k_q`, where `q` is in the complementary support and is perpendicular
  to every other line in that complementary support; call these indices `B`.

Thus `|A|<=d` and `|B|<=e`. If `d<=6`, then `B` is empty: a member of `B`
would put all its nonperpendicular neighbors into the support of `v`, but
those neighbors have rank seven. Similarly, `e<=6` implies that `A` is empty.
Also, `A` and `B` cannot both be nonempty. A common nonperpendicular neighbor
of one member of each would have to lie both in the support and its complement.

These statements prove (1) in all four cases:

| Case | Sum of all rank defects is at most |
|---|---|
| `d<=6`, `e<=6` | `(8-d)+(7-e)<=8`, since `d+e>=7` |
| `d<=6`, `e=7` | `(8-d)+|A|<=8` |
| `d=7`, `e<=6` | `1+(7-e)+|B|<=8` |
| `d=e=7` | `1+max(|A|,|B|)<=8` |

The words in each list are distinct. For E7, this also follows directly from
the checked description of `D_H`.

## From coset rank defects to ten centres

Let `K=A B^T`, where `A` and `B` have ten columns. Each column records the
binary source or reader support of one scalar retained total. Omit any term
with a zero column. If at most nine terms remain, the binary rank-nine bound
forces exactly nine terms. Both factors then have column space `C`, so each
source and reader support spans all eight rational dimensions. The total
pairing rank is 72.

Otherwise both factors have ten nonzero columns. Their ranks are at least
nine. They cannot both have rank ten: an injective `A` and a surjective `B^T`
would give rank ten for their product. Hence one factor, say `A`, has rank
nine. Its column space equals the column space of `K`, namely `C`, because
`K` is symmetric. Every column of `A` has rational support rank eight.
The rational source-reader pairing for a column therefore has rank exactly
the rational support rank of the corresponding column of `B`.

If `B` has binary rank nine, each nonzero column belongs to `C` and the sum is
80. If `B` has binary rank ten, its column space is a ten-dimensional space
`U` that contains `C`. Thus `U=C union (v+C)` for one `v` outside `C`.
The columns of `B` are independent, so they are distinct. All nonzero columns
in `C` have zero rank defect. Equation (1) bounds the sum of the defects of
the remaining columns by eight. Their rank sum is at least `80-8=72`.

This excludes every ten-centre copy-rank total below 72, including 71.

## Eleven centres when both factors have binary rank ten

Suppose `K=A B^T` has eleven columns in each factor, and both factors have
binary rank ten. Then the total source-reader pairing rank is at least 72.

First, a zero column lets us omit its term and use the ten-centre result.
If two columns of `A` are equal, merge the terms

\[
 a b_1^T+a b_2^T=a(b_1+b_2)^T.
\]

Let `U` be the rational support span of `a`, and let `V_1,V_2` be those of
`b_1,b_2`. The support span of `b_1+b_2` is contained in `V_1+V_2`. The rank
of its pairing with `U` is therefore at most

\[
 \operatorname{rank}(U,V_1+V_2)
 \leq \operatorname{rank}(U,V_1)+\operatorname{rank}(U,V_2).
\]

Thus merging does not increase cost. The resulting factorization has at most
ten terms, so the ten-centre result applies. The same argument applies to
repeated columns of `B`.

It remains to consider nonzero, distinct columns in each factor. Each column
space has dimension ten and contains `C`. Equation (1) therefore gives

\[
 \sum_{j=1}^{11}(8-\rho(a_j))\leq8,\qquad
 \sum_{j=1}^{11}(8-\rho(b_j))\leq8.
\]

For subspaces `U,V` of the nondegenerate eight-dimensional label space,

\[
 \operatorname{rank}(U,V)
 =\dim U-\dim(U\cap V^\perp)
 \geq \dim U+\dim V-8.
\]

Sum this inequality over the eleven terms. The total pairing rank is at least

\[
 (88-8)+(88-8)-88=72.
\]

An eleven-term factorization of rank-nine `K` must have factor ranks
`(9,9)`, `(9,10)`, `(10,9)`, `(9,11)`, `(11,9)`, or `(10,10)`, since their
rank sum cannot exceed 20. The cases in which one rank is nine and the other
is at most ten also have cost at least 72. To see this, first remove zero
terms or merge repeated columns as above. Otherwise, the rank-nine factor
has nonzero columns in `C`, so its rational support spans have dimension
eight. The pairing cost is the sum of support ranks on the other side. If
that side has binary rank nine, all eleven support ranks are eight. If it
has binary rank ten, the columns are distinct and (1) bounds their total
defect by eight, so their support ranks sum to at least 80.

This proof leaves factor ranks `(9,11)` and `(11,9)` for eleven centres.
The companion `ELEVEN-PROOF.md` closes those two cases. Neither proof covers
more than eleven centres.
