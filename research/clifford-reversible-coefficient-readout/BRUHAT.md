# Producing the factored input from a symplectic matrix

The tape consumes a factorization $N=i^g M_L^* H_b^\sharp M_R$. Every four-qubit binary symplectic map admits this format. The accompanying classical compiler accepts eight columns, in low-$Z$, high-$X$ order, and optionally a particular $16\times16$ Gaussian-dyadic matrix. It prepares a record once; its work is separate from each reversible query. When only the binary action is supplied, the compiler chooses one deterministic gauge. Supplying the absolute matrix preserves its Pauli translation and scalar phase.

Let $Z$ be the diagonal Pauli space and project $S Z$ to its upper four $X$ bits. Select a basis of this image of dimension $h$, keeping one lift $s_i$ of each selected basis vector. Extend the image basis to a basis of the full nibble. Let $A$ take this full basis to the standard basis. The symplectic basis change acts by $(z,x)\mapsto(A^{-T}z,Ax)$. In these coordinates $S Z$ contains precisely the diagonal directions $e_{h+1},\ldots,e_4$. The low $h$ components of the transformed $z_i$ for $s_i$ form the columns of a symmetric $h\times h$ matrix, by isotropy. Extend that matrix by zero on other axes to obtain $Q$. Adding $Qx$ to $z$ clears these $h$ diagonal components. Components beyond $h$ are already in the diagonal subspace and do not affect the resulting Lagrangian.

Compose this shear after $A$ to obtain $P_L$. It takes $SZ$ to the coordinate Lagrangian generated by $X_1,\ldots,X_h,Z_{h+1},\ldots,Z_4$. The partial Hadamard $H_b$ in the first $h$ bits maps it to $Z$. Hence $P_R=H_bP_L S$ preserves $Z$. Such a map has the form
$$
 (z,x)\longmapsto (A_R^{-T}(z+C_Rx),A_Rx)
$$
where $A_R$ is invertible, and $C_R$ is symmetric by the symplectic pairing. The monomial $M|v\rangle=i^{q(v)}|Av\rangle$ realizes this map when the coefficients of $v_i$ in $q$ have parity $C_{ii}$ and the coefficients of $v_iv_j$ are $2C_{ij}$ modulo four. For the left side the input-coordinate shear is $A^T Q A$. Consequently $M_L^* H_b^\sharp M_R$ induces exactly $S$. Here $H^\sharp=\frac{1+i}{2}\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\1&-1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$, so all lift entries and their denominators use Gaussian integers and powers of two.

For a supplied absolute lift $U$, the quotient of $U$ by the constructed lift has trivial symplectic action. Its signs on the Weyl basis are a character of the binary Pauli label space, hence match a Pauli displacement by nondegeneracy of the pairing. After removing this displacement the operator commutes with all Weyls and is scalar. A scalar of modulus one in $\mathbb Z[i,1/2]$ is a power of $i$, by unique factorization or the sum-of-two-squares identity for powers of two. Thus $U=i^g X^xZ^z N$ for a unique $x,z,g$. The implementation tests exact entries after cross-multiplication by the supplied powers of two. It rejects a table with no match. It absorbs the displacement into the left monomial: in $M_L (X^xZ^z)^*$, the row permutation receives shift $Ax$, and the new phase polynomial is $q(v+x)+2z\cdot(v+x)$ with its constant removed. The conjugate of this constant phase enters $g$. The resulting input still allows the caller to choose an independent queried Pauli label.

`bruhat.py request.json` accepts `symplectic` and optional `entries`, `denominator_bits`. Absolute entries are 256 pairs of signed integer numerators in **row-major** order. The output supplies `left`, `right`, `mask`, and `phase`; the tape query also needs `x`, `z`, `terms`, and `precision`. The absolute-table step tests at most $256\cdot4$ corrections and at most 256 entries per correction. In the named four-qubit model the only unbounded input here is integer precision.
