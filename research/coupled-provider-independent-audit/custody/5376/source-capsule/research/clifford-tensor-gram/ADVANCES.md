# Fewer widths and exact computation without dense matrices

Ali Romman, with substantial OpenAI Codex assistance. Apache-2.0.

This note extends [PROOF.md](PROOF.md). It uses the same group C_n, exact
global phases, complete register endpoints and coefficients that do not
depend on the positive tensor width f. Set D=2^n. No result changes kappa.

## 1. Eight widths suffice for every axis count

For n>=1, a fixed residual vanishes at every positive width if and only if
it vanishes at the eight widths

    8n-3, 8n-2, ..., 8n+4.

For n=0, use widths 1,2,3,4. For n=1, Section 3 improves the test to
widths 5,6,7,8. The number of tests and the largest tested width are
different quantities. The general maximum 8n+4 has not decreased.

Let E be the binary code generated by the four length-eight rows
11111111, 11110000, 11001100 and 10101010. It has dimension four. Its
weights are 0,4,8, and E=E-perp for the binary dot product. Define T_n
in (C^D) tensor-power 8 by summing the basis vectors whose n binary rows
all belong to E. This vector is nonzero.

The vector T_n is fixed by the eighth tensor power of each native generator:

- S on one axis contributes i to the power of that row's weight. This is 1.
- CNOT adds one code row to another. It permutes the summands.
- The native Hadamard is ((1+i)/2) times the binary Fourier matrix.
  Its eighth tensor power has coefficient 1/16. The Fourier sum of E
  is 16 times the indicator of E-perp=E. Thus it fixes T_n, with phase 1.
- The scalar iI has eighth power 1.

These gates generate C_n. Here is a direct reduction argument. The image
of Z_1 under a Clifford is a nonidentity Pauli. Single-axis H and S gates
convert each nonzero component to Z. CNOT gates reduce the resulting
nonempty Z string to Z_1. An X gate corrects its sign. The image of X_1
then anticommutes with Z_1. CNOT and controlled-Z gates with control 1
remove its components on the other axes. An S gate removes its remaining
Z_1 component, and Z_1 corrects its sign. These changes preserve Z_1.
The remaining operator fixes X_1 and Z_1, so it has the form I tensor V.
Repeat on V. The final scalar is a dyadic Gaussian unit of absolute value
one, hence one of 1,i,-1,-i, as proved in PROOF.md. Replace each ordinary
H in this argument by the native Hadamard. The Pauli action is unchanged;
the final scalar still belongs to the same four-element group.

Normalize T_n to a unit vector tau. The injection J_f(v)=v tensor tau
satisfies

    (sum_g c_g g^(tensor(f+8))) J_f
      = J_f (sum_g c_g g^(tensor f)).

Thus vanishing at f+8 implies vanishing at f. Every integer from 1 to
F=8n+4 can be increased by a multiple of eight to a member of F-7,...,F.
The first theorem in PROOF.md now gives the stated eight-width test.
Apply the argument separately to every input and output role.

This argument does not transfer the dimension-free stability constant
to the eight selected widths. The bound in Section 2 uses all widths
1,...,8n+4. A direct restriction argument loses powers of D.

The code invariant is classical. See Runge's prior results as credited in
[Nebe, Rains and Sloane, The invariants of the Clifford groups, Sections 4
and 6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0001038). The contribution here is its
use in the fixed-coefficient endpoint test, with the native phase checked.

## 2. Uniform separation improves from 14/15 to 75/76

For distinct exact operators and M=sum_g |c_g|^2, let
E_f=D^(-f)||sum_g c_g g^(tensor f)||_HS^2. Then

    max_(1<=f<=8n+4) E_f >= (75/76) M.

Use the same filter as PROOF.md. Write r=-1/4, m=2n and

    P_m(t) = product_(j=1..m) (t-r^j)/(1-r^j),
    q_n(z) = (z+z^2+z^3+z^4) P_m(z^4)/4.

The four residue classes of coefficients do not overlap. Therefore the
sum of absolute coefficients of q_n equals that of P_m.

In descending degree, the coefficient with index l has sign
s_l=(-1)^(l(l+3)/2), namely ++--++--. To check this, the elementary
symmetric function in r,...,r^m equals

    r^(l(l+1)/2) product_(k=1..l) (1-r^(m-l+k))/(1-r^k).

This identity follows by separating subsets that contain the last root;
the empty product gives the initial case. Every factor in the product
is positive. Include the coefficient sign (-1)^l to get s_l.

Let u_l be the absolute coefficients of P_(2n), L their sum, and U_odd
their sum over odd l. Add the next two roots -a,b, where
b=16^(-(n+1)) and a=4b. The new factor is
(t^2+(a-b)t-ab)/((1+a)(1-b)). The sign rule gives

    L_new - L = [2ab L - 2(a-b) U_odd]/((1+a)(1-b)).

For n>=1, the unnormalized coefficient of t^(2n-1) has magnitude
(1-16^(-n))/5 >=3/16. The sum of the unnormalized absolute coefficients
is at most product_j(1+4^(-j)) <=3/2. The last inequality follows by
expanding the product and bounding it by the geometric series whose
ratio is sum_j 4^(-j)<=1/3. Hence U_odd/L>=1/8. On the other hand,
ab/(a-b)=4b/3<=1/192. Thus L_new<L for n>=1.

For n=1, P_2(t)=(64t^2+12t-1)/75, so L=77/75. For n=0, L=1.
Consequently the positive coefficient mass of q_n is at most
(1+77/75)/2=76/75. The exact identity sum_f b_f E_f=M and E_f>=0
give the claimed bound. This is a bound for this filter; optimality of
the operator stability constant is not claimed.

The implementation forms the integer numerator
product_j(4^j t-(-1)^j) and the common denominator
product_j(4^j-(-1)^j) first. It reduces fractions only when it emits q_n.
This gives the same polynomial and avoids repeated large rational reductions.

## 3. The one-axis maximum cutoff is exactly eight

The frozen native C and S gates generate all 96 exact operators of C_1.
Their closure and phases are checked in verify.py. Let

    K[g,h] = sum_(f=5..8) (tr(g* h)/2)^f.

The file [ONE-AXIS-RANK.json](ONE-AXIS-RANK.json) contains an inverse of K
after reduction to F_65537 with i mapped to 256. The checker reconstructs
K, checks that 65537 is prime and 256^2=-1, and multiplies K by this
inverse. It does not use the elimination routine to check the inverse.
All original denominators are powers of two. A nonzero reduced determinant
therefore proves that the characteristic-zero determinant is nonzero.
The positive semidefinite Gram sum K has zero kernel. This proves that
widths 5,6,7,8 suffice for every fixed residual on C_1.

The Reynolds example in PROOF.md vanishes at widths 1 through 7 and is
nonzero at width 8. The maximum cutoff eight is therefore sharp.
At least four tested widths are required: for each residue r modulo four,
the four scalar operators give the residual

    sum_(k=0..3) i^(-rk) (i^k I)^(tensor f).

It vanishes unless f is congruent to r modulo four. A test set that omits
one residue cannot detect this residual. Thus four is also the minimum
number of widths for a fixed universal test on C_1. Neither conclusion
establishes a sharp cutoff for n>=2.

## 4. A phase-exact quadratic path calculation

[quadratic.py](quadratic.py) accepts the frozen native P, X, D, C and
phase gate grammar. It keeps a list of binary affine output bits and a
phase polynomial modulo four. The polynomial has arbitrary linear
coefficients and even cross coefficients. It also keeps an exact Gaussian
scalar factor. It never allocates an array indexed by the 2^n base states.

Start with n input variables. P and X change only the affine output bits.
D adds its stated linear and even quadratic phase. For C(q,s), use

    C(q,s) = ((1+s i)/2) (I + i^(-s) X_q).

Add one binary path variable a, multiply the scalar factor by (1+s i)/2,
add -s a to the phase and add a q to the output bits. Keep the literal
phase gate. The formula also covers q=0. The number of variables is
m=n+h, where h is the number of C gates in the literal word.

An affine parity has, modulo four, its constant and linear terms plus
even pair terms. Terms of degree at least three vanish modulo four.
Twice a product of parities needs only the product over F_2. Thus every
gate preserves this representation, including affine translations.

An entry fixes the input and output bits. A trace equates them. Binary
elimination either rejects inconsistent equations or gives an affine
parametrization with one assignment per solution. Substitute this map
in the phase. The remaining unconstrained sum is evaluated as follows:

- For an odd linear coefficient a of variable x, write its part as
  a x+2 x L. Summing x gives (1+i^a) i^(-a L). Remove x and add the
  indicated parity phase. This keeps the same quadratic form class.
- If every linear coefficient is even and a cross term remains, sum one
  variable x. The result is twice the sum subject to a binary affine
  constraint on its neighbors. Eliminate that constraint.
- If no cross terms remain, a linear coefficient 2 makes the sum zero.
  Otherwise each remaining variable supplies a factor two.

Each step removes at least one variable. The code uses direct polynomial
substitution. A conservative bound is O(m^5) arithmetic operations per
sum, plus O(L m^4) to build a path form for L native gates. It uses
polynomial space. This bound is not a
claim of optimal stabilizer simulation or constant-time integer arithmetic.
Coefficient bit lengths are polynomial in the literal word length.

The use of binary quadratic forms for Clifford operations is classical;
see [Dehaene and De Moor, Sections III-V](https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/0304125).
The new implementation derives its phases directly from the pinned native
kernel. The dense frozen kernel remains a separate finite test oracle.

## 5. Bitwise witness extraction removes the D^2 scan

For two operators g,h and fixed prefixes of the output and input bits,
define B(g,h) as the sum of conjugate(g[x,y])h[x,y] over the unspecified
bits. Combine two path sums, negate the left phase, equate their input
and output bits, and impose the prefix equations. Section 4 computes B
in polynomial time, with at most the sum of both path-variable counts.

With r tensor factors still after the current factor, the conditional
squared norm is

    sum_(g,h) conjugate(c_g)c_h B(g,h) (tr(g* h)/D)^r.

It is real and nonnegative. The two choices for the next coordinate bit
partition its sum. Choose the larger child. After 2n choices, the current
base coordinate pair is fixed; multiply each coefficient by g[x,y] and
continue with the next tensor factor. There are 2nf bit choices, each
with two children, instead of fD^2 coordinate trials.

At the start of one factor the conditional norm is D E. The 2n splits
lose at most D^2. Induction gives the same final entry bound as PROOF.md:

    |entry|^2 >= E_f D^(-f).

[compact.py](compact.py) also expands complete register endpoints as
signed native words. It retains every input and return role. It merges
two unitary words exactly when tr(g* h)/D=1; the identity
||g-h||_HS^2=2D-2 Re tr(g* h) proves this test. Global phases stay inside
the operator keys, not in coefficients outside the tensor power.

For expanded support s and a maximum literal path size m, pair traces
cost O(s^2 poly(m)). A witness costs O(n f s^2 poly(m)) arithmetic
operations, with polynomial space in these parameters. No 2^n matrix or
2^(nf) tensor is built. The symbolic support s can still grow exponentially
in the register-program length. This result does not remove that obstacle.

## Verification and limits

[verify_advances.py](verify_advances.py) checks the code invariant, exact
filter bounds, modular inverse certificate, exhaustive small quadratic
sums, frozen native entries and traces, conditional inner products, and
complete programs against the original dense compiler. It checks larger
native examples without a dense matrix. ADVANCES-RESULT.json records the
executed counts. Normal and optimized Python must produce the same receipt.

The proofs above are written arguments, not proof-kernel certificates.
The code invariant and quadratic-form method have classical predecessors.
The sparse full-program adapter, finite-width refinements and native phase
bindings are the present contribution. No complete current provider or
physical cost invoice has been run. No selected frontier changes here.
