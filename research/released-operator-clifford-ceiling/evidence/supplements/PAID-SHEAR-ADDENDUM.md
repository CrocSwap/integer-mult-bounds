## Collision, affine phases and sufficiently large width

Every H in the address group commutes with every X translation. A monomial
operator commuting with all translations has underlying permutation
`p(x+a)=p(x)+a` and multipliers `c(x+a)=c(x)`. Hence it is a scalar times
one X translation. This statement includes phases; a nonconstant signed or
quadratic phase cannot be silently retained while commuting with every X.

For a nonidentity H contribution to collide with an identity-address
contribution after the fixed source K factors, H would have to equal

`phase*K_(3,r')^-1 K_(3,r) K_(1,s)^-1 K_(1,s')`.

The right side is a zero-shift signed **linear** permutation and fixes address0.
If H were monomial, the preceding translation characterization therefore
forces its translation to vanish and H to be projectively I. Source disjoint
stage supports and distinct q_i then force r'=r and s'=s. Thus a grouped
identity coefficient B0[r,s] cannot be cancelled by a nonidentity H class or
a different intermediate owner pair.

With B0[r,s] nonzero, nonempty source column r and row s supply output/input
i,j with A[i,r]A[s,j] nonzero. Zero source diagonal gives r!=i and s!=j.
The original scalar-owner monomial criterion then makes its endpoint word
nonmonomial. Therefore B0 contributes a unique nonzero nonmonomial class.
Uniform tensor separation from the fixed finite proposed monomial endpoint
alphabet forces B0=0 whenever the endpoint E is declared free.

This uses the same correct projective convention as the main proof: reduce
equal one-column words first, raise their scalar cocycles/phases to f and
leave owner coefficients outside the tensor product. The X-Gaussian group on
fixed17 axes is projectively finite. Its source-product classes plus any
fixed finite free endpoint alphabet admit a uniform sufficiently-large-width
separation threshold. No all-small-width statement or practical threshold
is claimed. Width-dependent scalar coefficients do not evade the conclusion
at those widths. A growing/noncolumnwise alphabet is outside the theorem.

## Scalar owner rank bound and inverse condition

For T_k=S_k T_(k-1), exact telescoping gives

```
t0-I=sum_k u_k (v_k^T tensor H_k) T_(k-1).
```

Taking the grouped projective identity coefficient is linear. Since u_k is
scalar in owner space, it factors out of that extraction, yielding

```
B0-I=sum_k u_k w_k^T,
rank(B0-I)<=dim span{u_k}<=m.
```

B0=0 consequently implies112<=m. This remains true for arbitrary dense scalar
vectors and width-dependent scalar entries. It does not assert attainability
at112 shears. The explicit physical inverse S_k^-1=I-u_k v_k^T tensor H_k
requires v_k^T u_k=0; it uses H_k again, not H_k^-1. For a product, inverse
execution reverses the shear order. The rank lower bound itself does not need
nilpotence or any inverse-program sufficiency.

This coefficient-extraction step is precisely where **operator-valued**
owner/address gathers and scatters leave the proof. If the components of u_k
contain different K maps, extracting identity classes can produce columns
outside one fixed scalar span. No m>=112 bound is transferred to that class,
or to retained correlated native/query observations, without a new argument.
