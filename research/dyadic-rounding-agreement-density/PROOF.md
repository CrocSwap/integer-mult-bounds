# A sharp torsion bound for rounded-increment agreement

This scoped proof is produced by Nick Petalas. The affine-lattice model and
half-open rounding cells are inherited from woklerx's PR #1848. The new step
is a quantitative density bound and its sharp dyadic invariant-factor form.

## Statement

Let A be a rational d by n matrix, B a rational n by k matrix, and x0 a
rational n-vector. Let w>=0 and v>w be integers. Write g=v-w, D=2^w AB,
c=2^w Ax0, and

Q_b(t) = 2^(-b) floor(2^b t + 1/2)

componentwise. Choose any positive common denominator m of D. Define

H = {Dz modulo Z^d : z in Z^k},
K = H[2^g] = {h in H : 2^g h = 0 modulo Z^d}.

H is a finite subgroup of the rational torus. Over the complete parameter
period z in {0,...,m-1}^k, let rho be the fraction satisfying

Q_w(A(x0+Bz)) = Q_v(A(x0+Bz)).

Then

rho <= 1/|K|.                                             (1)

This fraction is independent of the chosen common denominator. It is also the
natural density over expanding integer parameter boxes. When H is a dyadic
group with cyclic invariant factors 2^e_1,...,2^e_r, where e_i>=1,

rho <= 2^(-sum_i min(g,e_i)).                            (2)

For every such finite list of exponents and every g>=1, a coordinate-aligned
lattice with zero offset attains (2). Thus no smaller upper bound depending
only on these invariant factors and g can hold for all embeddings and offsets.
The trivial group has rho either zero or one and the bound one is sharp.

These are claims about exact rounded-linear increments. They do not assert
that a selected implementation maintains this domain or distribution.

## Proof

In coarse-grid units put h=2^(-g). Coarse rounding equals fine rounding at a
scalar t exactly when t lies in

union over integers j of [j-h/2, j+h/2).

Indeed fine rounding must equal the same integer j as coarse rounding, whose
cell contains this smaller cell. Thus agreement in all coordinates is exactly
membership of c+Dz modulo Z^d in a product C of half-open circular arcs of
length h, each centered at zero. The half-open convention includes half-up
ties with their specified signs.

The map from (Z/mZ)^k to H induced by D is a surjective homomorphism. Each
fiber is a coset of its kernel, so all fibers have the same size. Therefore
rho = |(c+H) intersect C|/|H|. A larger common denominator gives the same
fraction. Periodicity also proves the natural-density assertion: in a box of
side length L, each of the finitely many residue classes has frequency tending
to m^(-k) as L tends to infinity. This also holds in symmetric boxes.

Partition c+H into its |H|/|K| cosets of K. A single coset contains at most one
point of C. Otherwise two distinct points differ by a nonzero element of K.
At least one coordinate of that difference is a nonzero multiple of h modulo
one. Two points in the same half-open circular arc of length h cannot have
that difference: lifting that arc to a half-open real interval makes their
absolute difference strictly less than h, whereas a nonzero h-grid difference
has circular distance at least h. This remains true at h=1/2, where excluding
one endpoint is necessary. Thus the number of agreeing points is at most
|H|/|K|, proving (1).

On a cyclic factor of order 2^e, exactly 2^min(g,e) elements are killed by
2^g. Multiplying these counts proves (2).

For sharpness take w=0, B=I, x0=0 and A diagonal with entries 2^(-e_i).
The complete residue image is the coordinate product of the cyclic groups.
In coordinate i the arc [-h/2,h/2) contains exactly
2^max(e_i-g,0) of its 2^e_i equally spaced points. If e_i<=g it contains just
zero. If e_i>g its length is exactly 2^(e_i-g) grid spacings, and the half-open
interval has exactly that many grid points, even when endpoints lie on the
grid. Multiplying gives equality in (2). This establishes sharpness for
arbitrary dimension, exponent list and precision gap.

## Restoration interpretation and limits

For unchanged source x and exact target additions, the pair

y += Q_w(Ax); y -= Q_v(Ax)

restores every arbitrary dirty target y precisely on the agreement set. The
defect is independent of y, so a nonzero defect cannot disappear for a special
choice of dirty target in this exact, unbounded arithmetic model. Equations
(1) and (2) therefore also bound the fraction of parameter residues on which
this pair restores. They do not bound approximation error or admit internal
rounding, saturation, modular targets, hidden source changes or aliasing.

The invariant-factor bound need not be the exact maximum for a fixed embedded
H. For instance H generated by (1/4,1/2), at g=1 and zero offset, has only one
agreeing point among four, although (1) permits two. Bounded input boxes need
not have the complete-period density; a singleton safe box can have density
one. Arbitrarily changing the parameter distribution also defeats a frequency
interpretation. Odd-order groups give a valid but potentially uninformative
bound because their 2^g-torsion is trivial.

No selected supplier, physical precision saving, complete multiplication
theorem, independent human review or formal proof-kernel certification follows.
