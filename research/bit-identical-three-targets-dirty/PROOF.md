# Nine-call minimum for three identical invertible targets with dirty workspace

This is a written proof in a formal abelian register model. It relies on
the named retained theorems below; the new finite controls do not replay
their full proofs or establish a multiplication theorem.

## 1. Statement, normalization and existing inputs

Let T be an invertible rational 3-by-3 matrix and let P be a rank-three
idempotent over F2 on any finite R-dimensional scalar register space,
where R >= 3. For a nonzero rank-one rational 3-by-3 matrix A and a binary
rank-one idempotent N, set

```text
D_N([A]) = I + ([A] - 1) N.
```

The symbols belong to the group algebra over F2 of additive rational
matrices, so `[A][B] = [A+B]`. We prove that

```text
product_j D_Nj([Aj]) = I + ([T] - 1) P                  (1)
```

requires at least nine factors, and that nine suffice. A basis adapted to
P gives the diagonal endpoint `[T], [T], [T], 1, ..., 1`. Thus arbitrary
dirty roles are restored, not assumed zero. Scalar conjugations preserve
the paid alphabet. Common rational left multiplication `A -> T^-1 A` is
an additive automorphism preserving rank and reduces the common target to
I3 when needed.

We use the following published statements, with their separate scopes:

1. **Three-register theorem.** Ali Romman's PR468, retained in
   `research/bit-three-register-lower-bound/README.md`, sections 1 and 10,
   excludes eight calls for three invertible lower-triangular targets on
   exactly three scalar registers. It applies to three identical
   invertible T after the common normalization above. Its proof includes
   a completed finite calculation; that calculation is not rerun here.
2. **At most one rational relation, arbitrary scalar registers.**
   woklerx's PR1456, head
   `83b6ad2ace819a96128131a3600ee914a66dd0dd`, proves that if k paid
   rank-one frames have rational span at least `k-1`, the sum of the
   rational ranks of diagonal target frames is at most k. Zero frames
   on restored dirty roles are explicitly allowed. No common triangular
   target flag is needed for that theorem.

The low-span and span-four projection arguments used below come from
section 3 of the retained PR468 proof and its eumemic PR288 / huxint
PR203 lineage. We give their needed identical-target/dirty-role forms
explicitly, including the block normalization step.

## 2. Projection tools and low frame span

All arguments can be restricted to the subgroup generated by the finitely
many paid frames. This group is finitely generated and torsion-free, so it
is free abelian. Its group algebra is a Laurent polynomial domain and has
a fraction field.

Write `N_j = x_j y_j^T`. For k nontrivial factors over that fraction field,
the ordered product has the form

```text
product_j (I + a_j x_j y_j^T) = I + X L Y^T,
X = (x_1 ... x_k),  Y = (y_1 ... y_k),
```

where L is upper triangular with nonzero diagonal `a_1,...,a_k`.
Consequently, if the product equals I, Sylvester's inequality gives

```text
rank(X) + rank(Y) <= k.                               (2)
```

More generally, a product-minus-identity of rank W requires both active
scalar spans to have dimension at least W. Rational linear projections
of the paid frames induce group-algebra homomorphisms, so these bounds
remain available after any such projection.

**Low-span lemma with dirty roles.** In address dimension m, suppose the
paid rank-one frame span has dimension at most m and the endpoint has W
invertible m-by-m targets plus any number of zero targets. Then at least
mW calls are necessary.

Every target frame occurs as a positional subset sum in the formal
product, hence belongs to the paid span. A span of fewer than m rank-one
basis matrices cannot contain an invertible target. At dimension m,
choose a basis `B_i = u_i v_i^T` from actual paid frames and write one
invertible target as `sum_i c_i B_i`. Its invertibility implies that both
the left and right factor matrices are invertible and that every c_i is
nonzero. Common rational left and right basis changes make the frame
span diagonal. Each nonzero paid rank-one frame then lies on exactly one
coordinate line. Every invertible target has a nonzero coefficient on
every line. Projection to one coefficient gives product-minus-identity
of scalar rank W, requiring at least W calls on that line. The m line
assignments are disjoint. Zero target frames add no rank.

**Two identical targets.** Two active copies of an invertible rational
3-by-3 T require six calls, even with arbitrary dirty workspace. It
suffices to exclude five: splitting a frame as `A/2 + A/2` with the same
idempotent pads every shorter positive-length word to five. If the
rational frame span d is at most three, the low-span lemma gives six.
If d is at least four, five calls have at most one rational relation, so
PR1456 gives the same bound. Zero calls cannot give the target.

On a one-dimensional scalar block, one active copy of T costs at least
three calls: every surviving scalar idempotent is 1, the target exponent
is the sum of its paid frames, and rational rank subadditivity applies.

## 3. Frame span at most four, with identical targets and dirty roles

Suppose a word has W copies of the same invertible rational 3-by-3
target and any number of zero targets. We show that rational paid-frame
span at most four forces at least 3W calls. The low-span lemma treats
span at most three.

At dimension four, choose a basis of rank-one paid matrices. The
determinant of a linear combination is nonzero for some coefficients.
Its nonzero terms use three distinct rank-one basis matrices: a repeated
rank-one summand contributes zero to a determinant. Therefore three
basis matrices have independent left factors and independent right
factors. Common invertible rational left and right maps send them to
`E11,E22,E33`. The fourth basis matrix becomes `B = u v^T`, and the
span consists of `D + t B` with D diagonal.

These maps also send the common target to an invertible matrix C. They
do **not** in general preserve the target I3; we retain C explicitly.

If every coordinate of u and v is nonzero, consider distinct i,k,l. The
minor of `D+tB` on rows i,k and columns i,l equals `D_ii t u_k v_l`.
Hence any rank-one matrix with nonzero t has D=0. Every paid rank-one
matrix lies on one of the four independent basis lines. Projecting to
one line requires W calls if C uses that line and zero otherwise. By
rank subadditivity an invertible C uses at least three lines. The sum
of these disjoint call requirements is at least 3W.

Otherwise u or v has a zero coordinate. Every vertex in a directed
cycle of the graph of B belongs to `supp(u) intersect supp(v)`, whose
size is at most two. Permuting coordinates therefore puts the whole
span into block upper triangular form with diagonal block sizes at
most two. A rank-one matrix in this form has at most one nonzero
diagonal block: nonzero entries in two diagonal blocks would force a
nonzero entry in the lower intervening block. Assign each paid call
to its nonzero diagonal block, if it has one. Calls with all diagonal
blocks zero contribute to none of the following projected words.

The common target C lies in this block upper triangular span, and
each of its diagonal blocks C_b is invertible. Project to one diagonal
address block, then multiply every projected paid frame and target on
the left by `C_b^-1`. This is an additive automorphism of that block,
preserves each nonzero projected frame's rank one, and changes the
common block target to I_b. It does not alter the disjoint assignment
of original calls to address blocks.

A one-dimensional address block needs W calls by scalar target rank.
For a two-dimensional block, let E count its assigned calls and U the
number whose normalized upper entry is nonzero. Projecting onto that
upper entry gives the identity endpoint, since the normalized target
is I2. Let r_X and r_Y be the scalar column and row ranks of those U
calls. Equation (2) gives `r_X+r_Y <= U`. For diagonal coordinate i,
let l_i count lower-triangular rank-one frames with nonzero ith
diagonal entry. The diagonal projection's product-minus-identity has
scalar rank W, so

```text
W <= r_X + l_i,     W <= r_Y + l_i.
```

A lower-triangular rank-one frame has at most one nonzero diagonal
entry, so `l_1+l_2 <= E-U`. Adding all four inequalities gives

```text
4W <= 2(r_X+r_Y+l_1+l_2) <= 2E.
```

Thus a two-dimensional address block needs 2W calls. Block dimensions
sum to three and assigned calls are disjoint, giving 3W in total.
This argument uses arbitrary scalar matrix size and permits all
restored dirty roles.

## 4. First-order augmentation bounds the scalar projector span

Suppose an eight-call word satisfies (1), and let H be the additive
subgroup generated by its eight paid frames. Its free-abelian rank d
is the rational dimension of their frame span. The nonzero formal
coefficient P at `[T]` shows that T belongs to H: at least one
positional subset in the expanded product has sum T.

Let `V = H/2H`, let a_j be the image of A_j in V, and let t be the image
of T. The a_j span the d-dimensional binary space V. For the
augmentation ideal I of F2[H], `I/I^2` identifies with V. Taking degree
one in (1) gives the exact tensor identity

```text
sum_j a_j tensor N_j = t tensor P.                    (3)
```

This uses the actual finitely generated H. Applying augmentation to
the divisible ambient rational group, or reducing rational matrix
entries modulo two, would not justify this step.

Set `S = span_F2{N_1,...,N_8}`. If t=0, every functional on V gives a
relation among the N_j. The surjection `F2^8 -> V` has injective dual,
so the d such relations are independent. Hence

```text
dim S <= 8-d.                                        (4)
```

If t is nonzero, applying a functional taking t to one shows that P
belongs to S. The `(d-1)`-dimensional annihilator of t yields independent
relations in the same way, so

```text
P in S,       dim S <= 9-d.                           (5)
```

Repeated frames and repeated projectors are permitted throughout.

## 5. A scalar span of dimension at most three compresses to three registers

Choose a basis of S from actual paid rank-one projectors, and let U
be the span of their image lines. Every N_j has image contained in U,
so every factor acts trivially on the quotient by U. In particular
the formal coefficient P of product-minus-identity has image in U.
Thus

```text
im P <= U,     3 <= dim U <= dim S <= 3.
```

It follows that `U = im P`. Restrict every factor to U. Each projector
retains rank one: its nonzero image vector belongs to U and is fixed
by the idempotent. The restricted endpoint is `[T] I3`, contradicting
the retained three-register theorem. This does not assume clean
workspace or a simultaneously invariant complementary subspace.

## 6. A four-dimensional scalar span containing P is reducible enough

Assume `dim S = 4` and `P in S`. Select a basis `N_1,...,N_4` of S
from actual rank-one idempotents. Their binary traces are one, while
`trace(P) = 3 mod 2 = 1`. P's expansion in this basis therefore has
odd Hamming weight. Weight one is impossible because P has rank
three. Relabel so that

```text
P = N_1 + N_2 + N_3.
```

Write `N_i = x_i y_i^T` for i=1,2,3, and set
`X = (x_1 x_2 x_3)` and `Y^T = (y_1^T; y_2^T; y_3^T)`.
Since `P = X Y^T` has rank three, X and Y^T have full column and row
rank. From `P^2 = P`, multiplication by a left inverse of X and a
right inverse of Y^T gives `Y^T X = I3`. Hence these first three
projectors are mutually orthogonal coordinate projectors on im P
and annihilate ker P.

Write `Q = N_4 = x y^T` and set `U = im P + span{x}`. Every paid
projector has image in U, preserves U, and retains rank one on U.
If U has dimension three, the previous section applies. Otherwise
choose coordinates on U with the first three vectors x_1,x_2,x_3
and fourth vector `(I-P)x`, which is nonzero and lies in ker P. In
these coordinates

```text
P = diag(1,1,1,0),
x = (a,1),    y^T = (b^T,c),
a dot b + c = 1,    a,b in F2^3, c in F2.
```

If c=0, the fourth coordinate line is annihilated by Q and by all
three coordinate projectors, and therefore by every paid projector.
Quotienting by that line gives the endpoint `[T] I3`. Each quotient
projector remains rank one. Otherwise its image would lie in the
common annihilated line, making its square zero, contrary to its
being a nonzero idempotent. The retained three-register theorem
again excludes eight calls.

If c=1, `a dot b = 0`. Because there are three active coordinates,
some i satisfies `a_i b_i = 0`. If `b_i = 0`, the coordinate line
`span(e_i)` is invariant under Q and the coordinate projectors. If
`a_i = 0`, the complementary coordinate hyperplane is invariant.
Thus every paid projector preserves one of these subspaces. The
restriction and quotient endpoints consist of one copy of `[T]`
and two copies of `[T]` plus one dirty identity role, in one order
or the other.

A rank-one idempotent preserving a subspace induces a rank-one
idempotent on exactly one of restriction and quotient and zero on
the other. Both diagonal blocks are idempotent and their ranks
sum to at most one. They cannot both be zero, since the resulting
strict block triangular matrix would square to zero. Therefore the
eight original calls partition between the two induced words.
Section 2 requires at least `3+6 = 9` calls in total, a contradiction.

## 7. Exhaustion of cases, shorter words and attainment

For an eight-call word with rational frame span d, section 3 excludes
`d <= 4` with W=3 active targets. For `d >= 5`, if t=0, (4) gives
`dim S <= 3`, and section 5 applies. If t is nonzero, (5) gives
`P in S` and `dim S <= 4`. Section 5 treats dimension at most three;
section 6 treats dimension four. This covers all rational frames
and every finite amount of restored dirty workspace.

Every shorter positive-length word can be padded to eight because

```text
D_N([A]) = D_N([A/2]) D_N([A/2])
```

for an idempotent N; both new frames remain nonzero and rank one.
The zero-length word does not realize (1). Therefore fewer than
nine calls are impossible.

For attainment, decompose T into its three column matrices
`T = sum_i (T e_i) e_i^T`. Each is nonzero rank one because T is
invertible. Apply these three frames on each of three active scalar
coordinate projectors, fixing every dirty coordinate. The nine
factors give three copies of `[T]`. A binary conjugation gives (1)
for every rank-three idempotent P.

## 8. Scope, attribution and remaining obligations

The theorem permits all rational rank-one paid frames, all finite
scalar register counts, arbitrary dirty inputs restored exactly,
and free binary scalar basis changes. It retains the formal
torsion-free abelian address model, binary rank-one idempotent paid
projectors, and three identical invertible dimension-three targets.
It does not prove the corresponding statement for different
targets, four active targets, another address dimension, higher-rank
paid projectors, nonabelian address operations or nonlinear gates.

The proof depends on Ali Romman's PR468 three-register theorem and
woklerx's PR1456 one-relation theorem. The low-span, flag and Laurent
tools retain the eumemic PR288 / huxint PR203 / Ali Romman lineage.
Tuafo's PR1604 supplies the preceding finite dyadic ternary case;
its alphabet census is not an input to this unrestricted-rational
argument. The new step is the augmentation/scalar-span reduction
and the rank-three idempotent analysis with arbitrary dirty roles.

This package provides a written extension and finite corroborating
checks, not a fresh full replay of PR468, a Lean proof, physical
supplier admission, a new kappa, or a complete multiplication theorem.
