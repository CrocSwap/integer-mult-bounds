# Two quadratic masks save one pairing rank under a five-cube rank cap

**Scoped structural result / research note. No new kappa or physical supplier.**

On the 32-port quadratic five-cube, let `p` be parity, `h=x0*x1`, and
`k=(1+x1)*x2`. Requiring every copied term to have pairing rank at most 14,
the common code `span_F2{1,p,h,k}` has exact minimum total cost **47**.
Every common three-dimensional subcode containing `span{1,p}` either cannot
decode under that cap or costs at least **48**. Thus using both nonlinear
masks together strictly helps this constrained objective, even with arbitrary
finite term counts allowed in the comparator.

The complete result covers **720 labelled codes** generated by two products
of signed coordinate literals on distinct two-coordinate supports. Every code
still has unrestricted minimum cost **32**. Under the inherited additive
stage ledger this cannot give a positive saving. The rank cap is an algebraic
pairing-rank restriction; it is not an implemented physical width contract.

## Exact model and complete result

Ports are `x in F2^5`. Following Ross Richardson's PR958 and @martinxxx9's
PR1046, put

```text
j(x,y) = 5 - HammingDistance(x,y)
G[x,y] = 2(j(x,y)-1)(j(x,y)-3)             over Q
B[x,y] = 1 + p(x) + p(y)                  over F2.
```

Choose two distinct unordered coordinate pairs `A,B` among the ten pairs.
Independently complement either literal in each product, giving masks `h,k`.
The permitted code is `C=span_F2{1,p,h,k}`. There are
`choose(10,2)*2^4 = 720` distinct four-dimensional codes.

Allow any finite factorization, including redundant and repeated terms,

```text
B[x,y] = sum_i f_i(x)*g_i(y)   over F2, for ALL 1024 ordered port pairs,
f_i,g_i in C,
cost = sum_i rank_Q G[supp(f_i),supp(g_i)].
```

Zero factors may be discarded. The masks are nonlinear functions of the port
index; the data messages remain linear XOR sums. Source and reader factors
must belong to the same specified code. This is an all-pairs identity, not a
masked identity with free off-diagonal corrections.

Cube isometries reduce the 720 codes to the following three classes, each
containing 240 codes. The certificate provides and checks a concrete transport
for every code, including preservation of `G`, `B`, and both mask functions.

| Class | Canonical `h` | Canonical `k` |
|---|---|---|
| Disjoint | `x0*x1` | `x2*x3` |
| Aligned overlap | `x0*x1` | `x1*x2` |
| Opposed overlap | `x0*x1` | `(1+x1)*x2` |

The exact minimum cost, with **each** term's pairing rank bounded by the
displayed cap, is:

| Rank cap | Disjoint | Aligned overlap | Opposed overlap |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 through 11 | Impossible | Impossible | Impossible |
| 12 | Impossible | Impossible | 48 |
| 13 | Impossible | 36 | 48 |
| 14 | Impossible | 35 | **47** |
| 15 | 44 | 35 | 41 |
| 16 or larger | 32 | 32 | 32 |

Here “impossible” has an exact binary linear separator, rather than a failed
search. Every finite number has both a lower certificate and an attaining
word. In particular, no optimal cost-32 word in this family avoids rank 16.
The cost-32 baseline is inherited: `B=1*p^t+(1+p)*1^t`, with costs `16+16`.

## The strict two-mask improvement

For the opposed class set `t=h+k`. This four-term identity attains 47:

| Reader factor | Source factor | Pairing rank |
|---|---|---:|
| `h` | `p` | 7 |
| `1+p` | `1+t` | 13 |
| `p+h` | `t` | 13 |
| `1+h` | `p+t` | 14 |

Expanding the four outer products cancels the `h*p^t` and `h*t^t` terms,
leaving `(1+p)*1^t+1*p^t=B`. All ranks are recomputed over the rationals,
and the literal identity is checked on every port pair.

There are exactly three three-dimensional subcodes of `C` containing
`U=span{1,p}`: add `h`, `k`, or `h+k`. These exhaust the nonzero lines of
`C/U`, including masks differing by an element of `U`. With cap 14, the
first two cannot decode: every allowed dyad has zero constant/constant
coefficient, while `B` has coefficient one. The `h+k` subcode has exact
minimum 48, attained by four rank-12 terms. Its lower certificate permits
arbitrarily many terms. The new word's cost 47 is therefore a strict gain
over **every common one-mask subcode of this C**, not over all possible
one-mask codes or unconstrained decoders.

## Why the certificate covers every finite word

The binary evaluation matrix `F=[1,p,h,k]` has rank four. Thus
`M -> F*M*F^t` is injective, as a left inverse of `F` recovers `M`. In this
basis `B` has coefficient matrix with ones at `(0,0),(0,1),(1,0)`.
Row-major encoding gives target integer 19.

Each of the 225 ordered nonzero factor pairs supplies a binary 4-by-4 dyad
and its exact rational pairing rank. A finite factorization is precisely a
path from zero to target 19 in the 65,536-state XOR graph. Repetition and
overcompleteness are included; there is no word-length cutoff.

For each attainable cap regime, the certificate supplies a byte-valued
potential `d` with `d(0)=0`, `d(19)=L`, and

```text
abs(d(M XOR dyad) - d(M)) <= rank(dyad)
```

on every allowed edge. Telescoping proves every word costs at least `L`;
the displayed word proves equality. For an unattainable regime, a linear
functional annihilates every allowed dyad but evaluates to one on target 19.
These proofs do not trust the shortest-path search. Regimes between successive
integer term ranks have identical edge sets, so all caps are covered.

`generate.py` uses integer elimination with gcd normalization and Dijkstra
search. The separately written `verify.py` imports no generator or predecessor
code: it builds `G` as a Gram of the 16 Walsh characters of degrees at most two,
uses `Fraction` elimination, and checks every certificate inequality. The two
Gram formulas agree because, for `z_i=(-1)^(x_i+y_i)`,
`G=1+sum z_i+sum_(i<j) z_i*z_j=2(j-1)(j-3)`.

The checked receipt records 675 ordered rational term ranks, 737,280 transport
pair checks, and 55,934,976 undirected edge inequalities, including the three
one-mask comparisons. Eleven deliberate corruptions must reject.

## Accounting consequence and a scope control

Under the inherited per-total additive copy pricing and stage ledger, for
`s>=3` and 32 ports,

```text
D <= (s-1)*32 - s*cost <= -32.
```

This grants source access, gathers, helper arithmetic, frame changes,
restoration, idle work, precision, hosting, and every additional recursive
expense zero extra cost. Nonnegative charges only worsen the inequality.
For the cap-14 opposed witness, the favorable deficit is at most `-32-15s`.
Consequently the one-rank gain does not rescue this format or imply a new
supplier exponent. A different stage/copy ledger would require a new argument.

The restriction to coordinate-product masks matters. With general quadratic
masks `h=p*x0`, `k=(1+p)*x1`, the four dyads
`(1,p+h), (1,h), (1+p+k,1), (k,1)` decode `B` with ranks `8+8+8+8`.
The checker verifies this word and that its code lies outside all 720 codes.
It disproves extending the rank-16 necessity to arbitrary pairs of quadratic
masks. It does not disprove a broader total-cost floor of 32.

## Open next questions

Can two unrestricted quadratic masks reduce total cost below 32? The displayed
parity-conditioned control already escapes the peak-rank penalty, so the 720-code
exclusion does not answer that question. A concrete first step is to specify one
different two-dimensional quadratic quotient subspace and certify its complete
dyad graph. The blockers are the much larger mask-pair domain and, even if a
better algebraic word exists, source access and a complete physical stage
construction. [TASK.json](TASK.json) retains this scope explicitly.
