# Polynomial dirty fanout with a unit demand ideal

## Contract and statement

Let K be any commutative field and R=K[x,y], with commuting indeterminates.
There are two arbitrary dirty rows a0,a1 in R^w and a disjoint preserved
source p in R^w, where w>=1. A fixed finite straight-line word must realize

    (a0,a1,p) -> (a0+(1+xy)p, a1+x²p, p)

for every input. The only gates are

    P_i(beta):  A_i <- A_i + beta p,        price mu_i > 0;
    E_i(alpha): A_i <- A_i + alpha A_(1-i), price c_i > 0.

Here i is 0 or 1, alpha and beta are arbitrary elements of R, and c_i
charges the direction into row i. Prices are stationary, inclusive abstract
prices and independent of coefficient. There are no extra rows, free basis
changes, observations, clearing or input-dependent choices. Every original
dirt column must return exactly. An inverse execution is charged separately.
This is the contract of #5045/#5139, with a polynomial coefficient ring.

**Theorem.** The demand ideal is R, but no legal word has exactly one
nonzero source insertion. The exact all-positive-price optimum is

    min(mu0+mu1, 2mu0+2c1, 2mu1+2c0).

The exact Pareto-minimal count vectors in order (P0,P1,E0,E1) are

    (1,1,0,0), (2,0,0,2), (0,2,2,0).

The statement includes characteristic two. It is a scoped algebraic result,
not a native supplier or a complete multiplication theorem.

## The inherited theorem

We use Cohn's classical example, without reproving or auditing it:

    C = [[1+xy, x²], [-y², 1-xy]] is not in E2(K[x,y]).

Here E2 is the group generated by upper and lower elementary shears.
The source is P. M. Cohn, *On the structure of the GL2 of a ring*,
Publications Mathématiques de l'IHÉS 30, 1966, pp. 5-53, §7, printed
p. 26, unnumbered example after Proposition 7.3. See
[the primary example](https://www.numdam.org/item/PMIHES_1966__30__5_0.pdf#page=23)
and [REFERENCES.md](REFERENCES.md). Cohn excludes the larger group GE2;
only the displayed E2 consequence is used. His field hypothesis has no
characteristic restriction. This theorem is an explicit cited dependency,
not a machine-certified conclusion of this package.

## From one insertion to an elementary column

Write lambda=(1+xy,x²)^T and A=C^T. The integer polynomial identity

    (1-xy)(1+xy) + y² x² = 1

shows that lambda is unimodular, so its entries generate R. Also det A=1.

Suppose a legal word has just one nonzero insertion P_i(beta). Delete any
zero-coefficient gates, which have positive price and no effect. Let Q be
the product of the row gates after that insertion, in execution order.
Before the insertion the source column is zero; afterward it is

    lambda = beta Q e_i,             Q in E2(R).

A column of an invertible matrix is unimodular, so the ideal generated by
beta Q e_i is (beta). Since lambda generates R, beta is a unit.

Both coordinate columns e_i lie in the E2 orbit of e0. For e1 use
J=[[0,-1],[1,0]], a product of three elementary shears. For a unit b,
diag(b,b^-1) is elementary: with U(t) upper and L(t) lower,

    U(b)L(-b^-1)U(b) U(-1)L(1)U(-1) = diag(b,b^-1).

Consequently Q J_i diag(beta,beta^-1) is an elementary determinant-one
matrix H whose first column is lambda. Here J_0=I and J_1=J.

But every determinant-one completion H of this column has

    A^-1 H = [[1,t],[0,1]]

for some t in R: the first column is e0 and the determinant is one.
Thus H=A U(t). If H were elementary, A=H U(-t) would be elementary.
Transposition preserves E2, since it transposes each shear and reverses
the order of a product. This would make C elementary, contradicting the
cited theorem. Hence exactly one nonzero insertion is impossible at
either row, for words of any finite length and coefficients of any degree.

This is the new consequence of the classical example. It answers
Open next question 1 in lee-phys's #5139: principality, even the unit ideal,
does not suffice beyond its finite-commutative-ring domain.

## Arbitrary-word lower bound

We use Krzysztof Wójt's #5045 directional return and source-count argument.
For completeness its short application follows. Set p=0 and track the two
independent original dirt columns. The product of row gates must be I2.
If a nonzero upper shear occurs exactly once, all lower shears before it
and after it combine, giving L(s)U(alpha)L(t). Its upper-right entry is
alpha, so it is not I2. Swapping rows gives the other direction. Every
used direction therefore occurs at least twice. Source gates do not
affect this dirt argument.

There is at least one insertion, since row gates alone leave the source
column zero. The previous section excludes exactly one. If both rows
receive insertions, the count vector dominates (1,1,0,0). Otherwise only
row i receives insertions, at least twice. The nonzero demand in row j
requires a row gate into j, hence at least two such gates by the return
lemma. Its counts dominate (2,0,0,2) or (0,2,2,0). These cases exhaust all
finite words, regardless of interleaving or cancellation. They prove the
claimed lower envelope for every positive price vector.

## Matching constructions

Put u=1+xy and v=x². The following words are in chronological order.
They are #5045's direct and repeated-insertion templates applied to this
new polynomial demand.

    D:  P0(u), P1(v)
    T0: E1(-1), P0(v), E1(1), P0(u-v)
    T1: E0(-1), P1(u), E0(1), P1(v-u)

Each word has the required complete affine endpoint, including both dirt
columns and the source row. In T0, the first row gates cancel on dirt;
the first insertion transfers v to row 1, then the last insertion leaves
u at row 0. The row-swapped argument proves T1. Every displayed gate has
nonzero polynomial coefficient over every field, including characteristic
two. Their costs are the three entries of the theorem.

The three vectors are incomparable and each is strictly cheapest at some
positive prices. In price order (mu0,mu1,c0,c1), examples are (1,1,1,1),
(1,10,1,1), and (10,1,1,1), respectively. Since every word dominates one
of them, these are exactly the Pareto-minimal count vectors.

## What the finite checks establish

The verifier checks the Bezout identity and both inverses of A as exact
integer polynomial identities. It multiplies every complete affine word,
its inverse, and ten one-call deletions. Six completion fixtures
corroborate the coset identity; its general proof is above. A changed
alphabet control conjugates one insertion by the full A and attains the
target, exposing the essential restriction to elementary row gates.

For all x,y and all three scalar data values over F2,F3,F5,F7, it checks
every attaining word and inverse. All 87 evaluated demand pairs also
have explicit one-insertion words over their finite fields. Thus these
evaluations cannot witness polynomial non-elementarity. The obstruction
uses the cited infinite-ring theorem, not an extrapolation from a bounded
word search or a finite-ring failure. No predecessor verifier is imported
or replayed.
