# The original-coordinate transform

Let $r\ge2$, let $t=2^\ell\ge r$, and label the first $r$ **actual wires** by $\mathbb Z/r\mathbb Z$. Only the coefficient constructor knows a dense matrix. On input data the circuit emits one- or two-coordinate gates, lowers them to a fixed word, and runs that word on the complete tensor. No application of a dense DFT, linear solve, or polynomial product to a data vector occurs in this construction.

## Two stable passes for a cyclic multiplier

Write $F$ for the unnormalized negative-character DFT and $P_s=F^{-1}\operatorname{diag}(s)F$. Suppose $0<m\le |s_k|\le M$. Among $r$ points on the unit circle there is a gap of at least $1/r$ turns. Rotate its midpoint to the negative real axis with a scalar $\tau$. Take the square root $\rho_k$ of $\tau s_k$ with positive real part. Then
$$
 \operatorname{Re}\rho_k\ge\sqrt m\sin(\pi/(2r))\ge\sqrt m/r,\qquad
 \tau^{-1} P_\rho^2=P_s .
$$
This choice bounds the inverse of **every leading section** of a root call, even if a leading section of $P_s$ itself is singular. Each pass acts as identity on the extra $t-r$ coordinates. Apply the final scalar only on the true block. The Lean proof also gives a different constructive choice among $r+1$ uniform rotations with the margin $\sqrt m/(2r)$. [Formal two-circulant theorem](lean/AccretiveFactors.lean)

With rational radii and rational phase turns, sort the turns with exact comparisons. Compute a lower dyadic square root of each radius by integer square root. Generate each first-column entry privately as
$$
 h_j=\frac1r\sum_{k=0}^{r-1}\rho_k\, e^{2\pi i jk/r}.
$$
The phase routine bounds its complex error by its requested grid step and returns an exactly unit rational complex number. Round each averaged column entry to the dyadic grid. For $K=\lceil\max(1,M,1/m)\rceil$ the implementation adds $4\lceil\log_2(2+\max(r,g))\rceil+4\lceil\log_2(2+K)\rceil+16$ guard bits up to integer rounding, where $g$ is the designated phase grid or $r$. At private grid step $u$, the error per entry is at most $(3+\sqrt M)u$. The row and column sums bound the full operator error by $r(3+\sqrt M)u$. The selected margin $1/(2\max(2,g)K)$ is strictly inside the resulting numerical range. On an even grid use rotation by half a grid cell before the root. On an odd grid the negative real axis is already between two grid points. These choices permit repeated phases. [Rational polar producer](spectral.py)

A second producer accepts the Cartesian components of arbitrary nonzero Gaussian-rational multipliers without phase data. It searches among $4r$ rational unit cuts with an exact chord test. It computes dyadic roots with a verified residual and returns the correction and a strict error bound on the cyclic multiplier. The required bit lengths depend on input height, minimum modulus, maximum modulus, $r$ and target accuracy. Both producers charge these privately. The two-pass statement concerns nonsingular multipliers. The Fourier service below also applies singular and nonseparable masks. [Cartesian construction and bounds](CARTESIAN.md) · [Streamed mask consumer](FILTER.md)

Put $C=\operatorname{circ}(h)$ for the stored column and $A=C\oplus I_{t-r}$. For the nilpotent downshift $Z$, the commutator $ZA-AZ$ is a sum of two outer products, including the edge at the first hole. The inserted identity commutes with $Z$. The formal theorem uses a periodic function $f(k)=h_{k\bmod r}-[k\equiv0\bmod r]$ to express $A=I+\mathrm{embedded}(f)$. It factors the commutator through two columns even at $r=t$. The exact Hermitian test in the adapter verifies the stored column at its declared margin. If $\operatorname{Re}C\succ\mu I$, every leading section of $A$ has inverse norm at most $1/\min(1,\mu)$ by the real-part inequality on supported vectors. Consequently the old recursive Schur and displacement-rank gate generator runs on this new input with its original rank allowance. The new adapter derives both required facts. It does not assert Toeplitz structure across a padding boundary. ## Fourier signs, normalization and the holes

Use $q_j=\exp(2\pi i\,j(j+r)/(2r))$, $D_j=\overline{q_j}$ and $G=\sum_jq_j$. Replacing $j$ by $j+r$ changes the quadratic phase by an integer number of turns for both parities of $r$. The chirp identity gives $F x=D\,\operatorname{circ}(q)\,D x$. Completing the square by translating an index gives $(Fq)_k=G D_k$. Parseval gives $|G|^2=r$. Thus the **mean** Fourier transform is
$$
 F x/r=(G/r) D P_D D x .
$$
These identities use neither the primality of $r$ nor a lookup for a Gauss-sum phase. [All-moduli identities and Gauss norm](lean/ChirpTransform.lean)

The actual forward call uses the even phase grid $2r$. Put $\tau=\exp(2\pi i/(4r))$ and take the centered root of $\tau D_k$. Its real part is at least $1/(2r)$. The source receives margin $1/(4r)$. The two root passes, the entry diagonal $D$, and the exit diagonal $(G/(r\tau))D$ give precisely $F/r$. The implementation computes $G/r$ by averaging conjugates of its actual entrance chirps. Both outer diagonals take value one at a hole. Conjugating the whole forward operation and its input gives the opposite-sign mean, with the same divisor. For the represented program the conjugate wrapper has the same error because conjugation is an isometry, including when two rounding rules choose different sides of a tie. [Spectral coefficient generator](spectral.py) · [Formal two-pass identity](lean/ChirpTransform.lean) · [Physical word](tensor_word.py)

Each ideal axis map preserves the true and the padded subspaces separately. Their tensor preserves every joint true/hole pattern. On a mixed-hole pattern other valid axes still transform the data. The multiplier owns zero-initialized holes and releases the entire workspace after extraction. The filter has a stronger contract and pays one additional buffer and full-field scans to restore arbitrarily dirty hole words exactly. [Filter and restoration proof](FILTER.md)

## A computable working precision

The following deliberately loose integers specify the fast controller. They never depend on the observed cancellation in a test. Put $W=64t$ for each Fourier axis. The rounded root differs by less than $2^{-P}$ in operator norm by the preceding column estimate. The root and its inverse on the whole axis have polynomial bounds. The source component envelopes satisfy $\Lambda\le4t,\ Q\le8t^2,\ \Lambda Q\le W^3$. Equations (6), (11), (14) and (15) of the full compiler proof give completed-atom norm below $W^{40}$, fragment norm below $W^{16}$, and physical prefix and inverse-prefix norm below $W^{64}$. For the fragment bound substitute $2\ell+8\le W$ in its displayed formulas for $\alpha_*,K_{G,*},H_*$. Applying the compiler at target $P+8$ bounds the defect of the **unrounded stored rational gate program** by $2^{-P-8}$ on vectors of norm one. The source bounds apply to its actual gate word. They do not yet cover the final tensor diagonal rounding. [Source constants and execution cuts](https://github.com/CrocSwap/integer-mult-bounds/blob/e54523517d0f03e58352e647c403a5af3e525a57/research/rational-unitary-fft/FULL-PROOF.md)

Any gate and its inverse have norm at most $W^{128}$, from adjacent cuts. In its sixteen-H word only the middle pivot diagonal and its inverse can exceed a fixed constant. A pivoted ratio has modulus at most one. Both arguments of the three-H identity have modulus between two and four. Bound each of the component shear diagonals and its inverse by 64, each swap factor by two and the pivot by $2W^{128}$ in both directions. A partial word or its inverse is bounded by $64^{40}(2W^{128})$. Completed routing switches are permutations. Even a cut inside one routed switch adds at most a factor four. Multiplying by the preceding original-layer prefix leaves each lowered root prefix and its inverse below $W^{400}$. Prefixes across both roots, including their bounded true-coordinate entry and exit scalars, stay below $W^{1200}$. An arbitrary interval costs at most the square of this bound. Local endpoint error from rounding the first column and the scalar factors as well as synthesizing the two rational circuits is below $W^{16}2^{-P}$, by the two-term product identity. In particular a bad local approximation is not compounded at every lowered gate.

For the tensor let $E=4096\sum_i(\ell_i+6)$. The preceding interval estimates give maximum-norm gain at most $2^E$ for any interval of the exact stored word. This includes the $\sqrt T$ conversion from spectral to maximum norm, any partial H pack, and the layers for entry and exit. An external tensor diagonal consists of at most $d$ factors. Any subproduct of its exact scalar factors is bounded by $\prod_i(64t_i)^{400}$. Rounding the individual factors, computing each suffix from its own coordinates, and applying it to the data give a local maximum-coordinate defect at most $2^{-P+2E}(d+1)^2(1+\|z\|_\infty)$. For example, unfold an odometer suffix as a chain of at most $d$ scalar multiplies. Each arithmetic error contributes at most one grid unit times the product of the magnitudes of the subsequent factors. Each rounded factor has magnitude at most twice its local polynomial bound. Unfolding costs at most a further $2^d$, already within $2^{2E}$. A native H on represented words is exact before its charged change of scale. Error in the rational-word endpoints against the true tensor operator is bounded by the $d$-term tensor product identity and the preceding local endpoint estimate.

The number of complete external arithmetic or H stages is at most $J=20000(d+1)(\max_i\ell_i+2)^6$. This follows from (15), the $2\ell_i-1$ routing rows in each direction, the seventeen gate diagonals, two root passes, and at most $d$ serial packs in total. $J$ is a conservative precision bound. The tighter count for time is polylogarithmic in $p$ because there are $O((\log p)^3)$ width classes and $O(\log p)$ additional packs. Put
$$
 P=4p+8E+4\operatorname{bitlength}(J(d+1))+64.
$$
For every prefix the bootstrap in the outer proof, with interval norm $2^E$ and the displayed local defect, gives raw error less than $2^{-4p}$ on every represented half-disk input after adding the endpoint bias. Since $\sum\ell_i=\log_2T=O(p)$ and $d\le\log_2T$, $P=O(p)$. Integer magnitude guards and the exact native internal grid add $O(p)$ bits. Private rational setup can visit much wider intervals. Its total time and historical space are $2^{O((\log p)^3)}=o(n)$. In the trace theorem take $M=R=2^E$, local defect $\epsilon=(d+1)^2 2^{-P+2E}$, and endpoint bias $\beta\le(d+1)2^{-P+E}$. The tensor endpoint telescope gives the last bound, including the single change of norm. The number of stages is at most $J$. Then $JM\epsilon<1/2$, and the represented raw error is bounded by $(4J(d+1)^2+d+1)2^{-P+4E}<2^{-4p}$. This instantiates the scalar inequalities for both signs with the same external grid. [Fused trace to integer product](lean/TraceProduct.lean)

For the physical-to-grid bridge, insert zeros at all input holes. The ideal tensor preserves each zero hole sector. The computed forward error at every padded coordinate is below $2^{-4p}$. Public truncation toward zero at step $2^{-p}$ therefore returns exactly zero in every hole on each operand. Their public pointwise products are zero there too. The padded field entering the opposite call has the same holes as the zero extension of its represented original-coordinate input.
