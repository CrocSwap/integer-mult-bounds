## Mathematical review

The paired W(D4) action preserves input/output norms and error. A pair of
orbits need not induce a complete bipartite graph. Transitivity at each end
instead makes it biregular. If orbit flow f(I,J) is spread uniformly over
its actual edges, every input vertex has fractional degree1 and every
output degree at most1. Summing over any input subset proves Hall's
inequality, yielding an integral finite injection. Conversely a finite
matching aggregates to a feasible quotient flow. This is a classical
symmetry/fractional-matching argument, applied to this explicit graph.

Every strict Hall cut is the complete union of its listed input orbits;
its output list equals the complete allowed neighborhood. A positive
strict deficiency and a saturated inclusive flow prove the exact optimum
without trusting binary-search coverage. The strict-lower receipts need
only the Hall cut; maximality of every strict cut is not assumed from an
unchecked flow value. At radius9, maximal deficiency64 is separately proved
by full-vertex matching. Radius10 follows by restricting the radius11 upper
injection and including the radius9 lower obstruction.
