\begin{abstract}
We connect the author's certified hybrid matrix circuit to Gaussian parity
arithmetic and to the community's finite integer-multiplication search.
The actual order-16 circuit has 2,208 mixed-input products and an integral
numerator $N=8AB$. We prove sharp finite-precision late-quotient contracts,
implement an exact Gaussian interpreter preserving the hybrid DAG's sharing,
and identify a valid commuting phase-operator domain together with its
physical-child closure obstruction. Domain-aware catalogues and exact
matching certificates prevent arithmetic counts from being substituted for
uncharged network ranks. We combine the exact characteristic-slack objective with a new finite compiler
search over retired dependency circuits, admissible carried-signal exchanges,
and consistently renamed point coordinates. The accepted variant changes the
literal words and their ordered fixed-basis child profiles. It gives conditional
$\kappa=5.10403292611078\times10^{-5}$, strictly above PR \#62's strongest stacked
witness. The new circuit is freshly checked on every input and dirty basis
vector in both orientations, with independent frame reconstruction and exact
bounded-minor/CRT profiles. 53 standalone Lean declarations include
universal dirty-complement and coordinate-conjugacy proofs and a concrete
characteristic/assembly certificate. The frozen old profile is provably
infeasible at the new bit saving. Analytic, compiler-transfer and tape hypotheses
retain their inherited scope. The matrix arithmetic remains separately scoped;
its scalar product count is not inserted as an uncharged network rank.
\end{abstract}

\section{The sharp late-quotient bridge}
Let an integer circuit have an independently established contract
$N(x)=dF(x)$, $d>0$. For every integer modulus $M$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:quotient}
 N(x)\bmod(dM)=d\bigl(F(x)\bmod M\bigr),\qquad
 \frac{N(x)\bmod(dM)}d=F(x)\bmod M.
\end{equation}
This follows from the integer scaled-remainder identity; the quotient is
exact. It also holds for a simulator proved congruent to $N$ modulo $dM$.
Arbitrary dirty values may first be subtracted in that same residue ring:
$((z+dF)\bmod dM-z\bmod dM)\bmod dM$ has the same exact quotient.

For the actual $d=8$ matrix circuit and $M=2^p$, evaluate its integer
instructions modulo $2^{p+3}$, then divide the output representatives by
eight. This recovers $AB\bmod2^p$. Simply reducing the numerator modulo two
loses the result: $A=B=E_{00}$ gives numerator residue zero but product one.
For every $p\ge1$, products $0$ and $2^{p-1}$ have equal numerator residues
modulo $2^{p+2}$ and different requested $p$-bit results. Consequently
\emph{no function of that smaller numerator residue alone} can recover all
outputs. The $p+3$ boundary is sharp for this interface, not a lower bound
on multiplication algorithms.

The universal recovery and collision statements are kernel checked in
\texttt{LateQuotient.lean} and \texttt{SharpQuotient.lean}. Fresh actual-circuit
controls cover all 256 binary $2\times2$ input pairs embedded in order 16,
42 signed dense cases, and precisions $1,2,3,4,8,16,31$. The lifted ring and
its extra three numerator bits must be charged; this is not a native
one-bit XOR-network implementation.

\section{An exact Gaussian DAG, with sharing retained}
Put $\pii=1+i$. Align every canonical Gaussian-dyadic input to a common
numerator tag $E$. Homogeneity makes the product grid $2E$. Since the
actual circuit identity holds over the commutative ring $\Gi$, both
coordinates of every output numerator are divisible by eight at this
grid. Exact coordinate quotients followed by canonical parity reduction
return the matrix product without an extra denominator beyond $2E$.
This does not assert that multiplying two $E$-tag inputs keeps tag $E$.

The shared interpreter first forms the actual 48 pairs of $4\times4$
outer blocks, executes their 46-product leaves, then recombines their

\section{A valid operator domain, and the remaining obstruction}
The scalar leaf cannot be inserted recursively on arbitrary matrix blocks.
For $U=E_{12}$, $V=E_{21}$, $B=0$, the first leaf output has the defect
$UV-VU=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)$ instead of zero, including a nonzero
characteristic-two specialization. More generally, the paired-inner
formulas have precisely the defects
\[
 \sum_h[A_{i,2h},A_{i,2h+1}]
 \quad(j=0),\qquad
 \sum_h[B_{2h+1,j},A_{i,2h+1}]\quad(j>0).
\]
Thus explicit required commutations can certify a restricted block domain;
scalar correctness alone cannot. With a fixed terminal leaf and repeated
valid 48-product outer levels, the recurrence has exponent $\log_4 48$;
the terminal count changes its constant, not that exponent.

There is a natural positive domain in the community's complex operators.
XOR translations $X_u$ commute, and
$C_u=(iI+X_u)/\pii$ lies in their commutative group algebra. The new
\texttt{CommutingPhases.lean} proves translation and charged raw-phase
commutation for arbitrary functions and masks. Four complete order-16
operator cases on dimensions two and three verify 1,024 outputs and 6,144
kernel coefficients independently by exact XOR convolution.

However, the \emph{algebra} being closed does not make every leaf operand
an eligible physical phase child. $C_1+C_2$ has eigenvalues
$2,1+i,1+i,2i$; it is invertible but not a phase child or a scalar multiple
of one. Its average still has unequal spectral magnitudes; $C_1-C_2$ is
nonzero and singular. Gauges also matter:
when $u\cdot v=1$,
$[C_u,Z_v]=(1-i)X_uZ_v$, a full-rank defect. Both closure and gauge
counterexamples are checked exactly. Accordingly, 2,208 operator products
cannot be counted as 2,208 cheap child calls without a paid implementation
and a complete rank/frame schedule. This is a precise search eligibility
criterion and obstruction, not an uncharged exponent argument.

\section{Profile-aware compilation and exact matching search}
This one-hour research run used only sources already available through PR
\#62, construction commit
\texttt{ad0f25ff7b23cff7f08ad237c2254e6ecf74257e}. No newer community
activity was consulted. The scalar interval/core-aware pair graph, complete
input/output inventory and fixed $I+J$ rational basis are retained. The compiler
changes which retired dependency circuit it clears, which admissible carried
input it retains, and, in the tested coordinate variants, the consistent
point-label assignment of the finite word.

Write $F_a(t)=t\exp(a\log(m/t))$ and
$\mathcal S_a=\sum_t n_tF_a(t)-mW$. Strict feasibility is
$\mathcal S_a<0$. This extends our previous all-cardinality bookkeeping:
the objective accounts for the complete child profile and wire normalization.
When $W$ and total rank are fixed, fewer wires or smaller linear rank growth
cannot distinguish the variants. The actual contiguous pivot blocks determine
the recursive cost. For $0<\alpha<1$, splitting a block of width $u+v$ into
$u,v$ raises its power cost, since $u^\alpha+v^\alpha>(u+v)^\alpha$.

\paragraph{Choosing a dependency circuit.}
For a frame transition $A\to B$, let
$C_a(A,B)=\sum_t n_t(A,B)F_a(t)$ use its actual fixed-basis profile.
Clearing a retired target at $A_s$ into $G$ with providers $z$ is priced by
\[
 C_a(A_s,G)-C_a(A_s,I)+
 \sum_z\bigl[C_a(A_z,G)+C_a(G,I)-C_a(A_z,I)\bigr].
\]
Every target/provider promotion and clearing XOR is emitted. The expression
includes changed deferred cleanup, under the local assumption that other
retired providers remain retired until cleanup. Future reuse couples the
choices; this is a search heuristic, not a global additive optimum.
All such target choices have the same linear rank change $r(G)-h$, and other
retired providers have zero net rank change after cleanup. A carried edge has
rank change $-h$. Therefore the implementation can price $F_a(t)-t$ to avoid
floating cancellation while preserving candidate order at fixed cardinality.
\texttt{RankFlowPrice.lean} proves these identities and constant-shift order
equivalence. Variable-cardinality comparisons retain the full rank correction.

\paragraph{Changing the carried signals.}
For a value produced at $P$, preserved through $G$ and consumed at $T$, the
no-reclamation edge price is
\[
 C_a(G,T)-C_a(G,I)-C_a(0,P)-C_a(P,T).
\]
Bounded single-edge donor/use exchanges retain cardinality, distinct uses and
linear independence modulo mandatory outputs. We also test two-edge cycles.
The price oracle uses actual rational pivot profiles; floating prices propose
choices and the complete exact final characteristic accepts them. No weighted
matroid optimum or general network optimum is claimed.

\paragraph{Dirty state and coordinate freedom.}
The inverse of a legal XOR encoder $M$ is its literal reversed word. With
source map $V$ and scatter $J$, the two-pass schedule
$M,J,M^{-1},V,M,J,M^{-1},V$ sends arbitrary $(x,y,s)$ to
$(x,y\mathbin{\oplus}JMVx,s)$. Thus $JMV=I$ is the separate fresh-transfer
contract, and dirty scratch cancels for different legal complements.
\texttt{DirtyComplementSafety.lean} proves this universally.
\texttt{FreshKernelReclaim.lean} proves that a zero fresh dependency still
permits nonzero dirty state. Future-aware unit and kernel completions are
explored, with their unsuccessful cases retained in the search record.

A point permutation renames every frame core/cover, source triple, output
triple and scatter-bank index together. Frame containment is preserved,
$I+J$ commutes with the permutation, and the full triple-pair data inventory
is unchanged. The emitted word records the new canonical ports explicitly.
\texttt{CoordinateConjugacy.lean} proves full-word conjugacy, mask containment,
triple-family bijection and the $I+J$ entry identity. Actual ordered profiles
are still freshly computed; symmetry alone does not determine their cost.

width $529$. Physical roles are 27,918 and
36,586; literal scalar XOR counts are
148,852 and 205,629.
There are 2,296,434,724 recursive child calls versus the frozen profile's
2,301,104,805. These counts are explicitly charged finite schedules, not
runtime measurements. Exact rational certification gives
\[
 a_b=\frac{510429345092917}{10000000000000000000},\qquad h=10^{-18},\qquad
 \kappa=\frac{255201646305539}{5000000000000000000}=5.10403292611078\times10^{-5}.
\]
The increase over $5.101691\times10^{-5}$ is approximately
$0.04590489\%$ in exponent saving. The dyadic corollary remains $2^{-15}$.
The old complete PR \#62 profile has a rigorous lower characteristic above
one at this new $a_b$, while the new upper characteristic is below one.
This verifies a structural profile improvement. All 47 assembly inequalities
and seven margins pass. The concrete Lean instance derives rounded weights,
the new moment upper, the old cubic-Taylor lower rejection and strict numerical
comparisons in the kernel. Logarithm/exponential enclosures and the inherited
all-size frame/compiler, tape, routing, recovery and eventual setup interfaces

profiles and CRT checks are freshly replayed.

For a fixed admissible carrier graph, a separate exact matching certificate
can bound what remains to search. For integer edge costs $q_{ij}$, row
potentials $u_i$ and column potentials $v_j\le0$ satisfying
$u_i+v_j\le q_{ij}$ give the lower bound
\[
 \sum_i u_i+\sum_j v_j\ \le\ \sum_i q_{i,p(i)}
\]
for every injective complete assignment $p$. Equality for a proposed
assignment certifies optimality for that recorded integer objective.
Private dummy columns and a proved sufficient penalty encode maximum
cardinality. Quantized objectives must remain distinguished from
unrounded logarithmic or characteristic weights; enclosing intervals and
rounding error are retained when deriving bounds for those real objectives.

An actual 10,639-edge example passes 17,619 integer dual inequalities with
primal equal to dual. Further examples exercise the searched graphs.
The new \texttt{MatchingDual.lean} proves weak duality, attainment and
optimality for their exact integer objective. Source-to-certificate binding
is independently replayed by Python, rather than claimed as a large
numerical Lean instance.

\paragraph{Removing an unnecessary cardinality restriction.}
Maximum-cardinality matching alone need not optimize the complete
characteristic. Write $F_t=t\exp(a\log(m/t))$, so
$\Phi=\sum n_tF_t/(mW)$. With $K$ selected carriers on one axis $h$,
repeat count $r$, unmatched width $W_0$, unmatched scaled numerator $A_0$,
and internal edge changes $\Delta F_e$, the actual cost model gives
\[
 W_K=W_0-rK,\qquad
 A_K=A_0+r\left(\sum_e\Delta F_e-K(F_h+F_{m-2h})\right).
\]
Consequently the fixed-target feasibility slack is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:allcard}
 A_K-mW_K=A_0-mW_0+
 r\sum_e\left(\Delta F_e+m-F_h-F_{m-2h}\right).
\end{equation}
Optimizing these complete edge costs with zero-cost private unmatched
columns compares \emph{all cardinalities}, including the change in wire
normalization. There is no maximum-cardinality penalty. The bookkeeping
identity is kernel checked in \texttt{FeasibilityNormalForm.lean}; its
geometric/profile input contract remains the explicitly replayed one.

At an earlier recorded fixed bit target, the actual order-25 graph passes 23,541
integer dual inequalities for this objective, with primal and dual both
22,172,308,087,581. It selects 7,894 carriers without forcing their number.
Rigorous rational cost intervals bound the proposed matching's true
unrounded slack-objective regret by $4.162\times10^{-9}$ before the repeat
factor and $(mW)^{-1}$ normalization. This certifies the quantized fixed-DAG
objective and bounds remaining true-cost room; it does not certify global
network or exponent optimality. This assignment is a search certificate,
not a replacement for the separately physically inherited candidate.
