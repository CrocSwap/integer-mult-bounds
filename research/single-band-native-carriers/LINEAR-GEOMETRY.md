$$W=128[4(s+1)+6]=512s+1280,\qquad d=2^{\lfloor\log_2(L/W)\rfloor}.$$

Distribute the $L$ bits across $d$ actual axes of adjacent widths. Write $\ell$ for their maximum. The construction uses the first coprime odd base in each alternating square band. ## A uniform elementary bound for the coprime search

For $s\ge512$, every interval of $H\ge2^{192s}$ consecutive integers contains an integer that is prime to any prescribed set of at most $2^s$ primes. Include 2 in the set when the answer must be odd. The following proof applies at every translated interval, including one adjacent to an arbitrary previous product.

Put $r=\lceil\log_2(2s)\rceil$, $z=2^{2^r}$. Then $2^{2s}\le z<2^{4s}$. Partition the primes at most $z$ into bands $j=0,\ldots,r-1$. In band $j$ take the dyadic slices $2^i<a\le2^{i+1}$ for $2^{r-j-1}\le i<2^{r-j}$. Put the prime 2 into the last band. Restrict each band to the prescribed primes. All primes in band $j$ are at most $z^{2^{-j}}$. At each dyadic cut all primes in $(m,2m]$ divide ${2m\choose m}\le4^m$. With $m=2^i$ there are at most $2^{i+1}/i$ such primes. Their reciprocal sum is at most $2/i$. There are $u$ slices with $u\le i<2u$ in a band, so their reciprocal sum is at most 2. Including the possible prime 2 gives $R_j=\sum_{a\in j}1/a<3$.

At a particular integer let $g_j$ be the indicator of no divisibility in band $j$. Let $U_j$ be the alternating inclusion–exclusion sum to even order $k_j=20+2j$. Let $O_j$ be the unsigned term of order $k_j+1$. If there are $v$ hits, the terms of degree $i$ are ${v\choose i}$. The alternating binomial identity gives $0\le g_j\le U_j$ and $U_j-g_j\le O_j$. By telescoping products of nonnegative factors,

$$\prod_j g_j\ \ge\ \prod_j U_j-\sum_j O_j\prod_{i\ne j}U_i.$$

`SquareSieve.lean` checks both pointwise statements for any list of bands and arbitrary even truncation orders. This step is needed because products of general signed lower approximations do not give a lower bound.

For the expected main term use independent divisibility indicators with probabilities $1/a$ for the distinct primes. This is exact over a period by the Chinese remainder theorem. Write $u_j=\mathbb E U_j$ and $o_j=\mathbb E O_j$. The good probability in each band is at least $\exp(-2R_j)>1/512$, since $\log(1-x)\ge-2x$ for $0\le x\le1/2$. Thus $u_j>1/512$. For $t=k_j+1\ge21$,

$$o_j\le R_j^t/t! \le (eR_j/t)^t < (9/t)^t<2^{-t}.$$

The first inequality follows by expanding the power and retaining only distinct-prime products. The factorial bound follows by integrating $\log x$ from 1 to $t$. Hence $\sum_j o_j/u_j<\sum_{j\ge0}512\,2^{-21-2j}<1/2$. Independence across bands gives a main term at least $2^{-9r-1}$ from the displayed lower polynomial.

In its expansion each divisor of each of the $r+1$ product terms has coefficient of modulus one, since factorization into distinct band primes is unique. Each such divisor is at most

$$ z^{\sum_{j\ge0}(21+2j)2^{-j}}=z^{46}.$$

An interval has a count of multiples of $D$ that differs from $H/D$ by less than one. The error on all the signed terms is at most $(r+1)z^{46}$. Prescribed primes above $z$ remove at most $H2^{-s}+2^s$ additional positions. For $512\cdot2^k\le s<1024\cdot2^k$, $r\le11+k\le16\cdot2^k\le s/32$. Thus $9r+2<s$ and $r+1\le2^s$. The final count is greater than

$$ H2^{-9r-2}-2^{185s}-2^s>0.$$

This proof bounds a deterministic first-coprime scan. The executed algorithm only needs integer square roots, exact gcd tests, and multiplication of its running prefix. It does not factor the operands or enumerate the bands or the inclusion–exclusion terms.
