# A lower bound on the predecessor's actual fixed setup

The largest binary installer of [PR #7981](https://github.com/CrocSwap/integer-mult-bounds/pull/7981) builds a table with at least $2^{989379999989304}$ distinct exit records. Its specified expanded chronological stream contains at least $2^{3366293830151418}$ records. The second count is larger than the upper bound on **all** fixed setup, programs and scratch of each new installation. This comparison does not use the old degree-20,000 upper bound. The old planner keeps labelled paid occurrences separate. At rank $n$ it sets $u=\lfloor n/4\rfloor$ and makes a forest of height $G$, the largest integer with $A k^G\le u$. Here $A=134^{480}$ and $k$ is the least retained productive rank. The cutoff gives $k\ge16^{480}$. A real tuple with 320 rank-eight factors and 160 rank-64 factors attains equality. Every path of length $G+1$ in the paid alphabet has its own key in the exit table. At depth $G$ the all-paid branch always has a real unit because every paid rank is smaller than $k$. The emitted stream processes each such exit. It runs at least one round and one full copy group for the recursive request. Plans shared by rank can reuse their static census. The specified compiler still emits the chronological child instructions at each occurrence. There are 107,460 positive paid labels in the old raw word. Every ordered 480-tuple gives at least one nonempty paid block. There are 3,108 labels of rank at least 14. In any tuple made exclusively from those labels, its first block has rank exactly $11^{480}$. Its first factor selects the high cap and the product is at least $14^{480}$. Distinct tuples keep distinct labels even if their ranks agree. For $n\ge2^H$ with $H\ge3394$, put $m(H)=1+\lfloor(H-2-3392)/1920\rfloor$. Rounding $n$ down to a multiple of four still leaves at least $2^H$. Since $A<2^{3392}$ and $k=2^{1920}$, there are at least $m(H)$ paid positions in every exit. The three integer power comparisons
$107460^{480}\ge2^{8022}$, $3108^{480}\ge2^{5568}$ and $11^{480}\ge2^{1660}$ suffice for the counts below. At the largest predecessor rank $H_0=236800000000000$ we have $m(H_0)=123333333332$. Thus just its root exit table has at least $2^{8022m(H_0)}$ entries. This first bound counts different keys in the actual dictionary and does not require chronological instruction expansion.

For the expanded stream choose only the first blocks of the high tuples for six successive recursive descents. Put $H_{i+1}=1660m(H_i)$. At each visited call there are at least $2^{5568m(H_i)}$ distinct such exits. Each one's child rank is at least $2^{H_{i+1}}$, since the exit rank is the product of its block ranks. At the seventh forest count every exit. Paths issued by different ancestors occupy different positions in the expanded stream. Each final request emits at least one instruction. The resulting logarithmic lower bound is
$5568\sum_{i=0}^{5}m(H_i)+8022m(H_6)=3366293830151418$. A different interpreter could share a subprogram across invocations. This lower bound refers specifically to the expanded installer described above.

## Earlier execution than the predecessor permits

The actual prior selector requires $\lfloor\log_2 e\rfloor\ge355760240964240$ for its sixth ordinary stage. Its outer requests have at most $p$ binary address bits. Conversion to ternary and previous-stage calls do not increase the width. At the first certified precision of each new installation, $p=2^A+2$ or $p=2^A+4$. All three satisfy $A+1<355760240964240$. The prior final stage cannot activate at any of those inputs. The new positive mixing operation reaches its own last ordinary stage and every earlier stage there.
