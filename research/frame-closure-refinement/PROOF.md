## Why the new frames give a legal finite construction

Let S_i be the conservative binary address span of the value at operation i.
Both registers incident to that operation use the same frame F_i. For each
role, the source or gauge, its operation frames, its root when present,
and the full endpoint must form a nested chain. A recipient's physical
chain is spliced after its donor and retains the same gauge and read time.

The search nominated contractions by predecessor closure. To replace F_i
by a smaller subspace C, require S_i contained in C. For each predecessor
operation j whose frame is not contained in C, replace its frame by its
intersection with C and propagate backwards through both register chains.
Reject a closure if a required value span or fixed starting frame is lost.
Unchanged successors still contain a contracted frame. The finite word
is acyclic, so this propagation terminates. The final explicit witness is
checked independently for every value containment, both incident roles,
every fixed endpoint, spliced donor/gauge inclusion and read chronology.

The inherited `notes/general-clifford-frames.tex` construction applies to
every binary subspace, including degenerate ones. It gives an exact
Gaussian-dyadic representative T_U for each frame. For nested U contained
in V, the actual transition T_V T_U^(-1) has Fourier rank
dim(V)-dim(U), with rank-zero adapters explicitly paid. Common operation
representatives, inverses and complements retain that contract. Thus a
new nested choice changes transition ranks without requiring a new scalar
identity, clean auxiliary state or a new field extension.

Contractions redistribute, rather than discard, transition rank. At a
fixed positive saving b, the cost r^(1-b) is concave. Moving dimension from
a smaller jump into a larger adjacent jump can therefore decrease the
moment. This is only search guidance: simultaneous changes on shared
registers are admitted through the complete recounted histogram below.
Floating scores nominated candidates; they are not used in acceptance.
No global optimum or exhaustive exclusion is asserted.

## Reproduction and verification boundary

Run, with Python 3.11+ and assertions enabled:

```sh
make frame-closure-verify
```

The verifier first checks dependency hashes and replays the immutable
predecessor, including its full bit supplier and bank schedule. It extracts
the same pinned PR181 archive into a temporary directory, copies the
unchanged PR186 code, and overlays only the new frame and profile data.
It then invokes the unchanged physical and terminal executors, rebuilds
the exact moment and assembly, and compares the entire derived receipt
with `certificate.json`. Normal verification writes only temporary files.
`FILES.json` pins every candidate file. No network or third-party Python
package is required. `--write` is an explicit authoring mode.

Finite replay establishes the supplied finite word and strict rational
inequalities. Its all-size realization and the multiplication transfer
remain conditional on the predecessor interfaces identified above.
