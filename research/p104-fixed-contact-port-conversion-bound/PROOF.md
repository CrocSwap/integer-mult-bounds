# Relative contact and separate body price

## Fixed native contact

At the first B port (index 15, central direction 81), the exact native owners are 17 and 129, with charts 16→127 and 3→143. The latter exit is the eight-dimensional orthogonal complement of 81. The pinned stage-1 caller contact at clock 43 has B roles 36/37 and compares to the four distinct A roles 1/0/18/19. At unit body the native contract is B = L(q⁻¹/2)U(−q) and A = L(q⁻¹) ⊕ U(−q), where L and U are lower and upper unipotent shears. This follows from the pinned native phase endpoints and caller binding. No input alias is permitted.

For a sign-specialized operator M write R=M_+⁻¹M_-. On B, R is the matrix [[0,1],[-1,0]], so R²=−I and (R−I)²=[[0,−2],[2,0]]. The two Type A shears give (R−I)²=0. If invertible input and output wrappers have the same normalized action at q=±1, the new relative is similar to R by the input wrapper. With uncoupled invertible native helper actions the relative is a direct sum and the original B summand still gives a nonzero square. This excludes the frozen J contact even if the uncoupled helper blocks depend on q. It does not cover changing the native signed contact, using a q-odd wrapper, or mixing the two compared phase fibres.

## Two separate full returns

The exact A and B caller target histograms coincide. On conversion the paid histogram changes by {1:+4,8:−30,9:−4}, leaving gross 30H8+4H9−4 before helper losses. Each converted B port adds two main owners at every J. The fixed public owner pool loses two original independent helper targets per stage. All 17 stage bodies act on eight independent axes. Each of the two extra outputs must receive the full stage body on its own arbitrary independent input.

In this model, each extra output consumes one completed native helper quotient of rank r and a finite serial list of separately charged positive-rank quotient jobs on that same full owner. Their monomial wrappers preserve the number of nonzero coefficients in each row, including under tensor lift. There are no uncharged body-dependent transforms and no paid jobs shared by both outputs. At column multiplicity f, a rank-k quotient has at most 2^(kf) nonzero entries in each row. This follows from monomial equivalence to the tensor product of kf binary C factors with identity spectators. The binary factor is dense. The required D_s body has exactly 2^(8f) row support. The maximum row support of a matrix product is at most the product of the individual row-support maxima. Consequently r+sum_j p_j≥8 for the declared paid ranks p_j on each lane. An r>8 displaced helper alone already exceeds the lower bound on charge.

For 0<κ<1, H_r=r^(1−κ) is increasing and subadditive on positive ranks. Thus lost H_r plus sum_j H_p_j is at least H_(r+sum p_j), which is at least H8. Retaining 34 independent returns costs at least 34 H8, with no double credit or shared job. The most favorable change is therefore at most 4(H9−H8−1), strictly negative by subadditivity with strict inequality for ranks 8 and 1. For m converted ports this independent-body upper multiplies by m. The directed receipts give the exact interval at the old crossing and independently check all nine original helper-rank bins. Since the price is already negative, extra phase, routing, setup, precision and tape charges cannot help this scope. A provider that couples returns, changes the native quotient, or preserves a separately certified displaced productive remainder has a different invoice.
