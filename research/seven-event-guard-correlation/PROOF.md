# Seven orthogonal full-guard events lie below the product of their marginals

This contribution was produced by Nick Petalas. Apache-2.0. Independent human
and formal proof-kernel review are unclaimed.

**Claim type: scoped counting proof resting on an exact finite certificate.**
The all-dimension step is a written argument. Its single computational input is
the exact signed spectrum of all 2^42 seven-vertex characters, produced by
`spectrum.c`, committed in `SPECTRUM_K7.txt` and regenerated by `verify.py`.

## Statement

Let U_1, ..., U_7 be pairwise orthogonal rank-one idempotents on F_2^m, m >= 7,
and let G be uniform in GL_m(F_2). Event E_t says that every leading principal
minor of orders 1 through m-1 of G(I-U_t)G^-1 is nonzero. Write n = m-1,
X = 2^m and s_m = 3^n / ((X-1) 2^n), the probability of one event.

**Theorem.** For every integer m >= 7, Pr(E_1 ∩ ... ∩ E_7) < s_m^7.

At m = 7 the joint probability is 0. At m = 8 it is 29/5201587077120, about
5.575e-12, against s_8^7 of about 6.063e-9. The ratio rho_m = Pr / s_m^7
tends to 1 from below, with 1 - rho_m = 42 (7/9)^(m-1) (1 + O((19/21)^(m-1))).

This is the k = 7 case of the product inequality that PR6515 proves for k = 6,
PR4607 for k = 5 and PR4599 for k = 4. Those proofs, and the k = 6 spectrum, are
not used below.

## 1. Inputs from the character law (cited, not re-proved)

PR4275 (`research/corankone-triple-header-law`) proves, for k pairwise
orthogonal rank-one requests over F_2 and n = m-1:

* Theorem 2: the number of passing headers is C |GL_(m-k)(F_2)| with
  C = 2^(-k(k-1)) N_n, where N_n = sum over zero-diagonal binary k x k matrices c
  of (-1)^|c| Z(c)^n, and Z(c) is the three-state sum
  sum over sigma in {0,1,2}^k of (-1)^(sum of c_ij with sigma_i = 1, sigma_j = 2).
* Theorem 1: the fraction of passing headers is C / D with
  D = 2^(k(m-k)) prod_(i<k) (2^m - 2^i).
* Theorem 3: a passing header exists if and only if m >= k + 1.

Dividing, Pr_k(m) = N_n / (2^(kn) prod_(i<k) (X - 2^i)). For k = 1,
N_n = 3^n and Pr_1(m) = s_m. Dividing Pr_7(m) by s_m^7, the factors 2^(7n)
and X-1 cancel, and the theorem is equivalent to

    N_n (X-1)^6 < 3^(7n) (X-2)(X-4)(X-8)(X-16)(X-32)(X-64)          (R)

for every n >= 6 (m >= 7, where X > 64). Summing out the label-2 coordinates gives the subset form

    Z(c) = sum over A subset of [k] such that every j outside A has even
           incoming parity sum_(i in A) c_ij, of 2^(k-|A|).

Group the characters by their value: H[z] = sum over c with Z(c) = z of (-1)^|c|,
so that N_n = sum_z H[z] z^n.

## 2. The spectrum of all 2^42 characters

A Gray-code walk over 2^42 matrices would take days. `spectrum.c` instead uses
two facts.

**Relabelling.** Z(c) is unchanged by renaming the vertices. Fix the first five
vertices. The 2^20 digraphs on them fall into 9,608 orbits under S_5, so it
suffices to take one representative of each orbit, weighted by its orbit size,
and to sweep every arc pattern between the representative and the last two
vertices.

**The last vertex in two tables.** Let c' be the digraph on the first six
vertices, with rows r_i, and let u (arcs from the six into vertex 7) and v (arcs
from vertex 7 into the six) be arbitrary six-bit sets. For A' a subset of the
six, let r[A'] be the xor of its rows. The condition defining Z(c) reads:

* A = A' (vertex 7 outside A): r[A'] vanishes outside A', and |u ∩ A'| is even;
  the weight is 2^(7-|A'|).
* A = A' + {7}: r[A'] xor v vanishes outside A'; the weight is 2^(6-|A'|).

Hence Z(c) = F1(u) + F2(v), with

    F1(u) = sum over A' with r[A'] outside A' empty and |u ∩ A'| even of 2^(7-|A'|),
    F2(v) = sum over A' with (r[A'] xor v) outside A' empty of 2^(6-|A'|).

F1 is a Walsh-Hadamard transform of 64 numbers and F2 is a pass over the v that
agree with r[A'] outside A'. All 2^12 patterns of the last vertex then cost two
tables of 64 entries and 4,096 histogram updates, each carrying the sign
(-1)^(|u|+|v|) and the orbit weight. In all, 9,608 orbits times 2^10 patterns of
vertex 6 give 9.8 million sweeps. One process finishes in about 40 seconds.

**Result** (`SPECTRUM_K7.txt`, 493 lines "z H[z]"):

| fact | value |
|---|---|
| nonzero entries | 493 (247 positive, 246 negative); every z is odd |
| sum of H[z] | 0 |
| sum of abs(H[z]) | 2^42, so no value of Z carries both signs |
| top levels | H[2187] = 1, H[1701] = -42, H[1539] = 210 |
| next level | H[1485] = -280, then H[1467] = 210, H[1461] = -84 |
| below 1539 | largest z is 1485; sum of abs(H[z]) is A = 2^42 - 253 = 4,398,046,510,851 |
| N_n for n = 0..6 | 0 |
| N_7 / 2^42 | 29,816,640 = 7! * 5916 |

The top levels are the single arcs (7*6 = 42, Z = 7*3^5 = 1701, odd, so sign -1)
and the two-arrow stars (7*6*5 = 210, Z = 19*3^4 = 1539), matching the equality
classification of PR6440 and the bound of PR5146.

**Why this table is trusted.** The checks that `verify.py` runs, none of which
uses the answer:

1. The enumerator equals labelled brute force over all matrices at k = 3, 4, 5,
   and (`--brute6`) over all 2^30 matrices at k = 6.
2. At k = 3 and 4 it equals the literal three-state definition, and the subset
   form equals the definition on every matrix at k = 3, 4 and on random samples
   at k = 5, 6, 7.
3. Its k = 3 spectrum is the closed form of PR4275, and its k = 4 and k = 5
   leading coefficients and remainder bounds are those printed in PR4599 and
   PR4607 (statements checked in the pinned files).
4. At k = 7, N_0 = ... = N_6 = 0, which is Theorem 3 of PR4275 (a header needs
   n >= k); N_7 is divisible by 7!, as it must be, because a decomposition with
   exactly k nonzero distinct rectangles can be ordered in k! ways; the counts
   add to 2^42 characters; and a one-unit change to any top coefficient breaks the
   vanishing moments (a control).
5. `--labelled7` repeats the k = 7 computation without the orbit reduction
   (every labelled five-vertex digraph, weight one) and gets the same table.

## 3. Dimensions m <= 401

For every 6 <= n <= 400 `verify.py` evaluates both sides of (R) with exact
integers from the full table and checks the strict inequality. At n = 6 (m = 7)
the left side is 0. For n < 6 the factors X - 2^j are not all positive and (R) is
not asserted. The ratio rho_m is about 0.00091954 at m = 8, 0.0107257 at
m = 9, 0.0961028 at m = 11, 0.264821 at m = 13, 0.617156 at m = 17,
0.827536 at m = 21, 0.981491 at m = 31 and 0.999858 at m = 51.

## 4. Every larger dimension

Let A be as above. Every z below 1539 is at most 1485, so for every n

    N_n <= 2187^n - 42*1701^n + 210*1539^n + A*1485^n
        =  2187^n - 3*1701^n - L(n),
    L(n) = 39*1701^n - 210*1539^n - A*1485^n.

The integers give L(187) < 0 and L(188) > 0. Also

    L(n+1) - 1701 L(n) = 210*162*1539^n + 216*A*1485^n > 0,

so L(n) > 0 for every n >= 188, and therefore

    N_n <= 2187^n - 3*1701^n            for every n >= 188.                (1)

For X >= 64 put a_j = (2^j - 1)/(X-1), which lies in [0,1]. Then
X - 2^j = (X-1)(1 - a_j), and the inequality prod(1 - a_j) >= 1 - sum a_j
together with sum_(j=1..6) (2^j - 1) = 120 gives

    (X-2)(X-4)(X-8)(X-16)(X-32)(X-64) >= (X-1)^5 (X-121).                  (2)

By (2), (R) follows from N_n (X-1) < 2187^n (X-121). By (1) this holds as soon as
120 * 2187^n < 3 * 1701^n (X-1), that is

    40 * 9^n < 7^n (2^(n+1) - 1).                                           (3)

At n = 7 it reads 191,318,760 < 210,003,465. Write f(n) = 7^n (2^(n+1)-1) / 9^n.
Then f(n+1) / f(n) = 7(2^(n+2)-1) / (9(2^(n+1)-1)) exceeds 1, because
7(2^(n+2)-1) - 9(2^(n+1)-1) = 10*2^n + 2 > 0. So f(n) > 40 and (3) holds for
every n >= 7, hence (R) holds for every n >= 188. Section 3 covers 6 <= n <= 400.
Together they cover every m >= 7. `verify.py` checks L(187) < 0, L(188) > 0,
the growth identity, (1) for 188 <= n < 1000 directly from the table, (2) and
the step in (3) for 7 <= n < 400, and (3) at n = 7. QED.

## 5. Rate

From the top three levels, N_n / 2187^n = 1 - 42 (7/9)^n + 210 (19/27)^n + O((55/81)^n),
and (X-1)^6 / prod(X - 2^j) = 1 + O(2^-n), where 2^-n and (19/27)^n are both
smaller than (7/9)^n by exponential factors. This gives the stated rate. The exact
ratios at n = 100 and n = 400 agree with 42 (7/9)^n to within 0.1%.

## Scope

**Covered.** Exactly seven fixed, pairwise orthogonal rank-one requests over F_2,
complete uniform headers, every m >= 7.

**Not settled.** k >= 8; odd characteristic; nonorthogonal or restricted request
families; negative association of six or seven indicators (a stronger statement
than the product inequality, with its own open questions in PR5860 and PR6515);
a human-checkable derivation of the spectrum, which rests on the program.

**Not claimed.** A physical saving, a supplier, a new κ, a selected-frontier
change, or proof-kernel review of the C program.
