# A ten-piece weight-seven residue closes after deletion

## Statement

Let `K` be any characteristic-zero field. Use the pinned 112 nonframe E8
root lines `r_t` and their nine-bit labels `ell(t)`. Write

```
C = {c_h : c_h(t)=<r_t,h>, h in K^8},
w(q) = rank_F2 {ell(t) : q(t) != 0}.
```

**Nine-piece theorem.** If nine independent pieces span `T0` and have
weight upper bounds `4,4,4,6,6,6,6,6,6`, then

```
dim(T0 intersect C) <= 4.
```

**Ten-piece consequence.** Ten independent pieces spanning `T` with
`dim(T intersect C)>=6` cannot have weight upper bounds `4^3,6^6,7`.
In fact, the tenth piece can be unrestricted.

This is one new application of Nick Petalas/PR3169's coordinate-budget
inequality, not a new inequality or a completed decoder-floor proof.
DreamingOfClouds/PR3154's historical TWOSIDE `(b,t)=(6,4)` filters retain
this exact ten-piece profile. Concurrent PR3195 already proves the distinct
eight-piece slice `5,5,6^6` and nine-piece caller `5,5,6^6,7`. That was our
initial target and is not counted as new here. The new profile has three
small pieces, nine retained pieces and a four-dimensional quotient.
No selected supplier or exponent changes here.

## Finite geometric premises

The retained `geometry.py` is byte-identical to PR3169's copy of PR3164's
geometry. The full replay reconstructs roots and labels and binds them to
`research/one-sided-six-multisets/data/data.txt`. Its finite checks support
the following premises from the retained predecessor proof.

1. A weight-four support has at most eight live coordinates: all labels
   have leading bit one, so the odd part of a four-dimensional binary
   space has at most `2^3` points. A weight-six support has at most twenty.
   Enumeration covers all 788,035 six-flats in `F2^9`. The replay also
   retains the five-flat capacity twelve, over all 3,309,747 five-flats,
   used in the historical filters and concurrent-source calibration.
2. A nonzero element of `C` has at least 53 nonzero coordinates. Every
   codeword with support below seventy is proportional to a root codeword.
3. The P112 Gram sum is `112 I` in doubled root coordinates. Thus evaluation
   `h -> c_h` is injective over `K`.
4. In the plane of two distinct E8 root lines, a third root line occurs only
   in an A2 subsystem, with coefficient ratio `+1` or `-1`.

For completeness, premise 2 uses the standard Weyl-chamber theorem, not a
finite short-vector search. At least 43 live root lines are perpendicular
to a direction of support below seventy. In a closed chamber, perpendicular
roots are generated by vanishing simple roots. All 28 six-node parabolics
contain at most 36 root lines. The eight seven-node face counts are
`42,28,22,14,16,23,37,63`; only the last reaches 43, and its normal is a
root. The 120 root directions have live support 53 or 56. This also gives
the distance 53.

For a field-valued direction, its perpendicular rational roots have rational
rank at most seven. If their rank were at most six, a generic real normal
to their span would contradict the same rank-six root-subsystem bound.
Their rational kernel is therefore one-dimensional, so the direction is
proportional over `K` to a rational normal. Apply the real chamber argument
to that normal. This includes complex coefficients and does not assume a
positive-definite norm on `K`.

The replay checks simple-root positivity, 960 simple reflections and all
7,140 root-line planes. The standard Weyl-chamber theorem is an explicit
written dependency. These checks do not formally prove that theorem.

## Nine pieces give six affine vertices

Assume for a contradiction that `dim(T0 intersect C)>=5`. Let `s1,s2,s3`
be the weight-four pieces and `q0,...,q5` the six weight-six pieces. Put

```
S = union_{j=1}^3 supp(s_j),    s=|S|<=3*8=24,
U = sum_i |supp(q_i)|<=120,    D0=T0 intersect C.
```

The quotient `T0/D0` has dimension at most four. The four images of
`s1,s2,s3,q_i` are independent for every `i`: any dependence would give a
nonzero codeword supported on at most `24+20=44<53` coordinates. The
combination is nonzero because the original pieces are independent.
Consequently the quotient dimension is exactly four and `dim D0=5`.

Modulo the span of the three small images, all six `q_i` images are nonzero
in a one-dimensional space. Rescale the `q_i` so these images agree. This
changes neither their supports nor independence. There are small-piece
combinations `a_i` such that

```
c0=0,    c_i=q_i-q0-a_i in D0,    i=1,...,5.
```

The five codewords are independent by their distinct nonzero coefficients
on `q1,...,q5`. Every nonzero difference `c_i-c_j` has support at most

```
24+20+20=64<70.
```

Thus all fifteen edges are root directions. Injectivity gives five
independent `h_i` with `c_i=c_{h_i}`.

## The inherited A5 coordinate budget

Here is PR3169's argument with the same hypotheses. Normalize the real
roots to norm squared two. Write `h_i=b_i alpha_i`. A third root line in
the plane of `alpha_i,alpha_j` forces `b_i/b_j` to be `+1` or `-1`.
Absorb signs into the roots. Then `h_i=b alpha_i` with one common nonzero
field scalar, and `<alpha_i,alpha_j>=1`. The six vertices
`0,alpha_1,...,alpha_5` form a root A5 simplex. The plane statement also
holds after scalar extension: a rational root in the field span of two
independent rational roots is in their rational span, using a nonsingular
rational coordinate minor.

At any root coordinate, the six vertex evaluations have pairwise
differences in `{-2,-1,0,1,2}`. If a difference has absolute value two,
the coordinate is that edge root, and the equality multiplicities are
`1,1,4`. There are nine unequal pairs. Otherwise the values occupy at
most two consecutive integer levels, again giving at most nine unequal
pairs. The common field scalar changes no equalities. Hence every
coordinate lies in at most nine of the fifteen edge supports.

Outside `S`, edge evaluations equal `q_i(t)-q_j(t)`. If exactly `k` endpoint
values are nonzero there, their unequal pairs number at most

```
k(6-k)+k(k-1)/2 = k(11-k)/2 <= 4k+1,
```

since `(k-1)(k-2)>=0` for integer `k=0,...,6`. This endpoint inequality
holds over any field. Summing it over the `112-s` outside coordinates,
and using the nine-incidence bound inside `S`, gives

```
15*53 <= 9s + 4U + 112-s = 112+8s+4U.
```

But `s<=24` and `U<=120`, so

```
795 <= 112+8*24+4*120 = 784,
```

a contradiction with margin eleven. This proves the nine-piece theorem.
The older `9s+5U` bound gives 816 and does not prove it. The replay retains
that losing control. At `s=26`, the new upper bound is 800, also insufficient;
the script must not report an exclusion at that boundary.

## Delete one piece, not one code dimension for free

For the ten-piece application let `T0` be the span after removing the
tenth piece. Independence gives `dim T0=9` and codimension one in `T`.
The map from `D=T intersect C` into `T/T0` has image of dimension at most
one and kernel `D intersect T0`. Therefore

```
dim(T0 intersect C) >= dim(T intersect C)-1 >= 5.
```

The remaining weights are `4^3,6^6`, contradicting the theorem. We do not
claim that all six original code dimensions survive, nor impose a free
weight-six tariff on the removed piece. Its support is never used. Thus
no weight-seven edge classification, rank-seven deletion census or new
consumer is needed for this particular profile.
