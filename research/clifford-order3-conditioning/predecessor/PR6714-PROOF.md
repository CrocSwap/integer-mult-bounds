# The three-qubit Clifford tensor threshold is ten

This is a scoped algebraic theorem about the complete Gaussian-dyadic
three-qubit Clifford alphabet. It gives no new multiplication exponent,
physical supplier, or implementation of the five assumptions retained by
the selected construction. Classical finite-group representation theory
and the cited character table are premises; the proof is not a proof-assistant
formalization or independent human review.

## 1. Statement and conventions

Let `D = Z[i,1/2]`, and let `G` be the group of all unitary three-qubit
Clifford matrices with entries in `D`. Its natural representation `V` has
dimension eight. Let `z=iI`. The scalar subgroup is

    Z = <z> = {I,iI,-I,-iI}.

Let `P` be the Pauli subgroup generated by `z` and the three pairs `X_j,Z_j`.
Then `|P|=256`, `G/P = Sp(6,2) = S`, and

    |S| = 1,451,520,
    |G/Z| = 64 |S| = 92,897,280,
    |G| = 4 |G/Z| = 371,589,120.

The usual Pauli and symplectic conventions are those of the retained
pointwise-faithfulness and two-qubit-centre packages. The Gaussian-dyadic
group has scalar kernel `mu_4`, not `mu_8`. Indeed, if `(a+bi)/2^k` has
modulus one, successive parity division in `a^2+b^2=2^(2k)` reduces it to
one of the four Gaussian units. The Gaussian-dyadic gates `S`,
`((1+i)I+(1-i)X)/2`, and CNOT realize the standard symplectic generators;
thus the quotient is the full `Sp(6,2)`. The Pauli action determines a
Clifford up to scalar.

Choose one unitary representative `U_a` for each `a in G/Z`, with `U_1=I`.
Define

    H_f(a,b) = (tr(U_a^* U_b)/8)^f,       f = 1,2,... .

This is the Gram matrix of `U_a^(tensor f)/8^(f/2)` in Hilbert-Schmidt
space. Changing the representatives conjugates `H_f` by a diagonal
unitary matrix and does not change its rank or spectrum.

**Theorem.** The least width at which this projective alphabet is linearly
independent is exactly **ten**. Equivalently, its least eventual pointwise
threshold is

    T(3) = 10.

At width nine the nullity is exactly `56^2 = 3,136`, so

    rank H_9 = 92,894,144.

For every integer `f >= 10`, the following additional, nonoptimal bounds hold:

    (945/262144) I <= H_f <= (762615/32768) I.             (1)

The resulting Gram condition-number bound is 6,456. We do not claim sharp
spectral constants. In particular, the threshold is not the extrapolated
value `4n+1=13` suggested by the one- and two-qubit cases.

## 2. Why tensor independence is an irreducible-support question

For each `r mod 4`, let `A_r` be the central-character ideal of `C[G]` on
which `z` acts as `i^r`. Its dimension is `|G/Z|`, and one representative
per projective class gives a basis after projection to this ideal.
The representation `V^(tensor f)` annihilates the other three ideals.

By semisimplicity,

    A_r = direct_sum_{rho : rho(z)=i^r I} End(rho).

The representation of `A_r` is injective exactly when every irreducible
in this sector occurs. A missing irreducible of dimension `d` removes
an entire matrix block of dimension `d^2`. Thus

    rank H_f = sum_{rho occurring in V^(tensor f)} (dim rho)^2.       (2)

If `m_rho(f)` is its multiplicity, the normalized Gram eigenvalue on its
regular matrix block is

    lambda_rho(f) = |G/Z| m_rho(f) / (8^f dim rho).                   (3)

One way to check the normalization is to use central convolution on all
of `G`, whose eigenvalue is `|G| m_rho(f)/dim rho`. Combining the four
scalar lifts multiplies the projective Gram by `4*8^f`; dividing gives
(3). The regular multiplicity is `(dim rho)^2`, not four times that value.

## 3. The odd central sector and a smaller character calculation

The Pauli group has a unique irreducible with `z` acting by `i`, namely
the eight-dimensional restriction of `V`. This follows directly from
the usual irreducible representation of the three Pauli pairs; its
matrix algebra has dimension 64. Consequently every irreducible of `G`
in central sector `i` has the unique form

    V tensor sigma,       sigma in Irr(S).                            (4)

For completeness, for such a representation `R`, the multiplicity space
`Hom_P(V,R)` carries the honest quotient action

    g . phi = R(g) phi V(g)^(-1).

Pauli elements act trivially on this space. Evaluation identifies
`R` with `V tensor Hom_P(V,R)`, and irreducibility on one side is equivalent
to irreducibility of the multiplicity space. No splitting of `G` over `P`
is being assumed. The actual natural representation supplies the extension
needed in (4).

Let `s in S`, and put `d(s)=dim_F2 ker(s-I)`. Among the 64 projective
Pauli lifts of `s`, exactly `2^(6-d(s))` have nonzero trace; on each such
lift `g`,

    |tr V(g)|^2 = 2^d(s).                                             (5)

To recall the argument, conjugation on the Hermitian Pauli basis is a
signed permutation. Its trace is `|tr V(g)|^2`. The signs on the fixed
label space form a binary character, so their sum is zero or `2^d`.
Multiplication by Paulis runs through all its characters equally often.
In particular there is always a nonzero-trace lift.

For a Gaussian-dyadic number of squared modulus `2^d`, its fourth power is

    (tr V(g))^4 = (-1)^d 2^(2d).                                     (6)

This includes the phase: writing the number as `(a+bi)/2^k`, the equation
`a^2+b^2=2^(d+2k)` implies that, after removing common powers of two,
either one coordinate is zero or the coordinates have equal absolute
value. The two cases give the sign in (6).

For `f=4t+1`, write

    V^(tensor f) = V tensor W_f.

The quotient representation `W_f` is honest, and the character product
on a nonzero-trace lift gives

    chi_W_f(s) = (-1)^(dt) 2^(2dt).                                  (7)

In particular,

    chi_W_9(s) = 2^(4d(s)).                                          (8)

This is also the permutation character of `S` acting on ordered
four-tuples of vectors in `F_2^6`. Thus the width-nine input to the proof
is an ordinary 30-character calculation for `Sp(6,2)`, rather than a
92,897,280-by-92,897,280 Gram calculation.

## 4. Exact width-nine decomposition

We use the published character table of Chin-Yen Lee, Wei-Hsuan Yu,
Yung-Ning Peng and Ching-Jui Lai, [arXiv:2309.14850v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.14850v2#S6.SS3), Section 6.3,
and its Section 5.2 identification of the adjoint character as
`1 + chi_10`. The package retains the exact transcribed table and the
class-fibre extraction. Inflated characters are those trivial on the
64-element projective Pauli subgroup. The executable checks character
orthogonality, class and degree sums, the quotient extraction, fixed-space
trace data, and every integer inner product below. Those checks validate
the transcription and calculation; they do not independently reconstruct
the published character table from group multiplication.

More explicitly, the nonidentity projective Paulis constitute one
63-element conjugacy class. A character is trivial on this subgroup
exactly when its value on that class equals its degree: equality in the
unitary trace bound forces the matrix to be the identity. These are
precisely the inflated quotient irreducibles. Two source classes have the
same quotient class exactly when all these characters agree, since
irreducible characters separate conjugacy classes. Each quotient class
size is the sum of its source class sizes divided by 64. Finally, the
maximum of the adjoint value `1+chi_10` over a quotient fibre is `2^d`
by (5). This identifies the fixed-space data used in (8), including the
63-element transvection class, without inferring `d` from class order alone.

The multiplicity-weighted dimensions sum to `8^8=16,777,216`. Let `sigma_0` denote the sole
missing character, of degree seven. On a symplectic transvection `tau`,

    chi_sigma_0(tau) = -5.                                          (9)

This is the class of size 63 with fixed-space dimension five. The
nonzero multiplicities in the table also satisfy

    (1/2) dim sigma <= m_sigma(9) <= 269 dim sigma.                  (10)

The only missing irreducible in `V^(tensor 9)` is therefore
`rho_0=V tensor sigma_0`, of dimension 56. Equation (2) proves the
claimed nullity and rank.

There is an explicit null witness. For a projective representative `U_a`,
let `s_a` be its symplectic image and define

    b_a = chi_rho_0(U_a^(-1))
        = conjugate(tr V(U_a) * chi_sigma_0(s_a)).

Then

    sum_a b_a U_a^(tensor 9) = 0.                                  (11)

Indeed, the central primitive idempotent

    (56/|G|) sum_{g in G} chi_rho_0(g^(-1)) [g]

acts as zero in `V^(tensor 9)`. Grouping its four scalar lifts gives
(11), since their factors `i^(-j)` and `i^(9j)` cancel. This is a
nonzero coefficient vector: its coefficient at the identity is 56.
It is a width-nine relation, not an all-width identity.

## 5. The even sector is controlled by quadratic forms

Consider any irreducible `mu` of `G` with `mu(z)=-I`, the sector of
`V^(tensor 10)`. Its restriction to `P` is a sum of one-dimensional
characters because `[P,P]=<-I>` and `mu(-I)=I`.

Write a binary label as `v=(a,b)` and use the Hermitian Pauli convention

    W_v = i^(a.b) X^a Z^b.

The one-dimensional characters with `z` acting by `-1` are indexed by
the 64 quadratic forms with polar form the standard symplectic form:

    Q_(alpha,beta)(a,b) = a.b + alpha.a + beta.b,
    lambda_Q(W_v) = (-1)^Q(v).

In a Clifford conjugation `W_v -> +/- W_(s v)`, the sign has no effect
on `lambda_Q`, since it sends `-I` to one. The quotient therefore acts
on these characters by its ordinary action on quadratic forms.

There are exactly two orbits, distinguished by Arf invariant,

    Arf Q_(alpha,beta) = alpha.beta.

Their sizes are 36 and 28. This is the standard symplectic classification
of nondegenerate binary quadratic forms: choosing successive hyperbolic
pairs gives one normal form for each Arf value. The counts also follow
directly by summing over `alpha,beta`.

Since `mu` is irreducible, its Pauli character spaces form one transitive
orbit and have a common dimension `a>=1`. Hence for one `N in {28,36}`,

    mu restricted to P = a sum_{Q in orbit} lambda_Q,
    dim mu = N a.                                                   (12)

For a nonzero vector `v`, the symplectic transvection is

    tau_v(x) = x + omega(x,v) v.

The quadratic identity gives

    Q(tau_v x) = Q(x) + omega(x,v)(Q(v)+1).

Thus a form is fixed exactly when `Q(v)=1`. Each Arf orbit has exactly
16 such forms. For example, use symplectic coordinates with `v=e_1`.
Then `alpha_1=1`; the four coordinates
`alpha_2,alpha_3,beta_2,beta_3` are arbitrary, and `beta_1` is uniquely
fixed by the prescribed Arf invariant.

Any Clifford lift `g` of this transvection permutes the Pauli character
spaces. Only the sixteen fixed spaces contribute to its trace. Since
the representation can be made unitary, each contributes absolute trace
at most `a`. Consequently

    |chi_mu(g)| <= 16 a.                                            (13)

This bound uses neither the character table of the even central sector
nor extensions of its stabilizer representations.

## 6. Every irreducible occurs at width ten

Suppose, for contradiction, that `mu` is missing from `V^(tensor 10)`.
Tensor reciprocity gives

    <V^(tensor 10), mu> = <V^(tensor 9), conjugate(V) tensor mu>.

The representation on the right has central character `i`. The sole
irreducible absent from `V^(tensor 9)` is `rho_0`. All multiplicities are
nonnegative integers, so absence forces

    conjugate(V) tensor mu = m rho_0,        m >= 1.

Dimensions give `7m=Na`. Take `g=S` on the first qubit and the identity
on the other two. Its symplectic image is a transvection and
`tr V(g)=4(1+i)` is nonzero. The character identity and (9) imply

    |chi_mu(g)| = 5m = (5N/7)a.

For `N=28` this is `20a`; for `N=36` it is `180a/7`. Both exceed `16a`,
contradicting (13). Therefore no even-sector irreducible is missing.
By Section 2, `H_10` is positive definite.

This argument is also a useful check against a phase error: conjugate `V`,
not `V`, is used in reciprocity, and the relevant Pauli character orbit is
the quadratic-form orbit for central character `-1`.

## 7. Quantitative bounds and every larger width

The same reasoning gives (1) without computing the full even-sector
spectrum. Decompose

    conjugate(V) tensor mu = sum_sigma n_sigma (V tensor sigma),
    d_mu := dim mu = sum_sigma n_sigma dim sigma.

Let `n_0` be the coefficient of the missing degree-seven character and
put `R=d_mu-7n_0`. At the transvection lift used above, the character
identity and the elementary bound
`|chi_sigma(tau)|<=dim sigma` give

    12n_0-d_mu
      = 5n_0-R
      <= |sum_sigma n_sigma chi_sigma(tau)|
      = |chi_mu(g)|
      <= 16a.

The first inequality is valid even when its left side is negative.
Using `d_mu=Na` and `N in {28,36}` yields

    R >= a(5N-112)/12 >= d_mu/12.

Reciprocity and (10) now show

    d_mu/24 <= m_mu(10) <= 269 d_mu.

Substituting into (3) gives precisely (1) at width ten. The lower bound
also proves positivity directly.

Finally,

    H_(f+1) = H_f Hadamard H_1.

The Schur product theorem and the unit diagonal of `H_1` imply

    alpha I <= H_f <= beta I
       => alpha I <= H_(f+1) <= beta I.

This proves (1), hence independence, at every `f>=10`. If any smaller
positive width were independent, the same Schur step would make width
nine independent, contrary to Section 4. The threshold is therefore
exactly ten, not just an upper bound.

## 8. Four fixed-residual tests with exact phases

Let a fixed residual in `C[G]` have coefficients `c_(a,j)` on the exact
operators `i^j U_a`, after equal operators are combined. For `r mod 4`, set

    beta_(a,r) = sum_{j=0}^3 c_(a,j) i^(jr).

At width `f`, its operator evaluation is

    sum_a beta_(a,f mod 4) U_a^(tensor f).

Pointwise independence at the four widths `10,11,12,13` makes these
evaluations zero exactly when all four sets of `beta` coefficients are
zero. Fourier inversion then gives `c_(a,j)=0` for every exact operator.
Consequently the following are equivalent for a fixed residual:

1. Every exact-operator coefficient vanishes.
2. Its evaluation vanishes at every positive tensor width.
3. Its evaluations vanish at widths 10, 11, 12 and 13.

If `M=sum_(a,j)|c_(a,j)|^2`, Parseval and (1) imply that at least one of
those four evaluations has normalized squared Hilbert-Schmidt norm at
least `(945/262144) M`. Coefficients must be fixed for this four-test
statement; pointwise independence itself allows coefficients to depend
on the chosen width.

Four residue classes remain necessary for unrestricted fixed residuals:
`sum_j i^(-rj)[i^j I]` vanishes outside residue `r` and is nonzero in that
residue. The four-test count and the scalar Fourier argument are retained
predecessor principles; this theorem supplies the earlier sufficient window.

## 9. Scope and attribution

The alphabet is the full projective three-qubit Clifford group, with arbitrary
complex coefficients at a fixed width. The theorem is not a statement about
arbitrary sums of Cliffords as individual alphabet elements, operators coupling
tensor columns, nonlinear rounded gates, or nonunitary stabilizer maps.
It supplies no paid-call bound without a separate source-bound caller argument.
The selected conditional multiplication result and its five assumptions are
unchanged.

Ali Romman's pointwise-faithfulness package supplies the general Clifford
framework, trace distribution, phase-residue interpretation, and open sharp
threshold question. MatShep's one- and two-qubit packages supply the earlier
sharp cases and the Schur-propagation method. The linear-lower-bound package
records the unresolved three-qubit case. The authors of arXiv:2309.14850
supply the published character table and adjoint identification; their table
is a cited mathematical input, not newly constructed here. Pauli representation
theory, tensor reciprocity, character orthogonality, binary quadratic-form
classification, central idempotents and the Schur product theorem are classical.

The new contribution is the exact width-nine decomposition and its missing
block, the even-sector transvection argument proving full support at ten,
and their quantitative and fixed-residual consequences. Prepared with
substantial OpenAI Codex assistance and separate agent proof/checking work.
