# Exact symmetry quotients for odd projector words

This contribution was produced by Nick Petalas. Apache-2.0.

## Statement

Let K=F_q be any finite field. Let X be the rank-one idempotents of
M_2(K), and put n=q(q+1). For an odd positive integer k=2m+1, consider
ordered tuples (N_1,...,N_k) in X^k. The following transformations act on
these tuples:

1. Simultaneous conjugation C_S by a monomial invertible two by two matrix
   S, with scalar multiples identified.
2. R(N_1,...,N_k)=(N_k^T,...,N_1^T).

Write epsilon=0 in characteristic two and epsilon=1 otherwise. The number
of orbits under the group generated by these transformations is exactly

\[
 \boxed{\frac{n^{2m+1}
 +2^{2m+1}\left(q-2+\epsilon\frac{q-1}{2}\right)
 +(q-1)(q+\epsilon)n^m}{4(q-1)}}.                 \tag{1}
\]

The term containing epsilon is zero in characteristic two. For k=5 the
orbit counts at q=2,3,4,5 are respectively 1,962; 31,256; 267,072; and
1,520,110. These counts are exact for this declared action. No maximal
symmetry group or minimal possible search domain is asserted.

The action preserves the *universal diagonal-pair existence problem* in
JoyboyBrian's PR6097 and Josie Elliston's PR6137. One representative per
orbit therefore suffices when searching all groups and label sets for a
width-two word of this length with distinct diagonal targets, each of
label length at least three. It also preserves their coefficient-class
collision relaxation. This is a search reduction, not a proof that the
representatives refute, and not a new six-call lower bound over any field.

## Why these are safe transformations

For a group G and g in a label set L, write

\[
 D_N(g)=(I-N)+[g]N\quad\hbox{in }M_2(K[G]).
\]

The universal target family is diag([a],[b]), where a and b are distinct
and neither is a product of at most two labels in L. A monomial S either
fixes E_00,E_11 separately or exchanges them. Thus

\[
 S D_N(g) S^{-1}=D_{SNS^{-1}}(g)
\]

preserves the target family, exchanging a,b in the second case. All
coefficients of the endpoint identity transform, so arbitrary dirty input
coordinates remain covered. A general scalar change of basis need not
preserve this diagonal target pattern. This is why the quotient uses its
monomial normalizer rather than all of GL_2(K).

There is an anti-isomorphism from M_2(K[G]) to M_2(K[G^op]) that transposes
the scalar matrices and keeps the named group elements. It reverses the
order of a product. Consequently an identity with projectors N_1,...,N_k
becomes an identity with N_k^T,...,N_1^T and labels g_k,...,g_1 over G^op.
The target is still diagonal. The set of group elements expressible by at
most two labels is unchanged: a length-two product in G^op is the reversed
product in G, and every ordered pair from L is allowed. Distinctness is
unchanged too. Applying the transformation twice returns the original
identity. Thus existence in this universal family is equivalent along R.

This does not grant R as a symmetry of an arbitrary *fixed* oriented group,
label set and target specification without transporting that specification.
Equivalently one can identify G^op with G by inversion and transport L,a,b
to L^{-1},a^{-1},b^{-1}. A caller keeping them fixed needs extra closure.

The collision relaxation is covariant for the same explicit reasons.
Monomial conjugation permutes the two diagonal equations and rescales or
exchanges the off-diagonal equations by nonzero scalars. It preserves their
zero class-sum constraints. Reversal sends a chronological subset
(i_1,...,i_j) to (k+1-i_j,...,k+1-i_1). It exchanges prefix and suffix
cancellation, preserves subset cardinalities, and transports target-marker
and nonidentity restrictions. Thus a feasible equality partition maps to
a feasible partition in both directions. This assertion concerns the
relaxation, not a particular branch order, node cap or proof format.

## The finite action

Represent the projective monomial group H by

\[
 D_t=\begin{pmatrix}t&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
 S_t=\begin{pmatrix}0&t\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad t\in K^*.
\]

It has order 2(q-1). Transposition gives

\[
 R C_S R=C_{S^{-T}}.
\]

Hence the abstract semidirect product generated by H and R has order
4(q-1), and every element is C_S or C_S R. Burnside's lemma applies to this
finite action, whether or not faithfulness is separately established.

An idempotent in X is specified by its image line and its complementary
kernel line. There are q+1 choices for the image and q for the kernel, so
|X|=n. Equivalently it has the form

\[
 N=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&1-a\end{pmatrix},\qquad bc=a(1-a).       \tag{2}
\]

Trace one excludes both zero and the identity in every characteristic.

## Fixed tuples without reversal

The identity fixes all n projectors. For t!=1, a projector commuting with
D_t is diagonal, so exactly E_00,E_11 are fixed. A projector commuting
with S_t must satisfy a=1-a and b=t c. In characteristic two the first
condition is impossible. In odd characteristic, a=1/2 and (2c)^2=1/t.
There are two solutions when t is a square and none otherwise. Precisely
(q-1)/2 nonzero elements are squares in odd characteristic.

It follows that the total number of fixed k-tuples, summed over H, is

\[
 n^k+2^k\left(q-2+\epsilon\frac{q-1}{2}\right).                 \tag{3}
\]

## Fixed tuples with reversal

Put T_S(N)=S N^T S^{-1} and h(S)=|Fix(T_S)|. A tuple fixed by C_S R has
N_i=T_S(N_{k+1-i}), including a fixed middle projector. Also

\[
 T_S^2(N)=(S S^{-T})N(S S^{-T})^{-1}.                           \tag{4}
\]

For S=D_t, the matrix S is symmetric, so (4) is the identity. The m
paired projectors are arbitrary and the middle has h(D_t) choices.
The equation for the middle is b=t c in (2). It is the condition that the
projector is self-adjoint for the nondegenerate symmetric form with matrix
diag(1/t,1). Every nonisotropic image line has exactly one such projector,
whose kernel is its orthogonal line. Isotropic image lines have none.
The isotropic-line equation is x^2=-t y^2. In characteristic two it has
exactly one projective solution for every t, because squaring is bijective.
In odd characteristic it has two solutions for half the nonzero t and
none for the other half. Therefore

\[
 \sum_{t\ne0}h(D_t)=q(q-1).                                   \tag{5}
\]

For S=S_t, direct calculation gives

\[
 T_{S_t}(N)=\begin{pmatrix}1-a&t b\\c/t&a\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Its fixed point condition includes 2a=1. There are no fixed projectors in
characteristic two. In odd characteristic, t!=1 forces b=c=0, which is
incompatible with bc=1/4. For t=1 there are q-1 choices, one for each
nonzero b with c=(4b)^{-1}. Since S_1 is symmetric, (4) is again the
identity. Every other S_t contributes zero, since it has no fixed middle.
Combining this with (5), the total fixed k-tuples over the reversal coset is

\[
 n^m(q-1)(q+\epsilon).                                        \tag{6}
\]

Adding (3) and (6), then dividing by 4(q-1), proves (1) by Burnside's lemma.
This proves all finite fields and every odd positive k. Finite execution
below corroborates the proof; it does not replace its universal argument.

## Canonical enumeration and its certificate

Order X lexicographically in a declared field encoding. Encode a tuple as
the corresponding base-n integer. Its canonical representative is the
minimum of its 4(q-1) images. This minimum is unchanged along an orbit,
and two tuples have the same minimum exactly when they lie in the same
orbit. No coefficient, label or target marker may be dropped when a later
solver transports an instance to that representative.

The standalone verifier constructs F_2,F_3,F_4,F_5,F_7,F_8,F_9 and checks
their field operations. It obtains X independently by all matrix entries
and by outer products u v^T with v^T u=1. It counts the fixed projectors
under every declared transformation and checks (1) at k=1,3,5. A separate
complete orbit enumeration covers every length-five tuple at q=2,3,4,5.
It marks each orbit in a bounded byte array, rejects intersections with
previous orbits, and requires every tuple to be covered. Its representative
digest and orbit-size histogram are generated from that enumeration.

The verifier also expands new formal positional words coefficientwise to
check conjugation and opposite-group covariance. It rejects transpose
without reversal and a nonmonomial scalar adapter as incorrect substitutes.
These checks import no predecessor scientific code and make no decision
about whether a collision representative is feasible.

The written theorem is a scoped algebraic proof. No independent human or
formal proof-kernel acceptance, new kappa, native implementation, complete
supplier invoice or discharge of the selected five assumptions is claimed.
