# Paired corank-two roots: exact commutants and the first coupled closure

**Status:** proved linear algebra over a field of characteristic other than two. Finite exact checks supplement the proofs. **No new κ.** No native, caller, or selected certificate is changed.

Base: `7c49fe32c55de02c2c873655c804b16e4c3fef0a`. The root/reflection duality is Theorem G of CrocSwap/integer-mult-bounds#6888, pinned at `936a8a19896a56afbf428da6f7c29c175f348935`. The new results are the block normal form and exact commutant correspondence below.

## 1. Setting and scope

Over a field F with characteristic different from two, take a chronological word of N nonzero isotropic rank-one transvections `T_k=I_n-2x_k y_k`, with `y_k x_k=0`, whose product (later factors on the left) is `-I_n`. Assume **corank two**, `N=n+2`. Partition the chronological indices into L full blocks of two **consecutive** units and t individual thin units. Require `L>=2` and, inside each full pair `(a,b)`, `y_a x_b=y_b x_a=0`.

Write `X_i=[x_a,x_b]`, `Y_i=[y_a;y_b]`, and `R_i=X_iY_i`. The full block is `I-2R_i`. Thin units stay separate. We study the algebra generated by these **grouped** operators and the thin rank-one operators; commuting with each full rank-one summand separately is not required.

Put `X=[x_1,...,x_N]`, `Y=[y_1;...;y_N]`, `G=YX`, `K=I+2 lower(G)`, and `Z=K-G`. The root identity gives `X K^{-1}Y=I_n`, so X is surjective and Y injective. Consequently `rank G=n`, `ker G=ker X`, and `rank Z=2`.

Choose a rank factorization `Z=PQ`, with P of size `N x 2` and Q of size `2 x N`; write the rows and columns as `p_k,q_k`. Then `p_k q_k=1`, and the dual reflections `r_k=I_2-2q_k p_k` have product `-I_2` by #6888. For completeness, the needed implication follows directly: `G K^{-1} G=G` gives `Z K^{-1} Z=Z`; cancelling the full-rank factors in `P Q K^{-1} P Q=P Q` gives `Q K^{-1}P=I_2`. The rank-one product formula gives the dual product `I_2-2Q K^{-1}P=-I_2`. Conversely this dual identity makes `K^{-1}Z` an idempotent of rank two, so `K^{-1}G=I-K^{-1}Z` has rank `N-2` and is idempotent. Any minimal factorization `G=YX` then satisfies `X K^{-1}Y=I`, hence gives the primal root. The product formula itself follows by induction, adding the last rank-one factor on the left.

All results below are over F. An F-basis is not automatically an admissible dyadic basis. A decomposition of constant operators is not automatically a decomposition of data masks, recursive jobs, or the paid invoice.

## 2. Full-block normal form

For full pair i, let `P_i` be its two p rows and `Q_i` its two q columns. Isotropy and orthogonality give `P_i Q_i=I_2`, hence `P_i=Q_i^{-1}`. Replace its two summands by

\[
\widehat X_i=X_iQ_i^{-1},\qquad \widehat Y_i=Q_iY_i.
\]

This preserves `R_i`, its full block operator, and `\widehat Y_i\widehat X_i=0`. The chronological sign is constant across distinct full blocks, since their pairs are consecutive. Thus

\[
\widehat Y_i\widehat X_j=\operatorname{sgn}(i-j)I_2\quad(i\ne j). \tag{1}
\]

For a thin index s and full block i,

\[
\widehat Y_i x_s=\pm q_s,\qquad y_s\widehat X_i=\pm p_s, \tag{2}
\]

with signs given by chronological order. Thin/thin entries stay `G_st=sgn(s-t)p_s q_t`. Each full dual pair consists of complementary reflections, with product `-I_2`. Therefore changing full-pair orientations alone introduces no modulus of the grouped full operators.

Omit hats from now on. Equation (1), with `L>=2`, proves that each `X_i` has column rank two and each `Y_i` row rank two; this is not an extra hidden assumption.

## 3. Exact commutant correspondence

Let C be the algebra of `S in M_n(F)` commuting with every `R_i` and every thin `x_s y_s`. Let D be the algebra of `A in M_2(F)` commuting with every thin reflection `r_s`.

**Theorem 1.** There is an algebra isomorphism `C -> D`, after the chosen normalization, characterized by

\[
S X_i=X_i A,\qquad Y_i S=A Y_i\quad\text{for every full }i. \tag{3}
\]

Its inverse is explicit. For `A in D`, set `lambda_s=p_s A q_s`. Let `D_A` act on `F^N` as A on each full pair and as scalar `lambda_s` on thin index s. Then

\[
S_A(Xv)=X D_Av\quad(v\in F^N),\qquad Y S_A=D_A Y. \tag{4}
\]

### From the primal commutant to the dual commutant

Full ranks imply `im R_i=im X_i` and `ker R_i=ker Y_i`. Commutation with `R_i` therefore gives `S X_i=X_i A_i` for a unique `A_i`. Substitution into `S X_iY_i=X_iY_iS` and cancellation of `X_i` gives `Y_i S=A_i Y_i`. For distinct full i,j,

\[
A_i(Y_iX_j)=Y_i S X_j=(Y_iX_j)A_j.
\]

Equation (1) gives `A_i=A_j`, so all equal one A. The same image/kernel argument for each nonzero thin rank-one operator gives `Sx_s=lambda_s x_s` and `y_sS=lambda_s y_s`. Combining with (2) yields

\[
Aq_s=\lambda_s q_s,\qquad p_s A=\lambda_s p_s. \tag{5}
\]

Thus A commutes with `q_s p_s` and `r_s`, and `lambda_s=p_s A q_s`. Uniqueness follows from full rank of any `X_i`.

### Construction of the inverse

If A commutes with `r_s`, it commutes with the rank-one projection `q_s p_s`, so (5) holds with `lambda_s=p_s A q_s`. We claim `G D_A=D_A G`. For full/full blocks this follows from (1); for full/thin and thin/full blocks from (2) and (5). For two thin indices s,u it follows from

\[
\lambda_s p_s q_u=p_s A q_u=\lambda_u p_s q_u.
\]

Diagonal blocks are zero. Hence `D_A` preserves `ker G=ker X`, so (4) defines an endomorphism, uniquely because X is surjective. Moreover

\[
Y S_A X=YX D_A=G D_A=D_A G=D_A YX,
\]

and cancellation of surjective X proves `Y S_A=D_A Y`. These relations prove commutation with all full and thin operators and recover (3). The constructions are inverse and preserve addition, multiplication and identity. QED.

**Consequences and distinctions.** A simultaneous direct-sum decomposition is equivalent to a nontrivial idempotent in C, hence in D. A common invariant subspace without invariant complement need not yield an idempotent. Scalar commutant proves indecomposability, not in general absence of all proper invariant subspaces. A dyadic direct-sum basis does not follow from this field-level argument.

**A graph test is insufficient for grouped stages.** The exact zero-thin example with `L=3` and full dual bases `I`, `[[1,1],[0,1]]`, `[[1,0],[1,1]]` has a strongly connected six-vertex Gram digraph. Direct primal equations give commutant dimension one for its six individual rank-one summands, but dimension four for its three grouped full stages. The latter decomposes by Theorem 2. Thus strong connectivity of a chosen rank-one decomposition cannot certify indecomposability of the native stage operators. No data-mask or cost claim is needed for this counterexample.

## 4. Uniform zero- and two-thin classification

Each full dual pair has central product `-I_2`. Therefore, in inherited chronological order,

\[
r_t\cdots r_1=(-1)^{L+1}I_2. \tag{6}
\]

Determinants show t is even; also `n=2L+t-2`.

**Theorem 2.** Every root in this setting with `t<=2` decomposes over F into two corank-one roots after the full-pair recombination of Section 2, uniformly in L, thin placement, and all full-pair parameters.

- `t=0`: L is odd. Each summand has L units on `L-1` dimensions.
- `t=2`, L even: `r_2r_1=-I`, so `r_2=-r_1`. The thin reflections are complementary. Each summand has `L+1` units on L dimensions.
- `t=2`, L odd: `r_2r_1=I`, so `r_2=r_1`. Both thin units lie in one summand. The unit counts are `L+2,L` and dimensions `L+1,L-1`.

**Proof.** For zero thin queries, D is `M_2(F)`. For two, D contains the two complementary rank-one eigenprojections of `r_1`. Lift them using Theorem 1. To identify the summands, diagonalize `r_1` over F (or choose any basis in the zero-thin case). Each thin pair then has `q_s=a_s e_j`, `p_s=a_s^{-1}e_j^T` for some nonzero scalar `a_s`. Rescale `x_s` by `a_s^{-1}` and `y_s` by `a_s`, which preserves its transvection. The normalized Gram matrix now splits by its two coordinate channels. Each channel is the scalar sign matrix `S_m`, with zero diagonal and entries `sgn(i-j)` in its own inherited chronological order. In the three cases, m is odd with the counts displayed above. This matrix has rank `m-1`: its kernel is the one-dimensional alternating vector, and its even-size leading principal minor is invertible. Equivalently this follows from the dimension-one root/reflection duality. Its minimal rank factorization yields a corank-one root of dimension `m-1`. Full-span factorization identifies the original summand with that realization. The dimensions are positive because `L>=2`, and zero thin queries force `L>=3`. QED.

This excludes an **algebraically indecomposable** zero- or two-thin construction in this model. It does not exclude masks coupling decomposable operators, larger corank, or a native implementing nonorthogonal pairs. It does not permit splitting an invoice after a field-only conjugation.

## 5. Sharp four-thin witnesses

For every `L>=2`, use standard basis q columns and p rows at each full dual pair. At one gap between full blocks insert four thin reflections `A,B,A,B` if L is even, or `A,B,B,A` if L is odd, with

\[
A=\begin{pmatrix}-1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
B=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Use `q=(1,0)^T,p=(1,0)` for A and `q=(1,1)^T,p=(1/2,1/2)` for B. These are dyadic reflections. In the even case `(BA)^2=-I_2`; in the odd case `ABBA=I_2`. Thus (6) holds in both cases. Duality gives a root with `N=2L+4`, `n=2L+2`.

A matrix commuting with A is diagonal; commuting also with B makes its diagonal entries equal. Hence `D=F I_2` and Theorem 1 gives `C=F I_n`. The root is indecomposable, also after every field extension of characteristic different from two. This sharply establishes four as the first possible thin count. It is not a claim of irreducibility in the stronger invariant-subspace sense.

Exact fixtures use `L=2` and `L=18`. For L=18, a full-span gauge matches the full operators and coordinate masks of the two `chain(9|9)` copies in #6902 after deletion of the two unused fields. The full/full Gram `S_L tensor I_2` is invertible for even L; its column basis plus the common full-row kernel supplies the gauge. The checker confirms all resulting entries are dyadic and verifies the product, support, orthogonal pairs and prefix-mask commutation over Q.

**Exhaustiveness of the large primal check.** For the even-L four-thin witness let U be the span of the `2L` full columns and K the common kernel of the full rows. Invertibility of `S_L tensor I_2` gives `V=U direct-sum K`, with `dim K=2`. Any matrix commuting with every full `R_i` preserves each `im R_i` and `ker R_i`, hence U and K. The full cross-Gram blocks force one common `2 x 2` action on all full column pairs. The action on K is an arbitrary `2 x 2` matrix. Conversely every such block action commutes with the full stages. Their full-stage commutant is therefore exactly eight-dimensional. The check constructs those eight matrices from primal columns and kernel vectors and solves their thin-commutator equations directly. This is exhaustive without invoking the thin-reflection correspondence.

## 6. Invoice cancellation and limitations

The L=18 witness has 38 fields and four thin queries, versus 40 and six in #6902. Full masks on surviving fields are unchanged. Each deleted empty-mask field removes one credited rank-one target per port; each deleted thin query removes one paid rank-one child. At 104 ports, T and P both lose 208 copies of rank one. Every other formal root-invoice term is unchanged, so `sum_r (T_r-P_r) r^(1-kappa)` is identical for every real exponent. This is exact cancellation, not a new crossing or caller admission. More generally `n-t=2L-2` at corank two.

No physical caller is submitted. A generic preliminary 38-field emission increased ordinary work; it did not establish the predecessor's `10^9` raw envelope. A new caller, ordinary bound, A6 owned-ascent convention, LAMBDA convention, BIT admission and the five inherited framework assumptions remain separate if this witness is later used for multiplication.

## Open next questions

1. **Mask compatibility.** Determine when the lifted idempotent commutes with all coordinate data masks and admits a dyadic basis. This is needed to turn the algebraic reduction into a cost theorem. A first finite test is to solve those mask equations for the explicit thin-two projection.
2. **Four-thin mask gains.** Find a thin placement or coordinate representation with a better complete paid/target profile and an actual merged-idle proof. Indecomposability alone yields no saving, as the witness shows.
3. **Larger corank or a changed native.** Escaping (1) requires changing corank, pair orthogonality, or the full-block interface. Each needs complete accounting.
