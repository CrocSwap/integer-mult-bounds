# Physical joins of the numerical Walsh program

This audit follows the [numerical layer](walsh-node.md) from its external bit order through the two kinds of recursion and back to rounded signed mantissas. A coarse row includes every fixed-width numerical record on all of its ordinary addresses. Row values can be arbitrary or correlated.

## A finite Walsh conversion divides by eight without taking more spectator bits

Let $P=q^{L_F}$, $W=m+1$, $s_0=W/16$, and let the current finite depth have $C=8^t$ copies of the base repair, with $t>0$. Let $\lambda$ denote the unused leading root reserve and let $\omega$ denote one whole ordinary address, including the active block and its original inactive prefix and suffix. The physical row priority is

$$
 [\lambda][c<C][\ell<P][i<W][\omega][K].
$$

There are two check arms $e=0,1$ with $s_0$ entries indexed by $h$ per arm. For a forward conversion compute $HPX,HX$ into a dense marked bank ordered

$$
 [\lambda][c<C][\ell<P][e<2][h<s_0][\omega][K].
$$

For fixed $c,\ell,e,h,i$, the checks select one source and one destination within each $\lambda$, and add the rational multiple pointwise in $\omega$. Each input/destination bank advances in order through its copies of the same $\lambda$, crossing intervening coarse rows on its own tape. These scans are counted even when just one seed or arm is selected. The check bank has actual bit length $V_r/8$ because $2s_0=W/8$.

Fix $\lambda$ and flatten the new coarse index as

$$
 d=((cP+\ell)2+e)s_0+h .
$$

The child expects $(c',\ell',i')\in[C/8]\times[P]\times[W]$. Decode $i'=d\bmod W$, $\ell'=\lfloor d/W\rfloor\bmod P$, and $c'=\lfloor d/(PW)\rfloor$. These are two interpretations of the same consecutive range, so interpreting the forward or inverse conversion requires zero payload movement. Their strides in $\omega,K$ and their outer $\lambda$ stride agree exactly. The finite child computes the same normalized transform separately on every incoming check row, including rows that belonged to different parents or labels. Its output preserves the coarse index $d$. Return to $h=d\bmod s_0$, $e=\lfloor d/s_0\rfloor\bmod2$, $\ell=\lfloor d/(2s_0)\rfloor\bmod P$, and $c=\lfloor d/(2s_0P)\rfloor$. The frequency table now treats both arms at the same true $\omega$. The inverse conversion uses the same bijection on its two outgoing arms in a different time interval. The fixed transposed lift adds results back into seed banks. All these finite offsets are less than $W_a=CWP$.

## A spatial child selects its own field and restores every other coordinate

For an actual one-axis event choose its finite row label $d$. On the parent bank, extract that label through every $\lambda$ and ordinary address $\omega$ in order. Write the child's dense bank as

$$
 [\lambda][p<P_o][x<2^f][z<S_o][K],\qquad
 \omega=(p2^f+x)S_o+z .
$$

The old prefix $p$ includes every slot to the left of the chosen active axis, including wholly inactive fields from earlier depths. The old suffix $z$ includes every slot and inactive field to its right. The child fixes $\lambda,p,z$ and applies its $f$-bit normalized transform in $x$. At a further nonbase descent the only newly allocated coarse field is in the leftmost reserve through the factorization $\lambda=\lambda'W_{a'}+d'$. The physical bank already has order $[\lambda'][d'][p][x][z][K]$. All finite conversions at this spatial depth consume only the $8^{h'}$ copy part of $W_{a'}$ described above. Each selected coordinate map updates complete $f$-slots inside the active field and uses an independently occupied restored third slot.

After any legal child reply, scatter the returned records into the very same coarse row $d$, in their new numerical value order, through each $\lambda$. The original bank has been parked and may contain arbitrary nonzero values, including those in all nonselected rows. Write no word outside that coarse row. The [displaced-head program](recount-handoff.md) prices save and restoration of every such bank. Charged common rows and transposed free destination rows both follow this convention. A whole-parent scan for extraction/scatter belongs to the ordinary bill. A guarded exchange sees only the extracted dense role, a volume $V_r/(CWP)$. The fixed 15-head `build_role` on two independent banks supplies this extraction and scatter, with physical suffix length $S=K\,\#\{\omega\}$. Set source/destination role counts to $(W_a,1)$ on extraction and $(1,W_a)$ on return, using the same selected index. The literal program preserves an arbitrarily occupied source and every untouched destination role. Its uniform clock is 103 times the sum of the two bank volumes, at most $206V$ per direction, and it visits at most $60V$ cells. An existing marked right boundary can be cleared and replaced by full marked scans of both banks under the separate handoff allowance. The binary descriptor for $S=K2^r$ consists of the binary $K$ and $r$ zeros. The program constructs its temporary unary ruler at linear cost already included in 103. [Fixed role program and all-size bound](node-streams.md). The maximum ordinary index takes at most the actual $k$ address bits. The within-record suffix of $K=100k$ bits for a raw interchanger fits the supplier's $8k+8$-digit control. On the adaptive global branch the root split into two exact integer groups leaves at least $2^{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}$ leading reserve rows and the product of all newly occupied spatial fields is at most $k^4$.

## True key and signed-return ports

The global root permutation makes the group's selected bits contiguous. For an odd group place its single unmatched bit at a boundary. At a deeper frequency-table stage all finite regroupings leave $\omega$ unchanged. Reading $X_{cj}, Z_{cj}$ and the common auxiliary parity therefore uses the designated positions of the *current* binary counter on this bank, with its current active slot and inactive prefix/suffix. Finite seed and label indices, including the basis associated with the current free rectangle, come from the fixed event descriptor. A monomial stage updates the numerical address and phase on the same specified role. A later selector reads its new address in normal counter order. On actual complete arrays there is no search for a word by the contents of its frequency key. The source [explicit key compiler](walsh-repair-compiler.md) visits a contiguous short-integer file of at most $(64W+128)H$ cells and rewinds it on each operation at its already included cost. At the global guard this entire file is shorter than $K$ since $H=8W^2(y+2)$ and $m^{128}\le k/32$. Each uniform root contour reuses the same marked origins.

For group $i$ choose the common odd modulus from all arities on its actual width chain. Writing $w_i=w+s_1+\cdots+s_{i-1}$, we have $w_i+s_i\le86k+1$, $\log_2 U<k$ and the resulting $M_i<2^{96k}$. The fixed $100k$-digit modulus and residue ports accept high padding. Copy an input mantissa with a single $\lfloor s_i/2\rfloor$-digit low zero run, converting negative integers by their canonical residue. Every adaptive coefficient is a valid exact residue quotient, including the genuinely odd-denominator defect table. Every internal normalized child has even bit width. The even core exactly cancels the initial shift and the optional odd direct bit supplies the last unnormalized Walsh factor. The absolute integer group output is below $2^{w_i+s_i}$, hence its canonical lift through $M_i/2$ is unique. The modulus is one fixed dedicated bank for this entire group. Every scalar returns this input head home. A child in the same group reads the same bank at the same place, and independent routing tapes keep its head still. Copying the modulus into a marked child descriptor instead would cost at most a fixed number of $K$-digit scans at each handoff and also fits its existing price. The next group may choose a new modulus because its input was lifted to an actual signed integer. Both selected groups can be odd. They give two raw integer transforms and the single final normalization uses their *total* width. Signed real and imaginary root banks can be extracted with two monotone passes on the interleaved input, and each output written back to its original component in the same order. Their correctly rounded final output and true head widths are in the [complete layer](walsh-node.md#direct-widths-recurrence-and-the-numerical-boundary) and the [independent direct replay](node-walsh-review.md).

## Independent address replay

The [coordinate replay](https://github.com/CubeJerry/integer-mult-bounds/blob/a2a17b61d943676c757e0bbfaef3f4fae03ab8e5/research/moonshot-overhead-program/sessions/2026-11-26-S008/walsh-join-check.py) checks 12 complete two-arm cases with 581,504 finite reply records. Each child performs an address- and role-dependent replacement of arbitrary 64-bit values in both directions. An independent parent traversal checks the returned value at every old arm, rank, prefix, active and suffix coordinate. It also checks 196 two-level spatial extractions on 508,032 original records. All 14,112 selected records receive their expected changed value and every other record keeps its incoming bits. These fixtures exercise layout and occupied continuation values. They do not execute the enormous nonbase adaptive Fourier instance or contribute to its transition bound.

The same coordinate replay now executes the [fixed 15-head role copier](../checks/node-tapes.py) on eight additional nested layouts. Its 32 independently occupied extraction and replacement calls performed 384,534 actual transitions. They returned 104 selected records through two nesting levels among 1,388 parent records, checking the unchanged surrounding payload, every descriptor and all 15 head origins at each physical return. The largest visited-cell count in one fixture was 18,450. The [six-head numerical key reader](walsh-repair-compiler.md#fixed-six-head-selector) then reads the source-frequency dot products from that preserved address order.

The [integer rounding proof](../S008/WalshRounding.lean) checks the final three-word midpoint computation for every magnitude and width. With the caller's $J=w+s+3$, it proves that $r$ satisfies $4t^2<2^s(2r+1)^2$ and either $r=0$ or $2^s(2r-1)^2\le4t^2$. These inequalities specify the unique nearest magnitude to $t/\sqrt{2^s}$ with a tie rounded upward. Its input is the actual integer square root $\lfloor\sqrt{2^{2J-s}}\rfloor$, and the candidate and optional increment are the same integer operations as the program.

The [two-group port proof](../S008/WalshGroups.lean) verifies that each actual positive denominator product gives the required centered range, and that every modulus under the outer $w_i+s_i\le86k+1$, $U_i<2^k$ guards fits one $100k$-bit word. From the first and second canonical integer endpoint tickets with their independent moduli, `two_group_return` derives the single dot-product sign on both selected groups, for every input collection and widths of either parity. `even_shift_spec` covers the exact binary shift and `real_midpoint_error` turns the squared midpoint test into a half-unit real error. `complex_layer_squared_error` proves for the $2\cdot 2^k$ real mantissas at $72k$ fractional bits that the sum of squared errors is strictly below $2^{-100k}$. These are ordinary kernel-checked declarations in the [pinned replay](../verify.py). The construction of the spatial tickets and their cost uses the finite repair and row mechanisms described above.

The smaller-global-width branch, valid at $k\ge2^{32}$ and $X<2^{24}$, bypasses the finite and spatial representations above. It uses one unsigned-residue register per word at the odd modulus aligned with $U=1$, executes all selected unnormalized integer butterflies, lifts once and invokes the same final signed normalization.
