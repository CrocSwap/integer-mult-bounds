# Numerical cost of the integer caller's address permutations

The [source permutations](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/3344da59991ed94f9ba711217b29c1d760badfa6/preprints/The-logarithmic-exponent-of-integer-multiplication-on-tapes-November-24-2026/build/permutations.tex) use eight shifts, repair exceptional records, permute address positions and route short-block permutations through Beneš networks. The [integer caller](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/3344da59991ed94f9ba711217b29c1d760badfa6/preprints/The-logarithmic-exponent-of-integer-multiplication-on-tapes-November-24-2026/build/integers.tex) uses them for CRT rotations and local Fourier pairings. We keep that schedule and specify its finite control costs.

Take integer $k\ge 2^{32}$, $b=\lceil\log_2(2k)\rceil$ and records of $k\le R\le 2^k$ bits. Write $V=MR$ for the payload bits in $M$ independently occupied records. A zero-based ordinary record address uses at most $k$ digits. Uncombined spectator extent counts fit $k+1$ digits, and combining an extent with the physical record width uses at most $2k+1$ digits. A leading positive range can be enumerated as an unchanged prefix. The outer caller has $M=2^k$. Let $J(u)\ge1$ be any monotone upper bound for steps per encoded bit in a completed binary interchange on two occupied fields of common width at most $u$, including returns, on the actual spectator ranges. Denote its visited-cell coefficient by $S_I$. The charge for a call on $W$ bits is $WJ(u)$. An array with shorter records can be padded to $R=k$ and charged on its padded bit volume. All other operations use a fixed number of finite-alphabet one-head tapes.

| Operation | Upper bound on steps, ordinary work and field exchanges combined |
|---|---:|
| Simultaneous XOR from positions in $X$ to distinct positions in $Y$, with an occupied auxiliary $Z$ and common field width $u\le k$ | $2^{34}bV(1+J(u))+k^{1105}$ |
| Specified permutation of $k$ binary address positions | $2^{42}bV(1+J(k))+k^{1110}$ |
| One controlled layer of disjoint blocks of at most $140\lceil\log_2k\rceil$ bits, with at most $3k/4$ common external control positions | $2^{72}b^3 V(1+J(k))+k^{1120}$ |
| Two forward CRT orders and one inverse order for arrays of at most $V$ bits each | $2^{75}b^4 V(1+J(k))+k^{1125}$ |

The controlled-layer bound applies to the CRT rotations below and to supplied Fourier permutation tables with empty external controls. The Fourier arithmetic and the production of its prescribed permutations are separate caller operations. Including one sequential interchange call, all operations visit at most $(S_I+2^{16})V+k^{400}$ cells. On the full outer box, $k^{1125}\le2^k\le V$ for $k\ge2^{32}$ by monotonicity of $k/\log_2 k$. The additive polynomials in the first row apply even to shorter streams.

## Generating actual controls and exceptional destinations

Take guard width $G=2048b$. At any guard prefix or exceptional record, the complete Boolean control list, rotation amount or corrected destination can be computed in $k^{1024}$ actual head steps. This includes CRT and Beneš generation and scratch cleanup. Put $P=(2k)^{140}\le k^{150}$. Every local block table has at most $P$ entries. Use scratch words of $w=8k+8$ bits. A run offset is at most $2^k R\le2^{2k}$, and every polynomial table index below fits in $w$ bits.

1. Store scratch arrays in marked intervals on tapes, delimiter-separated, with at most $A=4096(k+1)^3(P+1)^2\le k^{304}$ words. To read or write indexed word $i$, return to the left mark, copy the index, skip one entire word per decrement, access the chosen word and rewind. Even a zero test and a rewind of the $w$-bit counter at each slot gives at most $32(A+1)(w+1)^2$ transitions per access. A fixed number of further tapes holds arithmetic operands. The [independently traced word-access program](compiler-controller.md#an-exact-indexed-access-on-nonempty-occupied-memory) also gives a stronger bound for this individual operation at arbitrary word width. Count one logical instruction for a read/write, a loop comparison, or a word operation (copy, compare, add, subtract, Boolean operation, bit read/write, shift, multiply or quotient/remainder). A multiply takes at most $w$ conditional shifted-add stages. A binary division takes $w$ shift/compare/conditional-subtract stages. Locating each individual bit by a full sweep and return still uses fewer than $2^{12}(w+1)^4$ moves, taking at most $4w+4$ bit decisions per stage and $16w+16$ moves per decision. A word instruction allowing up to three indexed operands/results therefore uses at most $2^{14}(A+1)(w+1)^5\le k^{311}$ transitions.
2. The CRT has at most $k$ axes. Precompute $P_i=\prod_{j<i}a_j$ and $\mu_i=P_i^{-1}\bmod a_i$. Each $P_i$ has at most $k+1$ bits. Trying every $z<a_i\le(2k)^{20}$ for the inverse fits the instruction count below. At a tree depth, detect invalid left-child digits. For valid digits recover $t_j=(y_j-\mu_j T)\bmod a_j$ in increasing order, adding $P_jt_j$ to $T$ starting from zero in that left child. The rotation on each corresponding right axis is $\mu_i T\bmod a_i$. Negate for the inverse depth order. Use zero for an invalid left address and fix all padded values $\ge a_i$. By setting the other digits to zero, every $T$ is at most $\prod a_i-1$. Temporary products fit $w$.
3. A supplied Fourier permutation table on at most seven short axes has at most $2^{140\lceil\log_2k\rceil}\le P$ entries. For it or a CRT rotation table, pair the wires of each $d$-bit Beneš node at their high bit. Edges indexed by $x$ carry $\pi(x)$. Build $\pi^{-1}$ by one traversal. The two mates are $x\mathbin{\mathrm{xor}}2^{d-1}$ and $\pi^{-1}(\pi(x)\mathbin{\mathrm{xor}}2^{d-1})$. From each uncoloured edge cross alternately via these mates, toggling colour at each step. Even cycles, including doubled edges, give one edge of each colour in every pair. Store input and output switches placing each colour at its intended high bit. On either colour the input low bits map bijectively to the output low bits. Recurse on those two tables. For $d=1$ use the sole switch. Its stage pattern is independent of $\pi$. At a requested stage use the current high branch and the other bits to look up the switch. Inversion can start from $\pi^{-1}$.
4. Allow $2P$ nodes, at most $P$ entries per node, and 16 words per entry for input/output, inverse, two mates, colour, switches, offsets and loop state. Process blocks in sequence. The descriptors, at most $k$ block tables, arithmetic and the explicit switch lists fit $A$. At each node allow $P$ candidate starts and a walk of $2P$ steps per start, even if a previous start has coloured its component. Each step fits 64 logical instructions. Inversion, construction of both child tables and their switches, comparing keys and looking up the current switch use a further $128(k+1)(P+1)^2$ instructions per node. Including all $\le k$ blocks, CRT preparation, indexing masks and shift calculation gives at most $2^{16}(k+1)^4(P+1)^3\le k^{455}$ instructions. Reversing eight shifts to recover one exceptional destination can repeat the entire list computation up to 16 times. Cleanup needs at most $4(A+1)(w+1)$ transitions. These bounds establish the claimed $k^{1024}$ transitions and less than $k^{400}$ scratch cells with paid sequential indexing throughout.

## One guarded pass on occupied data

Mark the current ends of streams and reuse tapes at a common origin. A separate $R$-cell ruler traversed and rewound per record marks boundaries on initial copying. Pass raw bits to each interchanger with the within-record index as a suffix spectator. Strip or regenerate boundary marks by one complete source/destination pass if the enclosing routine uses them. These passes are covered below. A complete pass can enumerate the $k$ address bits with a binary counter, transfer an arbitrary $R$-bit record and return all heads for at most $64V$ transitions. In particular an elementary permutation of the four values of two address bits takes at most $256V+k^{10}$ steps. Stable-split into four tapes using the old bit pair. In the new address order read the next record from the bucket indicated by the inverse four-entry table. Deleting the two positions leaves precisely the same order of remaining coordinates within each bucket. This includes a single XOR between two bits. Masks selecting positions can be scanned alongside each address for direct linear cost per record.

Fix disjoint $G$-bit segments of $Y,Z$, one control at the low bit of each. Apply the source's eight shifts in its written order. For packed XOR, placing $X$ before both other chunks takes at most two width-$u$ interchanges and the same number to undo. In each of the eight rotations put the target after the other chunk if needed and restore their order. This charges at most 20 whole-$u$ interchanges on volume $V$. A general guarded batch has its controls before $Y,Z$ already.

At each prefix preceding the current target, compute the offset from the prefix controls and the other chunk. That prefix owns at least $2^u\ge 2^G$ records whenever a segment exists. Split its run at the wrap point, retaining all suffix blocks. Save the first part, copy the second part to output, then append the first part and clear/rehome that work tape. Computing the two run lengths in bits uses at most $2k+1$ bits. To copy a positive length $n$, load $n-1$ and stop on its first underflow. Across a whole run at most $2n+2k+2$ counter bits flip; returns after each borrow cost at most twice this. The zero-length flag is set during offset preparation. The controller work per nonempty prefix is absorbed by its payload because $2^G\ge(2k)^{2048}$. Per rotation $2^{11}V+k^{1026}$ covers 16 full scans/traversals, both countdown loops, and one prefix calculation/cleanup per fibre.

The ideal flip $D$ preserves every guard. If every $G-1$ bit guard avoids its first and last ten values, all eight shifts stay within the selected segments. By the integer square identity in the source, the executed $S$ agrees with $D$ there. Both bijections preserve the exceptional set. With $s\le k$ segment pairs, its cardinality $B$ satisfies $B/M\le80s2^{-G}$. Each of the $2s$ guards assumes all $2^{G-1}$ values equally often. To recognize exceptions scan the address and aligned segmentation mask once. For each guard in most-significant order, track its value and its bitwise complement capped at ten. At its end either one below ten signals an exception. This finite control costs at most $256V$ including rewinds. It applies after undoing temporary chunk order changes.

Extract the exceptional records. Attach the $k$-bit destination $DS^{-1}$ of the current address by the controller above, costing at most $B k^{1024}$ steps besides copies. Stable binary radix-sort their tags in $k$ split/merge passes, then merge the sorted records back into the exceptional slots in address order. A unary selector on each tag locates its sorting bit. Including full records and returns, these passes cost at most $256k(R+k)B+k^{10}$. Since $B/M\le80k(2k)^{-2048}$ and $k\le R$, both exceptional charges together fit $V$. Including marking, copies, all rotations and their interchanges, sorting, restoration and cleanup gives at most $2^{20}V(1+J(u))+k^{1100}$ for one nonempty guarded pass. Short or empty lists skip the prefix calculation.

## Emitting every requested permutation

* **Packed XOR.** Partition covered targets by their low-bit position modulo $G$. In each of at most $G$ classes all selected segments are disjoint. At most $G-1$ high target bits have insufficient guard space and use the individual four-bucket operation (this handles all $u<G$). The total fits $2^{34}bV(1+J(u))+k^{1105}$. The same proof holds with a lexicographic leading spectator prefix, including a leading incomplete binary range, provided its ordinary record addresses and true extent counts satisfy the preceding bounds. All $Z$ values are independently occupied and return to their original coordinates.
* **Position permutations.** For each cycle use the two reflections $i\mapsto-i$ and $i\mapsto1-i$ to obtain two involutions globally. Split the address into three chunks of width $\lfloor k/3\rfloor$ and at most two surplus bits. Pairs touching a surplus bit use at most two elementary swaps per involution. Between three unordered chunk pairs perform one bundle of genuine bit swaps each, costing three packed XORs per bundle with the third chunk restored. Within one chunk use distinct temporary positions in another, then the three bundled cross-exchanges $(i,t),(j,t),(i,t)$. This costs at most nine more XOR batches for each of three chunks. Across both involutions the total is at most 72 packed XORs and four elementary swaps. A scan of the supplied length-$k$ cycle table or a generated positioning plan is within the additive allowance. This proves the second row of the table.
* **Controlled short blocks.** Batch the blocks in their given order, in batches of at most $H=\lfloor k/(64G)\rfloor$ blocks. Our cutoff ensures $k\ge128G$, so at most $129G$ batches suffice since there are at most $k$ nonempty blocks. A batch has at most $140bH\le k/512$ active block positions. In a network stage keep all its nontarget block bits and the external controls, totalling at most $3k/4+k/512$. The union of the $Y,Z$ guard chunks, including current targets, needs only $2GH\le k/32$ positions. The necessary free positions therefore exist with slack. Place the controls first and each target at the low bit of its $G$-bit segment, reserving matching $Z$ segments from the remaining positions. This positioning and its inverse are the second row of the table. Apply the switched guarded pass between them. A batch has at most $280b$ stages, padding shorter networks by identities. There are at most $129\cdot 2048\cdot 280\,b^2<2^{27}b^2$ guarded stages over all batches. The third row follows. In particular actual target and guard fields keep their complete ranges and all borrowed positions are returned by each stage.
* **CRT.** Split each interval of axes into a left half of $\lfloor |I|/2\rfloor$ and the remainder on the right. At a fixed depth the left controls occupy at most $k/2+20\lceil\log_2k\rceil/2\le3k/4$ bits: replacing the single possibly shorter last field by a full one increases the total width by at most one full field and the left halves contain at most half the axes. Targets are disjoint complete fields with width at most $20\lceil\log_2k\rceil$. The preceding CRT generator gives the rotations for all valid and padded controls. Complete at most $\lceil\log_2 k\rceil\le b$ depths, for each of three transforms. The table's final inequality includes their combined cost.

Payload, exceptional arrays, extra tags and all ordinary exchange copies fit a constant number of reusable complete banks. A tag is at most one payload record. Each primitive above uses fewer than 32 bulk banks at a time. The guarded, XOR, position and block/CRT wrappers nest in at most five levels and call the interchanger sequentially. Reserving distinct origins for these bounded levels and all roots costs less than $2^{16}V$ ordinary cells. The controller stack, descriptors and tables use the explicitly bounded single arena. Range/ruler banks use at most $V$ each. Every head rewinds using the active mark. A shorter later call reuses the initial interval, so the same sum bounds the union of cells visited through halt.

## Short-axis preprocessing

The [short-axis sieve](address-axis-window.md) ensures that this bounded descending search succeeds for $k\ge2^{32}$. Its predicate tests whether each candidate is odd and coprime to all earlier axes. At most $k^2((2k)^{20}+1)\le k^{24}$ pairwise gcd tests suffice. A Euclidean gcd on these words makes at most $2(k+2)$ divisions because the larger of two successive nonzero remainders halves within two divisions. Even allowing $16(k+2)$ word instructions per gcd, the loop uses at most $k^{26}$ instructions. The arrays for the axis list, prefix products, trial inverses and shape checks fit $128(k+1)^2$ words; each of the preceding paid word instructions costs at most $k^{10}$ transitions with this smaller arena. Filling/clearing it, testing all window bounds, finding every $\mu_i$ by trial, and checking the final product and field widths bring the combined preprocessing below $k^{40}$ transitions and $k^6$ visited cells for $k\ge 2^{32}$. Failing the finite tests can branch to the specified fallback. A [sharper residual-prime product estimate](https://github.com/CubeJerry/integer-mult-bounds/blob/a2a17b61d943676c757e0bbfaef3f4fae03ab8e5/research/moonshot-overhead-program/sessions/2026-11-26-S008/recount-integration.md#short-coprime-axes-at-the-smaller-global-guard) also certifies the same descending predicate at smaller widths. The original search-success cutoff above is the retained supplier's, while the numerical costs here apply to every attempted search in range.
