# Literal quadratic register arithmetic for the dyadic caller

[The fixed transition generator](../checks/address-dyadic-words.py) produces three separate deterministic tuples, with 28, 52 and 80 states for multiplication, division, and integer square root. Multiplication and division use five tapes. Square root uses four. The generator runs before seeing any word width or input data. All loops in the resulting tables branch on a scanned bit or delimiter.

The ABI puts a left delimiter, exactly $w\ge1$ magnitude bits least-significant first, then a right delimiter on each input. Both immutable input heads start at the left delimiter. The output and the two scratch tapes start blank. The square-root machine takes one input. All three halt with heads at their respective origins, input words intact, one equally padded answer, and completely blank scratch. The output of multiplication is $ab\bmod 2^w$. For nonzero $b$ the quotient is $\lfloor a/b\rfloor$, and the square-root output is $\lfloor\sqrt a\rfloor$.

A low-to-high shift with incoming low bit takes $2w+3$ steps, including restoration to the first digit. A copy of one equal-width row into another takes $3w+5$. A ripple addition or subtraction and a magnitude comparison each take $4w+5$. In the latter two, one transition reads the incoming bit and a second reads the corresponding other operand bit. The result and the next carry, borrow, or comparison flag are held in finite control. Different higher bits override any comparison of lower bits. The bounds include returning both word heads from the right delimiter. Common allocation plus final clearing and returning takes at most $10w+21$ steps. The final encounter of the outer loop delimiter is included.

* In multiplication, preserve the multiplier and scan its bits from low to high. After $j$ bits the accumulator is $a(b\bmod2^j)\bmod2^w$, and the shifting source is $2^ja\bmod2^w$. Each next input bit costs at most $6w+9$ steps.
* In division, consume the dividend high to low. If the previous prefix has quotient $z$ and remainder $r<b$, for the next bit $d$ compare $2r+d$ with $b$, subtract $b$ exactly when needed, and append this choice to the quotient. The shifted remainder never exceeds the newly consumed full prefix, so it fits in $w$ bits even when $b$ has width $w$. Each next bit costs at most $12w+17$ steps.
* To take an integer square root, consume high-to-low pairs of bits as radix-four digits. An odd input width has implicit leading zero. If the old prefix is $P=z^2+r$ with $0\le r<2z+1$, a new base-four digit $d$ produces trial remainder $4r+d$ and threshold $4z+1$. Accept root bit one precisely when the trial reaches that threshold and subtract it on acceptance. The new root is $2z$ or $2z+1$. Its square and the new remainder sum to $4P+d$. Also $4r+d\le4P+d<2^w$, and $4z+1\le4P+1<2^w$ before a noninitial pair, since the old prefix occupied at most $w-2$ bits. For the initial pair $z=0$ and the threshold is one. Each radix-four digit costs at most $21w+32$ transitions.

Consequently each machine costs at most $64(w+1)^2$ transitions. Every head stays between positions zero and $w+1$ throughout. The cumulative space of one call is therefore at most $5(w+2)$ positions for multiplication or division, and $4(w+2)$ for the root.

These routines strengthen the serialized dyadic FFT branch. Use $w=L=8192(k+1)$ as a common magnitude capacity, padding shorter fields with zeros while copying them into registers. All unrounded intermediates fit its magnitude capacity by [the grid bounds](address-dyadic-caller.md). Thus the modular product returns the exact required product before rounding. A fixed sign field in a separate cell, conditional negation, omitting high padding or fixed-grid truncation require a bounded number of additional monotone tours. The existing numerical integer square roots are exactly those produced by the radix-four routine. Even a signed, rounded scalar operation with intermediate register copies, comparison and subtraction fits $2^{12}(L+2)^2$ transitions. Each uses at most sixteen of the above magnitude calls or their shorter signed tours, with 32 further whole-register copies of at most $4L+10$ transitions each.

There are at most $k+2$ roots and at most $32(k+2)$ ordinary word operations to generate the primitive generators, fewer than $32N$ to emit the twiddle archive, and $3kN/2$ butterflies with at most 64 numerical word operations each. A further 128 per butterfly suffice for complete records, ruler and counter scans, including concatenated archive staging: six full $N$-record archive tours per stage cost at most $6kN$ record visits. Root/readout/pointwise products cost fewer than $128N$ additional word operations. Even allowing 2048 further word operations per $kN$ for initialization, clearing, all raw formatting, and small parameter arithmetic gives fewer than $2^{12}kN$ word-operation equivalents. Hence, with $N=2^k$ and $k\ge4$,
$$
T_{\rm dyad}\le 2^{24}kN(L+2)^2 < 2^{54}N(k+1)^3.
$$
The four temporary register banks in a call fit within the already budgeted 128 scalar and controller banks, preserving the bound $S_{\rm dyad}<2^{28}n$ on all visited cells.

An extended run of the literal tables checked 1,460 multiplication, 1,398 division and 158 root instances, including widths up to 64, all input pairs through five bits where applicable, odd and even radicand widths, carry-heavy cases and exact zero handling. The largest trace lengths were 19,025, 34,589 and 41,690 transitions. Every case checked surviving input, answer, empty scratch, all head positions, and the cumulative interval bound. The mathematical charge above covers every positive word width.
