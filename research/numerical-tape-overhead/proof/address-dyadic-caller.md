# A uniform dyadic convolution branch at all widths $k\ge4$

For two contiguous $n$-bit binary inputs, set $b=\lceil\log_2 n\rceil$, $h=\lceil n/b\rceil$, $k=\lceil\log_2(4h)\rceil$, $N=2^k$ and $q=k+1$. For every $k\ge4$, one fixed finite-alphabet multitape program computes the exact $2n$-bit product, including leading zeros, in
$$
T_{\rm dyad}\le2^{54}Nq^3,\qquad S_{\rm dyad}<2^{28}n.
$$
The [literal fixed-width-register routines](address-dyadic-words.md) give the displayed quadratic-register time. The independent coarse register-tour derivation below also yields $2^{90}Nq^4$. The space includes a further $2^{14}$ home cells for the dormant fast or schoolbook heads. These estimates also meet the proposed $2^{120}Nq^4$ or $2^{160}Nq^6$ time budgets. They are uniform above any width at which the caller elects to leave this branch. The normalized radix-two butterfly and pointwise convolution are classical. The contribution here is an explicit tape, precision and cumulative-space allowance for the intervening lengths.

## Adjoint order and occupied row streams

Place the $h$ radix-$2^b$ digits in increasing order on $N$ sites, then append zeros. On each forward pass do normalized decimation in frequency. For descending $m=N,N/2,\ldots,2$, separate each contiguous $m$-record block into two halves. At offset $j<m/2$ with inputs $a,b'$ emit
$$
(a+b')/\sqrt2,\qquad e(j/m)(a-b')/\sqrt2 ,
$$
with the first outputs together followed by the second outputs. Here $e(x)=\exp(2\pi i x)$. Splitting the defining positive-sign normalized DFT into even and odd frequency indices proves that this order yields its bit-reversed spectrum. The other input receives the identical permutation. Multiply in this order. The inverse visits $m=2,4,\ldots,N$, multiplies each second input by $e(-j/m)$, and then applies the same normalized sum and difference. These are adjoints in reversed order. If $R$ is bit reversal and $U$ the DFT, the two spectra are $RUx,RUy$ and the inverse acts by $U^*R$. Thus $\sqrt N$ times the returned vector is the ordinary convolution. No index wraps, since $2h-2<N$.

To process one block, copy its first half from the incoming bank to a marked short-term bank and rewind that bank. Read it beside the second half still arriving from the original stream. Append each first output directly to the destination, and each second output to another marked bank. Append the latter bank to the destination before starting the next block. Clear and return both internal heads at each block. One marked table of $m/2$ twiddles is read in increasing order and returned to its own start between blocks. Store all stage tables consecutively with marked boundaries. There are $N-1$ complex table entries. Staging one table can scan the complete archive once per stage. The two forward transforms and one adjoint reuse the same tables and homes, conjugating the second component when required. Array banks alternate between stages. The first spectrum remains parked at a separate fixed origin while the second is computed. Each offset, group and row-loop extent is available as a binary word of at most $k+1$ bits. Even a fresh full comparison and rewind per record is included below.

## Exact-grid roots and error

Use $p=128q$ fractional bits and $Q=2^p$. A rounded real product divides its exact integer product by $Q$ and rounds to the nearest integer. Signed ties may use either deterministic direction. Compute $H=\lfloor\sqrt{Q^2/2}\rfloor$, so $H/Q$ approximates $1/\sqrt2$ to less than $\eta=1/Q$. The primitive roots for lengths 2 and 4 are exactly $-1$ and $i$. For subsequent levels, a stored root $(C+iS)/Q$ gives
$$
C'=\left\lfloor\sqrt{Q(Q+C)/2}\right\rfloor,\qquad
S'=\left\lfloor QS/(2C')\right\rfloor.
$$
These are the positive half-angle formulas. The input quadrant is the first quadrant throughout. If both coordinate errors are $\le\delta\le1/8$, the exact new cosine is at least $1/\sqrt2$ and the computed denominator $C'/Q$ is greater than $1/2$. The difference of the two real square roots is at most $\delta$ by rationalizing it, and truncation adds at most $\eta$. For the sine quotient, the input sine perturbation costs at most $\delta$, perturbing the denominator costs at most $2(\delta+\eta)$, and final truncation costs at most $\eta$. The next coordinate error is therefore at most $4(\delta+\eta)$. Starting with $i$, induction gives primitive-root complex error $\le2^{2k+3}\eta$; this is below $1/8$ for every $k\ge4$ and validates the induction.

Starting with one, form the successive powers for each stage by rounded complex multiplication with its primitive root. With primitive error $\delta_0=2^{2k+3}\eta$, previous power error $E$ becomes at most $(1+\delta_0)E+\delta_0+2\eta$. There are at most $N$ powers. Since $N\delta_0<1/2$, their errors are at most $2N(\delta_0+2\eta)\le2^{3k+5}\eta<2^{-100k}$. These bounds are absolute, including angles close to zero. Compute the final positive scaling as $\lfloor\sqrt{NQ^2}\rfloor/Q$, also within $\eta$.

Split each ideal stage into its unitary diagonal and its ideal disjoint Hadamard layer. At represented input norm at most $2^{13k}$, the diagonal has Euclidean error at most $2^{-100k}2^{13k}+2\eta\sqrt N$, including independent component rounding. The implemented Hadamard acts exactly with coefficient $H/Q$ before rounding. Its pre-rounding operator perturbation is at most $\sqrt2\eta$, and rounding adds at most $2\eta\sqrt N$. Each elementary layer therefore errs by less than $2^{-85k}$. The two stages per level, at most $2k<2^k$, produce total transform error below $\epsilon=2^{-84k}$ and preserve the input-norm allowance when starting below $2^{12k}$.

The digit arrays have norm $<2^{5k/2}$: $h\le N/4$, and for $b\ge4$, $b-1<\log_2n\le\log_2 b+k-2\le b/2+k-2$. Smaller $b$ also satisfy $b<2k$. The represented pointwise product differs from the true product of spectra by at most $\epsilon(2\cdot2^{5k/2}+\epsilon)+2\eta\sqrt N<2^{-80k}$ and has norm below $2^{6k}$. The adjoint adds at most $\epsilon$. Multiplication by $\sqrt N\le2^{k/2}$, its own scalar error $\eta$, and a final $2\eta\sqrt N$ for entry rounding leave the full Euclidean error below $1/4$. Round all real parts to integers, propagate the ordinary carry in radix $2^b$ and read out exactly the low $2n$ binary bits in MSB-first order. Each incoming carry is at most $h(2^b-1)$, and the product is below $2^{2n}$. All padded ranks above $2h-2$ have true coefficient zero.

## Fixed tape implementation and numerical costs

Use one common home per physical tape, a left and a right delimiter for each reusable word or bank, one binary data symbol per cell, and finitely many marked variants. A complex record has $r=512q$ cells. Its two components have one sign field each and magnitude fields of at least $145q+2$ bits, filled with zeros through the field boundary. The established norm bounds cover the stored components before final scaling, and $2^{13k}\sqrt N$ after scaling fits the same record if needed. Put $L=8192q$. Operating registers hold signed binary integers with their low digits next to home. Each working register needs at most $2L+4$ cells, even for an unreduced trial square. Products of represented components, integer numerators for the half-angle quotients and roots, $NQ^2$, and all carry words fit under $L$ bits before a trial square. A square-root trial uses at most $L$ bits for its proposed root. For example, an unscaled complex entry is bounded by $2^{13k}Q$ and its exact complex product by $2^{26k+1}Q^2$. Bounds, loop counts, and field positions are obtained from $q$ by shifts and unary marked rulers. The largest explicit unary ruler is $2L+4$ cells. Build $N$ in binary as a one with $k$ following zero bits.

Word arithmetic can use the following fixed finite loops. To select one binary word bit, scan the word from home with a separate ruler or countdown, then restore its head. To initialize a loop index use a marker on its own terminated unary ruler, advance one selected bit, and end on its delimiter. Signed add, subtract, compare and shifts make monotone sweeps between delimiters with one sign, borrow or carry in finite control. For a product consume multiplier digits, shift the marked multiplicand by one bit, and add it to the accumulator on the selected iterations. For division take the high operand bits successively, shift the remainder, compare against the divisor and subtract when permitted, and emit the next quotient bit. Register copies, truncations, zero tests, rounding and sign changes use the same shorter sweeps. These loops work with a variable occupied length and a marked end, including a single zero digit. Any product, division, or simpler whole-word operation, with clean work registers and all of its heads returned, costs at most
$$
U=2^{14}(L+1)^3
$$
transitions. Indeed there are at most $2L+4$ outer digits, at most $L+1$ bit selections per digit even if each index is reread from home, and at most 32 out-and-back tours of length $2L+4$ per selection. A simultaneous cell copy, delimiter check, ripple update and movement take at most four local transitions. The eight signs, initialization and closing sweeps fit the same bound for $L\ge 8192$. During long division the trial has at most one more bit than the divisor. For an integer square root, start at zero, try setting each bit high to low, square the candidate exactly, compare with the radicand, and retain precisely the admissible bits. At most $L+1$ trials of at most sixteen word operations each give at most $2^{18}(L+1)^4$ transitions, including clearing and returning auxiliary registers.

Count the concatenated raw input length or the first word using a binary digit counter, copy the two raw strings, read their copies backwards into $b$-bit digits for these least-significant-first arrays, and initialize the zero padding. A full tour to count or compare a $q$-bit counter for each raw digit is allowed. We have $n\le bh\le Nk$, so this and raw $2n$-bit output formatting cost less than $128Nq^2$ plus thirty word operations. During carry processing we may explicitly emit all $Nb$ low-to-high bits, truncate at the marked $2n$ boundary and read the retained bits backwards into the blank output tape. The raw scratch then has $Nb\le Nr$ cells.

There are at most $k+2$ integer square roots, one final scaling and one normalization among them, with fewer than 32 ordinary word operations per primitive root. Fill the $N-1$ archive entries sequentially with one rounded complex product per step. Replicating the active stage row and locating its boundary can scan the whole archive once per stage, granting up to two whole archive tours for each of the three transforms. Each of the $3kN/2$ butterfly visits takes one complex product, two complex scalar applications, signed adds/subtracts, rounding, sign selection, and copies, at most 64 ordinary word operations. Input, output, half-block parking, twiddle and ruler operations cost at most 32 further full record tours per visit, including cleared scratch and the rewind of each reused stage row. Each processed $r$-bit record permits an entire counter update or comparison in addition to those tours. A pointwise product, final scaling and one carry pass cost fewer than 64 ordinary word operations per site. Even charging every record tour or $q$-bit counter use as a whole word operation, all copies, bootstrap, clock setup, array delimiters, clearing and glue fit
$$
T\le 2^{20}(k+2)(L+1)^4 + 2^{24}kN(L+1)^3 < 2^{90}Nq^4.
$$
Here $k+2\le N$, $k\le q$, and $L+1<2^{14}q$. There is no indexed random read in the stream or the twiddle archive.

For the union of visited tape/address pairs, fewer than 64 bulk heads suffice. Their roles are the external input and output, two raw operand copies, retained and full output strings, two initialized input arrays, two alternating transform arrays, two parked spectra, one pointwise product, two half-block scratch arrays, one complete root archive and one active stage row. Every such tape is reused from its own fixed home. Its entire visited interval lies between $-(Nr+2)$ and $Nr+2$. Allow 128 further heads for word registers and controllers. They cover eight complex operands or results, eight integer multiplicative/remainder registers, four root-trial registers, sixteen temporary sign/copy words, sixteen binary counters, twenty unary rulers and scratch for their reuse. Their absolute extents are at most $2L+4$. A single fixed finite control dispatches all stages, so there is no tape per stage or per tape word. Dormant heads for another multiplication branch contribute at most $2^{14}$ initial cells. Counting both sides of every home therefore gives
$$
S\le64(2Nr+5)+128(4L+9)+2^{14}<2^{21}Nq+2^{14}<2^{28}n.
$$
For the first strict inequality use $N\ge16$. For the second use $k\le b+2$, $N<8h$, and $3\le b\le n$ on every admissible input to obtain $Nq<8(n/b+1)(b+3)\le16(n+b)\le32n$. This bound is for the union over the complete calculation, including stage-table construction before array operations.

The executable [address-dyadic-caller.py](../checks/address-dyadic-caller.py) uses integer square roots, divisions and signed nearest-grid products throughout. Its extended replay checked 51 pairs (zero, dense all-ones and fixed-seed random) from 7 through 4097 input bits, across $k=4,\ldots,11$. Every represented convolution coefficient rounded correctly, every full output word agreed with the native integer product, and the largest real or imaginary reconstruction error was below $2^{-624}$. The replay executes the numerical algorithm. The physical cost bounds follow from the stream and register tours above.
