# A numerical price for the ordinary outer transform

This note supplies the phase, schedule, word-arithmetic and numerical recovery costs used with the permutation and selected-Walsh suppliers. We use the exact occupied-coordinate identity of the [November integer-multiplication manuscript, integers.tex](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/3344da59991ed94f9ba711217b29c1d760badfa6/preprints/The-logarithmic-exponent-of-integer-multiplication-on-tapes-November-24-2026/build/integers.tex). All logarithms are base two. Write \(N=2^k,\ b_0=\lceil\log(2k)\rceil\), and take \(k\ge2^{256}\) for these local arithmetic bounds. The final all-length dispatcher uses this composite transform for \(k\ge2^{4096}\). The independent ledger for full-stream permutations is [routing-outer-permutations.md](routing-outer-permutations.md). The standalone actual tape multiplier is [word-toom.md](word-toom.md).

## Layer count

An axis of the chosen shape has binary width at most \(w=20\lceil\log k\rceil\) and length \(a\le2^w\). A block of four to seven axes has at most \(7w\) address bits, hence at most \((2k)^{140}\le k^{141}\) coordinates. With \(r\) the least power of two at least \(2a-1\), we have \(r<4a\). In each of eight batches of fibres, assign the first \(2r-a\) available inactive coordinates to each active fibre in increasing fibre order, using disjoint intervals in the enumerated pool. The other seven batches supply at least \(7a\) coordinates per active fibre. The occupied-complement identity below restores each borrow, even when it already holds output. Invalid padded entries on the active fibre outside its first \(a\) coordinates may remain idle.

On each virtual \(2r\)-tuple, implement the truncated chirp unitary \(V\) by two chirp diagonals, a radix-two transform and its adjoint on the first \(r\) coordinates, and one \(L(z)\) layer across the halves. One transform on those \(r\) entries needs a bit-reversal permutation and at most three layers per bit (twiddles, pair placement by permutation if needed, and the normalized two-point Walsh). Each butterfly layer pairs the corresponding wires directly before the subsequent pair-routing conversion. This costs at most \(6(w+2)+5\) raw layers for \(V\), since \(\log r\le w+2\). The amplified map \(B=[(2VPV^*-I)(I-2P)]^{64}V\) has 129 copies of \(V\) or \(V^*\) and 128 diagonal signs. The full \(D_1 B^*D_2 B D_3\) has two of these circuits and three extra diagonals; arranging and restoring the virtual tuples adds at most two permutations. Thus \(8192(w+1)\) raw layers per axis per batch suffice, also at \(w=1\). Signs extend by the identity on the idle complement of the virtual tuple. In particular, the minus sign on \(I-2P\) for the reflected \(2P-I\) belongs on the tuple.

Complete each matching with identity pairs and route to edges of one selected address bit by a block permutation. The manuscript's \(H D_{\rm out}H D_{\rm mid}H D_{\rm in}\) and the inverse routing require at most ten layers (two permutations, three two-point Walsh rounds, three diagonals suffice). The inverse \(L(z)^*\) uses \(\bar z\). Identity pairs use the same factorization with \(Q=H\); for a pair requiring \(H\), use \(H^3\). Cycle through permutation, phase and Walsh slots, advancing each unfinished block at least once per cycle. This gives at most
\[
8192\cdot21\cdot 10\cdot 8\cdot 7\cdot3\ b_0 <2^{29} b_0
\]
global layers per transform, since \(w+1\le21 b_0\). Each selected Walsh address set has at most one bit from each block, hence size \(\le k/4\) (the looser \(\le k/2\) is used in its supplier). The forward and adjoint have identical layer bounds.


## Constructing the phases with fixed elementary arithmetic

Use \(p=2048k\) fractional bits. For \(e(t/d)\), reduce \(t\bmod d\), take \(g=p+2\,\mathrm{bitlength}(p+8)+40\), and compute \(16\arctan(1/5)-4\arctan(1/239)\) on the \(2^{-g}\) grid. For each arctangent truncate the alternating terms \(1/((2j+1)D^{2j+1})\) toward zero and stop at the first zero. Fewer than \(g+3\) terms are required, and the exact tail is at most the first omitted term. The absolute error on \(\pi\) is at most \(20(g+4)2^{-g}\). Compute \(2\pi(t\bmod d)/d\) by rounded division; its modulus is below 8. Form the Taylor terms of the complex exponential, multiplying by the imaginary argument, dividing by the next index, and truncating both components. One pair of rounding errors contributes at most \(2e^8 2^{-g}\) through all its successors, by \((j+1)\cdots(j+l)\ge l!\). Take \(J=16(p+1)+64\) terms. Once a rounded term vanishes all its successors are zero. From \(j=16\) the ratio of successive exact term magnitudes is at most \(1/2\) and \(8^{16}/16!<2^{16}\). The exact tail beyond \(J\) is below \(2^{-p-8}\). The angle error transfers with Lipschitz constant 1. After conversion to \(p\) bits, the root has absolute complex error below \(4\cdot2^{-p}\).


Compute \(1/\sqrt a\) as \(\lfloor\sqrt{\lfloor2^{2p}/a\rfloor}\rfloor2^{-p}\) by division and bit bisection. Sum up to \(2a\) grid roots exactly to form each truncated chirp multiplier and circulant eigenvalue, then scale by the rounded \(1/\sqrt a\). The normalized sum has error at most \(16(a+1)2^{-p}\). For the lift multiply by \(\sin(\pi/258)=\Im e(1/516)\). The exact \(|z|<1/2\) by the manuscript's bound 40 on the multiplier. All entries of \(Q=HL(z)\) have magnitude at least \((\sqrt3-1)/(2\sqrt2)>1/4\); the completion square root has argument at least \(3/4\). Identity pairs have moduli \(1/\sqrt2\).

After a normalized sum, count each complex addition, multiplication or division, norm, square root and conjugation separately. Fewer than 96 operations lie along any expression for a table cell. The lift uses one completion square root, Hadamard mixing, three magnitudes/ratios and their phase products. Exact intermediates have magnitude at most 64. Divisor magnitudes exceed \(1/4\). Norm is 1-Lipschitz, and the completion square root is away from zero. If both errors are \(\le\eta\le1/128\), each output error including rounding is at most \(2^{20}(\eta+2^{-p})\); for division use \(|y'|\ge1/8\) and \(|x|\le64\). Thus error is below \(2^{1950}(a+1)2^{-p}<2^{-100k}\), verifying the small-error prerequisite inductively because \(a+1\le2^{k/8}\). Round local cells and each complex prefix product to \(80k\) fractional bits. Their absolute errors are \(\le u_0=4\cdot2^{-80k}\). If \(E\) is the prefix error, \(E'\le(1+u_0)E+2u_0\). After at most \(k\) factors, \(E\le2((1+u_0)^k-1)\le4ku_0<2^{-70k}\), using \(ku_0\le1/2\). For the adjoint, reverse layer order, invert permutations and conjugate phases.

[outer-phases.py](../checks/outer-phases.py) implements the integer grid generator. Its comparison of five complete lifted matrices, including the occupied complement, uses an independent 180-bit DFT oracle. At \(p=96\) every Frobenius error was below \(4.0\cdot10^{-26}\). The transcendental inequalities above are separate from this finite verification.


## Fixed-tape setup and phase streams

Enumerate the blocks, batches, local layers, and every address of one block, in increasing order. An axis is at most \(k^{21}\); a block has at most \(M=k^{141}\) addresses. For a batch enumerate active fibres and inactive addresses lexicographically, mark the latter sequentially as they are assigned, and produce the two-way virtual-address table. Within a radix stage pair each wire with its specified butterfly mate. For a lift pair with the corresponding opposite half. Match remaining wires in increasing order and number the resulting pairs in order of their first member. The radix low-stage-bit wire or first L half retains its distinguished first side regardless of physical address. The pair number and its ordered side give a bijection to edges of the lowest block bit, which supplies the routing permutation and its inverse. Signs, chirps and twiddles follow the virtual address and the explicitly stored stage index. A global table consists of one contiguous marked table per block and stage, including identity entries. To invert a permutation, sweep its local entries and store each inverse address. These are only short-block arrays.

For definiteness allocate at most \(k^{200}\) words of \(k^8\) bits with delimiters in a single reusable arena. At most \(k\) blocks, fewer than \(k\) global layers, \(M\) entries each and 64 words each suffice for the catalogue. Allocate one further disjoint working segment of the same size; all other counters, pi, basis-pair temporaries and fixed-point arithmetic use a fixed number of double-width registers. Integer lengths throughout this setup lie below \(k^8\) (even storing the last power in the Machin denominator takes fewer than \(32(g+3)\) bits). An indexed access returns to the left mark, skips words using a rewound binary index and copies the chosen word. A bound of \(64(k^{200}+1)(k^8+2)^2\) transitions suffices. Schoolbook multiplication or division on fixed marked registers takes fewer than \(2^{12}(2k^8+4)^4\) transitions by conditional shifts and adds or by trial subtraction per quotient bit. With at most four indexed operands and destination, one register instruction takes less than \(k^{240}\). Variable tape count and constant-time array access are unnecessary.

Per block/stage allow \(M^3\) iterations to select the next unpaired coordinate or a matching borrow by brute-force sequential ranking, with at most \(k^2\) word instructions per iteration. Each requested phase per entry takes at most \(a k^6\) word instructions, recomputing fresh roots using the preceding loops. Integer square roots use at most \(k^8\) instructions if scanning a full temporary, or \(O(p+k)\) trial bits by the known operand magnitude; the latter is below \(k^2\). The \(M^3 k^2\) allowance also covers each entry's phase generation. Add the arena initialization and rewinds. Across all blocks and layers this costs below \(k^{850}\) transitions and visits below \(k^{250}\) cells for the stated domain. No memoization is needed on tape; the cache in the finite replay only speeds tests.

For one full diagonal, stage the contiguous table for the next block onto a separate tape once, stripping wide setup-register padding to two signed words of at most 80k+3 bits per phase. Likewise emit permutation targets as compact addresses of at most k bits. The one-time compaction uses the billed catalogue scans. For each prefix coefficient rewind only that staged table, read it in order and output the extended prefix products. No coefficient requires a directory search. Prefix length at least doubles, so fewer than \(2N\) complex products are written, even with all prefixes already occupied. Alternate two banks, erase/rewind to their marks and keep the data array on a separate bank. A complete catalogue search once per block/layer is already covered by the additive \(k^{850}\) bill. All probes inside the streaming loop now use head-local coefficient words.


## Ordinary full-stream transitions and exact output

At a phase layer the coefficient stream needs fewer than \(2N\) complex multiplies; application to the data needs another \(N\). A complex product uses four signed real integer products plus additions, shifts and sign handling. All operand and intermediate signed magnitudes fit \(2^{12}k\) bits: during streaming, phases have magnitude at most 2, data norms at most \(2^{13k}\), and fractional grids have at most \(80k\) bits. Centered real/imaginary words can be stored in \(2^{10}k\) bits per full record. The wider setup registers above remain outside the per-record loop. A signed real word multiply costs \(2^{28}(2^{12}k+1)^{3/2}\le2^{48}k^{3/2}\) transitions. Moving its two input words to adjacent private ports, recovering the output and erasing local temporaries takes at most \(2^{12}\) full-width tours of the same words, i.e. \(\le2^{25}k\) transitions per product. Binary shifts and additions each take a fixed number of complete word tours with separate tapes; 256 more tours suffice per complex product. Positive multipliers in the final scaling are prepared once.

The original tape contains the most-significant-first words separated by one symbol. Scan it once and copy each word to its own marked bank; count the first word with a little-endian binary counter returned to its low bit after each increment. The interface promises both lengths equal n. The carry tours over \(n\) increments total at most \(4n+2\lceil\log(n+1)\rceil\). Reverse each input once to obtain its low bits first. Scan all \(N\) ordinary addresses, comparing its \(k\) bits/fields to a staged copy of the axis limits, reading at most \(b=\lceil\log n\rceil\) bits for any valid digit. Three binary counters for rank, padding and carries can be compared and rewound once per address in \(O(k)\) charged-bit sweeps (fewer than 128 per address before formatting). After inverse CRT, round each valid real coefficient and add the preceding carry, emit the low \(b\) bits, and carry the quotient. The coefficient is \(<h2^{2b}\), where \(h=\lceil n/b\rceil\); the carry is bounded by \(h2^b+2h\) by induction. Scan the final marked interval in reverse and emit exactly \(2n\) most-significant-first bits including leading zeros. Start the otherwise blank output tape at absolute zero, write exactly those bits forward, and halt without depositing a delimiter. There are \(S\ge2h\) valid addresses, so even inputs with the top digit fully occupied fit the output allocation. Rewinding the few full banks and the prefix tapes uses less than the number of tours already allowed. Together the three transforms' ordinary phase work, one pointwise product, final scaling, input preparation and exact readout take at most
\[
               k^{850}+2^{100} b_0 N k^{3/2}.
\]
For example the \(3\cdot2^{29}b_0\) phase layers cost at most \(16N\) real products or equivalents apiece, each below \(2^{49}k^{3/2}\) including input/output tours, with more than ten binary orders of slack. Fewer than \(2^{16}Nk+k^{250}\) ordinary cells are visited. The constant number of product, phase and prefix banks are always reused from marked origins. The permutation and Walsh calls have separate bills.

Let \(k=\lceil\log(4h)\rceil,\ N=2^k\). For \(b\ge4\), \(b-1<\log n\le\log b+k-2\le b/2+k-2\), so \(b<2k\). The norm of either padded digit array is below \(2^{5k/2}\). Each of at most \(2^{29}b_0<2^k\) layers has absolute Euclidean error below \(2^{-50k}\): unitary permutations are exact; the Walsh supplier rounds to the \(72k\)-bit grid; a phase has error at most \(2^{-70k}(2^{13k}+\sqrt N)\). Since an ideal layer is an isometry, boundary norms stay below \(2^{13k}\) and a transform on a represented input of norm \(\le2^{12k}\) has total error below \(2^{-49k}\). The pointwise product of the two rounded forward outputs differs from the exact product by at most \(2^{-49k}(2\cdot2^{5k/2}+2^{-49k})+\sqrt N 2^{-70k}<2^{-45k}\); its norm is below \(2^{6k}\). The adjoint adds at most \(2^{-49k}\). Multiply each entry by \(\sqrt S\le2^{k/2}\) computed by the same integer square root to absolute error \(2^{-70k}\), and round to the \(72k\)-bit grid. The vector error is below \(1/4\); nearest-integer recovery of every valid convolution coefficient is exact. This uses the normalized group-transform identity and the absence of wraparound since \(S\ge2h\).
