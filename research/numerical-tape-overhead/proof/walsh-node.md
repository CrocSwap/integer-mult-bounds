# A numerical cost for one complete selected Walsh layer

The input consists of $N=2^k$ complex records at a grid of $72k$ fractional bits, in lexicographic order, with norm at most $2^{13k}$. The selected-bit set is arbitrary. Let $X=\lceil\log_2(k+2)\rceil$, $b=\lceil\log_2(2k)\rceil$, $\Phi(x)=(x^2\log_2(x+2))^{1/3}$. The complete calculation applies at $k\ge2^{32}$. It uses the adaptive program at $X\ge2^{24}$ and an integral direct branch below that threshold.

The external numerical suppliers are as follows.

1. Binary interchange on volume $V$, slot width $u$, costs at most $VJ(u)$, where $J(u)=A_I\sqrt{1+u}\,2^{B_I\Phi(\log_2(u+2))}$, $A_I\ge1$, $B_I\ge0$. It uses linear visited space.
2. The [packed-XOR and position protocol](routing-outer-permutations.md) performs a transvection on two complete $u$-bit slots with an occupied restored third slot in $2^{34} bV(1+J(u))+k^{1105}$ steps. One arbitrary permutation of the root address positions costs $2^{42} bV(1+J(k))+k^{1110}$. The physical record width may lie between the number of local address bits and the number of local records. A larger global $k$ may upper-bound the address parameter and both additive bills.
3. The [word multiplier](word-toom.md) on two $w$-bit nonnegative words costs at most $2^{28}(w+1)^{3/2}$ steps and visits at most $256(w+1)$ cells. The adaptive rational weights use the [aligned scalar and key compiler](walsh-repair-compiler.md).

For a residue array of $V$ bits and positive even active width $a\le k$, put $x_a=\lceil\log_2(a+2)\rceil$ and

$$
   B_W=\max(48,B_I+9),\quad B_{\rm all}=B_W,\qquad
   A_W=2^{110}(1+A_I).
$$

After construction of the common modulus, the whole adaptive core costs at most

$$
     A_W bV\sqrt{\max(1,a)}\,2^{B_W\Phi(x_a)}.
$$

At every $k\ge2^{32}$, the complete selected layer, including two real components, gathering and restoring positions, modulus preparation and final rounding, costs at most

$$
                2^{18} A_W\, b\, Nk\sqrt k\,2^{B_{\rm all}\Phi(X)}.             \tag{1}
$$

The direct branch up to the chosen guard fits at coefficient 48, as shown in [the all-width proof](address-native-staircase.md). It visits $O(Nk)$ cells in total. The validity of the physical transfer depends on the numerical proofs of the external suppliers above.

## Representations and available volume

Split the selected positions into at most two disjoint groups of sizes at most $\lceil k/2\rceil$. Compute exact **integer** Walsh sums in the two groups, restoring the address order between them, then normalize once. If the original mantissas have magnitude width $w=85k+1$, the second group has width $w+s_1$, where $s_1$ is the first group's size and $s_1+s_2\le k$. Each group copies the original word once with low zero padding for the input shift of $\lfloor s_i/2\rfloor$ places and the optional unnormalized odd butterfly from the [modular Walsh proof](foundations/modular-walsh.md). Choose the modulus $M=1+2^{k+w_i+s_i+2}U$ for the arities on its actual chain, and pad all residues to $K=100k$ digits. The [explicit width proof](walsh-aligned-denominators.md#width-at-the-integer-caller) establishes sufficiency. Centered lifting and transfer between groups take word scans.

Each group's root has at least $2^{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}$ complete leading spectator rows. The [batch product bound](walsh-child-clock.md) applies down the entire path, including the current node. Thus even one complete finite-copy/label/seed or rank role contains at least $2^{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}/k^4\ge 2^{k/3}$ records. Concretely the leading binary rows can be indexed by the unused reserve followed by the current batch field. The old complete prefix, active field and suffix remain in their order to its right. Fixing the new field and collecting the same role through every prefix preserves this complete reserve. The slot children act on every setting of the other axes. Empty roles issue no data call. A per-key coefficient acts inside a word without issuing a spatial call. The external procedures see at most $k$ local address bits and $100k$ bits per physical record.

## Constructing the finite description

For nonbase $x=x_a\ge2^{24}$, use $y,m,W,L_F,q,D_F,A,f,h,W_a$ of the [child invoice](walsh-child-clock.md). Put $\mu=2^y=m+1$. In particular $m\ge2^{65536}-1$.

| Finite set | Upper bound | Derivation |
|---|---:|---|
| Labels and axes | $m^3,4m$ | $q^{L_F}\le m^3$, $A=2(m+D_F-1)$. |
| Free and charged rectangles together | $2m^6$ | Each disjoint nonempty free rectangle contains an ordered pair of labels. Charged rectangles have at most $2^{L_F}q^{L_F/2}\le q^{L_F}/m$ subset/common-word choices. |
| Fully unrolled batch roles | $(m+1)^4\le16m^4$ | Seed, label and $8^h$ finite-copy indices are included in $W_a$. Each check/repaired bank has at most this many roles. |
| Finite nodes and stage indices | $4m,4m$ | The finite tree has less than $2^{h+1}\le4m$ nodes including leaves. There are two predictor evaluations with at most $m$ Newton steps each and a fixed number of check/lift boundaries. |

Enumerate labels as their $L_F$ digits. Charged membership follows from a subset of positions and its common letters. For a free rectangle read an ordered pair and retain the unequal-position set, outside common letters, and first-differing bit, prefix and orientation on each unequal position. Discard repeated signatures by scanning earlier descriptions. Rectangle weights use integer products and finite differences as in the [denominator proof](walsh-aligned-denominators.md). Compute $\Gamma(v,w)=1-\Delta(v,w)\bmod 2$. For a numerical bound independent of a particular elimination implementation, enumerate the bits of a symmetric $D_F$-square binary matrix and one $D_F$-vector per label. Test nondegeneracy on every nonzero vector and check every prescribed pairwise product. The quotient construction, padded by orthogonal unit directions, supplies a successful candidate.

At each vertex obtain a basis and paired dual in its distinguished orthogonal complement. At each free rectangle obtain a basis of the span $H$ of the destination vectors, a complement, and the dual pair. At a source choose a complement to $H$ inside its distinguished orthogonal complement and use the dual construction at destinations. Enumerate binary coordinate matrices and test pairings, independence, and spans. A test of membership in the rectangle's whole generator span can enumerate subsets of at most $m^3$ generators. The source proves existence of these bases. Finite execution retains only their compact descriptions.

Basis changes and projections solve explicit binary linear systems. Signs are pairings of projected coordinates, including the changed coset representative with the partial frequency coordinate. A composition of such monomial maps is one linear coordinate map and one at-most-quadratic phase per column. Generate a linear map from its values on units, and a quadratic phase from values at zero, units and sums of two units. These rules compile the [actual common and free endpoint formulas](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/3344da59991ed94f9ba711217b29c1d760badfa6/preprints/The-logarithmic-exponent-of-finite-Fourier-transforms-November-24-2026/build/kernel.tex). The source identities already apply columnwise for all $f$. Both halves use normalized children. Store each binary map, phase form, and row-addition list. Gauss–Jordan elimination on an invertible $A$-square matrix requires at most $A^2$ row additions and $A$ exchanges. Replacing exchanges by three transvections uses at most $4A^2\le64m^2$ operations. The active address has a distinct complete third slot for each addition.

## Primitive events and finite setup

In each of the two predictor evaluations execute the Newton stages in order. At a charged rectangle collect the common-coordinate sum once per row and query, perform its two sets of one-axis transforms, then distribute with the row matrix and weight. At a free rectangle collect the shared source partial transforms once, retain them immutably through all queries, accumulate at each destination, then execute its shared destination partial transforms. Dense row arithmetic may scan source and destination for every ordered pair. It performs no extra Fourier transform. The two check conversions execute at disjoint times on one eighth of the rows each. The true terminal conversion executes the $A$ axis transforms. Check, lift, defect diagonal, direct predictor term, residual, and the final accumulation are finite row transfers. These actions are those of [division repair §5](foundations/pairwise-newton-division.md) and [the shared row schedule](foundations/nonstationary-rational-walsh.md#3-a-uniform-local-numerical-payload-schedule).

The following deliberately redundant enumeration bounds the number of scalar, record, monomial, and child-call events. An event is one matrix/phase on one fully specified finite role or one row transfer, with all numerical addresses on that role varying together. A purely finite description also specifies every true child event. A child event executes only at its prescribed point in the shared schedule.

| Finite choices or loop | Bound | Use |
|---|---:|---|
| Finite node and stage | $(4m)^2$ | Fixed input, either predictor or conversion, and its Newton index. |
| Rectangle and query | $(2m^6+1)(m+1)$ | The extra rectangle and query values cover pointwise and boundary terms. |
| Ordered row-role parameters | $(2W_a+1)^6$ | Source, destination, optional common/intermediate role, two check/lift or accumulator roles and a spare index. Each complete role includes its label, seed and finite copy. Ordinary selector/table entry indices are within these bounds. |
| Axis and basis indices | $(A+1)^4$ | Axis of a partial or full transform, two coordinate/basis indices, and an elimination or projection index. |
| Fixed transfers per tuple | $2^{10}$ | Gather, scatter, zero, copy and add on each of the input, transformed, common, accumulator and return banks, covering two directions, both predictors, and fixed diagonal/normalization phases. The elementary tape loops inside a scan are charged below. |

There is no loop over numerical addresses in this count. An axis event uses its incoming batch precisely once. A monomial event may additionally issue up to $4A^2\le64m^2$ packed-XORs. Thus the total number of above events is bounded by

$$
 E=2^{10}(4m)^2(2m^6+1)(m+1)(2W_a+1)^6(A+1)^4\le m^{48}.
$$

Indeed the product is at most $2^{60}m^{37}$ for $m\ge2$, and our $m$ exceeds $2^{65535}$. The quoted inventory applies even if arrays on every finite node are processed separately. The multiplicity of true recursive calls is still the sharper [spatial invoice](walsh-child-clock.md), since record visits and monomial operations never synthesize additional axis calls.

Encode a finite role, stage, branch, or counter in binary. Each event stores its fixed coordinates in the above tuple, weights of at most $8(m+1)^2(y+2)<m^4$ digits, a list of at most $4A^2$ elementary row operations, and at most four $A$-square binary matrices or phase masks. These fit below $m^6$ bits per event, including labels, endpoints, separators and index rulers. Gram entries, all basis witnesses by rectangle and label, complete incidence lists, and any duplicate of the event file fit together in $m^{55}$ bits. Coarse offsets in actual $k$-bit rows are formed when data run. Encoding such an offset is unnecessary inside the finite role tuple.

An explicit uniform setup bound is $2^{m^{128}}+k^{20}$ transitions. One can find all requested bases by binary enumeration. The total number of witness bits, including rectangular bases, vertex bases and Gram data, is less than $m^{55}$. A candidate checker loops through the above tuples, comparing the listed coordinate products, explicitly enumerating generator coefficients on at most $m^3$ bits when testing a span. There are at most sixteen nested such vector enumerations even if each source, destination, complement, dual relation and its two participating vectors are enumerated separately. Each matrix or pairwise comparison uses at most $m^{20}$ elementary bit/word operations on operands of at most $m^6$ digits. Tapes can scan the entire finite descriptor file on each such instruction. $2^{10}m^{61}$ steps pay this and schoolbook operations. A successful candidate yields the bases. Product tests at zero, units and pairs compile the linear/phase lists. Producing each finite role tuple and its coefficient has the same bounds, with Fourier gap coefficients delegated to the on-demand numerical compiler. A binary candidate counter, all unsuccessful probes and all final emission are covered because

$$
 2^{m^{55}+1} m^{48} 2^{16m^3} m^{20} 2^{10}m^{61}<2^{m^{128}} .
$$

The physical $k$-digit ruler and the few arity parameters are built separately in $k^{20}$. Enumeration uses polynomial finite scratch and reuses it for each candidate.

## Paying real tape scans

Order a bank as unused leading prefixes, then finite roles, then the old ordinary rows (with inactive address fields in their existing places). For fixed roles $i,j$, copy, accumulate, or compare by one complete pass over the source and destination banks. Advance each independently to the selected role inside the current prefix, process the equal-length ordinary row streams in order, then advance both to their next prefix. Store the ordered unary start/end masks for each required pair of slots when a matrix or phase event is prepared. The masks cost at most $2^{16}(A+1)^2(k+4)^3$ to generate by schoolbook integer bounds and permit one synchronized monotone address scan per pair and record. A binary row counter and a reusable ruler supply boundary marks in $O(k)$ steps per $K=100k$ data bits. The controller may scan and rewind the entire finite file on every record. Selectors on its bounded tuple and phases use at most $m^{60}K$ transitions per numerical record. The at most $4A^2$ mask terms require at most $2^{16}(A+1)^3(k+2)$ transitions across their address-mask reads. The coefficient from its m-bit key costs at most $2^{27}(m+1)^4K$ by the [integer repair compiler](walsh-repair-compiler.md), and four full $m^{55}$-bit descriptor scans, additions and marked-file rewinds also fit below the displayed bound. The original tuple uses one full-list search per event. The spatial children operate on the extracted dense role. Its flattened address uses at most $k$ bits. The fixed macro scans, extraction, restoration and ordinary file homing cost at most $m^{61}V$ per event, even when that role is a small subset of the parent.

At a finite stage keep at most 64 marked complete numerical banks. Four suffice for incoming, original, returning and accumulator data. Eight suffice for the two-array Newton predictor, saved inputs, intermediate products, and its output. Eight suffice for complete check/evaluation, residual and lift inputs and outputs. For a free rectangle retain the source subset, the shared transformed subset, the destination accumulator and its transformed form. A charged rectangle retains its common sum and current destination. Another eight banks cover gathers, monotone row transfer, maps and inverses. Finally eight cover zero, isolated role staging, child return and assembly. Free and charged rectangles are sequential. These counts also permit a transient source or destination clone before a parent releases its staging role. Finite conversion ancestors contract in actual volume by $1/8$ per generation, so at most $64(8/7)V<74V$ parked bits are exposed at any spatial handoff. The [marked-bank return](recount-handoff.md) can therefore pay all of their rewinds, returns and cleanup within $2^{12}V$ per child, plus finite descriptor and $k$-bit controls already allowed per event. Park all frames on a single tagged stack, pushing and popping at its current top. Older spatial ancestors remain on the stack without traversal. Each true active child has at most one half of its parent's numerical volume, since it extracts one seed/rectangle/finite row or belongs to at least one $1/8$ terminal contraction. The sum of live spatial bank volumes is therefore at most twice the root volume. An implementation uses one tape per bank and a constant number of tapes for numerical and route scratch. No tape is reserved per role, rectangle or recursion depth.

The guard $X\ge 2^{24}$ pays the complete finite catalogue and short-word constructors from the genuine spectator reserve. The arity satisfies $m^{56}\le k/32$. Previous-control and active-array extents are priced cumulatively in [the guard proof](address-native-staircase.md) and [the space calculation](routing-outer-space.md).

Collecting the bounds, every ordinary event costs at most $m^{61}V$, plus up to $64m^2$ copies of $2^{34}bV(1+J(a))+k^{1105}$ in a matrix event. Its child time is accounted separately. After the finite setup and descriptor addends, the entire nonrecursive bill is bounded by

$$
        (m+1)^{128}\, b(1+J(a))V .                                      \tag{2}
$$

Keep the stronger separated bill for the recurrence. The finite event count $E\le2^{60}m^{37}$ multiplies the record/compiler work by $m^{61}V$. Only monomial events initiate packed permutations and each has at most $64m^2$ of those. Thus, including the direct remainder below, the stronger total is

$$
  \left(2^{60}m^{98}+2^{22}m+1\right)V+
       2^{100}m^{39} bV\big(1+J(a)\big).                               \tag{3}
$$

The last $V$ in the first summand absorbs every numerical setup and additive. We charge the rectangular subsets under full parent $V$ only for ordinary work. The recursive calls retain their true spatial volumes.

## Only actual column maps use the interchanger

There is a sharper bound on (3) throughout the adaptive domain. The global guard above gives $m^{55}\le m^{56}\le k/32\le K$. Even scanning the entire finite role/weight file four times for every record costs only $4K$. A controller prepares the event's fixed slot indices and signs once. For the changing target/defect coefficient the separate word program reads the actual m-bit key, computes its integer table entry and applies it for at most $2^{27}(m+1)^4K$ transitions. Its loops access their own fixed short-word arena. No pass over the finite event file is attached to each bit step. Staged masks for one quadratic form take at most $8(A+1)^2(k+2)$ bits. Even rescanning all of them for each of $(A+1)^2$ terms costs below $2^{16}(A+1)^4K$. The synchronized counter access, phase sign, word homing, and ordinary modular additions fit the same allowance. The primitive finite-role transfers and the bounded bank handoffs above require at most 128 complete marked scans outside these per-record operations, including calls on an extracted subset. A deliberately loose bound for one ordinary event, including the finite role directory, is therefore $2^{45}m^8V$. Preparation of the $k$-dependent masks stays in the separately absorbed additive. A whole mask file is never rebuilt per numerical word. Over $E\le2^{60}m^{37}$ events this costs $2^{105}m^{45}V$.

For the volume-dependent packed-XOR bill, let $P_F=q^{L_F}\le m^3$ be the number of labels. At one live finite node of volume $V_r$, let $C_r$ be its number of base copies and let $W=m+1$ be the number of independent seeds. A specified copy, seed and label carries exactly $V_r/(C_r W P_F)$ bits, including all inherited, active, and suffix coordinates. Run each column map only on this extracted dense bank. Preparation and restoration of its surrounding layout are ordinary scans already priced. The predictor's actual query row coefficient is diagonal in the seed, namely $-(-1)^{\beta_j(t)}/(2h')$ at Newton step $h'$. The check, lift and frequency table can mix roles densely, but they do so at identical numerical addresses and initiate zero column maps. [Actual Newton recurrence](foundations/pairwise-newton-division.md#3-exact-newton-evaluation-without-a-clifford-root)

Fix an ordered pair of labels, a query and a seed. The pair belongs to at most one free rectangle and at most $2^{L_F}$ charged rectangles, one for each possible subset of common positions. At a charged rectangle the common kernel has $2D_F\le A$ one-axis transform boundaries. At a free rectangle its source partial transforms along $H$ and the transposed destination transforms along $H^\perp$ have a total of $2D_F\le A$ such boundaries. Between two boundaries adjacent column reindexings and signs compose to a single matrix and phase. Source and destination representative changes, common-coordinate changes, before/after phases, and the rectangle gather/distribute interface give at most $16(A+1)$ column maps on this pair, counting both endpoints. The actual schedule keeps the source endpoint before all queries and destination endpoint after them. Charging them once per pair overcounts those transformations without issuing extra child calls. Empty rectangles issue none. A common bank at a charged rectangle is covered by any one of its nonempty source and destination pairs. [Endpoint formulas](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/3344da59991ed94f9ba711217b29c1d760badfa6/preprints/The-logarithmic-exponent-of-finite-Fourier-transforms-November-24-2026/build/kernel.tex)

Summing the true volumes over copies and seeds cancels $C_r W$. Using at most $(1+2^{L_F})P_F^2$ incidences, $m$ queries, $1+2^{L_F}\le2m$ and $A+1\le5m$, the total volume receiving a column map in one pencil is at most

$$
  16(A+1)(1+2^{L_F})m P_F V_r\le160m^6V_r.
$$

Each map uses at most $64m^2$ packed-XORs of exact slot width $f$, with its other two participating slots occupied. At most $2d\le14\sqrt m$ predictor pencils run at that finite node. Across all finite interior nodes and leaves including multiplicity, $\sum V_r\le(4/3)V$. The conversion of $F$ to or from normalized Walsh uses a half-exchange and phases. Include up to $8(A+1)$ such boundary column maps per row at each finite node, including terminals. These facts give the entire volume-dependent movement bill

$$
                 2^{54}m^{17/2} bV\big(1+J(f)\big).                     \tag{4}
$$

For instance the predictor coefficient before source-XOR pricing is $(4/3)\cdot160\cdot64\cdot14<2^{18}$. The transvection price contributes $2^{34}$. The boundary term fits the remaining factor of four. The several $k^{1105}$ additive terms are still paid by the unweighted $64m^2 E$ count and the complete reserve above.

## Direct widths, recurrence and the numerical boundary

A direct transform on any chosen ordered list of address bits scans one bit at a time. For one bit stably separate the zero and one records on two banks. Their remaining addresses agree in order. Add and subtract paired residues modulo $M$, and stably return the two streams to their original positions with a counter on the complete ordinary address. The same operation handles an internal suffix or spectators on either side of the chosen bit. A linear-bit comparison, addition, subtraction and conditional reduction cost at most $256(K+2)$ per pair by a fixed sequence of forward and reverse scans, retaining separate operand and output tapes. On every second chosen bit, apply the rational half to both outputs by the [eleven-tape supplier](walsh-aligned-denominators.md). Its bound at $B=2$ is $10240(K+5)$ transitions per output. An unmatched final bit in an exact integer group uses the unnormalized butterfly. The independently implemented [direct tape program](https://github.com/CubeJerry/integer-mult-bounds/blob/a2a17b61d943676c757e0bbfaef3f4fae03ab8e5/research/moonshot-overhead-program/sessions/2026-11-26-S008/node-walsh-base.py) also supplies these primitive passes and a complete normalized four-point pass directly. One invocation on a single bit, including complete input, outputs, reverse scans, seeks, counters and halving, costs below $2^{19}V$ in the global guard. In particular the base costs at most $2^{19}aV$. For $2\le x<2^{24}$, one has $x/\log_2(x+2)\le 96^3$. Use $x\le2^{20}$ and $\log_2(x+2)\ge2$ in the first range. Above that point use $x<2^{24}$ and $\log_2(x+2)\ge20$. Thus $\sqrt a\le2^{x/2}\le2^{48\Phi(x)}$, and the entire direct branch fits $2^{19}V\sqrt a\,2^{48\Phi(x)}$. In a nonbase node the positive unblocked remainder $a-Af<2A$ is even and can be consumed in fewer than $8m$ passes, at most $2^{22}mV$. Include this in (2). The two normalized tensor factors commute, and the original coordinate order is preserved. The direct binary phase and half-exchange outside the adaptive block are single quadratic/matrix events already counted in (2).

For a global $k\ge2^{32}$ with $X<2^{24}$, use one integer group on all $s$ selected positions, with $U=1$ and $M=1+2^{k+w+s+2}$. This branch reads each signed mantissa directly into its residue bank and executes one unnormalized $H_2$ pass on each named bit. Its arbitrary inactive prefix and suffix are supported by the [direct supplier](node-walsh-review.md). The suffix-count descriptor is one followed by the required number of zeros. Read the named position, calculate this exponent on marked binary counters and append one zero for each remaining decrement. Even full counter scans for every zero and every already supplied index cost at most $2^{16}(k+4)^3$ per chosen bit. No Fourier rational coefficient is used. The exact group output is below $2^{w+s}<M/2$, so recover its signed value directly. The magnitude and modulus fit the same $100k$-digit word and both real components cost at most $2^{28}Nk^2$ before final rounding. Calculating all chosen suffixes costs at most $2^{20}k^4\le 2^k$ setup on this domain. The same final midpoint calculation below costs at most $k^6+2^{45}Nk^{3/2}$. All data movement uses a constant number of full banks. Over $33\le X<2^{24}$, $X/\log_2(X+2)\le2^{24}/24<96^3$ by monotonicity of $x/\log_2(x+2)$. Hence $\sqrt k\le2^{X/2}\le2^{48\Phi(X)}$, and these expenses fit (1) without a binary interchanger or spectator-batch hypothesis.

At a nonbase width write $\lambda=\log_2(x+2)\ge24$. Then $v_x\le(25/24)\lambda\le(65/64)^3\lambda$, since $24\cdot65^3>25\cdot64^3$. The cubic rounding costs at most one, and $\Phi(x)\ge64$, giving $y\le(33/32)\Phi(x)$. Using the separated bill (4), $f/a\le 1/(2m)$ gives the factor $m^8$ after normalization. The [three-slope proof](address-native-staircase.md) bounds the native contribution and pays the whole-layer exponent $499/8$ with the fixed guard.

For each of two real components and at most two groups, gather and restore the selected address positions, requiring at most eight root position permutations, and call the core at most four times on $100Nk$ bits. When the size is odd, one unnormalized direct bit is included after the even core. After exact centered lifting of the final integer $Z_y$, set $t=|Z_y|$. For odd total $s$, let $J_0=w+s+3<100k$ and precompute $A_0=\lfloor\sqrt{2^{2J_0-s}}\rfloor$ by at most $2J_0+2$ rounds of integer bisection with schoolbook squaring. Each round and all scan control cost less than $2^{15}(2J_0+4)^3$, so root preparation fits in $k^6$. Form $j=\lfloor tA_0/2^{J_0}+1/2\rfloor$, and increment precisely if $4t^2\ge2^s(2j+1)^2$ as in the [exact scalar caller](foundations/modular-walsh.md#correct-rounding-and-the-full-product-caller). The three full products per real record each cost at most $2^{41} k^{3/2}$ by padding operands to $256k$ bits. The remaining shifts, signs, comparisons and copies cost at most $2^{18}k$ per record. Even $s$ needs one shift with its midpoint test. Restoring the sign gives ties away from zero. The total complex Euclidean error from this one boundary is at most $\sqrt{2N}\,2^{-72k-1}<2^{-50k}$.

The root permutation adds $8\cdot2^{42}b(100Nk)(1+J(k))+8k^{1110}$. The core contributes at most $400A_W b Nk\sqrt k\,2^{B_W\Phi(X)}$. The direct odd bit, modulus preparations, lifting and the two real rounding passes have the bounds above. Since $A_W/(1+A_I)=2^{110}$, these sum to (1), including an empty group or empty selection. No finite timing determines any clock coefficient. The [physical join audit](walsh-joins.md) supplies the indexing of both check-conversion arms, the next spatial reserve, exact row returns, and the shift between the two integer-group moduli. The physical whole-program compilation, apart from the explicitly linked algebra and executable subroutines, is a written construction.
