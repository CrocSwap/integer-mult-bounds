# Sources, attribution and upstream overlap

## Published construction

OpenAI, *The logarithmic exponent of integer multiplication on tapes*, November 24, 2026. Theorem 1.1 gives one deterministic full-product multiplier in $n(1+\log n)^{1/2+o(1)}$ worst-case time with linear visited space. The upper bound is unconditional. The paper invokes variable-count Weak ETH for its converse. Section 2 constructs coordinate interchange with spectators and preserved registers. Sections 3–5 use short cyclic axes, reduction to Walsh layers, precision, and a full-product tape caller. Section 5 gives fixed candidates and section 6 schedules them uniformly. The fork prices explicit varying-arity choices inside those construction steps. The fixed-$\varepsilon$ corollary by itself does not supply any of the numerical constants here. [Paper](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/3344da59991ed94f9ba711217b29c1d760badfa6/preprints/The-logarithmic-exponent-of-integer-multiplication-on-tapes-November-24-2026/paper.pdf) · [Manuscript sources](https://github.com/openai/math/tree/3344da59991ed94f9ba711217b29c1d760badfa6/preprints/The-logarithmic-exponent-of-integer-multiplication-on-tapes-November-24-2026/build)

OpenAI, *The logarithmic exponent of finite Fourier transforms*, November 24, 2026, supplies the shared-pencil, sparse correction and repair identities. The fork constructs finite rational realizations and pays for their use on data tapes. Seven Lean modules are taken from the published source tree, including the original transition, execution trace and visited-cell definitions. Six are byte-identical. In the token port the two specific Mathlib imports replace its aggregate import, retaining the entire proof body. The replay reconstructs and checks the original Git blob hash in all seven cases. The upstream Lean code and this package are under Apache-2.0. ## Relation to #8102 and later upstream work

@zxv's #8102 specializes the published theorem to $O_\varepsilon(n(\log n)^{1/2+\varepsilon})$ for every fixed positive $\varepsilon$, or $\kappa<1/2$ using the repository convention $1-\kappa$. In particular $\kappa=0.4999$ gives exponent $0.5001$. Its source map and arithmetic certificate also exhibit a fixed candidate with $j=19998$ and a prime for that template. The $0.501$ example here gives a unit coefficient and an input threshold for another specified implementation. The branches share published mathematics and have separate source trees.
