# Exact coefficient valuations for every sparse tensor support

This contribution was produced by Nick Petalas. Apache-2.0.

Choosing nonconsecutive powers cannot reduce the quadratic maximum coefficient
bit height or cubic explicit coefficient-list storage order of the fixed
Gaussian-rational minimum-term tensor relation. The new theorem computes
every coefficient's two split-prime valuations for every integer exponent
support. Consecutive supports uniquely minimize their total valuation mass,
up to translation.

Claim type: scoped written proof with exact finite corroboration. This answers
the arithmetic-order part of Open next question 2 in
[Nick Petalas's #5934](https://github.com/CrocSwap/integer-mult-bounds/pull/5934).
That result covers consecutive powers. The new proof covers arbitrary gaps,
arbitrarily high degree and Laurent supports at the same term count. Actual
complex-height minimization remains open. No new kappa or supplier admission
is claimed.

## Result

Let f>=1, n=f+1, alpha=(3+4i)/5 and D=diag(1,alpha). Choose any integers
m_0<...<m_n and put delta_s=m_(s+1)-m_s. The unique primitive
Gaussian-integer relation, up to a Gaussian unit,

```text
sum_(j=0)^n c_j (D^m_j)^tensor f = 0
```

has no zero coefficients. At pi=2+i and rho=2-i its exact valuations are

```text
A_j = v_pi(c_j)  = sum_(s=j)^(n-1) (n-1-s) delta_s
B_j = v_rho(c_j) = sum_(s=0)^(j-1) s delta_s

sum_j (A_j+B_j)
  = sum_(s=0)^(n-1) [n-1+2s(n-1-s)] delta_s
  >= 2 binom(n+1,3).
```

Every gap weight is positive. Equality holds exactly when all gaps equal
one. Each extra missing power raises the mass by its displayed weight.
Other Gaussian prime factors are not determined by this formula.

Every nonzero integral relation on the support, including nonprimitive
multiples, consequently satisfies

```text
|c_j|^2 >= 5^(A_j+B_j)
max_j |c_j| >= 5^(binom(n,2)/2)
sum_j log_2 |c_j| >= log_2(5) binom(n+1,3).
```

The last inequality gives an Omega(f^3) lower bound even when a list stores
only the f+2 nonzero coefficients. The consecutive upper bounds from #5934
make both orders sharp. A factored or streaming representation has a
different storage contract.

For f=2 and support (0,1,3,4), one primitive relation has coefficients

```text
(24+7i), (-24+18i), (-24-18i), (24-7i).
```

Their valuation pairs are (4,0), (2,0), (0,2), (0,4). The mass is 12;
the minimum at this width is 8. This sample is generated by the verifier.
[PROOF.md](PROOF.md) gives the universal Vandermonde and normalization argument.

## Exact finite evidence

The new standalone verifier imports no predecessor executable. It constructs
the signed Vandermonde cofactors and separately solves the literal rational
kernel with its last coefficient fixed to one. Gaussian-integer gcd
normalization makes the resulting vectors directly comparable.

Its census has 246 supports, including 240 nonconsecutive ones:

- All supports containing zero with n=2,...,5 and remaining exponents chosen
  from {1,...,8}.
- Four specified gap families for each n=6,...,9.
- Translations by -7 and 11 of the first ten small supports, including
  Laurent examples.

It checks 1,190 coefficient occurrences and 944 exact root residuals, plus
1,776 literal diagonal entries of the lifted operators for the small and
translated supports. These counts refer to the main census; the printed
sample and rejection controls run separately. It checks the weighted mass,
its sharp equality condition and the coefficient norm lower bounds.

Thirteen controls reject invalid supports, an altered coefficient, a missing
coefficient, nonprimitive scaling, a zero coefficient and conjugated
coefficients. All scientific checks use explicit exceptions and remain
enabled under optimized Python. The all-width and all-support quantifiers
rest on the written proof. Finite enumeration is corroboration.

## Scope and implementation limits

The generator alpha, exact equality, Q(i) coefficient field and term count
f+2 are fixed. More terms, approximate relations and other generators need
new analysis. The result optimizes split-prime valuation mass; it does not
claim that consecutive supports minimize every coefficient's actual modulus.
It supplies neither a productive consumer nor coefficient generation,
precision, field-pass, restoration, tape or native costs. The five selected
framework assumptions remain. Independent human and formal proof-kernel
review are unclaimed. Selected cold replay, predecessor scientific suites,
Lean and Docker are unrun and outside this claim.

The verifier has no recursion, subprocesses or generated code. Its finite
domain has at most ten coefficients, a nine-row rational system, exponents
of absolute value at most 128 and support span at most 64. General power
evaluation has a 1,024-step limit. Gaussian Euclidean division halves each
positive remainder norm, and both gcd and prime division have explicit
bit-length iteration bounds. Functions are below sixty physical lines.
Python's managed arithmetic allocates dynamically, a Power-of-Ten rule 3
deviation. These fixed domains bound live data; there is no persistent cache
or mutable shared state. Warning-strict normal and optimized execution and
in-memory syntax compilation provide the available diagnostics.

## Open next questions

- Do consecutive supports minimize the actual maximum primitive coefficient
  modulus? Start at f=2 with the extra prime factors of the four cofactors.
  A bound on those factors is missing; the sharp valuation objective alone
  does not settle that comparison.
- Can one productive consumer use a factored or streamed relation? Specify
  its endpoint and charge arithmetic, passes, precision and restoration.
  A complete physical consumer is missing, and an identity producing zero
  is not itself that consumer.
