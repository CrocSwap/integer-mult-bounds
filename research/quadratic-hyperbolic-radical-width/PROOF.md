# Exact phase-preserving width for H plus one radical

Let V have ordered binary basis a,b,z. Its alternating Gram form has
B(a,b)=1 and B(a,z)=B(b,z)=0. Write the prescribed quadratic refinement as

    q(xa+yb+tz) = xy + alpha*x + beta*y + gamma*t  in F2.

The three parameters alpha,beta,gamma range independently over F2. A faithful
phase-preserving realization is an injective linear map f:V -> F2^m with

    f(v) dot f(w) = B(v,w),
    wt(f(v)) = 2*q(v)  modulo 4.

Every image therefore has even weight. On the even-weight space E_m define
Q(u)=wt(u)/2 modulo 2. The elementary weight identity gives

    Q(u+v) = Q(u)+Q(v)+u dot v.

Consequently checking the basis Gram matrix and the three basis phases checks
the prescribed refinement on every vector. The exact minimum m is

| gamma | alpha,beta | Minimum m |
|---|---|---:|
| 0 | 1,1 | 4 |
| 0 | 0,0 or 1,0 or 0,1 | 6 |
| 1 | any | 5 |

This is a finite-dimensional exact theorem, supported below by explicit lower
bounds and attaining maps. It answers only the first working example of Open
next question 1 in Nick Petalas's PR6456. That predecessor's general Gram-only
minimum is cited context; this proof does not require replaying it.

## Lower bounds

For m>0, E_m has dimension m-1. An injective three-dimensional image therefore
needs m>=4. At m=0 there is no injective image either.

At m=4 an injective image must equal E_4. The radical of the dot form on E_4
is the line generated by 1111: the perpendicular complement of the even-weight
hyperplane is the all-ones line, which itself has even weight. Injectivity and
Gram preservation send the nonzero radical vector z to 1111. Its Q is zero,
so gamma=1 cannot occur at m=4. This proves m>=5 when gamma=1.

Suppose gamma=0 and (alpha,beta)!=(1,1). At least one of a,b, call it v, has
q(v)=0. The vectors v and z are distinct nonzero vectors and B(v,z)=0. Their
images must therefore be distinct nonzero even vectors of Q=0 that are
orthogonal. In five coordinates, every such vector has weight four. Two
distinct weight-four vectors in F2^5 have intersection of size three, so their
dot product is one. This is impossible. Padding by zero coordinates preserves
all conditions, so the impossibility at width five excludes every smaller
width too. Thus these three phase choices need m>=6.

These are direct exclusions of widths below the displayed minima.

## Attaining maps

Integers below encode binary coordinate vectors with the low bit first.
The following rows are the images of a,b,z, in that order.

| alpha,beta,gamma | m | Images |
|---|---:|---|
| 0,0,0 | 6 | 15,23,57 |
| 1,0,0 | 6 | 24,23,57 |
| 0,1,0 | 6 | 15,24,57 |
| 1,1,0 | 4 | 3,5,15 |
| 0,0,1 | 5 | 15,23,3 |
| 1,0,1 | 5 | 12,23,3 |
| 0,1,1 | 5 | 15,20,3 |
| 1,1,1 | 5 | 12,20,3 |

For the first row, the weights are 4,4,4, pairwise intersections have sizes
3,2,2, and the three vectors are independent. Replacing the first or second
image by their XOR, 24, gives the next two rows. Since q(a+b)=1 in this seed,
these replacements give the indicated phases while preserving the Gram form
and injectivity.

At width four the seed 3,5,15 has weights 2,2,4 and intersection parities
1,0,0. The three vectors are independent, giving the fourth row.

At width five the seed 15,23,3 has weights 4,4,2 and intersection parities
1,0,0. Its third vector is independent of its first two. Adding that third
vector to the first or second vector preserves every Gram entry and toggles
exactly that vector's Q, because its pairing with the third vector is zero.
This gives all four gamma=1 rows. The polarization identity extends every
basis check to all of V. The lower bounds prove each minimum is exact.

## Finite corroboration and interpretation

The standalone checker uses two enumerations. The first visits every ordered
triple of even vectors at each width 0 through 6, including zero and dependent
triples before rejecting them. There are 37,450 candidate triples. The second
visits every multiset of m column vectors in F2^3 for the same widths, 3,003
multisets in total. For a multiset with counts c_j, it adds m!/product(c_j!)
labelled matrices. Column span checks injectivity; integer column incidences
check parity, Gram entries and phases. Both methods must give identical
embedding counts at every width for every phase choice. Neither census uses
the predicted minimum to prune its search.

The checker also verifies every vector phase and all ordered vector pairings
for each displayed witness, and rejects phase loss, collapsed radical, odd
weight, wrong Gram data, invalid dimensions and malformed input controls.
Finite corroboration does not constitute formal proof-kernel acceptance.

Compared with the cited Gram-only width four, the extra requirement costs zero,
one or two coordinates. Thus a phase-preserving native label reduction cannot
infer width four from Gram data alone, even on this smallest degenerate example.
The coordinate cost is exact. Native physical realization, source/read
incidence, dirty restoration, nested frames, precision, owner allocation and
the complete recursive bill remain separate obligations. No kappa improvement
or complete multiplication theorem follows from this example.
