# Conditional κ = 0.003530104 (+0.302% over #6911): flip mirror pairs around forward gates

**Result.** The exact-kernel crossing of the complete signed invoice is **0.0035301044238995680**, which is **+0.3017% over #6911** (0.0035194870841667551; ratio − 1 = 0.0030167).
The claim for the outer statement is **κ = 0.003530104** with tested price **0.00353010407** (grid 10⁻¹¹; the whole ledger, 54 strict and 4 functional inequalities, is run at that price), both strictly below the raw crossing, which lies in (0.00353010442389, 0.00353010442390).

The roots and the caller are unchanged.


**Conditional statement.** The result holds under the five framework assumptions of #6833; under assumption A6 (owned ascent) as declared in #6883; under the BIT saving of the open #6726 (necessary, below); and under the new form declared next, together with every declaration of #6911, which this package keeps (the reducible type A root and its three family lines, the per-port root pair, the joint contact, HOST2, same-label second mains, LAMBDA, IDLE_ONE, the row 19 accounting: a shared helper is one field, one job, one credit).

## What is new: valves, deletions and flipped mirror pairs
* **Counted against the #6911 word** (`cover_check.py`, definition: helper registers by initial and final rank, all other registers excluded): helpers of class (0, 9) 691 against 687 (+4: the four valve registers), class (5, 9) 91 against 99 (−8: the eight flipped helpers), class (1, 9) 15 against 16 (−1: one register dropped after the deletions); 962 helpers against 967. Core events: ADD 6639 against 6636, SCALE 739 (unchanged), MOVE 6379 against 6421; events before the core 25,686 (26,427), after it 18,378 (19,586). The individual valves, flips and deletions cannot be read off the word by identifier after renumbering, so their effect is what is counted and replayed.
* **The flip.** The word h += a·u*; h += c1·u; u += c2·h; u* += b·h has an inverse whose (h, u*) block is [[s + ab, −a], [−b, 1]] with s = 1 + c1·c2; with ab = s·c1·c2 the loop gain is s². The flips use SCALE gates of weight 1/2, so **a wrong scale must fail, and it does**: on 12 sampled core SCALE events of helpers a doubled scale, a missing scale and a scale replaced by ±1/2 are all rejected by the exact replay, and on 12 sampled ADD events that touch the helpers of the changed classes a wrong weight, a missing event and an event taken twice are all rejected (`cover_check.py`).
* **R alone is not F⁻ᵀ.** Nothing here relies on a relation of the R block: correctness of the word rests on the exact replay of every event (`check.py`), on the second replay as 512-component streams (`physical.py`), and on the controls above. A reader should not infer the inverse-transpose property from the flip identity.
* **Solved around the gates.** Each of the eight flips alone saves about 5.55 R0 (the sum is additive), and the valve helpers are re-chosen on the flipped state, because the valve choice of the unflipped word leaks on it. Neither fact is used by the checks; they are the history of the word.

## What is new: a per-port root
* **The root is a pair.** Type A (96 ports) is the root of #6902, as in #6911: two #6833 chain roots, n = 40, t = 0. Type B (8 ports) is the hosted root of #6889: n = 38, t = −1/2, pairs (−1, 1/2). Both have 18 stages with the same mask-rank profile, one stage variable per stage and M(D) = −diag(∏D^ε). A port instantiates only the roles of its own type; over the **78 roles** (type A 0-39, type B 40-77) the caller is **block diagonal**, with one stage value D_s per stage common to both types and **18 joint contacts**, each one native event serving both types (HOST2 at the type A ports, HOST at the type B ports). The cross-type blocks vanish (`check.py` replays the whole 78×78 matrix at the four central characters, 12 evaluations, 0 bad of 6,084 entries each; each type alone also replays).
* **Roots checked separately.** Type A by `root_check.py` (21/21 units of each chain regenerated from the closed form, cross Gram 0, SPEC-level identities, each stage variable once and affine); type B by `root_check_hosted.py`, the checker of the #6889 hosted root, every line passing. A wrong X entry in either root fails its checker; two swapped unit rows in the type A root fail.
* **Port types tied to the word.** The caller's type A ports equal the word's list of two-chain ports (96) and its type B ports the other 8; `check.py` asserts it and shows that exchanging the types of one port of each kind breaks the match. `cover_check.py` rejects, on a random-evaluation replay, a **wrong port type** (one two-chain port declared hosted) and a **swapped assignment** (a two-chain port and a hosted port exchanged), and also rejects a wrong weight, a missing event and an event taken twice on 16 sampled events that touch second mains or hosted-port mains.
* **The word.** Endpoint at the two-chain ports X = x, Y = x + y, X2 = x2 − y2, Y2 = y2; at the 8 hosted ports X = x − y, Y = (x + y)/2; helpers identity. **57,930 events, 1362 public registers (400 mains: 96·4 + 8·2, and 962 helpers)**, 6379 MOVEs; the opening gauge SCALE 1/2 acts on exactly the 8 hosted-port mains X. **Cross-type traffic, counted by definition** (an ADD or COPY whose destination and source are both mains and whose ports have different types): **161 events**, of which **70 involve a second main** (X2 or Y2 of a two-chain port with X or Y of a hosted port; 25 of those at a frame other than 127). They are legal (common chart, frame law clean), the endpoint rows of all 400 mains hold no input of a port of the other type, and transiently a main holds a foreign-type input after 3,158 events, which cancels. **352 helpers** have ADD events directly against mains of both types. Separately, of the 363 events that couple a second main with a first-plane main of any port, 351 lie in the two gate blocks and 12 inside the core (all with hosted-port mains, ports 17, 34, 42, 47, 55; the other definition, a subset in kind).
* **Invoice.** The root part is per port (type A for 96 ports, type B for 8), the native part is one call per stage: `check.py` recounts it from the emitted joint events and again from the two roots plus the word, and the two agree term for term. S = 1520. The #6911 word through the two roots reproduces its crossing 0.0035194870841667551 (the only comparison), and #6833 is rebuilt by the same code.

* Raw bound, all three definitions below 10⁹: literal **962,308,192**; actual per-call work **966,934,732**; reviewed scalar upper **976,942,552** (margins 37.7M, 33.1M, 23.1M). No term of the outer ledger reads it (`check.py` verifies that the ledger source has none).
* **Logarithm power: minimum 2166, written 2167.** The capacity line is taken on the literal carrier of **153** axes (9 base rows and 8 per stage × 18; the 8 complement rows of q lie in no target, so the carrier exceeds the largest target rank 145), since the fit m·c ≤ N of FP:111-114 is on the carrier. 2160 and 2165 fail, 2166 passes; the written value 2167 sits one above the minimum, and the tested price is strictly below the crossing. It is a free parameter of the compiler theorem (`research/higher-moment-strictification/vendor/LOG-MAJORANT.md:110-122`; stated with an integer q and 1/q in the bound, its literal conditions fail for the real exponent used, and the code uses the same argument with a real p, which a reviewer of #6884 re-walked and I did not). Rational interval engines at the tested price: α = 0.99999999952, γ = 0.9490, δ = 0.8097 (< 31/32); composite bound holds; 54 strict and 4 functional inequalities positive (their smallest value is the chosen parameter r0 = gap/10).
* Also declared, inherited: **LAMBDA 206 events** (124 type A, 82 type B), **IDLE_ONE 100 events** (32 type A, 68 type B) and the opening **gauge SCALE 1/2** on the 8 hosted-port mains.
* **LAMBDA is a review question.** Type A callers have 124 LAMBDA events and type B callers 82. If each LAMBDA had to be paid as a rank-1 job per port, the cost is 96·124 + 8·82 = 12,560 and S = 1520 − 12,560 = **−11,040 < 0**: no crossing exists. The κ rests on pricing LAMBDA as ordinary work, as in every caller of this line from #6726 on. **Reading, by an independent review, stated as the reading it is (not proved here, not on main):** Q = C^{⊗f} on (q,…,q) and C² = X (`upstream/build/sections/05-layers.tex:65`: "C^2=X, C^4=I"), so Q² = T, a pure translation, at every f; the reviews of #5599 and #6030 already use it ("Q^2 is the native translation with phase +1 at every width; scalar signs stay outside tensor powers", `docs/research/pr-intake-evidence/pr5599-completion/PROOF-REVIEW.md:59-60`; `pr6030-audit/PROOF-REVIEW.md:80-83`). Then LAMBDA = aI + bT with a, b = (1±i)/2 has row support 2 at every f (it is not the f-th tensor power), and compiles as `INIT t; COPY t = x; T(t); x := a x + b t` (numerator first); `CLEAR t`, with T the native translation: the macro of the thin query of the open #6900 (not on main) with its paid Q⁻¹ replaced by the ordinary translation. The lift is "substitution of the fixed coordinate, phase and scalar generators. It does not tensor a sum of matrix operators" (`research/higher-moment-strictification/vendor/p7-TENSOR.md:42-44`), native monomial maps and quadratic phases "lift column by column" (`research/kappa-rebuild/architecture/FINITE-PRECOMPILATION.md:243-248`), and finite compositions of permitted words are permitted (FP:45-46). Under this reading LAMBDA costs ordinary work and S does not go negative; I have not verified the tape-level implementation, and the κ is unchanged by it.
