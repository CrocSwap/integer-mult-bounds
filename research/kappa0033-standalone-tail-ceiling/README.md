independently pays eight whole rank17 jobs has negative necessary moment margin

other decoder work, zero additional route cost and no target losses. The theorem
also covers any larger paid bill or smaller target allowance. It closes one
specified standalone cost grammar; the goal of achieving conditional κ>=.0033

## Mathematical claim

```
P = {1:6611, 2:1236, 3:1530, 4:381, 5:120, 8:8},
H = {4:84, 7:21, 8:117, 9:954},
T = H + 240 H25.
```

All 240 main targets are unearned. At C=.0033 the optimistic raw budget is below
43, while an elementary binomial inequality makes eight H17 tails cost more than
128 at that anchor. Thus the remaining margin is below −85. At C*=.00330007,
rho=1e−9, the optimistic reserve-adjusted budget is below
`42.525751420427386531940225`; this positive counterfactual budget is not κ admission.

[PROOF.md](PROOF.md) then proves a separate analytic interval extension. Put
t=1−C and A(t)=T(t)−P(t)−8·17^t. Pair eight of the granted H25 terms with the eight
H17 costs and normalize by 6^t. The derivative of A(t)/6^t is strictly positive
on 0<t<1, so the negative anchor persists for every .0033<=C<1. This conclusion
comes from the analytic proof, not interpolation or a parameter sweep. The
anchor inequality “eight tails cost more than128” is not claimed near C=1.

The tail tariff is a declared protocol premise, not a universal rank/query lower
bound. Fusion/reuse, another observation or encoding, a genuinely lower base bill,
or extra independently earned targets changes the premise. Eight extra H1 charges
merely shift the displayed budgets by −8 exactly; the larger-paid theorem already
covers them without another power evaluation or a claim about a new physical word.
