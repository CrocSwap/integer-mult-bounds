The matching intuition behind the allocation is useful, but whole helper roles are unsplittable. We do not apply a unit-capacity cloning theorem to arbitrary unequal-width helpers. The three actual transferred roles all have width 9, and their particular source windows, family exclusions and capacities are checked directly.

The norm theorem and the reader adapter have different hypotheses. Family-local fibre unitaries preserve the mixed norm; the nonunitary reader involution does not acquire that property merely because it squares to the identity. The adapter therefore remains a concrete checked endpoint application, with no free insertion into the multiplication circuit.

The measurement and pilot tools solve exact erasure problems with stated sufficient hypotheses. They provide reproducible objects and independent checkers. They do not claim noisy-channel performance, hardware speedups, new MUB existence results or broad mathematical novelty.
