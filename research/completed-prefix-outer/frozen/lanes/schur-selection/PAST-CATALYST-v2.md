# A fixed past-space catalyst and the retained owner charts

A smaller cover cannot confine the actual centre lines to a proper
fixed past-coordinate space if it retains the selected stage routes.
The routes alone force the centre-line span to have dimension 73.
This result does not require the full orthogonal cover.

A direct compiler can change those routes, but the literal independent
entry and exit views are too expensive. With the first nine coordinates
designated past, they have projected active rank at least 1,615,856,
including the old idle remainders. The productive active rank is 917,504.
The borrowed helpers supply most of this cost. The paired construction
also fails this particular invoice even if all its new jobs are free.

These conclusions apply to a fixed common past space and to an unfused
view compiler. They are not a lower bound for a rewritten controller,
different native routing, or gauges that persist and combine across
owner jobs. No supplier or kappa improvement is claimed.

## The centre phase condition

Work first in the old 73 binary address coordinates. Tensor depth and
the separate real lift do not change the subspace statements. Let

    P = span(e_0,...,e_8),
    A = P perpendicular = span(e_9,...,e_72).

Both spaces are nondegenerate for the binary dot product. Let W_P be
the ordinary normalized Walsh transform on P. The original complex
line operator C_q is a nontrivial linear combination of I and the
translation X_q. It has C_q^2=X_q. Walsh conjugation on P makes C_q a
diagonal phase operator if and only if q belongs to P. If q has a
nonzero A component, its translation still moves that component after
the conjugation. The odd line operator then remains nondiagonal.

This statement retains the actual phase of C_q. Its two eigenvalues
are inherited fourth roots of unity. It does not replace C_q by an
involution. The original paired word and native phase review supply
the exact line convention:
[paired phase proof](../residual-syndrome/physical-algebra/ORIGINAL-PAIRED-GAP.md)
and [native review](../coherent-port/ORIGINAL-PAIRED-GAP-NATIVE-REVIEW.md).

At cover label g, the relevant direction is gq_p. The actual 112 port
labels q_p span P. Therefore, for a common cover label g,

    all gq_p belong to P  if and only if  gP=P.

For an orthogonal g this also implies gA=A. A common Fourier basis on
the past coordinates thus confines the allowable labels to the block
stabilizer O(P) times O(A).

The two centre maps use every actual port. Each column of R is nonzero,
and K=R^T/4. Since the source port contents are arbitrary, a missing odd
line cannot be removed by cancellation with another port's source.
Using only a smaller set of phase labels would require a new encoding
or a new interface; it is not a property of the existing R and K.

## A closure obstruction for every retained-route cover

For each port p, write its selected orthonormal basis of P as

    (q_p,b_p1,...,b_p8).

At stage s=1,...,8, the route rho_(s,p) exchanges b_pi with the i-th
axis of the fresh block E_s. It fixes q_p and all other fresh blocks.
In particular,

    rho_(s,p) P = span(q_p) direct-sum E_s.

Suppose a fixed past space W contains every centre direction gq_p,
where g ranges over a nonempty common cover domain D. Suppose also
that D is closed under right multiplication by the retained routes.
Choose any d in D. Since the q_p span P, W contains dP. Fix just one
port p_0. Closure under its stage-s route also gives

    W contains d rho_(s,p_0) P.

Adding these spaces for s=1,...,8 gives d applied to all 73 coordinates.
Thus W is the full address space. This proof applies to a common label
domain even when it is not presented as a subgroup.

The exact checker starts with the 112 labels and adds the images of
P under these eight routes for port zero. The span dimensions are

    [9,17,25,33,41,49,57,65,73].

The ordinary full cover is therefore not the only obstruction. Every
smaller common cover that retains these routes has the same problem.
Port-dependent or stage-dependent label domains are outside this proof.
They need a new owner matching rule and a new proof for arbitrary
independent inputs. A cover read f(d rho,z) cannot be replaced by the
payload map f(d,rho^-1 z) on the existing arbitrary cover fields.

## Partial Walsh conjugation of an address view

Let P also denote the coordinate projection onto the past space, and
write J=I-P. For the pure address permutation associated with an
orthogonal matrix g, conjugation by W_P has binary Fourier block

    B = J g P + P g J.

The two off-diagonal blocks have equal rank

    a = rank(J g P).

Their source and target blocks are disjoint. Hence rank(B)=2a. The
conjugated address map is monomial exactly when a=0, equivalently
gP=P. Affine translations add only a sign and a translation and do
not change this criterion.

One way to check the formula is to use the binary action on translation
and character labels. The partial Walsh map interchanges these labels
on P and leaves them unchanged on A. The orthogonal address map acts
by g on both. Multiplying these three block matrices gives B above.
For the rank equality, orthogonality identifies the two deficiencies
with dim(P)-dim(P intersect gP).

A normalized Clifford operator with Fourier block rank r has 2^r
nonzero kernel entries per row. Thus this is its exact Fourier rank.
For the selected route, the two subspaces are split orthogonally and
the formula has an explicit realization: the route exchanges a past
coordinates with a active coordinates, and the conjugated view needs
Walsh transforms on both sets. A one-past-bit/one-active-bit control
has four nonzero entries per row, not two. The checker verifies its
exact matrix with denominator two.

For every positive-stage data route, a=8 and the complete view rank is
16. At stage zero both ranks are zero. The checker verifies all 1,008
port-stage routes, including that each fixes its own q_p.

The original complete views can also contain Gaussian phases. The
counts below use their pure orthogonal chart parts. Such a quadratic
phase can add a P-to-P block to B; it cannot remove the two off-diagonal
rank contributions. The count is therefore a lower bound for the
complete views in this fixed full-past gauge. It is not a complete
finite scalar or phase invoice.

## The borrowed-helper contribution

Each selected helper chart sigma puts its occupied pending axes first,
then the required spectators, then the remaining axes. The port basis
preserves P. Every spectator in the actual table lies in P. If a of
the occupied axes lie outside P, then

    rank(J sigma P)=a,
    rank of one conjugated pure chart view = 2a.

gauge costs two such views. The literal old table has 69,120 complete

independent entry and each independent exit therefore still needs at
least a active rank. This generous model has the same lower bound 2a.
It does not cover fused views or a gauge that persists on active axes
between jobs.

## The paired candidate does not remove the dominant cost

The original-complex paired candidate promotes eight specified helper
roles in each of 144 calls. The 1,152 displaced old jobs have the
following active-axis profile:

    {1:218, 5:29, 6:2, 9:903}.

Their active mass is 8,502. Grant the candidate all of the following
without cost: both views of those displaced jobs, their direct
backfills, every new mode job, every new mode view, and the new decoder.
The old data views, the remaining ordinary helper views, and the old
idle remainders still have active mass at least

    1615856 - 2*8502 = 1598852.

Its candidate productive active mass is 64 times 15,488 = 991,232.
There remains an excess of 607,620. The actual accepted owner patch
permutes complete old jobs and retains their charts, so it does not
change this aggregate count. The count uses the exact mode-role list
in the pinned paired payload. It grants the new work free only to
make this negative comparison stronger.

## What a further attempt must change

A smaller common cover with the current routes cannot solve the fixed
past-space problem. A port-specific or stage-specific domain is a real
change to the construction and must preserve independent output width,
owner uniqueness, bridges, and arbitrary borrowed contents.

A direct compiler cannot discard the old helper views. To escape this
particular lower invoice, it must combine views across jobs or use a
persistent gauge that extends beyond the fixed past space. Such a
gauge needs a new preparation and restoration bill. It must also leave
the centre queries and injections accessible at their actual times.

For a proposed gauge history, the relevant local calculation is exact.
If a binary Clifford map has blocks A,B,C,D, and the input and output
Walsh gauges have projections P and Q, its new Fourier block is

    B_new = (I-Q)[A P+B(I-P)] + Q[C P+D(I-P)].

This formula permits a search over complete field histories, including
native events and all chart views. It is not by itself a compiler:
every map needs its phase, every inter-field ADD needs compatible
gauges or a paid conversion, and final arbitrary contents and physical
output frames must be restored. No such optimized history is supplied
by this note.

The outer-transform question is separate. The temporal-reuse lane has
developed completed-coordinate preparation and offset-alignment ideas.
Their availability does not remove the invariant-span or helper-view
obstructions proved here. Conversely, this note does not reject a new
native layout that changes the current route and gauge assumptions.
