# Exact kind A finishability in the frame game

This scoped written proof answers the finishing half of the second open question
in Jacob Sussman's [PR #7629](https://github.com/CrocSwap/integer-mult-bounds/pull/7629).
At every address width $m\ge 3$, a kind A part with nonzero rational
coefficient finishes by a final call alone exactly when its two terms have the
same frame and the corresponding binomial is invertible. This includes the
three-direction target with spare address bits. It rules out the distinct-frame
case for every final call that raises the plain term to level three.

This contribution was produced by Nick Petalas. New work is Apache-2.0.
Independent human and formal proof-kernel review are unclaimed.

## Model and statement

Let $G=\operatorname{Sp}(2m,2)$, let $L$ be the raw Lagrangian, and let
$P=\operatorname{Stab}_G(L)$. A term $g\in G$ is a formal basis element of
$\mathbb Q[G]$. Its frame is $g^{-1}L$, and its level is the distance of that
frame from $L$, namely $m-\dim(L\cap g^{-1}L)$. A free combination on the
left lies in $\mathbb Q[P]$. Paid calls are transvections with normal outside
$L$. Products act right to left.

Write $D,W,T$ for the first, shared and final calls. Let $M=WD$ have level
two and $TM$ have level three. For $\gamma\in P$ and $c\in\mathbb Q\setminus\{0\}$,
the kind A part is $y=M+cE$, where $E=W\gamma D$. Assume $E\notin P$, which
excludes an extra term that is a free image of the raw input. As in #7629,
free preparation and additions of earlier outputs are available, and the final
call acts on one prepared input. Thus the part finishes for this $T$ precisely
if $TM\in U+TU$, where
$$
U=\mathbb Q[P]\cdot 1+\mathbb Q[P]\cdot D+\mathbb Q[P]\cdot y.
$$

**Theorem.** For every such $T$, this membership holds if and only if $E$ and
$M$ have the same frame and $(-c)^n\ne 1$, where $n=\operatorname{ord}(\gamma)$.
In the same-frame case $EM^{-1}$ is conjugate to $\gamma$, so it has the same
order.

## Proof

The level is invariant under left and right multiplication by $P$. It is
subadditive because the group action is isometric for the frame distance
$d(F,F')=m-\dim(F\cap F')$. The individual calls have level one. Since $TWD$
has level three, subadditivity gives level two for $TW$.

Multiply all terms on the right by $D^{-1}=D$. The target becomes $TW$, and
the normalized generators of the free part are $P,PD$ and $pW(1+c\gamma)$
for $p\in P$. The paid part has the same generators with $T$ on the left.
Project onto the coefficients indexed by $TWP$. This coset is stable under
right multiplication by $\gamma$ and has level two throughout. All the
normalized free generators have support at levels zero or one, so they
project to zero.

Let $H=P\cap W^{-1}PW$. Index the coset by $h\in P$, with $e_h=[TWh]$.
The paid binomial columns that survive are
$$
e_h+c e_{h\gamma}\qquad(h\in H).
$$
Indeed $TpW=TWh$ is equivalent to $p=WhW^{-1}\in P$, and right
multiplication by $\gamma$ keeps both terms in the same coset. A singleton
$e_h$ can come from the paid copies of earlier inputs only if $Wh\in P$
or $WhD\in P$. The first is impossible because $Wh$ has level one.

The two frames coincide exactly when $EM^{-1}=W\gamma W^{-1}\in P$, or
equivalently $\gamma\in H$.

- If $\gamma\notin H$, neither $e_1$ nor $e_\gamma$ is an earlier-input
  singleton. This follows from $WD=M\notin P$ and $W\gamma D=E\notin P$.
  The column at $h=1$ is the only column touching either coordinate, because
  $H$ contains neither $\gamma$ nor $\gamma^{-1}$. Thus every reachable
  vector has coefficient at $e_\gamma$ equal to $c$ times the coefficient
  at $e_1$. The desired singleton $e_1$ violates that relation.
- If $\gamma\in H$, restrict further to the coordinates $e_{\gamma^j}$ for
  $0\le j<n$. All binomial columns with a start outside this cycle also end
  outside it. There are no earlier-input singletons in the cycle, because
  $W\gamma^jD\in PWD$ has level two. All $n$ cyclic binomial columns are
  available. Their span is the ideal generated by $1+cz$ in
  $\mathbb Q[z]/(z^n-1)$. It contains $1$ exactly when $(-c)^n\ne 1$. For that
  inequality, multiplication by $\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(-cz)^j$ gives the nonzero
  constant $1-(-c)^n$. At equality, evaluation at $z=-1/c$ kills the ideal
  and sends $1$ to $1$. When available, the inverse gives a combination of
  the actual paid columns from this cycle, so it attains $TW$ in the full
  space.

## Evidence and scope

The verifier constructs concrete symplectic generators on five and six address
bits and uses rational elimination in the target's right-multiplication orbit.
Each binomial lies within one such orbit, so the restricted calculation decides
membership there exactly. It checks distinct frames, both invertibility
outcomes for orders two and three, and a same-frame example where the free
element and shared call do not commute. One control makes the extra term a
free image of the raw input and finishes by subtracting its paid copy. Another
finishes only after adding a free translate of the last-call output. The
all-width result follows from the written proof.

## Open next questions

- Can one free operation on the final output finish a distinct-frame kind A
  part only when the two terms acquire the same frame after the call? First
  allow free translates of $TU$ and identify which additional columns meet
  the target coset. The verifier's post-call example shows that the present
  coefficient relation can fail with those columns.
- What changes when the two last-call outputs can be read together? First
  project both families of paid columns in a single three-direction setup
  with a spare bit. The fixed single-call coset does not isolate the second
  family.
