# Cyclic-line reduction with an arbitrary dirty fourth role

This is a written reduction using the **common-J lemma in Ali Romman's
PR468, README section 5.1** as an inherited premise. It does not require
PR468's full computer-assisted three-target theorem or its lower-triangular
hypothesis. The new step closes PR4119 section 7, question 2.

## Statement

Let A_1,...,A_k be nonzero rational rank-one 3-by-3 matrices, k <= 8, whose
rational relation space has dimension at most two. In M_4(F_2[H]), where H is
the additive subgroup generated by these matrices, consider

    P = product_j (I + ([A_j] - 1) N_j).

Each N_j is a binary rank-one idempotent. Suppose their nonzero off-diagonal
patterns lie on one cyclic line: each noncoordinate N_j has the same cyclic
off-diagonal pattern. If P is diagonal with entries [C_0],...,[C_3], then

    sum_i rank_Q(C_i) <= number of coordinate-projector calls <= k.

Consequently P cannot have three invertible 3-by-3 target frames, irrespective
of the fourth diagonal target. This covers the requested k=8, span=6 case.
There are no clean-input restrictions. The fourth input is arbitrary, and
C_3 need not be zero. Three invertible targets and one restored auxiliary have
the direct nine-coordinate-call realization; we do not assert that realization
has span six or uses a cyclic call.

## 1. The twelve projectors have a coordinate 3+1 block structure

Write N=xy^T, x,y in F_2^4 and y^T x=1. Let S be the intersection of their
supports. A directed cycle in off(N) implies |S| >= 2: each vertex on that
cycle occurs in both supports. Since |S| is odd, it is exactly three.
All x and y entries on S are one. At the remaining coordinate t, at most
one of x_t,y_t is one. After **only a coordinate permutation**, N is one of

    [ J  0 ]       [ J  1 ]       [ J  0 ]
    [ 0  0 ]       [ 0  0 ]       [ 1  0 ],

where J is the 3-by-3 all-ones matrix and 1 is a full column or row. There are
four choices of t and three choices of tail, giving twelve projectors.
No general GL_4 conjugation is used; the target remains diagonal.

A cyclic off-diagonal pattern determines N uniquely. Choose distinct a,b in
S; N_ab=N_ba=1 forces x_a=y_a=x_b=y_b=1. The entries N_ia=x_i and N_ai=y_i
then determine all remaining entries of x,y, including the diagonal of N.
Thus every noncoordinate call in this word uses this same N. The other calls
use one of the four E_ii. This includes any normalized free binary mixing
whose resulting projectors satisfy the stated pattern hypothesis; the theorem
does not assume the unnormalized gates themselves were coordinate mixing.

## 2. Project the literal product, keeping its diagonal targets

All five possible projectors have one common block-triangular orientation
relative to S and {t}. For the incoming-tail case use lower triangular blocks;
for the other cases use upper triangular blocks. Products preserve that
orientation over every ring. Taking the diagonal blocks is an algebra
homomorphism. No tail coefficient can feed back into a diagonal block.

On the three-coordinate block, N restricts to J, each E_ii with i in S
restricts to its coordinate idempotent, and E_tt restricts to zero. Deleting
the latter identity factors gives a literal product in {J,E_00,E_11,E_22}
with diagonal endpoint diag([C_i]: i in S). The retained frames form a
subset of the original list; its relation space injects into the original
relation space by filling deleted coordinates with zero. It therefore has
at most two independent rational relations. This argument does not require
that either relation avoid E_tt, nor that a subset remain saturated in a
chosen coordinate presentation.

On the one-coordinate block, N restricts to zero, as do E_ii for i in S.
Only E_tt acts. Hence

    C_t = sum_{j:N_j=E_tt} A_j,
    rank(C_t) <= number of E_tt calls.

The equality is in the actual torsion-free address group. It is not a mod-2
matrix-address equality.

## 3. The precise inherited common-J premise

PR468 section 5.1 states: for a product with projectors in
{J,E_00,E_11,E_22} and at most two independent rational frame relations,
if the output is diagonal (even allowing a single possible 01 entry), the
J frames make zero net contribution to its diagonal targets. Each diagonal
target is the sum of the frames on its coordinate-projector calls.

That section proves the premise through augmentation degree two: after the
first-order J relation, the two off-diagonal quadratic conditions have at
most one common linear divisor. The independent proper J-prefix sums reduce
all internal coordinate factors to one gap. The resulting product has form
U(b) D U(c), with U(z)=I+(z-1)J. Exact off-diagonal entries force D to be scalar
and bc=1, unless both runs are already identities. Thus the J contribution
cancels. The full argument, including the common-linear-divisor step, is
retained verbatim in the pinned predecessor README; it is not reclassified
as a new proof or replaced by our finite checks.

No restriction on the shape of C_i is used in section 5.1. In particular,
we do **not** invoke the lower-triangular target hypothesis of PR468's full
nine-call theorem. We also do not invoke its span-five scalar census.

Applying precisely this premise to the core product gives

    sum_{i in S} rank(C_i) <= number of coordinate calls on S.

Add the singleton bound from section 2. Every coordinate call is charged
once; every cyclic call remains paid but contributes no diagonal rank under
these hypotheses. This proves the statement. Three invertible data targets
would give rank sum at least nine, a contradiction for k<=8. The auxiliary
rank is merely nonnegative; no restoration assumption was introduced.

## Scope and remaining questions

The relation-nullity bound is essential to the inherited premise. This
reduction does not handle arbitrary many relations, multiple cyclic patterns,
the seventy-two flagless triples, span five, extra registers, clean ancillas,
nonabelian addresses or free address twists. It proves no native legality,
physical/compiler/precision tariff, supplier admission or new kappa.

The finite checker independently verifies all twelve block shapes and their
projection identities, both orientations and exact group-algebra controls.
It does not prove the common-J lemma by enumeration and does not audit its
entire written argument. The result is therefore explicitly dependent on that
retained written lemma, as mathematical reductions normally are.
