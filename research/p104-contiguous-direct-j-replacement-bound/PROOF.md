## Why a paid query pool has a rank floor

The central binary Gaussian C factor has Walsh eigenvalues 1 and i. All-plus body vectors select D_s=I on independent stage axes. For central multiplicity at least two, all-plus vectors give q=+1 and two minus factors give q=−1. These are legal specializations of the signed physical operators. Ordinary LAMBDA uses q² and agrees at both signs. Ordinary ADD, SCALE and SWAP also agree. On a fixed matched port and fibre, one q-sensitive scalar query acts through one input and one output direction. Its difference at opposite signs has rank at most one.

For an ordered sequence of operators, its opposite-sign difference telescopes into a sum with one changed factor in each summand. Multiplying a rank-one changed factor on either side cannot increase its rank. If the required 36-by-36 interval map has rank ρ at the difference E(+1)−E(−1), at least ρ signed scalar queries are necessary. Grant every old Type A QUERY inside the interval a full unit of this allowance, whether or not it is useful. With q_old such queries, the number of new paid ones per Type A port is at least max(0,ρ−q_old). The proof permits interleaved even wrappers and feedback. It does not permit a newly q-sensitive body or bypass, or an unpriced packed cross-port query.
