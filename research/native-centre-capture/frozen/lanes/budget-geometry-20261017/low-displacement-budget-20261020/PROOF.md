## Exact moment and terminal bounds

For a native histogram H, use

    M_H(b) = sum_r H[r] * r/(DW) * (D/r)^b.

The two pinned rational engines evaluate the complete three-call histogram. Their outward bounds are:

| Local response | Lower bound | Upper bound |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Rank thirty | 0.964177326889959935 | 0.964177326889959936 |
| Rank sixteen | 0.964968717039954015 | 0.964968717039954016 |

For each extra-child limit, both engines prove M<1 at the stated count and M>1 at the next count. The receipt includes these bounds for ranks 1, 2, 4, 8, 9, and 17. No floating-point result determines a threshold.

Both complete preterminal profiles have native mass 5,376. A terminal of mass 224 gives 5,600=DW. Every child rank is below D, so that full bill has M(b)>1 for every b>0. The full 224-H1 terminal must lose at least 25 H1 in the rank-thirty case or 29 H1 in the rank-sixteen case at the stated b.

The last column of the first table permits every legal integer child rank. The strict size guard gives maximum rank 17 because 2*17^2<25^2<2*18^2. For 0<b<1, r^(1-b) is increasing and concave. For a fixed sum of ranks, move mass from a smaller non-maximal rank to a larger one. This cannot increase the cost. Repeat until all but at most one nonzero rank are 17. Therefore the cheapest distribution of n ranks is floor(n/17) copies of rank 17 and one remainder.

For rank thirty, 11 H17 + H13 passes and 11 H17 + H14 fails. For rank sixteen, 11 H17 + H9 passes and 11 H17 + H10 fails. The cheapest cost increases with n. Thus no terminal and debt invoice of more than 200 or 196 total ranks can pass. These are favorable arithmetic bounds. They do not construct those grouped children.
