## 1. Contract and the meaning of a minimum

There are finitely many cuts and at least one cut. At cut j, the job multiplicity q_j is a positive integer, W_j is a nonempty finite set of positive integer widths, and c_(j,w) is a positive rational price paid for each whole invocation of width w. Invocations fill exactly their stated widths. Every cut must finish separately. Costs add without shared setup, pooling, discounts or uncharged lifetime effects. Compatibility of program endpoints and ownership/return contracts is an assumption, not an arithmetic conclusion.

For one cut, suppress j. At amount n>=0, define C(n) to be the minimum price of nonnegative integer counts k_w satisfying sum_w w*k_w=n, and +infinity when no such counts exist. At n=0 the empty fill has cost zero. Since every width is positive, any exact fill has k_w<=n/w. Thus only finitely many count vectors are possible at fixed n. A feasible minimum is attained; no infimum-attainment assumption is hidden here.

Put

    lambda = min_w c_w/w,
    B = {w in W : c_w=lambda*w},
    e_w = c_w-lambda*w,
    h = gcd(B),  g = gcd(W).

B is nonempty. All e_w are nonnegative, and e_w=0 exactly for w in B. Since B is a subset of W, g divides h. For any feasible amount n, its excess is the sum of its invocation losses e_w. In particular this excess is nonnegative. With +infinity for an impossible fill, write

    H(n) = C(n)-lambda*n.

Only a finite number is subtracted from C(n), so H(n) is well-defined even when C(n)=+infinity.

## 2. Residue minima exist and have the stated reachability

Take d=h, or take an anchor b in B and d=b. On residues modulo d, an invocation of width w moves r to r+w and has two-component label (e_w,w). Add labels componentwise and compare them lexicographically, first by loss and then by amount.

Any nonempty directed cycle has nonnegative total loss and strictly positive total amount. Deleting it strictly reduces a path's lexicographic label while preserving its final residue. Consequently every reachable residue has a minimum label attained by a simple path; there are only finitely many simple paths in this finite graph. At residue zero, the empty path is minimal and has label (0,0). A simple path has at most d-1 edges, so the minimum label's amount is at most (d-1)*max(W). This includes d=1, when the empty path is the only needed minimum.

Reachable residues form the additive monoid generated by W in the finite cyclic group Z/dZ. A submonoid of a finite group is a subgroup: a residue's inverse is a nonnegative multiple of it, obtained from its finite order. Therefore the reachable residues are the subgroup generated by W. As g divides d and gcd(W)=g, that subgroup consists exactly of the multiples of g modulo d. In particular this statement does not say that every nonnegative integer multiple of g is already an exact fill.

Denote the minimum label at residue r modulo h by (delta_r,A^(h)_r), and that at residue s modulo b by (delta^(b)_s,A_s). These labels are defined only for reachable residues; unreachable residues have no path and no finite label.

The usual certificate is sufficient without trusting the procedure that generated it: a claimed label at zero must be (0,0), each finite label must have an attaining nonnegative invocation vector, and every residue edge must satisfy

    L_(r+w) <=lex L_r+(e_w,w).

Induction along any path shows that the claimed terminal label is at most that path's label. Its attaining vector provides the reverse comparison with the true minimum. Reachability must separately match the subgroup above. This is a sufficiency argument for such certificates, not a claim that this review ran a certificate validator.

Two properties will be used below. First, every delta_r is nonnegative. Second,

    delta_r=0 if and only if r=0 modulo h.

Indeed, a zero-loss path uses only B widths, each divisible by h, so it ends at zero modulo h. Conversely the empty path witnesses delta_0=0.

## 3. The anchor criterion holds for every amount

Fix any anchor b in B. Necessarily h divides b. Fix a reachable residue s modulo b and let r be its reduction modulo h. We first prove

    delta^(b)_s = delta_r.                         (A)

Projecting any path ending at s modulo b to residues modulo h preserves its loss and ends at r. Hence delta^(b)_s>=delta_r.

For the other inequality, take an attaining minimum-loss path modulo h and let its actual total amount be t. Then t is congruent to s modulo h. In Z/bZ, the subgroup generated by B is exactly the multiples of h: gcd(B)=h and b is itself in B. Thus s-t modulo b is a sum of nonnegative B widths modulo b. Append those cheapest-width invocations to the chosen path. They add zero loss and bring its terminal residue to s modulo b. This constructs a path with loss delta_r and proves the reverse inequality in (A). The added actual amount need not be the smallest completion; that is why the amount tie-break A_s remains necessary.

By definition of the lexicographic label, A_s is exactly the least actual amount among all invocation vectors with residue s modulo b and excess delta_r. It follows, for every n>=0 with n congruent to s modulo b, that

    C(n)=lambda*n+delta_r  if and only if  n>=A_s.  (B)

For necessity, any equality fill has loss delta_r, so its amount cannot precede the minimum such amount A_s. The fixed-n feasible minimum is attained by Section 1. For sufficiency, n-A_s is a nonnegative multiple of b. Append that many anchor-width invocations divided by b to an attaining vector for A_s. The appended cost is lambda*(n-A_s), so the result has the claimed price. Every fill at n has loss at least delta_r by residue minimality, giving optimality as well as attainment.

Below A_s, the amount may be impossible or it may have a more expensive exact fill. In both cases H(n)>delta_r in the extended order. If a residue modulo b is unreachable, every amount in it is impossible. No cost equality is asserted there.

This proof includes n=0 and s=0: A_0=0 and the empty fill attains equality. Multiple cheapest widths cause no problem because their finite-group subgroup, rather than a single chosen coin, was used in proving (A); the particular anchor b is used only for the final exact amount increment.

## 4. The joint predicted sequence and its least eventual period

Restore cut subscripts and define, for positive integer replication a,

    E(a) = sum_j H_j(a*q_j),
    D = lcm_j [g_j/gcd(g_j,q_j)],
    P = lcm_j [h_j/gcd(h_j,q_j)].

For positive integers z and q, the condition z divides a*q is equivalent to z/gcd(z,q) dividing a. Applying this at each cut gives:

* If D does not divide a, at least one cut's amount fails its gcd condition, so that cut is impossible and E(a)=+infinity.
* If D divides a, every amount has a reachable residue modulo each cut's anchor and h_j. This allows a finite predicted residue loss, but the amount may still lie in an early gap. D divisibility is not claimed to imply present feasibility.

Since g_j divides h_j, every a satisfying all h_j divisibilities also satisfies all g_j divisibilities. In particular D divides P. Define the prediction on all positive replications by

    Ehat(a) = +infinity,                          if D does not divide a;
              sum_j delta_(j,a*q_j mod h_j),     otherwise.

For D|a, each finite delta is a lower bound for H_j(a*q_j). Equality holds exactly when every cut attains its prediction. If some cut is strictly higher, including +infinity, the total is strictly higher because the other defects are nonnegative. Cancellation is impossible. For D not dividing a, E and Ehat are both +infinity; one must not form their undefined difference +infinity-(+infinity).

Adding P preserves both the D-divisibility class and every a*q_j residue modulo h_j. Thus Ehat is P-periodic, with equality interpreted in the extended set of nonnegative rationals together with +infinity. Moreover its zeros are exactly

    Ehat(a)=0  if and only if  P divides a.        (C)

In one direction, P divisibility makes every residue zero and implies D divisibility. In the other, a zero is finite, all its summands are nonnegative, and Section 2 forces every h_j to divide a*q_j; the defining LCM then divides a.

Section 5 proves that E=Ehat except at finitely many positive replications. Hence P is an eventual period of E. To prove leastness, let T be any positive integer eventual period of E. Choose a sufficiently large multiple a of P so that a and a+T are in both the predicted tail and T's periodic tail. Then E(a)=0 and E(a+T)=0. By (C), P divides a+T and therefore divides T. Every eventual period is a multiple of P; in particular P is the least positive one. This reasoning still works when some tail values are +infinity, since it deliberately starts at a finite zero.

The assertion concerns E, not G(a)=F+E(a)/(S*a). Division by a does not preserve the same periodicity assertion.

## 5. A finite exact description of every positive exception

It is enough to examine a=D*t with t a positive integer. Choose any anchor b_j in B_j at each cut and set

    v_j = D*q_j,
    k_j = b_j/gcd(b_j,v_j).

The residue of v_j*t modulo b_j depends exactly on the class of t modulo k_j. Take representatives u=0,...,k_j-1 and write s_(j,u)=v_j*u modulo b_j. Because g_j divides both b_j and v_j, every such residue is reachable. The anchor labels A_(j,s) are therefore finite.

By (B), the j-th cut fails to attain its prediction at a=D*t exactly when

    t congruent to u modulo k_j,  and
    v_j*t < A_(j,s_(j,u)).

As all amounts are integers and v_j>0, the strict inequality is equivalent to

    t <= M_(j,u),
    M_(j,u) = floor((A_(j,s_(j,u))-1)/v_j).       (D)

This detects both early impossible amounts and early expensive amounts. It does not require a separate finite prefix optimization to distinguish those two kinds of exception.

With the nonnegative remainder convention, the largest integer in that congruence class not exceeding M_(j,u) is

    t_(j,u) = M_(j,u) - ((M_(j,u)-u) mod k_j).   (E)

If t_(j,u)>0, it is an actual positive exception for that cut; every larger positive integer in its class is not an exception. If t_(j,u)<=0, the class has no positive exception and must be discarded. Formula (E) is valid also for negative M; it uses mathematical floor and nonnegative remainder, not truncation toward zero.

For u=0, the sampled anchor residue is zero and A_(j,0)=0. Thus M_(j,0)=floor(-1/v_j)=-1 and there is no positive exception in that class. If k_j=1, this is the only class and that cut contributes no exception after the common D filter. For b_j=1, the residue graph likewise has only zero and all these formulas remain valid. If all cuts contribute no positive exception, the exceptional set is empty.

Let

    L = D*max of the positive t_(j,u),

and let L=0 when this maximum is empty. Every cut has finitely many classes, so this is a finite calculation over sum_j k_j classes. It enumerates the last exception without requiring a global-period scan. The no-cancellation statement in Section 4 proves that the union of these cut exceptions is exactly the set of global mismatches E(a)!=Ehat(a) on D multiples. Non-D multiples are never mismatches because both values are already +infinity.

Consequently E(a)=Ehat(a) for every a>L. If L>0 then L is itself an actual mismatch. This is the relevant form of masking: exceptions outside the globally allowed D residue class do not count, because another cut is permanently impossible there. One cannot take the maximum of the unfiltered last exceptions of the separate cuts.

## 6. Why the exact minimal positive onset is L+1

Define a period T to hold from positive onset a_0 when E(a+T)=E(a) for every integer a>=a_0. By Section 5, onset L+1 works with period P. If L=0, onset one is the smallest allowed positive onset, so the claim is immediate.

Suppose L>0. For the least period P specifically,

    E(L) != Ehat(L) = Ehat(L+P) = E(L+P).

The last equality holds because L+P>L. Thus P cannot hold from any positive a_0<=L.

The same obstruction excludes a purported earlier onset even if a larger period is allowed. Any period T holding from such an onset is an eventual period, so Section 4 gives P|T. Since L+T>L,

    E(L+T)=Ehat(L+T)=Ehat(L),

whereas E(L)!=Ehat(L). Therefore E(L+T)!=E(L), contradicting that periodicity holds at a=L. This is also valid when E(L)=+infinity: L is D-divisible and Ehat(L) is finite, and the shifted value has already reached that finite prediction. Hence **L+1 is the minimum positive onset over all positive integer periods**, not merely an onset for the selected period P.

## 7. Edge cases, exclusions and predecessor credit

The proof explicitly covers modulus one; all prices tied at the minimum density; singleton menus; non-coprime jobs and widths; multiple cheapest widths; rational rather than integral prices; the empty positive-exception set; the u=0 class; a temporarily impossible amount with reachable residue; permanently impossible residues; and a last exception whose excess is infinite. It does not subtract two infinities or infer exact-fill feasibility merely from gcd divisibility.
