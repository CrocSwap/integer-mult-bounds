## 4. Credited denominator argument and the global grid

We use the Gaussian-dyadic Clifford denominator method already credited to
Ross Richardson in `p104-retimed-internal-grid` and the earlier exact-grid
packages. A Clifford basis row is a stabilizer state. Its nonzero entries
occupy an affine binary support of size `2^k`, `k<=d`, and have equal magnitude
`2^(-k/2)`: diagonal Pauli constraints determine the support coset and
translation Pauli constraints determine equal magnitudes; unitarity normalizes
the row.

Every literal gate has Gaussian-dyadic entries, including its absolute phase.
Write a nonzero reduced entry `(a+ib)/2^m`, with at least one of a,b odd. Its
norm implies `a^2+b^2=2^(2m-k)`. If exactly one is odd, the right exponent is0;
if both are odd, the sum is2 modulo4, so the exponent is1. Therefore
`m=ceil(k/2)<=ceil(d/2)`. Products and the actual tensor lifts stay in this
gate class. No denominator is summed once per source Gaussian occurrence.

Combining this entry bound with (4) and the input grid proves every component
and every exterior source product belongs to

`2^(-p-ceil(d/2)-4) Z = 2^-F Z`.

During a direct native stage the product of the completed gates is still a
Clifford; during an in-place stage a written coordinate is a row from either
the old or the completed stage. Both satisfy the same bound. Signed monomial
phases and ordinary full-coordinate permutations preserve it. The argument
uses full array linearity, not a pure-tensor input assumption.

## 5. Deterministic arithmetic and quantizer identity

For real x define

\[
Q_F^{\uparrow}(x)=2^{-F}\lfloor2^Fx+1/2\rfloor,\qquad
Q_F^0(x)=2^{-F}\operatorname{sgn}(x)\lfloor2^F|x|\rfloor.
\]

Apply either separately to real and imaginary components. If `x=z 2^-F`
for an integer z, both return x exactly, including negative values. The result
does not rely on matching the two policies away from the grid.

For a literal source scalar `a/b`, multiply the stored signed integer by a,
then check divisibility by positive b before division. Equation(4) proves
that divisibility on every admitted source trajectory, including the product
temporary before ADD. All numerators have absolute value at most16 and all
denominators are powers of two at most16. The quantizer comparison is made
on the exact quotient, not on a discarded remainder.

For a C pair with sign s, let the stored complex integer inputs be `a+ib`
and `c+id`. Form the four numerators

`a-sb+c+sd`, `b+sa+d-sc`, `a+sb+c-sd`, `b-sa+d+sc`,

then divide each by2. The completed C-stage grid theorem proves all four are
even on the admitted trajectory. Partial sums of at most four signed integers
stay on the same grid. We explicitly exclude dividing `(1+si)(a+ib)` or
`(1-si)(c+id)` separately by2: those individual summands can be off-grid even
when their sum is on-grid. The checker has a rejecting control for that
incorrect evaluation order.

Induction over the literal operations now proves that inserting either
quantizer at every specified cut leaves the exact source execution unchanged.
This is an equality proof. It uses neither a Lipschitz claim about a rounded
map nor a relative error guarantee at arbitrarily small inputs. Final forced
downcasting to the original p-grid is not covered.

## 6. Signed magnitude and all live records

A unitary row on the retained d-coordinate factor has l1 norm at most `2^(d/2)`.
Each input complex component has magnitude below `sqrt(2) 2^h`. Equations(3–4)
therefore bound every stored or product component in magnitude by

`8500 sqrt(2) 2^(h+d/2)`.

Since `8500<2^14`, this is strictly less than `2^J`, where
`J=h+ceil((d+1)/2)+14`. The signed F-grid integer magnitude is then strictly
below `2^(F+J)`. A signed `F+J+1`-bit component suffices. Using
`ceil(d/2)+ceil((d+1)/2)=d+1` gives exactly W in(1).

The strict magnitude bound excludes the problematic most-negative two's-
complement value when a sign is reversed. A source scalar numerator can
multiply a stored code by at most16; a C numerator sums at most four signed
stored components. The separate product and final ADD have the bounds already
proved, and six additional signed temporary bits safely cover raw numerators,
partial sums and a carry/sign margin. Checked division by a power of two is
exact before the word is narrowed. A complex record contains **two W-bit
components**, plus whatever framing/control its actual host requires.

The 13,444 productive fields are independent resident data, not disposable
scratch. The 29-label local owned bank, its peak13 live values, the separate
thin-query field, arithmetic temporaries, routing buffers and retained erased
backing are distinct accounting categories. The source logical zero/reset
contract does not settle physical disposal or warm backing costs.
