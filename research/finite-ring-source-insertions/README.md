# Exact dirty-fanout source counts over finite associative rings

DONE — exact source-insertion counts over every finite associative ring. Scoped algebraic theorem with exact finite checks; no new kappa. No claim remains active.

The commutative criterion in [Discussion #5266](https://github.com/CrocSwap/integer-mult-bounds/discussions/5266) and the full-matrix-ring criterion in [Discussion #5269](https://github.com/CrocSwap/integer-mult-bounds/discussions/5269) extend to every finite unital ring, including noncommutative rings with a nonzero radical. The correct invariant is the **left** ideal generated by the demands.

**Contract.** Let R be a finite associative ring with 1!=0. There are k>=1 independent dirty rows a_i and a disjoint preserved source p, all in R^w, w>=1. Allow fixed input-independent words in

```text
E_ij(c): A_i <- A_i + c A_j,  i!=j,
P_i(b):  A_i <- A_i + b p,
```

with arbitrary coefficients in R, multiplied on the left. Require A_i=a_i+lambda_i p for all input values. No extra rows, clearing or observations are available. Count nonzero source insertions; row additions and coefficient preparation remain separately paid.

Let L=sum_i R lambda_i. Then the exact minimum source-insertion count is

```text
0 if every lambda_i=0;
1 if L is nonzero and principal as a left ideal;
2 if L is not principal as a left ideal.
```

One insertion can occur at any specified row, with at most 4(k-1) row additions. A two-insertion construction uses at most 2(k-1) row additions and works over every associative unital ring, without finiteness. No shortest total gate word or all-price tariff is asserted.

**Necessity.** With one insertion beta at row i, the final source column is C e_i beta, where C is the invertible product of subsequent row additions. Thus every demand is a left multiple of beta. Applying C^(-1) expresses beta as a left linear combination of the demands. Hence L=R beta. No insertion leaves the source column zero. These arguments use neither commutativity nor finiteness.

**Finite-ring shortening lemma.** If a finite left R-module M is cyclic and is generated by x_0,...,x_(k-1), there are a_j in R such that

```text
beta = x_0 - sum_(j>0) a_j x_j
```

generates M. Here is a proof that does not treat a left annihilator as a two-sided ideal.

Let J be the Jacobson radical. Classical finite-ring structure gives J nilpotent and R/J a product of full matrix rings M_d(F) over finite fields. Since M is cyclic, M/JM is a quotient of the left regular module R/J. In each matrix block, its component has the form F^(d by m), with 0<=m<=d, acted on by left matrix multiplication. The images X_j of the generators have stacked row spaces spanning F^m.

Put W=sum_(j>0) row(X_j). Choose an invertible row transformation P so the first m-dim(W) rows of P X_0 form a basis modulo W, and all remaining rows lie in W. There are at least dim(W) remaining rows because d>=m. Change those rows, using vectors of W, to a basis of W followed by zeros. Keep the first rows unchanged. The resulting matrix Y has full column rank m and differs from P X_0 by sum_(j>0) B_j X_j for suitable d-by-d matrices B_j. Thus X_0+sum_(j>0) P^(-1)B_j X_j has full column rank and generates the block module.

Do this in each semisimple block, with signs absorbed in a_j, and lift the coefficients through R -> R/J. The resulting beta satisfies M=R beta+JM. Iterating this equality gives M=R beta+J^t M for every t. Nilpotence of J yields M=R beta. This proves the lemma. The finite-ring structure and this Nakayama argument are classical inputs; the source-insertion consequence is the contribution here.

**Restoring construction.** Apply the lemma to the cyclic left module L. Then beta=lambda_0-sum_(j>0) a_j lambda_j generates L, so choose r_j with lambda_j=r_j beta. The ordered identities are

```text
lambda_j = r_j beta,
lambda_0 = (1+sum_(j>0) a_j r_j) beta.
```

Let C first perform E_j0(r_j) for all j>0 and then E_0j(a_j) for all j>0. It sends e_0 beta to lambda. Execute the literal inverse word for C, then P_0(beta), then C. All original dirt columns return to identity. This uses one insertion and at most 4(k-1) row additions. The order a_j r_j is essential; no coefficients were commuted.

For any demand, first perform E_j0(-lambda_j), j>0; insert P_0(1); undo those additions in reverse order; then insert P_0(lambda_0-1). The other rows gain lambda_j p and row zero gains lambda_0 p, with all dirt restored. If L is nonprincipal then lambda_0!=1, so both insertions are nonzero. The necessity result makes this count optimal.

**Wrong-side counterexample.** In the eight-element ring of upper-triangular two-by-two matrices over F2, take demands (E_11,E_12), using one-based matrix indices. Their generated left ideal is the two-dimensional space of matrices supported on the first row. It is not principal: a generator with nonzero bottom-right entry produces a nonzero second row, while one with zero bottom-right entry has a left ideal of dimension at most one. The source-insertion minimum is therefore two. Yet that same space is the principal right ideal E_11 R. Using right-ideal principality would give a false one-call conclusion.

**Evidence.** Warning-strict normal and optimized CPython 3.12.14 runs both pass six complete finite contexts: one, two and three dirty rows over UT2(F2), two rows over UT2(F3), two over M2(F2), and two over UT2(F2) x F2. All 1,825 demand lists are checked: six zero, 1,699 one-insertion and 120 two-insertion cases. An independent exhaustive elementary-column orbit search has 38 source orbits and agrees with left-ideal principality. Every constructed word is replayed on its full original-dirt/source coefficient matrix. The right-ideal counterexample and actual noncommutativity are checked directly.
