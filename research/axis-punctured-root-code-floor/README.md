# Sharp root-code floor after arbitrary axis punctures

For every n>=4, retain all signed edge lines e_i+e_j and e_i-e_j and any
k coordinate-axis lines. With the fixed dense binary orthogonality pairing,
the exact root-code Gram decoder costs at least

    n(n-1)+k = number of retained lines.

Coordinate factors attain the bound. Every optimum has exactly n nonzero
terms, even when arbitrary finite term counts and arbitrary coefficients
over a field of characteristic other than two are allowed.

This is a **scoped proof with finite corroboration**. The new case is
0<k<n. ywz65's PR2582 and PR2719 already settle the full-axis and no-axis
endpoints. This contribution was produced by Nick Petalas.

## New step and decision

[PROOF.md](PROOF.md) combines two cited ingredients with a retained-axis
rank charge. Restricting the exact coefficient identity to the k retained
coordinates forces at least k terms with nonzero retained projections on
both sides. Each such term contains an invertible n-by-n cross-star minor.
The signed-edge tariff and the credited one-extra coupling argument then
cover all finite term counts, including the odd-dimensional cheap terms.

Deleting axis lines alone therefore cannot produce a gain inside this
specified dense root-code Gram model. Stop searches in this family. A
changed signed-edge set, pairing, factor space or target is needed to reopen
it. The complete physical realization is outside the claim; no supplier or
kappa improvement is inferred.

The original `complex-rank-shape-decoder` card is explicitly changed to this
punctured-alphabet obstruction. Its five-percent construction gate is not
claimed. The current selected-source binding control passed. The historical
card's fixed-invoice ceiling is outside the new algebraic question, so it
is not used as an acceptance test or replayed as research evidence.

## Open next questions

1. Which specified deletion of signed edges first permits a strict gain
   with an exact root-code Gram factorization? This changes the premise
   responsible for the floor. Begin with a complete proposed factorization
   and its literal dense support ranks, not a cheaper isolated term. The
   new graph's support tariff and its physical consumer remain blockers.
2. Can a sparse fitting pairing lower the bill of one intermediate-axis
   configuration? Begin with n=4,k=1 and one shared pairing across all
   coordinate terms. Entry deletion is not rank-monotone; full pairing
   compatibility and any physical realization must be established.

Structured first steps and blockers are also in [TASK.json](TASK.json).
