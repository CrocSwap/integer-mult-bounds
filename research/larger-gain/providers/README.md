## Tensor geometry screen

The exact prototype identity is
`dot(p_s,p_t) = 1 + dot(r_s,r_t)/2 (mod2)`. Literal binary tensor labels preserve
the required orthogonality because their dot product is the product of the
factor dot products. Their binary Gram rank is exactly `9^s`, so that literal
Gram cannot be represented in fewer than `9^s` binary dimensions.

A stronger geometry-independent obstruction within the full Cartesian tensor
port set comes from an explicitly checked eight-clique of mutually orthogonal
odd labels. Its tensor power gives `8^s` independent odd orthogonal labels,
forcing `h >= 8^s` for any compatible encoding. A constant-augmented direct
sum fails already for two adjacent factor pairs: the real tensor coefficient
is1/4 but the binary dot product is1.

For s=2 there are 12,544 ports and 64 real centers. Ignoring every helper and
compiler cost, **data alone** contributes at least `2(h-1) >= 126` frame mass
per port in any compatible encoding. For literal product coordinates, each
center's support spans a tensor product of two rank-eight hyperplanes, so its
rank is 64, not 80. The exact literal data-plus-center mass per port is
`160 + 64*64/12544 = 160.3265`, versus 16.5714 for the base word. No center-rank
lower bound is claimed for arbitrary compatible encodings. This is a data-frame-depth
obstruction to a cheap tensor lift, **not a universal κ bound**. A different
port subset, outer code, packet factorization or analytic construction remains
outside the screen. `tensor_geometry_screen.py` verifies the finite identities
and clique witness; `discovery/tensor_geometry.json` retains its receipt.

The clone-removal screens likewise preserve the original packet factory and
packet sums. They do not exclude a joint packet/output-basis redesign. Such a
redesign must move packet source frames into the required output intersections,
not merely replace pairwise matrices by a dense Walsh transform.

## Next structural search: jointly select kernel and donor bases

The completed screens do **not** rule out a better simultaneous choice of
entrance kernels, donor bases and gauge bases. They keep the literal scalar
word fixed, except for the explicitly enumerated source exchanges and inserted
suffix kernels. `screen_response_nullity.py` computes the exact rational rank
of the current four-cold eight-provider dirty-response matrix: rank 98 among
504 helper columns, hence nullity 406. Of these, 216 are already zero-response
coordinate axes; the other 288 columns have nullity 190. The receipt is
`discovery/response_nullity.json`. These are algebraic dimensions, not 190
additional frame-legal entrances.

A concrete next algorithm is a bounded simultaneous basis exchange:

1. Freeze the center, scalar cut and target basis initially. Reconstruct the
   exact suffix response matrix at that cut. Enumerate candidate entrance
   frames E of rank one, two and three from actual first-frame intersections.
   For a fixed eligible helper set S, require each donor's current frame to
   lie inside E and E to lie inside its next fixed frame cap; a new pivot must
   also be untouched before the selected cut. Choosing response-independent
   donor columns inside a fixed eligible set is a linear-matroid basis problem. The
   ideal unconstrained kernel dimension is `|S| - rank(C_S)`.
2. Enumerate small exact response circuits and their alternative pivots. Keep
   only dyadic coefficients, recording every scalar addition and its cleanup.
   Rational independence alone is insufficient: changing a donor basis can
   introduce denominators with odd factors. Initially unlock two to four
   existing entrances in one connected donor-overlap component and allow
   simultaneous replacement by rank-two or rank-three entrances.
3. Select circuit recipes with binary variables. A helper has at most one
   selected pivot role; pivot/donor and pivot/gauge conflicts are forbidden
   unless an explicitly ordered triangular scalar realization is included.
   Reused donor frames must form chains. Preserve cut restrictions, data and
   center locks, target frames and final caps. Gauge choices require their own
   literal scalar transformations and cannot be treated as free relabelings.
4. Price **shared frame states**, not a sum of independently priced recipes.
   Each affected gate gets one admissible frame-state variable, with only
   legal nested transitions along every register chain. Transition variables
   count each paid H-rank once, and endpoint states determine the ordered
   residual widths. Scalar count, coefficient size, cleanup, compensation and
   eventual phase costs remain charged. The current capacity dual can rank
   candidate profile/width changes during discovery; final exact moments and
   a literal owner assignment decide admission.

Matroid intersection can help the fixed-frame donor subproblem, but it does
not by itself solve the coupled problem: frame joins, gauge conflicts and
non-additive shared transitions need discrete state constraints. A small
circuit-selection MILP with exact rational verification is a practical first
experiment. Any selected solution must be reconstructed as a complete scalar
word, then pass the same native/phase/omission checks and global admission.
This search could exchange several old entrances for fewer, deeper ones or
share donor work, changing the local cost curve instead of repeating the
already priced affine entrance promotions. It remains prospective.
