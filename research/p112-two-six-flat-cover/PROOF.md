# A sharp fifteen-line supplement for two six-flats

Use the 112 nonframe E8 root lines and their pinned nine-bit labels `ell(t)`.
For a binary label space `V`, write `Lambda(V)={t:ell(t) in V}`. Over a
characteristic-zero field `K`, put

```
C = {c_h : c_h(t)=<r_t,h>, h in K^8},
Off(h) = {t : <r_t,h> != 0}.
```

The roots in the replay are doubled, with squared norm eight. This common
scaling changes no support. The labels are a leading one followed by the
eight simple-root coefficients modulo two.

**Theorem.** For any two binary label spaces `V1,V2` of dimension at most
six and any nonzero `h in K^8`,

```
|Off(h) \ (Lambda(V1) union Lambda(V2))| >= 15.
```

The constant fifteen is attained. For a root direction the exact minimum,
over the two label spaces, is fifteen for each of the 112 nonframe roots
and eighteen for each of the eight frame roots.

This is a cover theorem, allowing the additional lines to be arbitrary and
direction-dependent. It is not a bound on the span of directions after
remaining supports of binary rank five, six or seven are admitted.

## Finite root-cover theorem

The complete census establishes the following fact without a symmetry
reduction. For every one of the 120 root directions `a`,

```
max_(dim V1=dim V2=6)
  |Off(a) intersect (Lambda(V1) union Lambda(V2))| = 38.
```

Root supports have sizes 53 and 56, so this gives the stated root minima.
Each row of `RESULT.json` includes two attaining flat masks and the exact
uncovered lines. They have label rank six. A separate XOR-closure check
reconstructs every flat and verifies every attaining cover.

Every lower-dimensional space extends to dimension six, so it suffices to
enumerate the six-dimensional spaces. The primary enumeration uses all
rank-three annihilators in reverse reduced row echelon form in `F2^9`.
There are 788,035 such spaces, yielding 776,806 distinct live masks including
the empty mask. The maximum single-flat size is twenty.

The independent enumeration works in the affine hyperplane where the leading
label bit is one. A six-dimensional linear space meeting that hyperplane
has an affine five-dimensional intersection. Enumerate all 97,155 linear
five-spaces in `F2^8` by direct reduced bases, then all eight cosets of each.
These 777,240 affine spaces produce exactly the same complete mask set.
Linear six-spaces in the even hyperplane have empty live intersection and
are accounted for by the empty mask. No predecessor flat or orbit table is
read by either enumerator.

For a fixed root, intersect every flat mask with its support. To check a
pair bound of 38, it suffices to retain intersections of size at least
18. Any discarded intersection has size at most seventeen and the other
has size at most twenty, giving union size at most 37. The retained set
has 320 masks for each nonframe root and 336 for each frame root. Every
unordered pair, including equal pairs, is checked by its exact bit union.
All unions have size at most 38, and each root has an attaining pair.
Thus all root directions and all pairs are covered; there is no assumption
that the frame stabilizer is transitive.

## From root directions to arbitrary characteristic-zero directions

The retained geometric proof uses the standard real Weyl-chamber theorem.
A real direction can be moved to a closed chamber, where its perpendicular
root system is generated by the simple roots on which it vanishes. The
finite replay checks the simple system, positivity, 960 simple reflections,
and these parabolic counts:

- A subsystem on six simple nodes has at most 36 root lines.
- The eight seven-node subsystems have line counts
  `42,28,22,14,16,23,37,63`.
- The normal to the 63-line subsystem is a root direction.

A nonzero direction supported on fewer than seventy live lines has more
than 42 perpendicular live roots. The only qualifying chamber ray is the
root ray with 63 perpendicular roots. Consequently such a direction is
proportional to a root. This is the inherited root-rigidity argument of
PR3164/PR3177, with its finite inputs recomputed here.

For a `K`-valued direction, its perpendicular rational roots have rational
span of dimension at most seven. If that span had dimension at most six,
a generic real normal to the span would have exactly those roots
perpendicular, contradicting the six-node bound of 36 when there are more
than 42. Hence the span has dimension seven. Its one-dimensional rational
normal space contains the given direction after scalar extension. The real
classification therefore applies over `K`. The live Gram sum is `112 I`,
so evaluation is injective over every characteristic-zero field.

Suppose the theorem failed with at most fourteen uncovered live lines.
Each six-flat contains at most twenty live lines, so

```
|Off(h)| <= 20+20+14 = 54 < 70.
```

The direction must be a scalar root. The finite root-cover theorem leaves
at least fifteen lines outside the two flats, a contradiction. An attaining
nonframe-root row supplies a cover with exactly fifteen extra lines, proving
sharpness. The extra lines carry no binary rank restriction in this theorem.

The Weyl-chamber theorem is an explicit mathematical dependency. Neither it
nor the characteristic-zero argument is a proof-kernel formalization.

## A quotient basis above the cardinality threshold

Let independent pieces `q_1,...,q_n` span `T`, and put `D=T intersect C`.
Suppose a subfamily `B` is supported inside

```
Lambda(V1) union Lambda(V2) union P,
dim V1,dim V2 <= 6, |P| <= 14.
```

The images of `B` in `T/D` are independent. A dependence would give a nonzero
combination of the independent original pieces belonging to `C`, supported
inside this union. The cover theorem excludes that codeword. If
`|B| >= dim(T/D)`, equality follows and those images are a quotient basis.
This replaces the sufficient cardinality certificate `|union supp B|<53`
for this particular cover shape.

The concrete caller is the actual TWOSIDE `(b,t)=(6,5)` profile

```
2,3,4,6,6,6,6,6,6,6,6.
```

A piece's weight is the binary rank of the labels on its support. Weights
two, three and four give support caps two, four and eight because the labels
lie in the leading-one affine hyperplane. Select the five pieces of weights
`2,3,4,6,6`. The last two have supports in two six-flats, and the first three
contribute at most `2+4+8=14` arbitrary lines. Their total support capacity
is 54, so the old strict-below-53 certificate does not establish independence
of this preferred five-set. The new cover theorem does.

Under the original assumption `dim(T intersect C)>=6`, the quotient has
dimension at most five. The selected five pieces therefore give a quotient
basis and `dim(T intersect C)=6`. Subtracting their appropriate combinations
from the other six pieces gives six independent codewords. Outside the
two six-flats and the same common fourteen-line set, their supports have
binary rank at most six. Bounding their common-set span by five is the
remaining concrete implication; it is not proved here.

Every five-piece subset has support-cap sum at least 54, since the five
smallest weights are already `2,3,4,6,6`. Thus these profile-based capacity
sums certify no five-piece subset by the strict-below-53 test. Actual support overlaps could give a smaller union;
no lower bound on that union is claimed. The new theorem
supplies the missing independence certificate for the named five-set.
This is a quotient implication, not a profile exclusion.

The unchanged PR3154 `bk2b.py` reproduces all 24 original cell counts. The
named profile passes the necessary prefix support gates with capacities
`74,94,114,134,154,174`, and raw excess 35 is below thirty-six. Its five
smallest weights sum to twenty-one, so neither the total-weight-seven nor the two
weight-six-bin Basis-kill rule applies. It is one of the original 174
survivors in this cell. This is a historical input binding, not a claim
that all other 173 profiles currently remain unresolved.

## What this result leaves open

The new theorem does not bound the span of the six residual directions.
A common-fourteen-cut rank-six span bound of five would exclude the named
configuration. PR3162's twelve-cut rank-five span witness and PR3167's
separate-fourteen-cut, one-anchor control warn against silently reusing a
stronger span bound. They do not decide this common-set question.

An earlier calibration draft used `2,3,4,5^2,6^5` at `(5,5)`. Independent
review identified that PR3169 already excludes exactly that profile by
`795<=768`. It is not an unresolved caller and no new coverage is claimed
for it. This correction changes the application, not the cover theorem.
PR2985's earlier root-target covering censuses are additional context.
They concern three-flat covers and small leftovers; this package supplies
the separate sharp two-flat floor, all 120 directions and two complete
label-space enumerations. Their broader results are not recertified here.

No profile exclusion, full C5, `C(P112)>=64`, decoder implementation, physical
supplier admission or new kappa follows from this package. The complete
TWOSIDE reduction remains inherited. The result is a sharp cover lemma and
one quotient-basis implication for its actual caller.
