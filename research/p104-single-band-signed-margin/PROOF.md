## All-size stopping cost

Let the fixed positive rank be \(M=2^{1600000}\). At an active call, let \(h\) be the original selected width, and let \(g=1/D\) for a fixed integer \(D\ge2\). The two stop tests are \(e\le M\) and \(e^D\le h\), where \(e\) is the actual selected width. The comparator uses squared products capped at \(h+1\), so changing \(D\) has a fixed \(O(\log D)\) factor in the number of arithmetic operations on \(O(\log h)\)-bit integers. Each complete active step pays all residual axes and recurses on every issued whole child of width \(\lfloor e/M\rfloor\).

At depth \(j\) the quotient is \(e_j=\lfloor h/M^j\rfloor\). The identity for nested integer quotients makes this exact. A stopped positive width satisfies \(e_j\le\max(M,h^g)\le Mh^g\). For the full admitted normalized paid ratio at most \(M^\sigma\), with \(\sigma=1-b\) and \(0<b<1\), count the leaves by

\[
M^{\sigma j}e_j \le h^\sigma e_j^{1-\sigma}\le M^b h^{1-b+gb}.
\]

At every nonterminal depth the individual suffix axes still have total invoice at most \(Mh^\sigma\) times the same fixed row factor. The depth bound \(2+\lceil\log_M(h+1)\rceil\) follows from the quotient recurrence for either stop. The stock in the stripe certificate already allows that entire depth, and the head restoration uses the same chronological owner rules. In particular reducing \(g\) adds no new depth or ownership hypothesis. At the root \(h>M\) implies \(h^D>h\), so the real installed action is reached at the same sufficient public entrance for a fixed set of ranks. The actual profile reaches this path through both `prepare_native` and the numerical `Product.h` dispatcher. The positive supplier cache includes the denominator.

All local precise-field sizes and grid closures retain their fixed rank and projected width. The recurrence proof bounds work with the larger admissible family of descents. The checked `10^-17` margin changes the cost comparison and discrete early stop, while the leaves, normalized source operators, and final recovery remain the same computations at each visited site.
