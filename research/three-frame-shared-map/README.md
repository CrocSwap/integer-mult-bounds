# Exact shared-map compatibility for three frame subspaces

For two ordered triples of subspaces in F_q^n, a common invertible map exists
exactly when their three individual dimensions, three pairwise intersection
dimensions, triple-intersection dimension and total-sum dimension agree.
[PROOF.md](PROOF.md) gives the exact number of realizing maps and a constructive
all-dimension proof. At ambient dimension nine there are exactly **15,792**
ordered compatibility types, independently of the finite field.

This contribution was produced by Nick Petalas. Its claim type is a **scoped
proof with finite exact corroboration**. The classical three-subspace normal
form is background, with no literature-priority claim. The new repository
contribution is an explicit shared-native-map compatibility and multiplicity
contract, addressing the prelude-contact question in Cao Yifan's
[PR4610 package](../kappa-round3-warp-20261022/README.md). This is not a replay
or audit of that contributor's result.

If m_S are the eight Boolean-atom multiplicities and t is the triangle
multiplicity defined in the proof, the number of realizers is zero for
incompatible triples and otherwise

```
q^R |GL_t(F_q)| product_S |GL_(m_S)(F_q)|,
```

where the proof gives R explicitly. Counting types gives
`sum_(t=0)^floor(n/2) binomial(n-2t+7,7)`.

The literal checker constructs vector sets, all subspaces and every invertible
matrix in its declared domains. It computes complete orbits independently of
the invariant classifier, then compares compatibility and every orbit's map
multiplicities. The domains are F_2 in dimensions zero through three and
F_3 and F_5 in dimension two. They contain 4,958 ordered triples in total.
The binary dimension-three domain alone has 4,096 triples and 128 orbits.
All 15,792 dimension-nine normal-form tuples are separately enumerated and
reconstructed from their dimensions, with an ordered digest. No nine-dimensional
GL enumeration is claimed. Seven invalid-input controls
and two incomplete-invariant examples accompany the complete orbit checks.
