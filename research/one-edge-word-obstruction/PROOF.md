# Three relations leave only two productive labels

Ali Romman with substantial OpenAI Codex assistance. Apache-2.0.

Use Nick Petalas's alphabet from
[PR #7749](https://github.com/CrocSwap/integer-mult-bounds/pull/7749),
in coordinates a,b,f,g:

```text
P=(1,3), Q=(3,2), R=(5,9), S=(11,6).
```

The pair (x,y) denotes xy^T over F2. Each call is I+([z]-1)A for one
of these projectors. Its label z belongs to the torsion-free abelian
group H generated by all m paid labels. Equivalently H=Z^m/L, with
L saturated. Later calls act on the left. Every type is present, every
paid label is nonzero, and rank(L)<=3. Require a diagonal endpoint on
all four original coordinates; arbitrary changes of register basis
are not included.

**Theorem.** The word is diagonal exactly when its chronology satisfies
the following three conditions and its exact type totals satisfy (1).

1. No P or Q call lies between the first and last R calls.
2. No Q call lies between the first and last S calls.
3. After deleting P and Q, the R/S word has at most three runs. Thus one
   type is a single block inside the other type, or the types form two blocks.

```text
total(P)=total(Q)=u,       total(R)=total(S)=0.          (1)
```

The full endpoint is diag([u],[u],1,1). The two fresh coordinates have
identity targets. This classification holds at every finite word length
under the stated three-relation contract.

For rational matrix labels and every 0<t<=1, the target rank-power sum is
at most the paid rank-power sum. No favorable rank-power tariff is available
in this class, even before physical costs are added. This is not a universal
word obstruction for the alphabet when more relations are allowed.

## 1. A unique private writer cannot give a diagonal endpoint

Only R writes coordinate f, and its covector a+g has a nonzero coordinate
outside f. Suppose there is exactly one R call, at a nonzero label z.
Write its earlier word as U and later word as V. Both exclude R. Their
f rows are identity rows. The final f row is

```text
e_f^T + ([z]-1) (a+g)^T U.
```

The group algebra F2[H] is a Laurent polynomial domain. If the endpoint
were diagonal, each component outside f of (a+g)^T U would be zero.
Apply augmentation, which sends every group monomial to one. Every call
and U then become identity, so this row becomes (a+g)^T. It has nonzero
components outside f, a contradiction.

The same proof applies to S and g, since only S writes g and its covector
is b+f. Thus any diagonal word with all four types and nonzero labels
needs at least two R calls and two S calls. It needs at least six calls
in total, without any relation-rank assumption.

This floor is sharp. In H=Z^3, use independent nonzero u,r,s and the word
P(u),Q(u),R(r),R(-r),S(s),S(-s). It has exactly three saturated relations
and endpoint diag([u],[u],1,1). The R/S calls remain paid.

## 2. The minimal relation quotient

Let p,q,r,s be the four positive type counts. Section 1 permits us to
assume r,s>=2. The off-patterns have exactly three independent generators:
off(P)=off(Q), off(R), off(S). The degree-one off-diagonal endpoint
therefore forces

```text
sum(P forms)=sum(Q forms)=u,
sum(R forms)=0,           sum(S forms)=0.              (2)
```

These are three independent relations among the m call variables before
quotient. Since H is paid-generated and L is saturated, its degree-one
augmentation space has dimension m-rank(L), and its graded algebra is
the polynomial ring on that space. The limit rank(L)<=3 makes (2) the
complete set of degree-one relations; rank(L)=3 in every diagonal case.

Choose the common u, all but one variable of each P and Q type, and all
but one variable of each R and S type as coordinates. The P variables
are a basis of their p-dimensional span; the Q variables are a basis of
their q-dimensional span. The R variables have exactly one dependence,
their full sum zero, and the same is true of S. The R and S variable
spaces are disjoint from each other and from the P/Q variable spaces.
The latter two share only u.

This is a statement about the paid-generated group. An unrelated larger
ambient exponent group must not supply the augmentation coordinates.
Nor does degree one alone imply the exact integral identities (1).

## 3. Four commutators force the chronology

P,Q are orthogonal, as are P,S. The remaining four off-commutators
[P,R], [Q,R], [Q,S], [R,S] are independent modulo the pattern span.
Here [A,B]=AB+BA. The following finite certificate uses bit 4i+j for
matrix entry (i,j), after deleting the diagonal:

| Row | Hexadecimal encoding |
| --- | --- |
| off(P) | 0002 |
| off(R) | 0908 |
| off(S) | 6046 |
| off([P,R]) | 030a |
| off([Q,R]) | 0202 |
| off([Q,S]) | 2044 |
| off([R,S]) | 1098 |

Row elimination gives 6046,2044,1098,0908,030a,0108,0002, with distinct
leading positions 14,13,12,11,9,8,1. Thus all seven rows are independent.
The checker also tests all 127 nonempty subsets.

Compare the candidate chronology with the order P then Q then R then S.
The reference word's degree-two off-diagonal part lies in the pattern
span: its R/S totals have zero degree-one part, and P,Q commute. Each
interchange of two calls contributes the product of their linear forms
times the corresponding commutator. Independence makes each of the four
inversion polynomials zero.

For P/R, that polynomial is the sum over P calls of its variable times
the sum of variables of all earlier R calls. Independence of the P
variables and their products with the disjoint R space makes each such
R subset sum zero. The R variables have no proper nonempty zero-sum
subset. Hence each P call is before every R or after every R. The Q/R
and Q/S polynomials give the corresponding statements for Q. These
are conditions 1 and 2.

For R/S, form the r-by-s binary matrix M with entry one when S_j is
before R_i. The map from its rows and columns to the R/S variable
spaces has all-one kernels. Its tensor image is zero exactly when

```text
M_ij = alpha_i + beta_j.
```

One can check this by eliminating the last variable on each side: every
remaining coefficient is M_ij+M_i,s+M_r,j+M_r,s. In chronological order,
each row of M is a prefix of ones among the S calls. Two rows can differ
only by zero or by the all-one row. If one prefix is proper and nonempty,
every row has that same prefix, so all R calls form a single R block
between S blocks. Otherwise the prefixes are empty or full, so all S
calls form a single S block between R blocks. This is condition 3.

## 4. Exact cancellation, not just parity

If R is the inner block of the R/S chronology, conditions 1 and 3 make
its calls consecutive: neither P,Q nor S can occur between them. Combine
them into one call at their exact sum. Section 1 forces that sum to be
zero, so the block is identity. Only P can remain between S calls, by
condition 2. P commutes with S, so combine the S calls and use the same
private-row argument to force their exact total to zero.

If S is the inner block, condition 2 excludes Q and condition 3 excludes
R from its interval. Commute P past S and combine the S calls first.
Their total is zero by Section 1. Condition 1 then lets the R calls
combine, and their total is zero too. The same reasoning covers two
disjoint blocks.

Only P and Q remain. They commute and their product on a,b is

```text
[ [v], [v]+[w] ]
[   0,     [w] ],
```

where v and w are their exact totals. Diagonality forces [v]=[w], hence
v=w=u. This proves (1), necessity and the full endpoint. Conversely,
the same block reduction proves sufficiency from conditions 1--3 and (1).

Give the first target all P paid labels and the second target all Q paid
labels. Give the two identity targets empty subsets. Rank subadditivity,
monotonicity and subadditivity of x^t for 0<t<=1 give

```text
2 rank(u)^t <= sum_(P and Q calls) rank(z)^t
             <= sum_(all paid calls) rank(z)^t.
```

The R/S cancellations are not free calls. Additional physical charges
cannot reverse this inequality inside this stated rank-power model.
