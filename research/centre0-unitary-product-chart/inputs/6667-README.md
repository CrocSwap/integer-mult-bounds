# A common complex product chart for the actual centre0 pair

This scoped construction was produced by Nick Petalas. Apache-2.0.

The arbitrary-complex-chart question in JoyboyBrian's
[PR5130](https://github.com/CrocSwap/integer-mult-bounds/pull/5130) has a
constructive answer. For the actual centre0 code G of Chris R /
Clear1nsight's [PR5101](https://github.com/CrocSwap/integer-mult-bounds/pull/5101),
put A=G direct-sum I_(N-112), B=G^T direct-sum I_(N-112). There is an
explicit common invertible complex chart S such that

```text
(S A S^-1 x)(S B S^-1 z) = (S B S^-1)(xz),
```

where products are in C[y]/(y^N+1), for every x,z and every N>=112.
This includes every power-of-two padding N>=128 requested by the claim.
The output map is explicitly S B S^-1. Claim type: scoped written
algebraic construction with exact finite corroboration. No new kappa.

## Construction

[PROOF.md](PROOF.md) decomposes the pair into seven common active planes
and a 98-dimensional fixed complement. On each plane, GG^T has eigenvalues
937 +/- 12 sqrt(6097). An eigenvector and its G-image put G and G^T into
weighted swaps with the same permutation. This makes their coordinatewise
product identity exact. The inverse negacyclic evaluation map then supplies
the common chart. Its entries lie in Q(sqrt(6097), zeta_(2N)).

[chart.py](chart.py) constructs the exact 112-dimensional basis W and its
inverse in Q(sqrt(6097)); the proof gives S=E_N^-1 W_N^-1 explicitly.
The new result addresses the complex case. PR5130's Gaussian-rational
exclusion and the original natural-coordinate failure remain unchanged.
The predecessor results are cited inputs, not audit or replay deliverables.

## Evidence and reproduction

The standalone checker constructs the new basis and uses generic exact
matrix application to check both inverse orders and both source endpoints
on every basis column. It checks all 12,544 split basis products, 2,401
quadratic products, 18 literal negacyclic vector pairs at N=128,256,512,
and 11 controls. The 224 inverse-column and 224 endpoint-column equalities
are exact in Q(sqrt(6097)). Literal polynomial convolution over the declared
finite prime fields independently corroborates evaluation, normalization,
padding and wrap signs. The written argument supplies the all-size theorem
over C; the finite-field checks do not establish it by themselves.
