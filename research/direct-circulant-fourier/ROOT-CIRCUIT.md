# A gate circuit for the padded circulant root

Let r be a positive integer, and let t = 2^ell ≥ r. Use the first r of the t physical coordinates for one cyclic axis. Every other coordinate contains independent arbitrary data. Let U be an invertible circulant on the r coordinates, with known Fourier multipliers of magnitudes in [m,M], where m>0. Use a unit scalar γ and a positive integer j to select roots of its rotated multipliers. They define a circulant R with R^j=γU. The root condition is Re R ≥ a I for a known a>0, where Re R=(R+R*)/2. All Fourier eigenvectors here form one unitary basis. A single stored first column defines an exactly circulant rational C. Component error ε in its r entries gives ||C−R||₂ ≤ r√2 ε by the row and column sums. Use r√2 ε<a/2 and a target operator accuracy. Complement the root map with an identity on arbitrary pad data.

For a unitary circulant, principal cube roots work with γ=1 and a=1/2. The two square roots of a unitary multiplier at −1 both have real part zero. For a general invertible circulant with r eigenphases, there is an open arc between successive phases of length at least 2π/r, counting multiplicities. Put its midpoint at −1 by multiplication by γ. A principal square root of each rotated multiplier has real part at least √m sin(π/(2r)) ≥ √m/r. The scalar γ⁻¹ acts only on the first r coordinates after the second root. It therefore preserves the complement. The Fourier code handles both parities with a half step on the 2r-grid. Its proved root margin is 1/(2r), and the exact adapter uses μ=1/(4r). Separate odd-order fixtures also exercise a cube and a sixteen-fold root. [Production phases and both signs](PROOF.md)

The new input to the circuit compiler is A = diag(C,I) on all t wires. A contains a circulant logical axis of arbitrary size and a disjoint identity. The predecessor's general Toeplitz input test fails on A even for r=3, t=4. The following two facts supply the actual root-rank and conditioning requirements to its unchanged downstream gate generator. ## The two source hypotheses

Let J insert an r-vector in the first r slots. Its adjoint restricts to those slots. Let V be the cyclic forward shift on r slots and Z the forward shift on t slots truncated at the last slot. Set D=C−I_r, so A=I_t+JDJ*. In the t-slot basis use e_r=0 when r=t. Direct action on basis vectors proves

    ZJ = JV + (e_r−e_0)e_(r−1)*
    J*Z = VJ* − e_0 e_(r−1)*.

The input and output sizes of the second terms are those of the left sides. Since VC=CV, subtracting AZ from ZA gives

    ZA−AZ = (e_r−e_0)e_(r−1)* D J* + J D e_0 e_(r−1)*.

Its rank is at most two for every r and t. Both terms persist at an interior padding cut. Treating A itself as Toeplitz can change the operator there. The identity complement is important for arbitrary data and for conditioning.

The adapter accepts the stored dyadic column and a separate rational margin μ with 0<μ<a−||C−R||₂. It forms the Hermitian matrix (C+C*)/2−μI on the r original coordinates and performs exact rational elimination. Each pivot must be real and strictly positive. Congruence by the elimination factor gives that pivot and the next Hermitian Schur complement. Induction proves that this check is equivalent to strict positive definiteness. Let B be any leading section of the full A. For every x, Re⟨x,Bx⟩ ≥ min(1,μ)||x||₂², also when B cuts through the cyclic block. Thus ||Bx||₂ ≥ min(1,μ)||x||₂. B is square, so ||B⁻¹||₂ ≤ 1/min(1,μ). The maximum row or column sum of |Re|+|Im| is at most √(2t) times the spectral norm, by Cauchy–Schwarz. The source component bounds in the code are Λ=max(1,norm_upper(A)) and Q=2t/min(1,μ). Here ||A||₂≤max(1,M^(1/j)+||C−R||₂), so both envelopes are polynomial in t, M and 1/μ. This proof includes every leading section without an inverse on a data tape.

## Why the retained rank and gate bounds apply

All internal roots are contiguous Schur descendants of A in the same elimination order. Write the two block rows of M as (A₀,B₀) and (C₀,D₀). Set K=A₀⁻¹B₀, L=C₀A₀⁻¹ and S=D₀−C₀K. Take U_L to be the block row (−L,I) and V_K to be the block column with top −K and bottom I. Splitting the two shift blocks leaves a single rank-one link E at their boundary. Block multiplication gives

    Δ(S) = U_L Δ(M) V_K − S E K,
    rank Δ(A₀) ≤ rank Δ(M)+1,

where Δ(X)=ZX−XZ. The top-right and bottom-left blocks of Δ(M) control Δ(B₀) and Δ(C₀), respectively, with at most two extra outer products. The product and inverse commutator formulas then give rank Δ(K) ≤ 2 rank Δ(M)+1 and rank Δ(L) ≤ 2 rank Δ(M)+3. Finally X−ZXZ* = −Δ(X)Z*+X e₀e₀*. Thus every source Stein shear has rank at most 2ell+8. The root rank-two proof above matches the exact original bound. These identities are valid for the padded matrix at every split. ## Every gate can use one common Hadamard schedule

The retained numerical FFT butterfly has rational coefficients a,b approximating 1/√2. The exact stored gate can differ from an exact Hadamard followed by a diagonal. For every invertible two-coordinate gate G with ||G||₂, ||G⁻¹||₂ ≤ L, choose its largest-modulus entry, using exact comparisons of rational squared moduli. Two optional pair swaps put that entry u at the top left, giving a matrix with rows (u,v) and (w,z). Factor it in matrix order as

    lower(w/u) diag(u,z−wv/u) upper(v/u).

The two shear parameters have modulus at most one. We have |u| ≥ 1/(√2L), |u| ≤ L and |z−wv/u| ≤ 2L. The reciprocal of z−wv/u is a diagonal entry of the inverse, hence has magnitude at most L. The optional swaps act on the same pair. Each uses two H factors with the middle diagonal (1,±1) and scalar 1/2, where H is the unnormalized binary Hadamard. The six-H upper shear combines the retained three-H identity at parameters −3 and v/u+3. Both parameter moduli lie between two and four. Reversing the diagonal sequence gives the lower shear by transpose with no complex conjugation. The code joins adjacent diagonals in execution order. Each original gate now has exactly sixteen H factors and seventeen diagonals. Its partial prefixes and inverses increase the existing norm bound by at most a fixed polynomial in L. Coefficient bit heights increase by a constant factor because the lowering uses a fixed number of rational operations. Pair the idle and scalar wires to complete each even matching. The one-wire dimension uses a scalar directly. The retained Beneš route and its inverse synchronize pair matchings for different axes. They use 8ell−4 more H layers per original matching, hence the total depth is O(1+ell^5). ## Setup, storage and finite checks

Prepare one axis program on private tapes. Roots are obtained from their supplied symbols by direct coefficient calculation. The retained Schur matrices are ratios of minors of the original rational root after denominators are cleared. This bounds their bit lengths by a polynomial in t and the root input precision. Rationals in the exact orthogonalization are also of polynomial bit length. In particular, every residual is the original column minus the projection onto the span of earlier independent columns. The entries of the projection follow from the inverse of their Gram matrix, so they are ratios of polynomial-size minors. The common power-of-two normalization of the residual and its reciprocal row both have polynomial bit length. The triangular parity recursions take principal sections and principal Schur complements of each rational triangular root; their stored rational entries have the same type of minor bound. Exact series division computes each finite triangular quotient by forward substitution. Its j-th coefficient has a denominator dividing a fixed cleared leading coefficient to power j+1 times the cleared input denominator. These facts bound intermediate reduced rationals too. Taking products and sums of at most a polynomial number of such bounded terms in a single arithmetic expression preserves a polynomial bit bound. Exact rational elimination, integer phase rectangles and the retained roundings use polynomial time and storage in t and precision on a fixed number of sequential tapes, including scans replacing setup-time indexed reads. In an all-size machine, recursion state and arbitrary integers are written on these tapes. A Python host can have its own recursion and integer-print limits.

This private setup bound can exceed a polynomial in the outer precision p. In the square family log t = O((log p)³), so poly(t,p)=2^o(p) for the complete setup over polynomially many axes. Only rounded O(p)-bit gates and their index descriptors are used for whole-array arithmetic. This proof permits the larger private setup precision explicitly.
