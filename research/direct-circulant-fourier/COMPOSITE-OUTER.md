# Composite outer geometry and complete caller

## Statement and physical addresses

Let the binary input lengths be at most n. Let b = ceil(log₂ n), B = 2ᵇ and p = 6b. Let T be the unique power of two in [4n/b,8n/b). Write L = log₂ T and V = Tp. Thus 24n ≤ V < 48n. All asymptotic statements concern n beyond a fixed finite cutoff. Handle the finite initial range by elementary binary multiplication. The fast branch uses radix-B digits and the original CRT permutation from the complete mixed-rank caller. Its new outer transform acts on the true physical coordinates of each axis.

The local circuit to which this proof applies has the following explicit requirements. Each true odd side rᵢ sits inside a dyadic side tᵢ = 2^ℓᵢ. For either sign, the exact target is the true Fourier mean on [0,rᵢ) extended by the identity. The generated rational gate word approximates this target on arbitrary signals with operator error ≤ 2^(−P) tᵢ^O(1). It uses a polynomial in ℓᵢ matching layers. Each pair map and its inverse have 2-norm bounded by a fixed power of tᵢ. At complete matching-layer boundaries the norm of every prefix and its inverse are bounded by a fixed power of tᵢ. Tables, routings, control words and dyadic coefficients to P fractional bits can be constructed in poly(tᵢ,P) time and space on sequential tapes. Constants and polynomial degrees are uniform in i,n. A single-axis table must include dummy rows. The direct spectral-root construction in this package supplies these requirements for the original-coordinate Fourier transform. They also describe other circuits that can use the same multidimensional tensor executor. Only circuits that realize the stated Fourier mean give the integer-product conclusion here.

## Construct a family of odd square sides

Set s = ceil(log₂(p+2)), w = 2¹⁶s³ and let d be the greatest power of two ≤ L/w. Start with widths floor(L/d) or ceil(L/d), placing the larger widths first. Write h₀ = floor(L/d), a = floor(h₀/8) and H = d/2 for d ≥ 2. For i = 0,…,H−1 transfer eᵢ = a−(i mod (a+1)) actual address bits from axis H+i to axis i. Call the resulting declared widths ℓᵢ. The sum is still L. Axis zero has maximum width; the minimum is at least 7h₀/8 and the maximum at most 9h₀/8+1. Thus max ℓᵢ ≤ 2 min ℓᵢ, d = Θ(p/s³), and every ℓᵢ = Θ(s³). Eventually H ≥ a+1. The widths of the first a+1 axes are strictly decreasing because their initial widths are nonincreasing and their respective transfers decrease by one. Hence there are Θ(s³) distinct binary widths. The difference between the first and its paired target is at least 2a−1, so their padded *side lengths* can differ by a factor at least 2^(2a−1). For d = 1 retain its unique width. [Executable controller](square_geometry.py)

For each target tᵢ choose an odd square rᵢ = xᵢ² in its own relative-deficit interval. For an even i use

    1/(8d) ≤ 1 − rᵢ/tᵢ ≤ 1/(4d).

For an odd i use

    1/(32d) ≤ 1 − rᵢ/tᵢ ≤ 1/(16d).

In increasing i order, scan the corresponding consecutive possible x ≥ 3. Choose the first odd x coprime to twice the product of the previously chosen bases. Use integer square roots, exact divisions with specified floor or ceiling, and the Euclidean algorithm. The controller code makes each choice deterministic. Its bounds depend only on operand length.

Here is an elementary count that proves every scan succeeds eventually, uniformly over the previous choices. Put M = 2∏_{j<i} xⱼ and let q₁ < ⋯ < qₘ be its distinct prime divisors. The algorithm does not need this factorization. Since m ≤ log₂ M ≤ L+1 ≤ p+1 and qⱼ ≥ j+1,

    A = ∏ⱼ (1−1/qⱼ) ≥ 1/(m+1),
    μ = ∑ⱼ 1/qⱼ ≤ ln(m+1).

Put k = 8s+1, an odd integer. Let Eᵥ be the elementary symmetric sum of degree v in the 1/qⱼ, with E₀ = 1 and Eᵥ = 0 for v > m. The finite Bonferroni inequalities put A between Aₖ = Σ_{v≤k}(−1)ᵛ Eᵥ and Aₖ+Eₖ₊₁. One way to see the signs is to select independent Bernoulli coordinates with probabilities 1/qⱼ. On an outcome with h ≥ 1 selected coordinates the truncated alternating binomial sum is (−1)ᵏ binom(h−1,k). For h = 0 the sum is 1. Also

    Eₖ₊₁ ≤ μ^(k+1)/(k+1)! ≤ (e μ/(k+1))^(k+1) ≤ 2^(−k−1).

The first inequality counts tuples with distinct indices in the full expansion. The second follows by integrating log x on [1,k+1]. The last follows from μ ≤ s ln 2 and e ln 2 < 4. Hence Aₖ ≥ 1/(2(m+1)). In X consecutive integers the count divisible by any square-free product of these primes differs from X divided by that product by less than one. The number of terms through degree k is at most (m+1)^k, by padding the increasing tuple of indices with null symbols. Apply the same odd binomial bound to each integer. The count coprime to M is at least

    X/(2(m+1)) − (m+1)^k.

It is positive for X > 2(m+1)^(k+1). The narrower square band has real base width at least √tᵢ/(64d), by taking the difference of the square roots and bounding the sum of their roots by 2√tᵢ. Rounding the two endpoints loses at most two. More explicitly, m+1 ≤ p+2 ≤ 2ˢ, d ≤ 2ˢ and ℓᵢ ≥ 2¹⁵s³. The required X is at most 2^(1+s(8s+2)); the available count is at least 2^(2¹⁴s³−6−s)−2, which is larger for every s ≥ 1. Thus a square is available whenever the nonempty width rule and the stated size bounds hold. Establishing the count requires no result on the distribution of primes in intervals.

The chosen sides are pairwise coprime composites. Put S = ∏ᵢ rᵢ. The product inequality ∏(1−δᵢ) ≥ 1−Σδᵢ for 0 ≤ δᵢ ≤ 1 gives S ≥ 3T/4. Each individual tᵢ/rᵢ ≤ 1/(1−1/(4d)) and the full storage expansion T/S is at most 4/3. Each operand has at most l = ceil(n/b) digits. Thus 2l−2 < 2n/b ≤ T/2 < S. Also S < T ≤ B for b ≥ 8. Each cyclic coefficient equals its linear coefficient and is less than B³. For this controller every axis lies outside the previous upper-deficit bound 1/(2p²) eventually. Adjacent even and odd fields lie in disjoint relative-gap bands. The same complete executor below works for *any* pairwise coprime odd rᵢ strictly within its matching dyadic bounds, with ∏rᵢ ≥ 3T/4, axis zero widest, all ℓᵢ = Θ(s³) and max ℓᵢ ≤ 2 min ℓᵢ. The square scan realizes these conditions from every large input length.

Generate all one-axis tables and the full list of square sides once in d·2^O(max ℓᵢ) poly(p) = 2^O(s³) = o(n) bit operations. This allows a bounded-degree polynomial in tᵢ and P. The same bound covers the largest interval ever visited on the private tapes, including erasure. These table sizes may exceed a polynomial in p. No full tensor is used at this private precision.

## Tensor stages on a complete field

Run one width class at a time. There are O(s³) classes. Within a class all axes use the same significance at each stage. Complete shorter local programs with identity pairs. For any invertible two-coordinate gate G exchange rows and columns to place its largest entry by modulus in the leading pivot. Its modulus is at least 1/(√2 ‖G⁻¹‖₂), both triangular shear ratios have modulus at most one, and the reciprocal of the second pivot is an entry of the inverse of the permuted matrix. The emitted fixed word uses two optional two-H exchanges, two six-H shears and the pivot diagonal, with exactly sixteen H factors. It therefore also covers the stored rational numerical FFT butterflies that need not factor as H times a diagonal. The forward and reverse Beneš programs for the matching each use 2(2ℓᵢ−1) H factors. The total per layer is 8ℓᵢ+12. Thus O(ℓᵢ⁴) matching depth costs O(ℓᵢ⁵) H calls per class, before pack division. All private controls and coefficient tables are generated in polynomial time in tᵢ and P, with both permutations included. Each such H acts on one bit of each active axis across the *complete padded tensor*, including all spectators and holes. Take P = C p for fixed sufficiently large C. Let s' = ceil(log₂(P+2)), G = 4s'+6 and K = 128G. For significance j on a width-ℓᵢ axis take common offset ρ = min(j,K−1) and start the K-bit window at j−ρ. Its highest index is < ℓᵢ because ℓᵢ ≥ K eventually. This includes the most significant bit, which a window that always starts at j would miss.

Split a class into packs of at most max(1,floor(d/s)) bits. There are O(s) packs within any class and O(s³+s) packs summed over classes for a common local depth. The corresponding tensor factors commute and leave every other bit a spectator. For h bits of a pack, gathering and restoring the real neighborhoods moves k ≤ 2hK = O(hs) positions. The complete short-record suffix of (s')² untouched bits exists away from these moves because

    (k+1)(s')² = O_F((d/s)s³ + s²) = O_F(p/s) = o(L).

Reserve and place the suffix first. Take the additional three O(kG)-bit reservoirs in its complement. Their sizes satisfy kG = o(L) as well. The source re-computes the control positions and colors after physical placement and removes the suffix after inverse exposure. A coarse-label-dependent diagonal is applied only when the full original stream has returned. Such a suffix on a fixed-label subarray would not give the same resource.

For clarity, the row bound in this application is the proved stock for the actual fixed mixed-rank frames. The logarithms of all simultaneous ordinary and complex row stocks add. Their product requires at most O_F(log(h+1) + h^(1/250)(s')³ + 1) further complete chunks. The two front fields and the back field use at most 3h/128+O(1) chunks. For h larger than a fixed multiple of (1+s')⁵, the sum is ≤ h/16, since 5(1−1/250)>3. Defer the selected bits in these chunks. Execute the current native word on every other selected bit, restore every dirty borrowed coordinate, remove temporary whole-row padding, then process the deferred bits. The positive powers of the shrinking widths have a convergent geometric sum. Below this threshold perform each selected kernel separately in O(V polylog p). The exact common dyadic grid for a complete pack has fractional precision P+O(h), magnitude exponent O(p)+O(h), and O(p) temporary width. Converting the normalized H to raw H costs a full shift scan by 2ʰ. Every intervening displacement, row encoding, materialization, return and cleanup is priced. The proof uses h ≤ d and the displayed physical-space inequalities directly. A power saving h = O(p^(1−ε)) with a fixed ε > 0 would fail for this controller.
