# Joint frequency filters with exact restoration of mixed holes

Let d≥1, let rⱼ≥2 be arbitrary, and use tⱼ=2^ceil(log₂ rⱼ) physical coordinates per axis. Set S=∏rⱼ and T=∏tⱼ. A caller supplies T complex grid words. The S original words are independent and may be complex. All other words are borrowed dirty input. A second stream supplies exactly S complex grid multipliers w(k), in original frequency order, with |w(k)|≤M. Zeros are permitted. No factorization across axes is required. For non-grid weights, the caller pays for conversion and includes its error below. [Filter](spectral_filter.py) · [Physical replay](check_filter.py)

Write E for the true unitary tensor DFT with the negative sign. The direct transform implements the Fourier mean U=E/√S on the all-original subspace. Its opposite has mean V=E*/√S there. The desired filter on original inputs is

    P = S V diag(w) U = E* diag(w) E.

The code constructs the signed local Fourier programs for those same rⱼ,tⱼ and issues their tensor word. On the entry scan it saves each mixed-hole word and writes a zero in its working field. It applies the forward program. The next scan consumes one multiplier for each original frequency and zeros every mixed frequency. It applies the opposite program and multiplies each original result by the exact integer S. It restores the saved mixed words in their original physical order. This costs one saved word per mixed hole. An independent dirty value is never reconstructed from a rounded transform. The input-masking step also makes the numerical result independent of dirty values of any magnitude that fits the incoming word. The output has S independent valid slots and T−S bit-exact returned holes.

## Error and scan cost

Let X be the 2-norm of the original input. Suppose the numerical forward invocation has full-field Euclidean error at most e₁ on this input extended by zeros. The exact projected frequency multiplication has operator norm at most M. Nearest-grid rounding at each of S two-component outputs contributes eₘ≤√S·2^(−q), at q fractional bits. For a zero-one mask eₘ=0. The actual input to the opposite transform has norm at most M(X/√S+e₁)+eₘ and vanishes on all mixed holes. On this radius let that program have Euclidean error at most e₂. Since ||V||₂=1/√S on this block, exact integer rescaling gives

    ||out−Px||₂ ≤ S e₂ + √S (M e₁ + eₘ).

If the stream approximates the intended frequency mask uniformly to η, replacing its represented mask by the intended mask adds at most Xη. When the transform guarantee is in maximum-coordinate norm, multiply it by √T to obtain each eⱼ. On the all-size complete geometry, log₂T, log₂S and q are O(p). Thus for fixed bounds on log₂(1+M)/p and log₂(1+X)/p a fixed larger multiple of p controls the amplification, both incoming radii and any prescribed O(p)-bit absolute target. An arbitrary small requested error can instead be priced by the given bounds without an O(p) assertion.

The original rectangle visits its frequencies in physical order once mixed coordinates are skipped. The mixed-radix counter in the code keeps the number of currently invalid digits. Each digit update changes at most two comparisons. A carry crossing k places occurs at most T/2^k times, including the last wrap. All complete membership scans take O(T) digit updates and O(d) initialization. On a sequential counter tape the same sum charges the outward and return movements, with an O(maxⱼ log tⱼ) bit cost per visited digit. The controller stores a constant number of full fields and a backup of T−S words. The multiplier stream, its length and norm checks, both zeroing operations, the backup write and read and the final multiplication by S are all charged. An array-backed finite host also copies its input and result. Each copy fits one additional O(Tq) scan.

For q-bit fraction, magnitude and gain widths, let A(q) be the charged scalar integer multiplication time. Beyond two complete tensor transforms and their one-time private setup, all operations take O(T A(q)+Tq+T maxⱼlog tⱼ) bit operations and O(Tq+∑log tⱼ) backing. An external cost to generate an arbitrary supplied table is additional. The parity mask below is generated by two coordinate counters within the displayed bound. On the composite outer geometry a Karatsuba choice gives A(q)=O(q^log₂3). With q=O(p) and complete bit volume Θ(Tp), the additional bit-time factor is O(p^(log₂3−1)); it is below the stated grouped Fourier factor. A different geometry uses its actual T and its own charged Fourier time. A general table can have S unrelated entries, and their reading cost is present.

## A projection with increasing separation rank

A zero-one frequency mask gives a self-adjoint idempotent of rank equal to its number of ones, since E is unitary. On two axes, the mask that retains exactly equal parity is nonseparable. Its restriction to frequencies zero and one on each axis has determinant one. The diagonal-equality mask on the same architecture has separation rank min(r₀,r₁), as witnessed by its identity submatrix. Therefore the filter can act on families of masks whose separation rank grows without bound, at one streamed multiplication between the same two transform calls.

The parity fixture takes 3×5 original coordinates inside 4×8. Its eight retained modes and seven rejected modes give a rank-eight projection on the 15-dimensional original signal space. A separate geometric identity checks every physical numerical result. For an odd r, let ω=exp(2πi/r). Then

    Kᵣ(a) = (1/r)∑ₖ(−1)^k ω^(ak) = 2/[r(1+ω^a)].

The parity equality mask is (1+(−1)^(k+j))/2. Its output at (i,l) is therefore half the input at that point plus half the sum of x(s,t)K₃(i−s)K₅(l−t). The replay uses this expression and fresh certified unit roots at 145 bits. Since |1+ω^a|≥2/r and r≤5, approximating ω^a by a unit root within δ=2^(−145) changes Kᵣ(a) by at most rδ. The true kernels have magnitude at most one. The product of approximated kernels differs by less than 15δ. The 15 inputs have magnitude below three, so the reference error is below 2^(−135) per coordinate. For each of two independently seeded fields, all computed original records agree with this reference to 2^(−44) and a second application agrees to the same threshold. All 17 dirty records return bit-exactly each time. The idempotence of the ideal operator follows from the Fourier proof. The finite threshold concerns rounded execution.
