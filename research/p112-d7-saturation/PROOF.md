# A D7 saturation obstruction for `2,4,5^9`

Let `K` have characteristic zero. For the pinned 112 E8 root lines, write

```
C = {c_h : c_h(t)=<r_t,h>, h in K^8},
w(q) = rank_F2 {ell(t) : q(t) != 0}.
```

The labels are a leading one followed by the eight simple-root coordinates
modulo two. Common scaling of the roots changes no supports.

**Theorem.** Eleven independent pieces spanning `T`, with
`dim(T intersect C)>=5`, cannot have weights bounded by `2,4,5^9`.
This excludes one actual TWOSIDE `(b,t)=(5,6)` profile in
DreamingOfClouds's PR3154. It does not settle the whole cell or full C5.

The new finite ingredient is sharp: inside any 70-coordinate D7 codeword
support, at most three pairwise disjoint supports can each have twelve
coordinates and binary rank at most five. The hypothetical pieces force five.

## Root geometry and its field of definition

The replay reconstructs 120 root lines and removes the eight pinned mutually
orthogonal frame lines. Its doubled roots have squared norm eight. The live
Gram sum is `112 I`, so `h -> c_h` is injective over `K`.

We use the standard real Weyl-chamber theorem. A real direction can be moved
to a closed chamber, where its perpendicular roots are generated by the
vanishing simple roots. The replay checks the simple system, all 960 simple
reflections, and these finite counts:

- Every subsystem on six simple nodes has at most 36 root lines.
- The eight seven-node subsystems have `42,28,22,14,16,23,37,63` lines.
- The ray with 63 zeros is a root direction. Its live support is 53 or 56.
- The ray with 42 zeros is the D7 ray, represented here by the last
  coordinate axis. Every other non-root seven-node face has at most 37 zeros.

Consequently the code has minimum support 53. A codeword of support at most
70 is either a scalar root codeword or a scalar D7 codeword. In the second
case it has exactly 70 nonzeros: all 42 perpendicular root lines are live,
and all eight removed frame lines are nonperpendicular.

Here and below the real assertions extend to `K` by rational linear algebra.
The rational roots perpendicular to a field-valued direction span rational
rank at most seven. If that span had rank at most six, a generic real normal
to it would have exactly those roots perpendicular. The six-node bound would
give at most 36 zeros, whereas support at most 70 requires at least 42 live
zeros. Thus the span has rank seven. Its one-dimensional rational normal
space supplies the same projective root or D7 classification over `K`.

A two-dimensional subspace of `C` has common support at least 76. Its common
perpendicular rational roots span rank at most six, since their normal
space contains two independent `K`-directions. A generic real normal again
bounds their number by 36. The common support therefore has at least
`112-36=76` coordinates. This conservative bound suffices; no claim that it
is sharp is needed.

The executable checks the finite root facts. The Weyl-chamber theorem and
the generic-normal argument are written mathematical dependencies, not a
proof-kernel formalization.

## Select five pieces with a controlled common support

Put `D=T intersect C` and `Q=T/D`. Then `dim Q<=6`. Choose four pieces `B`
of weights `2,4,5,5`. Their support union has size at most
`2+8+12+12=34`. Their quotient images are independent, since a dependence
would be a nonzero codeword supported on fewer than 53 coordinates.

There are seven remaining pieces, all of weight at most five. Their images
in `Q/span(B)` are nonzero: a zero image would give a nonzero codeword on
at most `34+12=46<53` coordinates.

No proportionality class of those seven images has three members. Otherwise
the four original pieces and three such members span a seven-dimensional
space with quotient image dimension five. Its intersection with `D` has
dimension two and common support at most `34+36=70<76`, a contradiction.

Thus `Q/span(B)` has dimension two, and the seven images partition into
classes of size at most two. An odd number of points in such classes has
a singleton. Adjoin its original piece to `B`, obtaining five pieces `A`.
Their images are independent and their span contains none of the other six
piece images. The union `S` of their supports satisfies

```
|S| <= 2+8+12+12+12 = 46.
```

This extends PR3174's four-piece contraction beyond its support-32 gate.
The older gate is insufficient here because `34+36=70` is not below 70.
The common two-dimensional support bound supplies the missing implication.

## Six endpoints and the all-root branch

Call the six other pieces `q_0,...,q_5`. In the one-dimensional quotient
`Q/span(A)`, rescale them to have equal nonzero images. Supports do not change.
There are combinations `a_i` of the five selected pieces with

```
c_0=0,    c_i=q_i-q_0-a_i in D, i=1,...,5.
```

The five `c_i` are independent by their distinct coefficients on the original
nonbasis pieces. Every edge `c_i-c_j` is nonzero and has support at most
`46+12+12=70`. The preceding classification gives root or D7 directions.

Suppose all fifteen edges were root directions. Three root lines in a plane
form A2 whenever there is a third line. The replay checks all 7,140 root-line
pairs and every possible third root by exact Gram determinants. In normalized
squared-norm-two roots, this makes the five independent directions
`h_i=z alpha_i` for one nonzero `z`, with `<alpha_i,alpha_j>=1`.
It is the affine A5 root simplex of PR3164/PR3174. The rational plane fact
extends to `K` by solving an invertible rational two-coordinate minor.

At a live root coordinate, evaluate the six vertices. All pairwise differences
are in `{-2,-1,0,1,2}`. If a difference has magnitude two, equality in
Cauchy-Schwarz makes this coordinate its edge root. The six values then have
multiplicities `1,1,4`, with nine unequal pairs. Otherwise all values occupy
at most two consecutive integer levels, with at most `3*3=9` unequal pairs.
A common nonzero scalar changes no equality classes over `K`.

Each root edge has support at least 53. Inside `S`, each coordinate lies in
at most nine edge supports. Outside `S`, an unequal pair of endpoint values
has a nonzero endpoint, and each nonzero endpoint meets at most five pairs.
The retained PR3164 incidence argument therefore gives

```
795 = 15*53 <= 9*|S| + 5*sum_i |supp(q_i)| <= 9*46+5*72 = 774.
```

The contradiction has margin 21. No new root-star census or stronger
support-spectrum estimate is needed for this branch.

## A D7 edge forces saturated disjoint blocks

There is therefore a D7 edge `c_i-c_j` with support exactly 70. Its support
is contained in the union of the five selected supports and the two endpoint
supports. Their capacity sum is exactly

```
2+8+12+12+12+12+12 = 70.
```

Equality throughout forces all seven supports to attain those sizes and
to be pairwise disjoint. In particular, five disjoint twelve-coordinate
supports, each of binary rank at most five, lie inside that D7 support.
This conclusion uses support containment, so possible coefficients and
cancellations cannot evade the equality argument.

## Sharp D7 packing bound and complete finite coverage

Move the D7 direction by a Weyl transformation to `h=(0,0,0,0,0,0,0,1)`.
Its full 120-coordinate support has size 78. The transformed frame consists
of eight mutually orthogonal root lines inside that support.

Enumerating every such frame gives 240. Reflections in the 42 perpendicular
D7 root lines have one orbit on these frames. The replay constructs the
entire permutation action and checks it against the full frame inventory.
One representative therefore suffices.

Weyl transformations preserve binary support ranks. Root reflections have
an integral linear Cartan action on simple-root coordinates, and preserve
the leading-one coordinate. Changing a root representative's sign has no
effect modulo two. The replay checks the induced linear action on all
120 labels for every one of the 42 generating reflections. No arbitrary
permutation of root labels is assumed.

Enumerate all 3,309,747 five-dimensional linear spaces in `F2^9` by the unique
reverse row-echelon bases of their four-dimensional annihilators. The census
checks the full120 support capacity of twelve, not merely the punctured
capacity. This distinction matters: a saturated twelve-point live support
cannot have additional frame roots in the same flat. Lower-rank supports
extend to five-flats; thus every saturated support is the full intersection
with one of the enumerated flats.

There are 5,040 distinct saturated intersections inside the full D7 support.
Exactly 756 avoid the representative frame. Join two of these 756 sets when
they are disjoint. The graph has 16,464 edges and 30,016 triangles. It has
no four-clique: for every edge, the verifier enumerates its larger common
neighbors and checks that no two are adjacent. A separate accumulated-union
enumerator reproduces every packing through size three and finds none of
size four. An explicit triangle is retained in `RESULT.json`, so the sharp
maximum is three.

Five are required by saturation. At most three exist. This contradiction
proves the theorem.

## Binding to the original residue and limits

The profile `2,4,5^9` has raw excess 29, strictly below 30, and survives the
original TWOSIDE feasibility and BK/BK7 rules in the 77-profile `(5,6)`
inventory. The replay runs the unchanged PR3154 filter and separately
reconstructs that inventory. Every six-piece subset has support-cap sum
at least 58, so the old strict-below-53 support-only quotient-basis
certificate does not apply. PR3174's three explicit shapes differ.

This is one scoped exclusion and a sharp finite support-packing lemma.
It is not a claim that the other 76 original profiles remain open: several
are handled by other packages. The complete original decoder reduction,
the other C5 cells, `C(P112)>=64`, physical suppliers and a better kappa
remain separate obligations. The selected frontier is unchanged.
