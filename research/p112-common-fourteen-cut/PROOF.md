# One common fourteen-line cut

## Statement

Let `r_t`, for `t=0,...,111`, be the doubled E8 roots outside the eight-line orthogonal frame, in the source order. Let `ell(t)` be their nine-bit labels. The label's low bit is one; its other bits are the simple-root coefficients modulo two. Write

```
Lambda(V) = {t : ell(t) belongs to V},
Off(h) = {t : <r_t,h> != 0}.
```

For binary spaces `V1,V2` of dimension at most six and ONE set `P` of at most fourteen live lines, define

```
H(V1,V2,P) = {h : rank_F2 ell(Off(h) \ (Lambda(V1) union Lambda(V2) union P)) <= 6}.
```

**Finite theorem.** Over every characteristic-zero field, the span of `H(V1,V2,P)` has dimension at most five.

The result is an upper bound, not a sharpness claim. The common-set quantifier is essential. Separate deletion sets for individual directions are not substituted anywhere in the census.

## Complete direction list

The complete binary-flat enumeration gives at most twenty live labels in any six-dimensional label space. Consequently every qualifying nonzero direction satisfies

```
|Off(h)| <= 20 + 20 + 14 + 20 = 74.
```

At least 38 of the 112 live root lines are perpendicular to it. Use the standard real Weyl-chamber theorem: after a Weyl transformation, the perpendicular roots are the subsystem generated by the simple roots with zero pairing. The replay checks the simple coordinates and all eight simple-reflection actions. A subsystem generated by at most six simple roots has at most 36 root lines. The eight seven-node parabolic counts are

```
42,28,22,14,16,23,37,63.
```

Thus only the D7 and E7 normal rays can occur. Their complete Weyl orbits have 1,080 and 120 projective directions. Of these 1,200 directions, exactly 1,144 have live support at most 74. Their support histogram is

```
53:112, 56:8, 70:128, 73:896.
```

All are rational projective directions. In particular, testing only root directions would omit 1,024 possible directions and would not prove this theorem.

The same completeness argument applies over any characteristic-zero field `K`. Let `Z` be the rational span of the rational roots perpendicular to a `K`-valued direction. Its rank is at most seven. If its rank were at most six, a generic real normal to `Z` would have exactly the same perpendicular roots, contradicting the 36-line bound. Hence `Z` has rank seven and its normal is a rational line. The original direction is a scalar multiple of that rational normal. Rational ranks of the resulting direction list are unchanged by scalar extension.

The Weyl-chamber theorem and this field-extension argument are written mathematical dependencies, not proof-kernel formalizations. The geometry implementation is adapted from Nick Petalas's PR3162, not presented as code-disjoint verification of it.

## Binary spaces and symmetry

Every space of dimension at most six extends to a six-space. Enlarging either fixed cover cannot remove an admissible direction. It suffices to consider all six-spaces in `F2^9`.

Reverse reduced row echelon bases enumerate all 788,035 rank-three annihilators. A separate enumeration by successive least coset representatives independently recovers the identical complete mask inventory. Their kernels give 776,806 distinct live masks, INCLUDING the empty mask. The twelve pinned label-linear permutations have 160 orbits on these masks. The empty mask is checked explicitly, rather than discarded using a smaller deletion budget's cardinality argument.

The replay checks that each generator is a binary-linear map on labels, permutes all 1,144 support masks, and lifts to a rational orthogonal transformation of the root vectors through its signed Gram matrix. Thus it preserves characteristic-zero ranks, arbitrary shared sets `P` and binary residual ranks. One first-flat representative per orbit and all second flats therefore cover every pair.

A further safe reduction samples at most 32 Schreier loops that fix the first live mask. Each loop is a composition of the twelve checked generators and their inverses, obtained from two paths reaching the same mask. Every retained loop is checked to fix the first mask. Sampling stops after at most 512 processed walks and 4,096 stored walks; completeness of this optional stabilizer sample is not assumed.

A second mask is omitted if one of these fixing maps sends it to a smaller second mask. Each orbit under the sampled maps has a minimum that survives this test. Closure of the fixing maps need not be computed. This reduction does not assume independent deletions or change the shared-cut budget.

## Exact cuts

Let `S` be a residual label set of rank `r`. If `r<=6`, the empty cut suffices. Otherwise let `k=r-6`, so `k` is one, two or three. An ambient character `c` gives a binary word

```
w_c = {t in S : <c,ell(t)> = 1}.
```

A deletion set leaves rank at most six exactly when it contains the support union of `k` linearly independent nonzero character words. In one direction, extend annihilators of the retained span to the ambient label space. Conversely, the common kernel of `k` independent forms has dimension `r-k=6` inside the span of `S`.

Deduplicating equal character words quotients out characters vanishing on `S`, so it preserves independence. Two distinct nonzero binary words are independent. Three distinct nonzero words are independent exactly when the third is not the XOR of the first two. Every word in a union of size at most fourteen has size at most fourteen. These facts justify the finite one-, two- and three-character loops.

The first oracle asks whether ANY fourteen-line cut works. If all separately admissible directions already span at most five, so do those admitted by any common cut. Otherwise `minimal_cuts` constructs every inclusion-minimal feasible cut for every separately admissible direction. Removing a cut that contains another feasible cut loses no common-set solution.

## Shared unions and exact rank

For a pair requiring the common-set test, start with state `{empty}`. For each direction's cut family, keep existing states and add all unions of an existing state with ONE cut from that family whose total size is at most fourteen. Deduplicate after each family.

Suppose a common set `P` admits six independent directions. Each has a minimal cut contained in `P`. The union of these six cuts is a state generated by the procedure, has size at most fourteen, and admits those same six directions. Thus it is enough to test the generated states; enumerating all arbitrary fourteen-subsets is unnecessary.

For each state, collect every direction with a cut contained in that state and test its rational rank. The native replay uses fraction-free elimination with arbitrary-precision integers and a fail-closed bound on GCD steps. A rank of six is a failure, not a tolerance comparison. Capacity exhaustion, malformed input, timeout, missing output rows and a mismatching receipt also fail.

`RESULT.json` records every first-flat orbit, symmetry reduction and oracle/state counts. Both complete 80-orbit ranges must pass. No row count or prefix is accepted as the complete 160-orbit result. Native controls include all minimal rank-seven/eight/nine basis cuts, an impossible repeated-basis cut, separate eight-line cuts whose common union costs sixteen, and independent/dependent rational rows. Python supplies separate XOR-span controls, 96 native/Fraction rank comparisons and a direct short-lattice reconstruction of all 1,144 normals.

## Rechecking the quotient-basis cover

The native replay also recomputes PR3185's precise premise: for every one of the 120 root directions, two six-flats cover at most 38 of its live support lines. For each direction, retain the distinct flat/support intersections of size at least eighteen. A discarded intersection has size at most seventeen and any other flat has size at most twenty, so their union cannot exceed 37. The retained families have sizes 320 and 336; every unordered pair is checked and the computed maximum is exactly 38.

A nonzero codeword supported inside two six-flats plus fourteen lines would have support at most 54. The complete direction classification above then forces a root direction. Such a direction has support 53 or 56 and leaves at least fifteen lines outside the two flats, contradiction. Thus this application rechecks the finite root-cover premise rather than silently relying on an unrun predecessor census. This is dependency validation of PR3185, not a second claim of novelty for its cover theorem.

## Excluding the actual profile

Retain the original TWOSIDE hypotheses from DreamingOfClouds's PR3154. Let eleven independent pieces span `T`, and let `C` be the P112 root code. The actual `(b,t)=(6,5)` profile is

```
2,3,4,6,6,6,6,6,6,6,6.
```

Its original necessary filters are replayed; this is not a claim that all 174 original profiles still remain open. The code intersection has dimension at least six, so `T/(T intersect C)` has dimension at most five.

Choose the five pieces of weights `2,3,4,6,6`. The first three have support capacities `2,4,8`, hence a common support union of size at most fourteen. The other two lie in two six-dimensional label flats. PR3185's sharp fifteen-line supplement theorem implies that this union supports no nonzero codeword. Therefore these five pieces have independent quotient images. They are a quotient basis and `dim(T intersect C)=6`.

For each of the other six pieces, subtract its unique quotient-basis combination. The six resulting codewords are independent because each has its own nonbasis coordinate in the original independent piece basis. Outside the two six-flats and the SAME common fourteen-line set, each resulting codeword is supported on the corresponding weight-six piece. Its residual labels have rank at most six. The finite theorem bounds these six independent directions by five, a contradiction.

This excludes the named historical profile. The application reuses PR3185's cover argument with its finite premise rechecked above, and inherits PR3154's original reduction. This package does not independently reprove that entire reduction. It does not prove full C5, `C(P112)>=64`, a physical supplier, a multiplication theorem or a new kappa.
