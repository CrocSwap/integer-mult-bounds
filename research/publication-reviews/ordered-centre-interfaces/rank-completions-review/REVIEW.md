## Pure-cycle proof

The lower bound is valid at tensor width at least three. At each axis,
frequency choices 100 and 111 give opposite directional-C eigenvalues
but equal uniform-translation eigenvalues. The choices are independent.
The actual ten-owner orbit has one common orthonormal basis, so the
same sign tests apply to every permitted field and owner operation.

For a product of operation matrices, the difference of two tested
products is a sum with one changed factor in each term. Rank cannot
increase under multiplication. Rank subadditivity therefore bounds the
output difference by the sum of the factor differences. Input embeddings
and output projections preserve this inequality. Thus zero allocation,
owned CLEAR, noninvertible scalar operations, and arbitrary borrowed
columns do not create a gap in the argument.

Each complete one-field coordinate child contributes rank one exactly
at the axes it uses. The required full-transform block on N independent
productive inputs changes by rank N at all 73 axes. Summing gives 73N.
The ten-owner multiplicity gives `2*m*112*73` per cover copy, or 65,408
for four pairs. This requires independent productive data at every
owner. It does not apply to an unpriced correlated cache or a repeated
data restriction.

The native frame and atomic-child premises are material. A rank-one
non-coordinate odd direction can change under three sign tests. A
single-copy translation also fails invariance. General cross-port
mixing, edits inside child wrappers, and nonlinear finite-grid maps are
outside the proof. The approximate statement is only for linear circuits
with the stated operator error at both members of each sign-test pair.
The cost consequence uses positive ranks `1<=r<=73` and its stated
recursive cost model. It does not price a full supplier.

## Rank-nine contraction and cost

The contraction keeps the source adapter, stage route, exit translation,
inverse-triple owner map, and separate scalar minus sign. The inverse-line
translation cancels the transported old terminal translation in the
stated order. The remaining nine directions are orthonormal on every
actual path. The equality of signed Pauli action fixes each unitary
operator up to a scalar; its exact nonzero state image fixes that scalar.
The equality therefore holds on arbitrary inputs. Tensorization applies
after cancellation of the common scalar sign; one sign is then restored
on the full field.

The checker verifies that no later local event reads or writes a deferred
Y field and that the final scalar gauge uses only X. The signed exchange
works on arbitrary incomplete-frame arrays. The output-port permutation
and invertible owner maps give a source bijection. This justifies deferral
without an extra bank copy. It does not price the packed address map.

The sharp lower bound is for the complete target in its fixed physical
charts. For the upper-right binary Pauli block B, composition gives
`B12=A1*B2+B1*D2`. Its rank is at most the sum of the two B ranks.
Monomial Clifford factors have B=0, and a native rank-k child has B rank
k. The checked target has B rank nine. Both the old 7+2 word and the new
rank-nine word attain this bound. Cross-field scalar mixing and changed
targets are excluded.

The complete profile changes from 1,456 to 1,392 children and keeps rank
mass 5,600. The 64 new children use 128 monomial wrappers. The 64 complex
minus signs retain the prior price of 128 real primitives. The signed
bank exchange remains, and no owned or private field is added. At equal
field volume, the native moment decreases for `0<sigma<1`. Under the
inherited D25 normalization, rank mass is exactly `224*25` and every
child rank is below 25. The normalized native moment is therefore still
above one for every positive saving exponent. The source makes no
complete map, precision, cover, or recursive-hosting price claim.
