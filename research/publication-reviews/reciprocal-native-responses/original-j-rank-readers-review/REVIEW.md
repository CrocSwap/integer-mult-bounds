## Original-J rank theorem

The theorem fixes the actual matrices K and R with RK=14I8 and the
family J=I-KR, E=J+KMR, G=J+KNR. It measures the ranks of the two
specified correction queries MR and NR, which equal rank M and rank N
because R is onto.

Direct expansion, in the stated order, gives

    EG-I = K [12I-13(M+N)+14MN] R.

K is injective and R is onto. Thus EG=I is equivalent to the vanishing
of the bracket. The same condition is

    (14M-13I)(14N-13I)=I.

For square matrices over Q, a right inverse is also a left inverse.
This proves the reversed identity and MN=NM without an assumption that
M and N commute.

For dyadic M and N, reduction modulo 13 gives MN=I over F13. Both
matrices have rank eight over Q. The correction-rank sum is therefore
16, and M=N=I attains it. This example also makes E=G=I. It prevents
the theorem from being used as a lower bound on every way to implement
the full responses.

Over Q, the equation

    12I = 13M+(13I-14M)N

gives rank M+rank N at least eight. In an equality case, v in ker N
implies Mv=(12/13)v. The rank equality then gives ker N=im M. The
reversed equation gives ker M=im N. These two spaces form a direct
sum. Hence every equality case is

    M=(12/13)P, N=(12/13)(I-P), P^2=P.

Conversely, each such projection gives a reciprocal pair. Orthogonality
is not required. The proof of the one-sided 13-integral restriction is
also valid: if M is 13-integral, 13M-12I is invertible, so the reciprocal
equation forces N to have full rank. A four/four split therefore needs
a factor 13 in both coefficient sets.

The independent finite check reconstructs K and R from the clean
payload, tests all 256 coordinate projections, checks nine full
112-column examples in both orders, and rejects three false variants.
The general bound and equality classification follow from the algebra
above, not from this enumeration.
