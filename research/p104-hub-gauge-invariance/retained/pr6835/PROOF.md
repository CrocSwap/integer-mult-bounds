# Relay-hub normal form, wall bound, scheduling invariance and the pair-exchange law: statements and proofs

No κ is claimed. Sections 1 to 5 are linear algebra and frame bookkeeping; section 6 lists the exact replays on the
literal word of #6824 (`retained/pr6824/WORD.json.gz`, payload SHA-256 `d3ccfce7…2821`, the digest published in #6824).

## 0. Setting

A word acts on public registers `V` (772 on the literal word) and private registers. Every register has a frame, a
subspace of `F_2^9`. Events: `ADD(d,s,c)`, `COPY(d,s,c)`, `SCALE(d,c)` act on registers that are at rest at one common
frame (the event's frame); `MOVE` changes the frame of one register; `INIT`/`CLEAR` open and close a private lifetime
(rows start and end at 0). **Legal words** (the checks of `hubmodel.check_legal`): a public `MOVE` goes to a strictly larger
frame, a private `MOVE` to a strictly smaller one, co-rest for `ADD`/`COPY`, final frames as declared. The endpoint is
`A = I + E` with `E` supported on rows `Y` and columns `X` (here `E(Y_r,X_r) = 1` for the 104 ports `q_r`),
`init(X_r) = <q_r>`, `final(Y_r) = q_r^⊥`, `init(Y_r) = {0}`, `final(X_r) = F_2^9`.
The invoice is a function of the histogram of helper ranks (`dim final − dim init` of non-main public registers) and of
`MOVE` ranks (`|dim change|`), as in #6831 section 0 and #6345; nothing below changes those histograms unless said.

Notation. `K` = registers with `init = {0}`, `R` = registers with `final = F_2^9`, `J = K∖R`, `I = R∖K`,
`H = K∩R`, `M = V∖(K∪R)` (called `Hnn`; it is non-empty on the literal word, 4 registers), `K̄ = V∖K`, `R̄ = V∖R`.
`E` is supported on `J × I` (true for the main registers: `Y ⊂ J`, `X ⊂ I`), so `E² = 0` and `A^{-1} = I − E`.
`D = Z[1/2]`.

## 1. The wall

Public registers only climb and co-rest is required for `ADD`/`COPY`, so a descent-free public path from input `i` to output `j` needs
`init(i) ⊆ final(j)`; with `init(X_r) = <q_r>`, `final(Y_s) = q_s^⊥` and odd `q_r` this gives `P = 0` on all 5824 odd pairs (incl. the diagonal) and the
E8 consequences. That statement and its replay are **#6831 section 6.1** and are not repeated; Proposition 2.1 below contains the same argument for a general up-set.

## 2. Crossing bound for up-sets (extension of #6831 section 6)

Let `U` be an up-set of subspaces (closed under inclusion upwards), `I_U = {i : init(i) ∈ U}`, `J_U = {j : final(j) ∉ U}`
and `c(U)` the number of private `MOVE`s from a frame in `U` to a frame outside `U`.

**Proposition 2.1 [proved].** In a legal word with endpoint `A`, `rank_Q A[J_U, I_U] ≤ c(U)`.

*Proof.* Replace only the rows of the `c(U)` crossing private moves by symbols `z_k`. Induction over events: a public input
`i ∈ I_U` occurs in the row of a register only if that register's frame is in `U`: initially `r = i` at `init(i) ∈ U`;
`ADD`/`COPY` copy rows between registers at one frame; a public move climbs (stays in `U`); a private move that does not
cross keeps a frame in `U` in `U`; a crossing move replaces the row by a symbol; `INIT`/`CLEAR` empty it. At the end a row of
a register `j ∈ J_U` sits at `final(j) ∉ U`, so it has no input of `I_U`. The endpoint is `A = P' + Z W` with `P'` the
coefficients of public inputs, `Z` (`V × c(U)`) the coefficients of the symbols and `W` the expansion of each symbol's source row
over the public inputs; `P'[J_U, I_U] = 0`, hence `A[J_U,I_U] = Z[J_U,:] W[:,I_U]` has rank at most `c(U)`. ∎

**Corollary 2.2 [proved; ω checked].** Let `S` be a set of ports that are pairwise odd (`<q_r,q_s> = 1`, `q_r` odd so every
port is odd with itself) and `U_S` the up-set generated by the frames `<q_r>`, `r ∈ S`. Then `rank A[J_U,I_U] = |S|`, so a
legal word has at least `|S|` private moves crossing `U_S`; in particular at least `ω` private descents, `ω` the clique number of the
odd graph on the ports.
*Proof.* `A[J_U,I_U]` has no entries `A[j,j]` (`init(j) ∈ U` forces `final(j) ∈ U`); its entries are `E(Y_r,X_r)` with
`<q_r> ∈ U_S` i.e. `r ∈ S`, and `final(Y_r) = q_r^⊥ ∉ U_S` i.e. `q_r ∉ q_s^⊥` for all `s ∈ S`, which holds for `r ∈ S`.
So the block is a partial identity of rank `|S|`. ∎
Literal word [checked]: `ω = 8`, there are 759 maximum odd cliques (the 759 orthoframes inside the ports), and **every one of
them is crossed by exactly 8 of the 24 private descents**: the bound is attained. (#6831 shows the descents form 8 chains of mass 7; the
count above is independent of that analysis.)

## 3. Hub normal form

Let `T ∈ GL_n(Q)` be the public map of a word with its two hub parts removed (section 3.4). For `G0` supported on `K`
(`G0 e_v = e_v` for `v ∉ K`, and `G0 e_k ∈ span e_K`) and `G9` supported on `R` the word `G0` at the zero frame, then `T`,
then `G9` at the full frame computes `G9 T G0`.

**Theorem 3.1 [proved].** Let `A = I + E` with `supp E ⊆ J × I`. There are invertible `G0` on `K` and `G9` on `R` over `Q`
with `G9 T G0 = A` **if and only if**

* **C1** `T[R̄,K̄] = A[R̄,K̄]`,
* **C2** `T[R,M] = 0`,
* **C2'** `T[M,K] = 0`,
* **C4** `rank T[R,K] = |H|` (given `T` invertible and C2', `≥` is automatic, so `≤ |H|` suffices).

Then `T^{-1}[K̄,R̄] = A^{-1}[K̄,R̄]` (**C3**); in particular `T^{-1}[K̄,J] = 0`. C1 and C3 alone do not suffice when
`M ≠ ∅` (Remark 3.6).

*Proof.* Write `D = G0^{-1} = diag(D_K, I)` and `L = G9^{-1} = diag(L_R, I)` (first block on `K`, `R`). The equation is `T = L A D`.
Entries of `A`: `A[R̄,K̄]` as in C1; `A[R̄,K] = [I_J | 0]` on rows `J` (rows `M` zero); `A[R,K̄] = [I_I | 0]` on rows `I` (columns `M` zero);
`A[R,K] = ` identity on `H` (all because `E` lives on `J×I`).
*Necessity.* Block by block: `(R̄,K̄)`: `T[R̄,K̄] = A[R̄,K̄]`, C1. `(R,M)`: `T[R,M] = L_R A[R,M] = 0`, C2. `(M,K)`: `T[M,K] = A[M,:] D[:,K] = D[M,K] = 0`, C2'.
`(R,K)`: `T[R,K] = L_R A[R,K] D_K`, of rank `|H|`, C4. C3: `T^{-1} = D^{-1}A^{-1}L^{-1}`; rows `K̄` of `D^{-1}` are unit rows and
columns `R̄` of `L^{-1}` are unit columns.
*Sufficiency.* Put `D1 = T[J,K]`. By C4 write `T[R,K] = L2 D2` with `L2` of size `R×H` and `D2` of size `H×K`. Let `D_K` have rows
`D1` (on `J`) and `D2` (on `H`), and `L_R = [T[R,I] | L2]`. The four blocks above hold by C1, C2, C2' and by construction
(`(R̄,K)`: rows `J` give `D1 = D_K[J,:]`, rows `M` give C2'; `(R,K̄)`: columns `I` give `L_R[:,I] = T[R,I]`, columns `M` give C2; `(R,K)`: `L2 D2`).
`D_K` is invertible: if `D_K x = 0` then `T[J,K]x = 0`, `T[R,K]x = L2 D2 x = 0`, `T[M,K]x = 0`, so `T x = 0` and `x = 0`.
`L_R` is invertible: if `T[R,I]v1 + L2 v2 = 0`, choose `w` with `D_K w = (−E v1 ; v2)` (on `J`, `H`) and let `u = (v1 on I, w on K, 0 on M)`;
rows `R` of `T u` vanish, rows `J` give `A[J,I]v1 + D1 w = E v1 − E v1 = 0` (C1), rows `M` give `T[M,I]v1 + 0 = A[M,I]v1 = 0`. So `T u = 0`, `u = 0`, `v2 = D2 w = 0`.
Put `G0 = diag(D_K^{-1}, I)`, `G9 = diag(L_R^{-1}, I)`. For C4 with `≥`: `|K| = rank T[:,K] ≤ |J| + rank T[R,K] + rank T[M,K]`, and `|K| = |J| + |H|`. ∎

**Theorem 3.2 (no dyadic obstruction) [proved].** If `T ∈ GL_n(D)` satisfies C1, C2, C2', C4, then `G0` and `G9` can be chosen in `GL(D)`.
Conversely, if dyadic `G0`, `G9` exist then `T = G9^{-1} A G0^{-1} ∈ GL_n(D)`. Hence: a dyadic gauge exists iff the conditions of
Theorem 3.1 hold and `T ∈ GL_n(D)`.

*Proof.* `T^{-1}[K̄,J] = 0` (Theorem 3.1, C3) gives `N = T^{-1}[K,J]` over `D` with `D1 N = I_J` (rows `J` of `T T^{-1}`) and
`T[R,K] N = 0` (rows `R` of `T T^{-1}`, `R∩J = ∅`). So `D^K = N D^J ⊕ ker D1`, and `ker D1` is a direct summand, free of rank
`|H|` over the principal ideal domain `D`. Let `Γ = [N | M]` with the columns of `M` a basis of `ker D1`; `Γ ∈ GL(D)`. Rows `J` of `Γ^{-1}` are `D1`
(because `D1Γ = [I|0]`); let `D2` be the other rows. Then `T[R,K] = (T[R,K]M) D2`, so `L2 = T[R,K] M` has entries in `D`, `D_K = Γ^{-1}`,
and `det T = det L_R · det D_K` (`det A = 1`) shows `det L_R` is a unit of `D`, so `L_R ∈ GL(D)`. ∎
**Explicit completion used in the replay.** Columns of `N` are reduced by column operations with dyadic unit pivots (greedy, sparsest column first):
`N U = N'` with `N'` equal to a permutation matrix on the pivot rows `P`, `U ∈ GL(D)`. For `C = K∖P`, matched to `H`, set `g_c = e_c − N D1 e_c`; then
`Γ = [N | g_C]` has `det Γ = det N_P` (a unit) and `Γ^{-1}` has rows `D1` on `J` and `e_c − Σ_{j∈J} N'[c,j] e_{p_j}` on `H`.
`G9 = A D T^{-1}` is then block-diagonal on `R` (it equals `L^{-1}`). The replay checks every one of these identities exactly. A unit pivot was
found at every step; for other `T` the greedy rule may fail even though Theorem 3.2 guarantees a completion (not constructed here).

**3.3 Realisation [proved].** `D` is Euclidean, so every `G ∈ GL_k(D)` is a product of dyadic elementary matrices and a diagonal matrix of
units (`±2^m`). An `ADD`/`SCALE` sequence of those factors, all at the zero frame on registers of `K` (which rest at `{0}` initially), realises `G0`; at the full
frame on registers of `R` (which rest at the full frame finally) it realises `G9`. A `MOVE` is not affected: if `W = pre · mid · post` is the word with
`pre` and `post` replaced by the new gauges, its `MOVE` events, per-register frame chains, co-rest conditions and helper/paid histograms are those of the original.

**3.4 The two hub parts of a word [proved/checked].** Call `post` the events at the full frame on public registers or never-moving full-frame private lifetimes
that no later event outside `post` touches, and `pre` the events at the zero frame on public registers that no earlier event outside `pre` touches. Moving
such an event past an event outside the set never crosses a shared register, so `pre + mid + post` has the per-register event sequences of the
word, hence the same legality and endpoint. `pre` acts on `K` (a register at rest at `{0}` has `init = {0}`), `post` on `R`.
Literal word [checked]: 5766 `pre`, 5500 `post`, 7356 `mid` events; the 14 never-moving full-frame private lifetimes lie entirely in `post`.

**Corollary 3.5 (feed-forward) [proved from 3.1 and 3.2].** Assume `H = V∖(X∪Y)` consists of registers with `init = {0}`, `final = full` (`J = Y`, `I = X`, `M = ∅`),
and the mid map has: rows `X` equal to unit rows; columns `Y` zero outside `Y`; `T[Y,Y] = I`; `T[Y,X] = E`. Then `T ∈ GL_n(D)` iff `T[H,H] ∈ GL(D)`, and in that case a dyadic gauge
exists (C1 is `T[Y,X] = E`, C2 and C2' are empty, `T[R,K] = diag(0, T[H,H])` has rank `|H|`). Without private registers and with unit scalars `T[H,H]` is automatically in
`GL(D)`. Hypotheses that matter: all helpers span-9, no mid event writes `X`, no mid event reads `Y`. The literal word is **not** of this form
(`H0n`, `Hn9`, `Hnn` non-empty, mid events that write `X` or read `Y`); it needs Theorem 3.1 as stated.

**Remark 3.6 (the two-condition form is false).** Registers `(a,b)`, `a ∈ M`, `b ∈ H`, `A = I`, `T = [[1,0],[1,1]]`: C1 and C3 hold, C2 fails, and
`G9 T G0 = [[1,0],[g9, g9 g0]] ≠ I` for every nonzero `g0, g9`. Also `T = [3]` on one register of `H` satisfies C1 to C4 over `Q` and has the gauge `G0 = 1`, `G9 = 1/3`, which is not dyadic.

## 4. Scheduling invariance

Let `≺` be the partial order generated by `e ≺ e'` when `e` precedes `e'` in the word and they share a register (`MOVE`, `INIT`, `CLEAR` included).

**Proposition 4.1 [proved; checked on random extensions].** For every linear extension `W'` of `≺`: `W'` is legal; every register has the
same event sequence, hence the same chain of frames; the row of every register after each of its events is unchanged; the endpoint is unchanged;
the multiset of `MOVE`s and the helper and paid histograms (hence the whole invoice) are unchanged. What a schedule can change is the number of
simultaneously live private registers (not part of the invoice) and the position of events.
*Proof.* Events with disjoint register sets act on disjoint coordinates and frames evolve register by register, so adjacent events with disjoint
register sets commute; any two linear extensions are connected by such swaps. ∎
Literal word [checked]: 10 random linear extensions (86,111 adjacent positions out of the word's order in total) are legal with identical endpoint and
histograms; their private peak ranges from 14 (the word) to 29. Scheduling is therefore no lever for a fixed network; a change of price needs
a change of the per-register event or frame sequences. Control: moving an `ADD` of a register before the `MOVE` that brings it to that frame is not a
linear extension and fails co-rest.

## 5. Pair-exchange law

**Proposition 5.1 [proved; checked on the literal word].** In a legal word let `e1`, `e2` be events at one frame `G` (for instance the two `ADD`s that
exchange the contents of registers `u`, `v`). Suppose the value written by `e1` reaches the final value of a public register `s1`, and that written by `e2` reaches `s2`, along
dependency paths that contain **no private `MOVE`**. Then `G ⊆ final(s1) ∩ final(s2)`; and, for public `u`, `v`, every earlier frame of `u` and `v` lies in `G` (public frames only climb).
In particular if `final(s1) ≠ final(s2)` are 8-dimensional, `G` has dimension at most 7.
*Proof.* Along a descent-free path the frame never shrinks (each hop is an event at the common frame of two registers, a register's frame only climbs between
hops, a private register that is not moved keeps its frame), and the last event writes `s` at a frame inside `final(s)`. ∎
Literal word [checked]: for each of the 15,072 `ADD`/`COPY`/`SCALE` events and each of the 645,102 (event, sink) pairs reached without a private descent, `frame(e) ⊆ final(s)`.
**The descent-free hypothesis is needed**: if paths may pass through a private move, 1,559 events violate the inclusion. Instance: registers 679 and 686 exchange at frame 1011
(events 12007, 12008: `ADD 679 ← 686`, `ADD 686 ← −½·679`); the output of the first reaches the sink 164 (final frame 175), that of the second the sink 218
(final frame 226); `dim 175 = dim 226 = 8`, `175 ∩ 226` has dimension 7 and equals frame 1011 exactly; the earlier frames 3, 1049, 881, 888 of the two inputs lie in it.
Scope: the law concerns an exchange realised in place at one frame and flows without private descents. It does not say what an alternative (copying the input contents
to both sinks, or routing one output through a private descent) costs, and no price is claimed.

## 7. Scope

Everything is stated for legal words of the model in section 0 and checked on one word. The rewritten word is a statement about the freedom of the hub gauge,
not a replacement word: it has more events than the original, and the native chart checks and the five framework assumptions of #6824 are not re-run
(its `MOVE` events and chart pairs are identical). Not covered: the greedy unit-pivot rule on other `T`, hub gauges that are not supported on `K` and `R`
(for example a gauge at an intermediate frame), words whose endpoint is not `I + E` with `E` on `J × I`, and any priced change.
