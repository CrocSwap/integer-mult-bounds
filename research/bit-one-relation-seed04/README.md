# A paid-rank saving needs two frame relations, at any register count

**Scoped negative result; no new kappa.** In the abelian rational-address bit
model, adding scalar registers cannot save paid rank when the paid frames have
at most one independent rational relation. This includes any number of extra
registers restored on arbitrary dirty inputs. The result extends the
at-most-one-relation argument in Ali Romman's [PR468](https://github.com/CrocSwap/integer-mult-bounds/pull/468),
section 4 of the [pinned predecessor](../bit-three-register-lower-bound/README.md),
from three registers to arbitrary register counts. Targets need not be
invertible or triangular. It does **not** extend PR468's full nine-call theorem
to arbitrary frame-relation spaces or arbitrary register counts.

For example, four rational rank-four targets require at least 16 unit
rank-one calls in this class. A proposed 15-call mechanism therefore needs
at least two independent frame relations, even if it adds restored dirty
registers and treats scalar mixing as free. This is a necessary condition,
not evidence that such a mechanism exists. The certificate includes an
attaining 16-call example with four different invertible permutation targets.

Base: `cd91ac8e2652bfee6bf09da3b8724253bcf78f5f`. OS assignment seed **4**,
bit-supplier track; four scalar registers are the principal finite test case.
The base selects PR1094's conditional kappa `0.001672660197076918`, with PR589
as its bit supplier and the complex side binding. Neither supplier is changed.

## Model and theorem

Work over the group algebra of the additive group of rational `m x m`
matrices, with coefficients in F2. The symbol `[A]` satisfies
`[A][B] = [A+B]`. Rational addresses are **not** reduced modulo two.
There are `W >= 1` scalar registers and `k` paid factors

$$
D_{N_j}([A_j])=I+([A_j]-1)N_j,\qquad
N_j=x_jy_j^T,\quad x_j,y_j\in\mathbb F_2^W,\quad y_j^Tx_j=1,
$$

where every `A_j` is a nonzero rational rank-one matrix. Each factor costs
one paid call. The desired equality is an equality of **all formal
coefficients**:

$$
\prod_{j=1}^k D_{N_j}([A_j])
 =\operatorname{diag}([C_1],\ldots,[C_W]).                 \tag{1}
$$

An extra dirty register is restored by setting its target `C_w=0`, so its
diagonal entry is the identity `[0]`. Arbitrary free invertible scalar basis
changes are already included by permitting every rank-one idempotent `N_j`.
To see the normalization, move the scalar maps to one end, conjugating each
coordinate projector; augmentation `[A] -> 1` makes their total product the
identity. A final output permutation can be undone by relabeling outputs.
Free address transformations between calls are outside this model.

**Theorem.** If

$$
\dim_{\mathbb Q}\operatorname{span}\{A_1,\ldots,A_k\}\ge k-1,
$$

then there are pairwise disjoint subsets `I_w` of the paid-factor indices
such that `C_w = sum_{j in I_w} A_j`. Consequently

$$
\sum_{w=1}^W\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Q}C_w\le k.       \tag{2}
$$

Some indices may be unused because a block cancels. No bound on rational
numerators, denominators, word length or register count is assumed in the
proof. The finite checks below support specific algebraic steps; they do not
replace this universal argument.

## Proof

**1. Reduce the scalar patterns.** The case `k=0` is immediate. For `k>0`, let
`H` be the group generated by the paid frames. It is a finitely generated
torsion-free abelian group, so `F2[H]` is a Laurent polynomial domain.
Every `C_w` in (1) belongs to `H`, because it appears in the product's support.
Introduce independent variables and the multilinear matrix polynomial

$$
P(t)=\prod_j(I+(t_j-1)N_j).
$$

If the frames are independent, their subset sums are distinct. Since (1)
is diagonal, all off-diagonal coefficients of `P` vanish. Differentiating
at `t=(1,...,1)` shows that each `N_j` is diagonal, hence a coordinate
projector. Its frame belongs to that coordinate's disjoint sum.

Otherwise the integer relation lattice is generated by a primitive vector
`r`. If some `|r_j|>1`, subset sums are still distinct: a collision would be
a nonzero integer multiple of `r` with all entries in `{-1,0,1}`. The same
coefficient and derivative argument applies.

It remains to consider `r_j in {-1,0,1}`. Write `S={j:r_j!=0}` and
`g=t^(r_+) - t^(r_-)`. The kernel of the Laurent substitution
`t_j -> [A_j]` is `(g)`: a primitive integer vector extends to an integer
basis, in which the relation is one variable minus one. Each off-diagonal
entry of `P` is therefore `g` times a Laurent polynomial. Differentiation
at one shows that the off-diagonal part of `N_j` is a common binary pattern
`M` for `j in S`, and zero otherwise. This follows from `g(1)=0` and
`partial_j g(1)=r_j mod 2`. Outside `S`, every projector is a coordinate
projector. If `M=0`, all projectors are coordinate projectors and we are done.

**2. Classify a shared nonzero pattern.** For a binary rank-one idempotent
`N=xy^T`, set `X=supp(x)`, `Y=supp(y)` and `J=X intersect Y`. The number
`|J|` is odd. Its off-diagonal directed graph has the edges `i -> j` with
`i in X`, `j in Y`, `i!=j`. Every vertex on a directed cycle lies in `J`.
If `|J|=1`, the graph is acyclic. If `|J|>=3`, its cycle vertices are exactly
`J`, because the graph on `J` is complete in both directions.

If `M` is acyclic, a topological coordinate order makes **all** the
projectors upper triangular. Each has exactly one diagonal one, since an
acyclic rank-one idempotent has `|J|=1`. The diagonal entry of each paid
factor is `[A_j]` at that coordinate and one at every other coordinate.
Taking product diagonals assigns every paid frame to exactly one target.

If `M` has a cycle, the graph recovers every diagonal entry from its cycle
vertices. Thus all projectors on `S` are one and the same matrix `N`.

**3. Cancel the cyclic block without overlooking interleaving.** Write
`U(z)=I+(z-1)N`; idempotence gives `U(z)U(w)=U(zw)`.
Because the sole relation is supported on `S`, the group splits as
`H_S direct-sum Z^(S-complement)`. Set all outside monomials to one in
(1). The product becomes `U([sum_{j in S} A_j])`. It is diagonal, and
`N` has a nonzero off-diagonal entry, so `sum_{j in S} A_j=0`.
Primitivity now forces `r=+/-1_S`.

Keep just one outside factor, on coordinate `a`, setting every other
outside monomial to one. Let `B` be the sum of the `S` frames preceding
that factor, `z=[B]`, and `s=[A_j]` for its own frame. The specialized word is

$$
U(z)(I+(s-1)E_{aa})U(z^{-1})
=I+(s-1)Q_a(z),\quad Q_a(z)=U(z)E_{aa}U(z^{-1}).          \tag{3}
$$

Here the specialized ring is `F2[H_S direct-sum Z]`, still a domain, and
`s-1` is nonzero. Diagonality of
(3) therefore requires `Q_a(z)` to be diagonal. Direct multiplication gives

$$
Q_a=E_{aa}+(z-1)NE_{aa}+(z^{-1}-1)E_{aa}N
 +(z-1)(z^{-1}-1)x_ay_aN.                                \tag{4}
$$

If `a in J`, choose `b in J` different from `a`; the `(a,b)` entry of (4)
is `1+z`. If `a in X minus Y`, choose `b in Y`; the `(a,b)` entry is
`1+z^-1`. If `a in Y minus X`, choose `b in X`; the `(b,a)` entry is
`1+z`. Thus every outside factor whose coordinate belongs to `X union Y`
forces `B=0`. The only zero-sum subsets of `S` are empty and all of `S`,
because its only relation is `1_S`. Such a factor must therefore lie
before all `S` factors or after all of them.

An outside factor on any other coordinate commutes with `N`. Move these
commuting factors past the `S` factors. The latter now form one block,
whose product is `U([sum_S A_j])=I`. The remaining coordinate projectors
assign disjoint frame sets to their target coordinates. This proves the
claimed decomposition. Rank subadditivity and `rank(A_j)=1` prove (2).

## Cost and scope

The bound already permits invertible scalar preparation and restoration for free,
all binary scalar basis changes, and arbitrarily many restored dirty
registers. Paid calls on those registers are included in `k`; they cannot
be omitted from the bill. Any additional nonnegative costs for scalar
work, copies, source access, frame changes, restoration, dispatch or
recursive implementation cannot turn this class into a saving in **paid
rank-one calls**. This is an optimistic obstruction, not a new physical
supplier or a wall-clock lower bound.

For sharpness, at `m=W=4` use the 16 independent frames
`A_(w,i)=E_(i,(i+w) mod 4)` with scalar projector `E_ww`. Target `C_w` is
the corresponding cyclic permutation matrix, of rank four. Every gate is
paid, no workspace is used, and `sum rank(C_w)=k=16`.

Two or more rational relations, nonabelian address multiplication, higher
scalar-rank paid calls, nonlinear operations and free address changes are
outside the theorem. It does not decide an unrestricted four-register
search. It does not alter the selected bit paid profile or any kappa.
No all-size compiler, mutable-source realization, hosting, native replay,
precision/tape, exact-recovery or analytic-transfer obligation is discharged.

The primary engine enumerates projectors as outer products, uses packed
binary coefficient matrices, and performs rational Gaussian elimination.
The separate engine exhausts all 66,066 binary matrices at sizes 1 through

## Open next questions

Can a four-register model with **two** frame relations defeat the disjoint
frame assignment? The first step is to classify two-dimensional spaces of
off-diagonal projector patterns on four coordinates and look for a
nontriangular pool. The blocker is a uniform rational relation argument,
not a larger random search. A candidate would still need its complete
source/frame/restoration and recursive bill before any supplier claim.

The graph and conjugation lemmas are also small targets for formalization.
The first step is to formalize the cyclic-pattern diagonal recovery for
binary outer products; arbitrary Laurent-group-algebra normalization is
the subsequent blocker. These are optional follow-ups, not completed work.
