# The November direct multiplier in the repository's κ convention

## Claim and supporting construction

OpenAI's *The logarithmic exponent of integer multiplication on tapes* (November 24, 2026) supplies a construction of one deterministic machine that multiplies exactly at every positive input length, visits linear space and takes worst-case time at most $n(1+\log n)^{1/2+o(1)}$. This is the uniform theorem, `thm:uniform-main`. The paper first builds fixed-exponent algorithms and then constructs one machine that schedules them. The same machine witnesses every fixed saving $0<\kappa<1/2$ below. [Theorem statement](source/intro.tex) · [Fixed-exponent construction](source/integers.tex) · [Uniform machine](source/uniformity.tex)

This is an actual multiplication upper-bound claim, obtained by specializing the cited construction to the repository's contract. Its algorithmic input is OpenAI's construction. The repository contribution is the explicit specialization, contract comparison, selected record and bounded executable checks. The [construction note](CONSTRUCTION.md) identifies a candidate with $j=19998$, target exponent $10001/20000$, compatible intermediate exponents and an admissible odd label prime. It traces the source results that supply the remaining templates, correctness, time and space bounds. Those templates and the complete transition table have not been generated here.

## Machine and output

The repository specifies two unsigned, most-significant-bit-first strings of equal length $n\ge1$, separated by a delimiter. The required output is the unique $2n$-bit encoding of their product, including leading zeros. The upstream specification loads those same strings, requires a halting trace on every pair, and requires the entire output tape to contain a string of length $2n$ with value equal to the product, with all other cells blank. These conditions determine the required string even on zero operands. The alphabet, state set, tape count and deterministic transition rule are fixed before input length is quantified. Each head reads and writes at its current cell and moves at most one position per step. The time function is the attained worst case across all input pairs at each positive length. Visited space counts distinct tape/address pairs throughout the computation and is bounded by $O(n)$. [Repository contract](../../upstream/build/sections/00-introduction.tex) · [Independent comparator specification](source/NearSquareRootMultiplication.lean)

The actual solution's `UniformWorstCaseSpec` and `haltingTimes` agree with the comparator after whitespace normalization. Its input, output, trace and step definitions were also compared at the pinned revision. This is a source-level contract review; it is not a comparator execution. [Actual solution specification](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/92b516ef8e2abf66c3db7aa31438d3010bc7956f/lean/OAI/Computability/NearSquareRoot/UniformWorstCase.lean) · [Tape semantics](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/92b516ef8e2abf66c3db7aa31438d3010bc7956f/lean/OAI/Computability/IntegerMultiplication/TMModel.lean)

## Quantifiers and the selected witness

Let $\kappa$ be any fixed real number with $0<\kappa<1/2$ and write $g=1/2-\kappa$. In the uniform bound let $\delta(n)$ denote the excess exponent tending to zero. For all sufficiently large $n$, $\delta(n)\le g/2$. Hence the log exponent is at most $1/2+g/2<1/2+g=1-\kappa$. Changing from $1+\log n$ to the repository's $\lg n=\max(1,\lceil\log_2 n\rceil)$ costs a constant factor for each fixed exponent. This also accounts for Lean's use of the natural logarithm. The finitely many smaller lengths have finite worst-case times, so enlarging the constant covers every $n\ge1$. Thus the same machine has time $O_\kappa(n(\lg n)^{1-\kappa})$ for every fixed κ in that interval.

The numeric record uses $\kappa=4999/10000$. Here $g=1/10000$, and the eventual exponent $10001/20000$ is strictly below the target $5001/10000$. The construction note also gives a fixed candidate with this stronger exponent. The theorem is an asymptotic existence statement: this submission computes neither a running-time constant nor a crossover length.

Every fixed value strictly between 0.4999 and 0.5 is already supported by the same uniform theorem. There is no largest supported witness. Changing the displayed number to 0.499999 would select another corollary, without a new algorithmic improvement. Constants and cutoffs may depend on κ; the result does not justify a prescribed input-dependent target $\kappa(n)\to1/2$ at a claimed rate.

## The endpoint and the conditional lower bound

The family has supremum $1/2$, and the paper leaves the exact $O(n\sqrt{\log n})$ endpoint open. To deduce the endpoint from the reported estimate, one would need $(1+\log n)^{\delta(n)}=O(1)$, equivalently an eventual upper bound on $\delta(n)\log(1+\log n)$. Convergence $\delta(n)\to0$ alone does not supply that bound. For example, with $L=1+\log n$ and sufficiently large $n$, the excess $\delta(n)=\log\log L/\log L$ tends to zero while $L^{\delta(n)}=\log L$ is unbounded. A different construction could establish the endpoint without extracting a rate from this scheduler.

Under variable-count Weak ETH, `thm:lower` supplies the stronger statement that for every exact deterministic multiplier there are $c>0,n_0$ such that at every length $n\ge n_0$, some operand pair needs at least $c n\sqrt{\log n}$ steps. Thus it excludes every $o(n\sqrt{\log n})$ multiplier, including all fixed $\kappa>1/2$. It does not exclude attainment at $1/2$. Within the family of fixed logarithmic powers this leaves endpoint attainment open; the precise smaller-order overhead above the endpoint is also unresolved. [Upper and lower theorem statements](source/intro.tex)

Here variable-count Weak ETH rules out deterministic 3-SAT time $2^{O(v^{1-\eta})}L^{O(1)}$ for every fixed $0<\eta<1$, where $v$ is the number of variables and $L$ is the input length. This formulation is weaker than ordinary ETH; the paper explicitly allows a $2^{O(v/\log v)}$ algorithm. Only the lower bound uses this hypothesis. The selected upper theorem uses no complexity hypothesis, so its record has `assumptions: []`. That field describes the theorem's mathematical premises, not the completeness of this review.

## Closely related consequences

The same paper's `cor:near-root-applications` gives the corresponding single-machine, linear-space upper bounds for exact quotient and remainder and for floor integer square root. Its `cor:division-root-lower` gives the conditional $\Omega(n\sqrt{\log n})$ lower bounds even for quotient alone and floor square root, at every sufficiently large length. These are context, not additional claims of this certificate. Upstream explicitly lists those arithmetic corollaries as unformalized. [Paper corollaries](source/uniformity.tex) · [Formalization scope](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/92b516ef8e2abf66c3db7aa31438d3010bc7956f/lean/docs/379.md)

The repository's grouped assembly has scoped ceilings. Its enforced supplier range $a<(1-\beta)b<b<1/32$ and layer balance $\kappa<a/(1+a)$ give $\kappa<1/33$, and its retained Gaussian range independently gives $\kappa<1/4$. Larger supplier savings do not remove these caps. The direct construction is a different multiplication algorithm and is not subject to them.
