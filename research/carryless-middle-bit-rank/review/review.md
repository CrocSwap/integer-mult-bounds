The separated coefficient-digit width theorem, the raw product-bit rank formula, and the odd five-bit nonlinear witness pass review at their stated interfaces. The primitive-field extension attains the minimum possible coordinate total on each side for one odd-input product bit at every size \(m\ge3\). ## Separated pre-carry digit

For \(r=2^j\), the \(j\)-th bit of a nonnegative coefficient \(c\) is \(\binom cr\) modulo two. The \(z^r\) term of \((1+z)^c\) selects the contribution of the \(j\)-th digit of \(c\) under its characteristic-two factorization. Restricting the truth table at antidiagonal \(k\) to matching weight-\(r\) supports gives an identity matrix. On the whole truth table, the expansion into the same support monomials gives exact rank \(\binom{t_k}{r}\), where \(t_k\) is the antidiagonal length.

Each output of a joint linear decoder sums rectangular Hankel bilinear forms. Independently of the operand-local preprocessing, even when each channel chooses a different shorter side, the contribution rank is at most the channel's shorter width. Double differencing at the zero operands reduces a joint affine decoder to the same factorization on centered encoded coordinates because the target vanishes on both anchored axes. It also cancels additive one-operand terms. The middle antidiagonal therefore forces shorter widths summing to at least \(\binom{\min(m,n)}r\).

The reflected subset construction assigns each antidiagonal term its unique left translation, shape and right translation. Reflection reverses the successive gaps of the right support. The degree shift by the span of the shape places the term in the original antidiagonal. Every normalized shape of span \(d\) has exactly \(\min(m,n)-d\) translations in the shorter operand. Pooling the truth table across outputs and fixing opposite supports gives an independent monomial for each eligible subset of either operand. This proves individual optimal left and right totals as well as the shorter-width optimum. Empty operands, \(r>\min(m,n)\), and edge antidiagonals with \(t_k<r\) have zero required contributions.

## Rank of the raw product bit

In the contracted residue algebra, the sign element \(s=[-1]+1\) has square zero. For each valuation \(i<m-1\), the sign pair complements the top bit, so \(L(t^is)=1\) and \(L(t^isu^a)=0\) for \(a>0\). Multiplying the latter vectors by arbitrary basis monomials keeps them in the radical. The remaining exceptional vector \(t^{m-1}+t^{m-2}s\) also lies in the radical because its terms pair identically with \(1\) and vanish against the other basis vectors. The sign block has rank \(2(m-1)\) from its invertible antitriangular off-diagonal block. Its inverse leaves the pairing on positive powers of \(u\) unchanged under elimination, since these powers couple to the first half of the block and the corresponding inverse block is zero.

For \(m\ge4\), consider the valuation-zero \(u\) block. Its greatest power on which \(L\) is nonzero is \(H=2^{m-3}\). The block on \(u^1,\ldots,u^{H-1}\) is invertible with rank \(H-1\). Its inverse vanishes at indices \(a,b\) with \(a+b<H\). Every cross vector from a positive valuation is supported in indices at most \(H/2-1\), so the second elimination also leaves the remaining pairing unchanged. Subtracting one from each positive valuation gives the cyclic block at size \(m-2\) after discarding coordinates that pair to zero. This gives \(R_m=R_{m-2}+2^{m-3}+3\) for \(m\ge4\), with \(R_2=2\) and \(R_3=4\). The closed form \(R_m=\lfloor 2^{m-1}/3\rfloor+\lfloor3m/2\rfloor-1\) follows, and \(R_1=1\) by direct inspection. The odd restriction has rank \(2^{m-3}+1\) for \(m\ge3\). The same low-bit statement holds on \(m\)-bit signed inputs via residue reduction modulo \(2^m\).

With a joint linear decoder, rank gives the minimum total coordinates \(2R_m\) on the full residue domain. A single rank-factorization channel attains it by reversing one encoder and reading the middle coefficient. On all residues, the zero anchors also cover affine decoding and additive one-operand terms. On odd residues, an affine decoder of product coefficients can add a global constant. Adding the all-ones matrix preserves target rank because column negation permutes the original matrix into its complement. If the odd-input decoder may also add arbitrary one-operand terms, the lower bound for that wider interface uses the mixed difference. Its rank at \(m=5\) is three, with a rank factorization of the mixed difference achieving three on each side once the side terms are supplied.

## Odd five-bit nonlinear witness

Among odd residues, the class \(e=t+4s\bmod8\) for \(x=(-1)^s5^t\bmod32\) gives eight distinct nonconstant target rows and columns. An arbitrary decoder of product coefficients needs eight distinct encoded vectors on each side. At width three one would be the all-zero vector, forcing a constant target row or column. The four-coordinate witnesses achieve this lower bound. The affine lower bound remains five on each side even with a global constant, so degree two is the minimum possible at width four.

Separate exhaustive evaluation reproduced all 256 ordinary product bits and 30 distinct channel returns. It also reproduced the direct bit encoders' agreement with the eight masks and the declared collisions. The two encoders use 3 AND and 8 XOR gates each. The forced constant term of each encoder leaves the channel with 9 variable-by-variable products and 9 XOR gates. The decoder shares \(z_1+z_5\), \(z_4+z_6\) and \(z_2+z_3\), using 3 AND gates and 10 XOR gates. The resulting circuit uses 18 AND and 35 XOR gates, with hardwired constants, free static wiring and fan-out, and all eight polynomial positions counted as width.

The independent AND-minimum check used the 30 reachable channel values. It formed all 128 affine functions on the six variable received wires. The first AND yields one of 2,732 distinct truth functions. For each such first gate, either factor of a second AND may use any affine function with or without the first gate added. Direct enumeration checked 86,717,952 unordered factor pairs against all admissible final affine combinations. This excludes every decoder with at most two ANDs for this encoding. The result concerns the fixed encoding and leaves gate optimality over alternate encoders and XOR schedules open.

## All-size odd-input extension and degree bounds

For \(m\ge3\), put \(q=2^{m-2}\) and \(w=m-1\). In the odd unit representation, product bit \(m-1\) is \(h(e_x+e_y\bmod q)\), with \(h(t)=\operatorname{bit}_{m-1}(5^t\bmod2^m)\) and \(e_x=t+(q/2)s\bmod q\). The translation by \(q/2\) complements \(h\). A smaller nontrivial period would generate a subgroup containing \(q/2\) since \(q\) is a power of two. Thus the matrix has exactly \(q\) distinct nonconstant rows and columns. The zero-vector argument requires at least \(w\) explicit coordinates per side at any decoder degree.

For a primitive \(\alpha\) in \(\mathbb F_{2^w}\), encode the coefficient vector of \(\alpha^{e_x}\) in the power basis of a fixed field modulus, and similarly encode \(y\). One ordinary carryless polynomial product followed by fixed linear reduction gives \(\alpha^{e_x+e_y}\). The field multiplicative order is \(2q-1\), so the exponent sum in \(0,\ldots,2q-2\) is unique given the reduced nonzero element. A direct table indexed by the reduced value gives the target, using \(2q-1\) reachable entries. The table is exponential in \(m\). Ordinary schoolbook work before reduction is at most \(w^2\) AND and \((w-1)^2\) XOR gates, and fixed linear reduction takes at most \(w(w-1)\) XOR gates by direct row evaluation. The reduced return depends only on the exponent sum. The raw unreduced product remains the channel output and can contain more information than the reduced value. An independent search for primitive moduli and replay covered 349,520 odd operand pairs across \(m=3,\ldots,10\).

For a degree-\(d\) decoder, subtract its constant term and expand in the supplied local coordinates. Products of received coefficients use at most \(d\) distinct input features on each side, and each monomial's support in the channel list is identical on both sides. Within a support block, the sum of per-channel maxima of the two monomial sizes must also be at most \(d\). This proves the stronger block bounds in the decoder-degree proof and the per-side consequence

\[
2^{m-3}+1\le \sum_{i=1}^{\min(d,W)}\binom Wi
\]

for each total side width \(W\). Constant degree requires exponential width, polynomial width requires degree \(\Omega(m/\log m)\), and linear width requires degree \(\Omega(m)\). At minimum side width \(w=m-1\), the sum exceeds \(2^{w-2}\), so the binomial lower tail gives \(d\ge w/2-O(\sqrt w)\). The field-based decoder has an ANF of degree at most \(w\) in the \(w\) reduced bits and hence also in the original coefficients by linearity of reduction. The decoder AND and depth bounds follow respectively from Boolean degree at most \(g+1\) with \(g\) AND gates and degree at most \(2^h\) at multiplicative depth \(h\). The separate charged-encoder lower bound follows from restricting one operand to \(2^m-1\) and computing the elementary full-domain output polynomials in the proof.

The minimum side widths and their sum are distinct from the sum of channel-wise shorter widths once arbitrary nonlinear decoding is allowed. With two channels of widths \((m-2,1)\) and \((1,m-2)\), hardwire the short side of each to one and route each combined class to the decoder. The shorter widths sum to two and each total side width is \(m-1\). A shorter-width sum of one would force a single channel with a one-bit side, which cannot represent \(q\ge2\) distinct nonconstant rows or columns. The primitive-field realization instead accomplishes the width optimum in a single channel and uses the joint reduced field product.
