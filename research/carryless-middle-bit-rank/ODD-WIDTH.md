# Optimal encoded width with nonlinear decoding on odd operands

Let $m\ge3$. Both inputs are odd residues modulo $2^m$. The required output is bit $m-1$ of their ordinary product. A fixed list of carryless products receives encodings that depend on just one operand each. The decoder sees the product coefficients and a possible fixed constant. No separate operand-dependent side information is supplied to the decoder.

**Theorem.** The least possible total number of left input coordinates is $m-1$, and the same lower bound holds on the right. One carryless product attains both minima simultaneously. With an affine decoder, each minimum is instead $2^{m-3}+1$. The matching construction for arbitrary decoding uses a table of exponential size.

## The statistic that both operands contribute

Put $q=2^{m-2}$ and $H=q/2$. The odd units are $x=(-1)^s5^t$ with $s\in\{0,1\}$ and $0\le t<q$. The induction $v_2(5^{2^j}-1)=j+2$ gives $5^H=1+2^{m-1}\pmod{2^m}$. Multiplying an odd residue by $5^H$ flips its highest bit. Negating an odd residue also flips that bit. Define
$$
 e_x=(t+Hs)\bmod q,\qquad h(t)=\operatorname{bit}_{m-1}(5^t\bmod 2^m).
$$
Then the output on $x,y$ is $h(e_x+e_y)$, with $h$ extended periodically. In particular $h(t+H)=1+h(t)$. Every nonzero subgroup of the cyclic group of order $q$ contains $H$. Thus $h$ has exact period $q$, and its translates give $q$ distinct nonconstant rows of the output table.

## Necessity and construction

At least $q$ distinct left encodings are needed to distinguish those rows. An all-zero left encoding makes every carryless product zero for every right input and would give a constant row. Thus $w_L$ left coordinates satisfy $2^{w_L}-1\ge q$, or $w_L\ge m-1$. The same argument applies to the right. It allows unequal channel widths. The affine result follows from the odd-by-odd rank in the [raw product-bit proof](raw/PROOF.md). Adding an intercept complements the entire matrix, which is the same matrix with its rows permuted by $x\mapsto-x$.

Take $w=m-1$. In the binary field $K=\mathbb F_{2^w}$, choose a polynomial basis and a primitive element $\alpha$. Encode $x$ as the $w$ coefficients of $\alpha^{e_x}$. Use the ordinary integer representative $0\le e_x<q$. The carryless polynomial product of the two encodings, reduced by the defining polynomial of $K$, is $\alpha^{e_x+e_y}$. The multiplicative group of $K$ has order $2q-1$ and is generated by $\alpha$, and the unreduced exponent sum lies between $0$ and $2q-2$, inclusive. Every such exponent is distinct in the group. Store $h(S\bmod q)$ at address $\alpha^S$ for each $0\le S\le2q-2$. Reading this address returns the desired bit. All $2q-1$ nonzero addresses occur. The reduced product depends only on the sum $e_x+e_y$, so it discards the individual exponents.

## Cost and scope

The explicit upper construction can tabulate the operand maps on the $2^{m-1}$ odd inputs and the decoder on $2^{m-1}-1$ reachable field elements. All tables, their construction and access must be paid for in a uniform algorithm. A schoolbook width-$w$ carryless convolution takes $w^2$ AND and $(w-1)^2$ XOR gates. Fixed field reduction is linear. This tabulated implementation costs exponential work or storage as $m$ grows.

If a decoder has Boolean degree $d$, expansion of its monomials limits the row span on a side with $w$ coordinates to the nonconstant squarefree monomials of degree at most $d$, after taking out its fixed intercept. Thus on odd inputs
$$
 \sum_{i=1}^{\min(d,w)}\binom wi\ \ge\ 2^{m-3}+1.
$$
At optimal width $w=m-1$, this forces $d\ge w/2-O(\sqrt w)$ by the binomial lower-tail bound. Conversely, any table on the $w$ reduced bits has a multilinear representation of degree at most $w$. At $m=5$ the [explicit circuit](nonlinear/PROOF.md) achieves width four using degree two, with every gate charged. The per-side width theorem applies to this single output on odd operands. It does not price a scalable nonlinear decoder or supply a complete integer multiplier.

## Two adjacent bits and the full odd residue

For $m\ge3$, requiring both bits $m-2$ and $m-1$ gives a different answer. The minimum total coordinate count is **$m$ per operand** in the channel-list model. The same minima suffice for the entire odd product modulo $2^m$.

The map $x\mapsto e_x$ above is a surjective group homomorphism with kernel $\{1,2^{m-1}-1\}$. The exact period of $h$ shows that two inputs have the same top-bit row precisely when their quotient is in this kernel. Put $a=2^{m-1}-1$. For two candidate rows $x$ and $ax$, choose $y=x^{-1}$ modulo $2^m$. Their products are then $1$ and $a$, which have different bits $m-2$. Thus the joint table has all $2^{m-1}$ odd rows distinct, and every row is nonconstant already on bit $m-1$. Excluding the zero code gives $2^{w_L}-1\ge2^{m-1}$, proving $w_L\ge m$. Symmetry gives $w_R\ge m$.

For the matching upper bound, each odd operand has $m-1$ variable binary digits. Use channels with widths $(m-1,1)$ and $(1,m-1)$. In each, hardwire the shorter side to one and place all variable digits of the other operand on the longer side. The decoder receives both operands' variable digits and may compute their ordinary product. Both the transmitted widths and the two hardwired positions are counted. This upper bound measures interface width with unrestricted decoding. Its decoder still performs the multiplication.

There is also a single-channel construction on $m+1$ coordinates per side in which the two encoded operands are genuinely multiplied. Put $q=2^{m-2}$ as before. Write $x=(-1)^{s_x}5^{t_x}$ and choose $0\le t_x<q$, $s_x\in\{0,1\}$. Encode $x$ by the field element $\alpha^{3t_x+s_x}$ with primitive $\alpha$ in $\mathbb F_{2^{m+1}}$. An unreduced binary polynomial product followed by fixed field reduction returns $\alpha^S$, where
$$
S=3(t_x+t_y)+s_x+s_y,\qquad 0\le S\le6q-4<8q-1.
$$
The field group has order $8q-1$, so the decoder table can distinguish all these exponents. As $s_x+s_y\in\{0,1,2\}$, the remainder modulo three recovers the sign sum, and the quotient recovers $t_x+t_y$. Store the odd product $(-1)^{S\bmod3}5^{\lfloor S/3\rfloor}\bmod 2^m$ at the corresponding field address. All $6q-3$ sums occur. The reduced return determines the two summed exponents, and generally does not determine either input. This produces the low $m$ bits of the ordinary product on odd inputs and has an exponential table. For one joint polynomial product, the two-bit and full odd-residue minimum therefore lies between $m$ and $m+1$ on equal-width inputs.
