For any fixed c, the algorithm below implements the normalized tensor of broad expansions and the normalized tensor of narrow compressions with maximum-coordinate error at most (1+R)2^(-cp) on actual inputs of maximum component modulus R<=1. All public and intermediate whole-field words have O(p) bits. The total dyadic normalization has O(d log p)=o(p) bits. The fixed-tape time is O(V p^(1-k)) and peak backing is O(V). The theorem uses no Gaussian or inverse service as a primitive. The simultaneous Hadamard, BIT and their support routing, the middle Fourier operation, CRT and exact outer product remain separate proof obligations.

The complete dyadic field is allocated before the first Hadamard call. In one lexicographic scan of the padded CRT output, copy exactly the tuples with every source coordinate j_i<r_i to a compact tape. In a second scan over the entire target rectangle, compute per axis j=max(0,ceil(r_i(2v-1)/(2t_i))). Membership is j<r_i and 2t_i j<r_i(2v+1). If every coordinate is selected, consume the next compact record. Otherwise emit zero. The product of increasing injections is increasing in lexicographic order. Thus this produces exactly tensor(E_i) on the true sources, including independently arbitrary source values. For the broad operator all axes and all intermediate groups stay on the full dyadic rectangle, and its output is that full rectangle. After all narrow H_i^-1, extend D_i diagonally by 1 on holes, then capture only the tuples with all q_i images valid and restore the conventional r-padded rectangle in an ordered scan. Operations on distinct axes commute, so globally moving the insertion/extraction across the other axes' factors preserves the operator. A partial non-dyadic spectator field would fail the native contract. There are always T records, with T/product(r_i)<=exp(d/p²)=O(1). The full rank-d predicate evaluates rational floors with O(d ell² polylog p) bit work per address and O(d ell)=O(p) control. Its full time is O(V p^(1-e) polylog p). This is a separate row from the O(V) forming scan at one balanced group.
