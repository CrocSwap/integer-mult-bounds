# Clean-helper call bound with algebraic target flags

**Result:** in the exact scalar-idempotent model, `t` invertible rational
`m`-dimensional targets and any number of clean helper registers require
at least `mt` paid calls when the paid rank-one frame span has dimension
at most `m+1` and the relative targets admit a common complete flag over
an algebraic extension of `Q`.

In particular, **every pair of invertible rational targets requires at
least `2m` calls** under this span condition. A common rational target
flag is unnecessary. The proof can use an extension of degree at most
two, even when `m > 2`. The bound also covers any number of targets whose
relatives commute, including invertible members of a matrix pencil.

This is a scoped mathematical extension of Nick Petalas's pending
[PR #6698](https://github.com/CrocSwap/integer-mult-bounds/pull/6698),
pinned at `e7b08ac9ef4ea5b5517d9d325ae6aeae445d3f91`. That predecessor
proves the clean-helper bound under a common **rational** flag and
explicitly leaves the irreducible-quadratic pair open. The present note
supplies the missing field argument and a family strictly outside that
hypothesis. It makes no new kappa or supplier-admission claim.

## Exact model and proof

For `n=t+r`, a paid call is

```
I_n + ([A]-1) x y^T
```

with `rank_Q(A)=1`, binary vectors `x,y`, and `y^T x=1`. The product is
an exact matrix identity over the binary group algebra of the additive
group of rational address matrices. Its output is
`diag([C_1],...,[C_t],1,...,1)`, where the `C_s` are invertible and every
helper exponent is zero. The helper condition holds on arbitrary
inputs. There are no interposed free address maps.

[PROOF.md](PROOF.md) contains the complete argument and source credit.
The critical new distinction is between the address field and the
coefficient field. We embed rational address matrices into matrices over
a finite number field `K`, while retaining `F_2` coefficients. The
additive group generated by any finite set of projected exponents is
finitely generated and torsion-free, hence free abelian. Its group
algebra is a Laurent polynomial domain, so the predecessor's fraction
field rank inequalities still apply. Algebraic multiplication does not
supply relations in the additive exponent group: `[i]^2=[2i]`, for
example.

The proof also states the rank-one-generated premise in the span
normalization explicitly. An invertible matrix can belong to a
one-dimensional matrix space; the relevant lower bound on dimension
uses a span generated by rank-one frames. The normalization leaves only
one potentially difficult two-dimensional block. An algebraic common
flag passes to this invariant subquotient, where the clean-helper
upper-entry cancellation count proves the missing `2t` contribution.
For two targets there is only one nontrivial relative matrix, so its
rational two-block has an eigenline over an extension of degree at most
two. Diagonalizability is not required.

## A sharp pair with no rational flag

Take

```
C = [[1,0],[0,-1]],   D = [[0,1],[1,0]].
```

The relative matrix `C^-1 D` has characteristic polynomial `z^2+1`, so
there is no rational invariant line. Four rational rank-one calls attain
the new bound: `E_11,-E_22` on the first data register and half the
all-ones matrix together with `-1/2 * [[1,-1],[-1,1]]` on the second.
Their common span is `Sym_2(Q)`, of dimension three. Helpers can remain
untouched. Explicit coordinates over `Q(i)` make both targets diagonal
and verify the cancellation used by the proof.

The written proof extends this to sharp examples for all `m>=2`,
`t>=2`, and `r>=0`: use invertible rational members of the same pencil
in the two-block, append nonzero diagonal coordinates, and decompose
each target into exactly `m` rank-one calls within a common span of
dimension at most `m+1`.

## Open next questions

A three-target family can fail to have a common algebraic flag even
inside this low-span model. For
`I_2`, `diag(1,2)`, and `[[2,1],[1,2]]`, the two nontrivial relatives
have an invertible commutator. A common invariant line would be killed
by that commutator, so no such line exists over any characteristic-zero
extension. These targets have a legal six-call word in the
three-dimensional rank-one-generated space `Sym_2(Q)`. This is a
rejection control for an overbroad flag claim, not a counterexample to
the conjectured lower bound.

Two follow-ups are recorded in [TASK.json](TASK.json):

1. Resolve whether such a genuinely nontriangularizable triple can use
   five calls with arbitrary clean helpers, or find a lower-bound
   invariant that does not require a common target line. The displayed
   six-call word and invertible-commutator obstruction give exact
   starting controls; a finite check alone would not settle all helper
   counts.
2. For the original broader register-model card, specify a genuinely
   different allowed scalar/address action and pay for restoration and
   every caller obligation. This theorem closes a loophole within the
   present model; it does not produce a cheaper multiplication gadget.

The original seed-13 card, narrowed scope and justification are retained
in `TASK.json`. Frame spans above `m+1`, dirty helper outputs,
interposed address maps and target families without a common algebraic
flag in the remaining two-block case are outside the theorem.
