# Nine calls for arbitrary invertible targets

**Theorem.** In the three-register abelian bit model of PR468, three arbitrary
invertible rational `3×3` target frames require at least nine nonzero rank-one
paid calls. Nine suffice. The targets need not be lower triangular, commute,
or admit a common rational flag.

This strengthens the target domain of Ali Romman's
[PR468](https://github.com/CrocSwap/integer-mult-bounds/pull/468). The new step is
to extract its dimension-independent low-corank argument, apply it to arbitrary
`2×2` target triples, and use that bound in the previously flagged span-four
case. Its remaining finite search is unchanged. This is a scoped
computer-assisted lower bound, with no new multiplication exponent κ and no
claim about additional registers or free address transformations between calls.

## 1. Model and proof plan

For the additive group of rational matrices, write `[A][B]=[A+B]`. Scalars are
in `F₂`, while frame entries and frame ranks are over `Q`. A paid factor is

\[
D_N([A])=I+([A]-1)N,\qquad
N=xy^T,\quad x,y\in\mathbb F_2^3,\quad y^Tx=1.
\]

The endpoint is `diag([C₁],[C₂],[C₃])`, with equality of every formal
group-algebra coefficient. Finite-address coincidence is insufficient.

For a word of length `k`, let `d` be the rational span dimension of its frames.
We call `k−d` its relation corank. The key lemma is stronger than a rank count:

**Disjoint-subset lemma.** Let the frame exponents belong to a torsion-free
abelian group. If their integral relation lattice has rank at most two, a
three-register word with diagonal monomial endpoint has pairwise disjoint
subsets `S₁,S₂,S₃ ⊆ {1,…,k}` such that

\[
C_w=\sum_{j\in S_w}A_j.
\tag{1}
\]

Some indices may be unused. In particular, for rational matrix frames,

\[
\sum_w\operatorname{rank}C_w
\le \sum_{j\in S_1\cup S_2\cup S_3}\operatorname{rank}A_j
\le \sum_j\operatorname{rank}A_j.
\tag{2}
\]

No address dimension, target invertibility, or target flag is required in
this lemma. We prove it before using the span-four argument, avoiding a
circular dependence. Sections 2–4 expand the low-corank mechanism of PR468
§§4–5 with its original numerical specialization removed.

## 2. Zero or one relation

Work in the subgroup generated by the finitely many exponents. It is free
abelian, so its group algebra is a Laurent polynomial domain. Before imposing
relations, put `P(t)=∏ⱼ(I+(tⱼ−1)Nⱼ)`. Its entries are multilinear.

If there are no relations, all subset sums differ. Every off-diagonal
coefficient of `P` vanishes; differentiation at `t=1` makes every `Nⱼ`
diagonal. Its one diagonal 1 assigns that factor to exactly one target.

If the relation lattice has rank one, let its primitive generator be `r`.
When some `|rⱼ|≥2`, subset sums still cannot collide and the same argument
applies. Otherwise `r` is a cube vector, with entries in `{−1,0,1}`. The
Laurent substitution kernel is generated by

\[
g=\prod_{r_j=1}t_j-\prod_{r_j=-1}t_j.
\]

Differentiating each off-diagonal entry, a multiple of `g`, shows that all
projectors on `S={j:rⱼ≠0}` have a common off-diagonal pattern. Outside `S`
they are coordinate projectors. For a binary rank-one idempotent `xyᵀ`, the
supports of `x,y` intersect in one or three coordinates. Intersection three
gives the all-ones matrix `J`; otherwise its off-diagonal graph has no directed
cycle. Unless the common pattern is that of `J`, one coordinate order makes
all projectors triangular. Product diagonals assign disjoint frame sums.

For the exceptional pattern, all projectors on `S` are `J`. Set outside
variables to one; they are free of the sole relation. Diagonality forces
`∑ⱼ∈S Aⱼ=0`, so the relation has all plus signs, up to overall sign. Keep
one outside factor `Eₖₖ`, with exponent `a`, and let `B` sum the preceding
`J` frames. Writing `z=[B]`, an off-diagonal entry away from row and column
`k` is `([a]−1)(z+z⁻¹)`. The outside exponent is a free generator. The
domain property and torsion-freeness force `B=0`. The only zero-sum subsets
of `S` are empty and full, so every outside factor precedes or follows all
`J` factors. The `J` block is the identity; the remaining coordinate factors
give (1).

## 3. The common-J lemma with at most two relations

Consider any-length words whose projectors lie in `{J,E₀₀,E₁₁,E₂₂}` and whose
endpoint has no off-diagonal entry except possibly `01`. Suppose the exponent
relation lattice has rank at most two. We show that deleting all `J` factors
leaves the same endpoint.

Let `yⱼ=tⱼ−1`, and let `L` be the sum of the variables belonging to `J`
factors. If a `J` factor occurs, `L` is a nonzero linear form in the universal
variables. The first-order off-diagonal condition says it vanishes after
passing to the generated exponent group `H`.

Here is the precise second-order justification. At augmentation,
`gr(F₂[H])=Sym(H/2H)`. Because the relation lattice is saturated, the kernel
on degree one has dimension at most two. Quotient the off-diagonal matrix
space by `span(J_off,E₀₁)`. In that quotient the **universal first-order
coefficient is identically zero**, before imposing relations. Thus nonlinear
terms in the substitution of Laurent variables cannot correct the degree-two
term. After eliminating `L`, any vanishing quadratic belongs to an ideal
generated by at most one further linear form.

Put `Kᵢ=offdiag(JEᵢᵢ+EᵢᵢJ)` and

\[
q_i=\sum_{j:N_j=E_{ii}}y_j\sum_{a<j:N_a=J}y_a.
\]

Since `K₀+K₁+K₂=0`, and `K₀,K₁` are independent modulo
`span(J_off,E₀₁)`, the two quadratic conditions are given below. Indeed,
mixed J/coordinate pairs reduce to `∑ᵢ Kᵢ qᵢ` modulo `L`; J/J pairs and
coordinate/coordinate pairs vanish in the matrix quotient.

\[
q_0+q_1=0,\qquad q_0+q_2=0.
\tag{3}
\]

Eliminate the last of the `h` J variables using `L=0`. The `h−1` proper
prefix sums `p₁,…,pₕ₋₁` are linearly independent. Each quadratic in (3) is
bilinear between J variables and coordinate-factor variables. A nonzero
bilinear polynomial with a linear divisor must be the product of one linear
form from each variable set: compare its components of bidegrees `(2,0)`
and `(0,2)` in the polynomial domain. Thus its coefficient matrix has rank
one. Its nonzero columns are proper prefix sums, so they must all be the
same prefix. Every coordinate factor contributing to that quadratic lies
in one internal gap between J factors.

If both quadratics are nonzero and an internal `E₀₀` occurs, its column in
both makes the gaps equal. If no such factor occurs, their coordinate-variable
supports are disjoint (`E₁₁` versus `E₂₂`). They cannot share a nonzero linear
factor in those variables, so their common divisor must be the same J-prefix
form. If only one quadratic is nonzero, it gives the single-gap statement;
if both vanish, there are no internal coordinate factors. This includes the
case `h=1`, with no proper internal prefix.

Strip the exterior invertible diagonal factors. Consecutive J factors combine,
leaving

\[
U(b)D U(c),\quad U(z)=I+(z-1)J,\quad
D=\operatorname{diag}(d_0,d_1,d_2),
\]

where all letters are group monomials; either J run may be empty. For `i≠j`
the entry is

\[
(b-1)d_j+(c-1)d_i+(b-1)(c-1)(d_0+d_1+d_2).
\tag{4}
\]

The required zeros at `10,20,02,12` imply
`(c−1)(d₁−d₂)=(c−1)(d₀−d₁)=0`. If `c=1`, any required off-diagonal zero
forces `b=1`. Otherwise the domain property gives `d₀=d₁=d₂=d`, and the
product becomes `d U(bc)`. A required zero gives `bc=1`, so the product is
exactly `D`. Restoring exterior factors proves the assertion, including the
vanishing of the possible `01` entry. Target exponents are the disjoint sums
assigned by the coordinate factors.

## 4. Finish the two-relation disjoint-subset lemma

Let `L ⊆ Zᵏ` be the saturated relation lattice of rank two. If cube relations
span at most one rational dimension, lift to `Zᵏ/L₀`, where `L₀` is their
saturated span. Every difference of two subset indicators is a cube vector,
so this lift preserves **every coefficient fiber appearing in the product**.
It preserves its diagonal monomial endpoint as well. Section 2 assigns
disjoint sums in the lifted group; map them back to the actual frames.
No rank-one property is required of the artificial lifted exponents.

Otherwise choose two independent cube relations `r,s`. If their reductions
modulo two are independent, an odd 2×2 minor is ±1, since cube minors have
absolute value at most two. This proves their lattice is saturated. If the
reductions coincide, replace them by `(r+s)/2,(r−s)/2`. These are integral
cube vectors with disjoint nonempty supports and a unit minor. In either
case they form a saturated cube-valued basis of `L`.

The first-order off-diagonal patterns therefore have the form

\[
\operatorname{offdiag}(N_j)
=(r_j\bmod2)B+(s_j\bmod2)C.
\tag{5}
\]

Pattern dimension zero means coordinate projectors. Dimension one is the
acyclic common-pattern case of section 2 or the common-J case of section 3.
Both give (1).

At pattern dimension two, `B,C` are independent. A zero pattern forces
`rⱼ=sⱼ=0` as integers, because the basis entries are cube-valued. Let `S`
index noncoordinate projectors and `T` the remaining indices. All relations
are supported on `S`, so `H=H_S ⊕ Zᵀ`.

There are 28 binary rank-one scalar idempotents, 19 nonzero off-diagonal
patterns, and 129 planes generated by two such patterns. The complete finite
classification has 123 noncoordinate pools with a common scalar flag. Its
six exceptional pools are the coordinate permutations of

\[
P=\begin{pmatrix}1&1&0\\0&0&0\\0&0&0\end{pmatrix},\quad
Q=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0\\0&1&0\\0&0&0\end{pmatrix},\quad
R=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\1&0&1\\1&0&1\end{pmatrix}.
\]

This is PR468's existing classification; the independent checker supplied
here enumerates outer products and all 168 binary bases, and returns an
explicit flag witness for each of the 123 pools. It does not infer the
classification from a sample of words.

For a common-flag pool, project to `H_S` and use that scalar flag basis. Every
factor is triangular with exactly one nonzero diagonal entry. Its product's
diagonal entries assign disjoint subsets of `S`. Characteristic-polynomial
factorization over the Laurent fraction field identifies these three
monomials, with multiplicity, with the original projected targets. Repeated
or identity target monomials cause no problem: choose a matching of the
multisets.

For an exceptional pool, first project to `H_S`, removing the `T` factors.
The involution
`S₀=I+E₀₁` sends `(P,Q,R)` to `(E₀₀,E₁₁,J)`. Conjugating a diagonal endpoint
introduces only a possible `01` entry. Section 3 therefore gives the same
disjoint assignment. Coordinate permutations cover the other five pools.

Finally, project onto each free generator indexed by `T`. Its sole surviving
factor is a coordinate projector, assigning that generator to exactly one
original target. Combining these assignments with the matched `S` subsets
proves (1) and the rank budget (2).

## 5. Six calls for arbitrary invertible 2×2 targets

Suppose a three-register word with `k≤5` nonzero rank-one `2×2` frames has
three invertible targets. If its frame span has dimension at most two,
PR468's low-span lemma gives `k≥2·3=6`. That lemma requires invertibility,
not triangularity: after left/right changes of basis, a two-dimensional
rank-one-generated space containing an invertible matrix is diagonal, and
each independent line needs three calls.

If its span has dimension at least three, then `k−d≤2`. The disjoint-subset
lemma gives `6=∑ rank(Cw)≤k`, again impossible. Six calls suffice by taking
the two column frames of each target on its coordinate scalar register.
This corollary is for **three scalar registers**, not arbitrary register count.

## 6. The new span-four argument in address dimension three

Suppose eight rank-one frames span a space of dimension at most four and
produce three arbitrary invertible `3×3` targets. Spans at most three are
already covered by the low-span lemma. At dimension four, select three
rank-one basis matrices with a nonzero mixed determinant coefficient. Such
a triple exists because an invertible target lies in the span, and repeated
rank-one summands contribute zero to the determinant expansion. Their left
vectors and right vectors are each independent. In suitable common rational
row and column coordinates, the span is

\[
\{D+tuv^T:D\text{ diagonal},\ t\in\mathbb Q\}.
\tag{6}
\]

If all coordinates of `u,v` are nonzero, the minor on rows `i,k` and columns
`i,l`, for distinct `i,k,l`, is `Dᵢᵢ t uₖ vₗ`. A rank-one element with
`t≠0` must therefore have `D=0`. Paid frames lie on the four independent
rank-one lines. Projecting onto each line's coefficient shows that its call
count is at least the number of targets with nonzero coefficient there.
Each invertible target needs at least three such coefficients, so the total
call count is at least nine.

Otherwise `u` or `v` has a zero coordinate. A directed cycle in the support
graph of `uvᵀ` uses only `supp(u)∩supp(v)`, which has at most two vertices.
The strongly connected components give a common block upper-triangular form
with address block sizes `1+1+1` or `1+2`. Each target has invertible diagonal
blocks.

A rank-one block upper-triangular matrix `xyᵀ` has at most one nonzero
diagonal block. If blocks `a<b` were both nonzero, then `x_b≠0` and `y_a≠0`,
making the strictly lower block `x_b y_aᵀ` nonzero. Thus calls can be assigned
disjointly to their nonzero diagonal block. Projecting onto a diagonal block
is an additive group map; nonzero projected frames remain rank one, while
zero projections make that factor the identity.

Each one-dimensional address block needs three calls by scalar target rank.
Each two-dimensional block needs six by section 5. Either partition needs
nine calls. No ratios of targets or common target flag were used. We do not
extend PR468's old arbitrary-register-count span-four lemma beyond its stated
flag assumption.

## 7. Complete nine-call result and strict enlargement of scope

For eight calls, spans at least six have relation corank at most two and
are excluded by (2), since total target rank is nine. Section 6 handles spans
at most four. Span five is exactly the unchanged finite case of PR468:

| Inherited obligation | Why arbitrary targets are permitted |
| --- | --- |
| Scalar irreducibility | Equal target monomials define coordinate groups; one-register rank and the two-register bound use only invertibility. |
| Row and column distance at least three | A span of two rank-one frames contains no invertible `3×3` target. |
| Cube-relation lift and dimension-three flag filter | The disjoint-subset argument and characteristic-polynomial matching do not require target triangularity. |
| Complete scalar catalogue and relation branching | Depend on binary projectors, subset collisions, saturated relations and the preceding filters. |
| Terminal rejection | A target in the span of two paid rank-one frames has rank at most two, regardless of its coordinates. |

The inherited two exact engines cover 2,276 families and 21,317,520 orders.
Every one of their 415,398 diagonal terminals has a rank-at-most-two target;
there are zero survivors. Neither the catalogue nor the engines are changed.
Fewer than eight calls are excluded by repeatedly splitting
`D_N([A])=D_N([A/2])D_N([A/2])`. Nine calls suffice for any invertible targets
by `Cw=∑ᵢ(Cw eᵢ)eᵢᵀ`, using scalar projector `Eww` for each of its three
nonzero column frames.

The removed hypothesis is substantive. Let

\[
C_1=I,\qquad C_2=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&2\\1&0&0\\0&1&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad C_3=C_2^2.
\]

The characteristic polynomial of `C₂` is `x³−2`, irreducible over `Q` by
Eisenstein at 2. If common invertible left/right maps made all three targets
lower triangular, the ratio of the first two transformed targets would
make `C₂` rationally similar to a lower-triangular matrix. Its characteristic
polynomial would split over `Q`, a contradiction. The checker verifies these
exact matrices and a nine-call formal construction for this target triple.
