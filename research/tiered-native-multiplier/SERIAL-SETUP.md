# Serial arithmetic in fixed installation

With the fixed indexed-action and width bounds of the quadratic installer, the complete two-tier setup takes fewer than $2^{228}$ individual tape reads, writes and moves. This includes the original $2^{32}$ multiplier for parsing, hashing, initialization and cleanup. The improved price comes from the arithmetic implementation and a sharper sum of the existing charges. It does not give an elapsed-time ratio. [Predecessor fixed census](https://github.com/aliromman/integer-mult-bounds/blob/cc21f7040cb3376709b30fc42f289827836d8be5/research/quadratic-native-installation/COST.md)

Store a magnitude as a delimited binary word, most significant digit first. Zero is the one digit `0`. Store a sign separately. Use separate work tapes for the operands, output, quotient, alignment and temporary sums. In the reference program a forward or backward word iterator is one sequential traversal. Reverse emission uses a separate tape. Removing leading zeros, concatenation, and making a copy with the last digit omitted each use one or two sequential passes. The last operation uses one symbol of lookahead. A temporary tape is rewound and cleared. The comparison of equal-length digit strings reads both tapes from the same end. The reference uses this representation for all unbounded arithmetic. Its only primitive division, `divmod(digit, 2)`, acts on a carry and two one-bit digits. [Serial operations](serial_setup.py)

**Division.** Let the two positive magnitudes have bit lengths $a\ge b$. Scan their inputs together for the first $b$ digits. For each of the $a-b$ remaining dividend digits, write a zero on a separate tape. Append these zeros to a copy of the divisor. This aligns its high digit with the high digit of the dividend. The dividend is initially less than twice the aligned divisor since both have $a$ digits. Emit one quotient digit per alignment. Compare the entire remainder to this divisor, then subtract it if the result is nonnegative. Shift a copy of the divisor one position to the right for the next iteration. After a comparison the remainder is below the old aligned divisor. Before the next comparison it is below twice the new aligned divisor. The last comparison uses the original divisor. This proves the quotient and remainder identity with remainder strictly smaller than the divisor. A shorter dividend returns quotient zero and restores a copy of the dividend. A zero divisor is rejected.

Let $h$ bound the two input lengths. The setup, copies and final truncation need fewer than 16 whole-word passes. One quotient digit needs fewer than 16 passes over words of at most $h+4$ tape symbols. This includes comparison, two rewinds, subtraction, the backward and forward output copies, shifting the divisor, resetting both argument heads, advancing the unary loop and clearing or reusing the bounded scratch. Moving to the opposite end before a reverse scan counts as a pass. In one such pass allow 64 transitions per position. The local state reads at most four current symbols, writes at most three and moves at most four heads per symbol. End tests, delimiters, the sign, carry, and a constant number of shifts fit the allowance. Thus $2^{11}(h+4)(a-b+3)$ suffices for division with clear work tapes and the quotient and remainder returned. The short and zero cases use fewer transitions.

**Euclid and signed operations.** The sign-normalized first division has $a\ge b$. Write $a_i,b_i$ for the successive bit lengths and $k$ for the number of nonzero divisors. At every nonfinal step $a_{i+1}=b_i$, so $\sum_i(a_i-b_i)\le h$. In two steps a positive divisor falls below half its previous value or the procedure finishes, which gives $k\le 2h+2$. The sum of all alignment lengths is therefore at most $h+k$. This counts quotients with one bit, including consecutive such quotients near the Fibonacci worst case.

The program also computes both Bézout coefficients. Their recurrence takes each of the two columns $(u,v)$ to $(v,u-qv)$. It preserves each remainder as the signed combination of the two inputs. The nonzero signs of each column alternate from the initial pair of unit rows, allowing a zero at the start. After the initial terms, the magnitudes are nondecreasing, since $q\ge1$ and $|u-qv|=|u|+q|v|$ when $u$ and $v$ have opposite signs or one is zero. The final zero row is a primitive integer relation because every Euclid row replacement has determinant $-1$. For positive inputs $A,B$, its two magnitudes are $B/g,A/g$ for $g=\gcd(A,B)$. This bounds every intermediate coefficient by $\max(A,B,1)$. It includes the product $q|v|$ as well. A zero original operand uses the unit row directly. Swapping inputs or restoring their signs changes neither bound.

To multiply $v$ by $q$, process the quotient from its high end. Double the accumulated magnitude, then add $|v|$ if the next digit is one. Every prefix product is at most $q|v|$. One quotient digit requires fewer than 16 passes of the same $h+4$ symbols, including leading-zero deletion, addition and scratch release. The two signed additions following the two products each take fewer than 16 passes. The quotient has at most $a_i-b_i+1$ digits. The divider, both products, the two updates and the row and input copies fit $2^{16}(h+4)(a_i-b_i+3)$ per Euclid division. The full Bézout result, including the initial swap, sign handling, final copy and cleanup, costs at most $2^{16}(h+4)(7h+9)$. The three extra units include these boundary passes. [Quotient and Bézout implementation](serial_setup.py)

**Whole installer.** Use $w=2^{38}$ for the length of an individual indexed datum. Both scalar operands can have this full width. Signed rational addition uses two cross-products and a sum in its numerator and one product in its denominator. Multiplication and division use two products. All unreduced magnitudes have at most $h=2w+2$ bits. Reduction uses the preceding Euclid implementation followed by two exact unsigned divisions. In addition to the Euclid term, $8\cdot2^{16}(h+4)^2$ suffices for all products, signed sums, exact divisions, normalization and erasure. Thus one complete rational operation takes fewer than $2^{110}$ local steps. Long serialized strings may occupy the whole $w$-bit field during lookup or parsing. The scalar proof permits the same width for the numerators and denominators. It does not assume that they have the smaller sizes seen in timing runs.

With $2^{68}$ actions and records of up to $4w$ cells, every old value and every new value fit in $2^{108}$ cells together. The original complete lookup costs $8\cdot2^{108}\cdot512\cdot128=2^{127}$ transitions with the rewrite stream, cursor and key restoration. Let $E=2^{16}(h+4)(7h+9)+8\cdot2^{16}(h+4)^2$. The setup bill is $(2^{68}+1)(2^{127}+E+2^{120})2^{32}<2^{228}$. The $2^{120}$ allowance and the parsing and cleanup multiplier remain charged. The input-dependent calendar construction and every active numerical tape keep their separate recurring price. The code checks the exact integer inequality together with 4,071 quotient or Bézout inputs and 7,396 rational operations. This finite test supplements the proof for all operand lengths.
