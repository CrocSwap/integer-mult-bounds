# Rank-8 rigidity at P107: every input except zero exclusion holds (no new κ)

**Status: scoped reduction. No κ claim, no construction, and no rigidity theorem at P107 yet.** It continues the
rank-8 line of PR1212 (burntout76, open, head `57099cb5`), PR1399 (`4e1ba3a1`), PR1419 (`81b8997b`) and the
P108 package (PR1474, `311dd4e1`). It is the complementary part of an664's
[discussion 1502](https://github.com/CrocSwap/integer-mult-bounds/discussions/1502) claim, which targets a
structural (I2) zero-exclusion lemma toward P107. This package does not touch (I2).

PR1419's extension lemma makes P112 minus X rigid whenever X lies inside a frame of P112. For |X| = 5, that
leaves **1607 of the 1614 orbits** of 5-subsets. These are orbits under the group H generated by `verify.py`'s 8
FRAME0-stabiliser generators, which has 172032 elements; the counts come from `make_units.py`'s census.

The PR1212/PR1399 method proves one such unit rigid from a pivot frame F inside the live set and five per-unit
inputs:

- (I2) zero exclusion;
- (I3) with live witnesses on every complete class;
- (I4): all 14 blocks complete;
- gluing through shared pivot pairs;
- PR1399's Lemma 9 connectivity, with its sign identity and rank-3 algebra, for the remaining lines.

**Result.** Every one of the 1607 open P107 units has a pivot that passes (I3), (I4), gluing and Lemma 9. The
pivots are listed in `UNITS.json`, and there are 223 distinct ones. **So at P107, (I2) is the only per-unit
obligation left.** A structural (I2) lemma for these pivots would make every P107 unit rigid, together with
PR1419 for the 7 frame-contained orbits. Through PR1212 §8 that gives PR968's floor 59 = 72 − 13 for masked
decoders with exactly eight costly terms. No P107 unit is blocked by the other inputs.

The pivot choice is not vacuous. On most units the first frames inside the live set fail (I4), because
removing five lines empties both classes of some block. The verifier checks this as a control on the first unit.

**Coverage without enumerating 134 million 5-subsets.** This extends PR1474's candidate rule by one level:

- Every quadruple is equivalent to a triple-orbit representative plus one line. `COVER.json` carries each of
  these 2517 candidates onto one of 180 quadruple representatives by an explicit generator word.
- So every 5-subset is equivalent to a quadruple representative plus one line. There are 18782 such
  candidates. 72 lie inside a P112 frame, which is invariant under the group and is PR1419's case. Each of the
  other 18710 gets a word of length at most 10 from one of the 1607 units.

`verify.py` replays every word. That the 1607 units lie in distinct orbits comes from `make_units.py`'s census
and is not needed by the argument. That census sums the 1614 orbit sizes to exactly C(112,5) = 134153712.

## Reproduce

From the repository root, Python 3.10+, standard library only, about 2.6 minutes:

```sh
python3 -I -B research/complex-rank8-p107-pivots/verify.py
```

Expected final line:

```text
PASS_COMPLEX_RANK8_P107_PIVOTS: checks 7; every P107 unit outside a P112 frame (1607 units) has a pivot passing (I3), (I4), gluing and Lemma 9; (I2) zero exclusion is the only per-unit input left
```

Per unit, `verify.py` checks the following, using the code of the P108 package with the CNF and RUP parts
removed:

- the pivot is a frame inside the live set, with PR1212 Lemma 2's D4 structure;
- (I3): 12 witness conditions per complete class, each with a live witness;
- (I4) on all 14 blocks, and gluing;
- Lemma 9 connectivity, sign identity and exact rank-3 algebra on every remaining line;
- the PR1212 §7 assembly identity on all 107 live lines, for random rational gauge parameters.

To regenerate `UNITS.json` and `COVER.json` (about 3 minutes with 10 processes), run the command below. It
needs numpy; numpy only proposes the grouping and the pivots, and the verifier checks every word and pivot.

```sh
python3 -I -B research/complex-rank8-p107-pivots/make_units.py --out-dir /some/new/dir
```

## Open next questions

1. **(I2) on the 1607 (unit, pivot) pairs, by a structural lemma.**
   - *Why it matters.* It is the only per-unit input left at P107. With PR1419 it gives every P107 unit.
   - *First step.* Check an664's discussion 1502 first. Otherwise, test whether the *trans* and *diag* families
     alone refute a zero on these pivots.
   - *Blocker.* No structural lemma is known. Per-orbit DRUP certificates would be on the order of 50 MB here,
     scaling the P108 package's 6 MB for 168 units.
2. **Do the non-(I2) inputs survive at P106 and P105?**
   - *Why it matters.* PR968's floor goes to P105. A unit with no passing pivot there would be an exact
     obstruction to this method, whatever (I2) lemma is found.
   - *First step.* Run `make_units.py` one level deeper. The candidates are P107 orbit representatives plus one
     line, keyed by the canonical forms of their sub-5-subsets.
   - *Blocker.* The pivot search costs about 0.1 s per frame, and the orbit counts grow toward order 10⁴ at P106
     and 10⁵ at P105. It needs a better pivot order or a counting argument.
