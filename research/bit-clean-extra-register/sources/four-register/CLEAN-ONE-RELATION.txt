One-relation reduction for a clean register
==========================================

Statement
---------
Consider eight nonzero rank-one rational 3 by 3 paid frames. Let the
active block of the scalar product be diag([C_1],[C_2],[C_3]), where
each C_i is invertible and lower triangular. Other input registers
start at zero, and their outputs are unused.

If the paid-frame span has dimension eight, no such word exists.
If it has dimension seven, every paid scalar projector must have zero
active off-diagonal entries. The latter conclusion reduces this case
to the zero-active-off-pattern alphabet. It does not by itself exclude
that alphabet.

Small active-target bounds
--------------------------
One active invertible target needs at least three calls, regardless
of the number of clean registers. Its frame is in the span of all
paid rank-one frames, and fewer than three cannot span a rank-three
matrix.

Two active invertible lower-triangular targets need at least six calls,
again with any number of clean registers. With at most five calls, a
frame span of dimension at most four is covered by CLEAN-SPAN-FOUR.txt.
The only other case has five independent frames. Each active target
then has one unique subset-sum exponent. Differentiate the formal
active-block identity at all variables equal to one. Each compressed
scalar projector is diagonal, and it has rank at most one. Thus each
paid variable occurs in at most one target. Rank subadditivity requires
at least three variables in each of the two targets, for a total of six.

Independent frames and a long relation coefficient
-------------------------------------------------
For eight independent frames, the same differentiation argument gives
at least nine calls for three active targets.

At frame span seven, the integer relation lattice has one primitive
generator r. If some |r_j| is at least two, no distinct subsets of the
paid frames have the same sum. Any subset difference has entries in
{-1,0,1}, whereas every nonzero integer multiple of r has an entry of
absolute value at least two. The same unique-subset differentiation
argument therefore again gives at least nine calls.

The remaining relation has entries in {-1,0,1}. Let S be its nonzero
support. The Laurent ideal of relations is generated by

  g = product(t_j : r_j=1) - product(t_j : r_j=-1).

This follows by extending the primitive vector r to an integer basis.
Each active target has a representative monomial t^(q_w), where q_w
is a subset indicator. The active-block polynomial minus this target
diagonal is divisible entrywise by g.

Differentiate at all t_j=1 over F_2. The derivative of g in a variable
outside S is zero. Thus, for each j outside S, the active compression
of N_j is diag(q_1j,q_2j,q_3j). Its rank is at most one, so that variable
belongs to at most one target. On S, all active compressions have one
common off-diagonal pattern.

Rank-one diagonal completions
-----------------------------
Let a nonzero off-diagonal pattern in a binary 3 by 3 matrix be fixed.
Consider all rank-one matrices with that pattern. One of the following
three descriptions applies.

1. There is one matrix M.
2. All matrices are supported on one coordinate plane.
3. There are two matrices with one common coordinate image or one
   common coordinate row.

To prove the classification, the difference of any two completions is
a diagonal matrix of rank at most two. If some difference has rank two,
its image and row space are one coordinate plane. Both rank-one
summands have image and row space in that plane. No third completion
can have a nonzero remaining diagonal entry: that entry and an existing
nonzero off-diagonal entry would force a nonzero off-diagonal entry
outside the plane.

Otherwise every nonzero difference has rank one and equals one E_ii.
There can be at most two distinct completions; differences at two
coordinates would give a rank-two difference. Two rank-one matrices
whose sum has rank one have a common image or a common row. Their sum
is E_ii, so that common image or row is coordinate i. This proves the
classification.

Project onto the relation support
---------------------------------
Suppose the common active off-diagonal pattern on S is nonzero. Set all
variables outside S to one. This operation is valid because the sole
relation is supported on S. The resulting subword still has a diagonal
active target.

In case 1, the active image and row vectors of every factor on S are
the same x and y. Its active product has the form I+f x y^T. Since
x y^T has a nonzero off-diagonal entry and the active target is diagonal,
f=0. No target changes.

In case 2, all active image and row vectors lie in the same coordinate
plane. The subword can change at most those two target roles.

In case 3, the active product difference has its image in one coordinate
line, or its row space in one coordinate line. Since its active target
is diagonal, it can change at most that one target role.

Let T be the set of possibly changed roles. Thus |T| is zero, one, or
two. For each role outside T, its original invertible target frame is a
sum of paid frames outside S. Each such target needs at least three of
those frames. The variable assignment above is disjoint between target
roles, so at least 3(3-|T|) outside calls are assigned to roles outside T.

If T is empty, this already requires at least nine calls. Otherwise,
set those assigned outside variables to one and remove their factors.
This is a valid projection because the sole relation is still supported
on S. The target frames on T do not change: their monomial representatives
do not use any removed variable. Initialize the other registers at zero
and discard their outputs. The remaining circuit has |T| unchanged
invertible targets and at most

  8 - 3(3-|T|) = 3|T|-1

calls. This contradicts the one- or two-active-target bound. Therefore
the common pattern on S must be zero. The pattern outside S was already
zero, which proves the claimed reduction.

Verification status
-------------------
This is an analytical proof. It does not depend on a finite circuit
search. The open_pr_survey agent independently reviewed and approved
the completion classification, the compressed-product constraints, and
the removal of outside variables. A direct finite check of all 49
nonzero binary rank-one active matrices also confirms the classification:
16 nonzero off-patterns have one completion, six have completions in a
coordinate plane, and six share a coordinate image or row.

For exactly one clean extra register, a separate complete check excludes
the zero-active-off-pattern alphabet. See independent/clean-zero/PROOF.txt
and its result and independent audit. That finite result is required
to exclude span seven; the analytic reduction alone is not sufficient.
