# Negative results from a search for a new coding mechanism (no new κ)

Scoping note, **no new κ**: one README, six standard-library checkers and one data file. No existing file changes.

**Question.** Every witness on the frontier, 7.43e-4 (#285) at the time of writing, gets its saving from one
mechanism: the label (index-coding) decoder of the paired-cube line. Each data role saves one rank unit through a
free entrance label, and a centre-plus-producer decoder pays for it. The per-level coding gain is G ≈ 1.003 at
m ≈ 110–120, so the saving exponent a ≈ ln G / ln m is under 10⁻³. We looked for a different mechanism that could
multiply that exponent by 2 or more, on both the bit and the complex side.

**Answer.** We found none. This note records what we tried, what is proved, what was computed, and what is still
open, so that others need not repeat the same routes. Section 1 covers a search run on 9–10 October 2026. Section 2
lists earlier routes from 8–9 October that we discarded at the time.

**Relation to other lists.** This complements Swapnil Jain's
[closed-ideas list](https://github.com/Swapnil-jain/integer-mult-kappa/blob/main/docs/CLOSED_IDEAS.md) and its
papers P1 ([doi:10.5281/zenodo.23265442](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23265442)) and P2
([doi:10.5281/zenodo.23266393](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23266393)). Where a row overlaps theirs, we say so. We use
their status labels: **[proof]** a complete argument; **[cert]** an exact computer certificate; **[exp]** a decisive
experiment or a floating-point computation named as such; **[arg]** a short argument, not written as a full proof.
Every closure holds only in its stated model. Nothing here is a lower bound for integer multiplication.

## 0. Model and the constraint that shapes everything

The two recursive tape primitives are the address-field interchange (bit side, payload F₂, frames are rational
shears Φ_M:(H,D)→(H+MD,D) over Z/q^b with q an odd prime) and the simultaneous H₀ layer (complex side, frames are
exact Gaussian-dyadic Clifford operators acting as g^{⊗f} on f columns). A frame network splits rows into W role
streams; pointwise gates act at a common frame; a frame change of rank t costs one child of width t·f. The saving a
is the root of the moment Σ_e (t_e/m)^{1−a} = W. For small a, a ≈ D/E, where D = Wm − Σ t_e is the deficit and
E = Σ t_e ln(m/t_e) is the entropy.

For lower bounds we use the general register (dilation) model, which contains every frame network and allows gates
between registers at different frames:

    ∏_i ( I + ([g_i] − 1) N_i ) = [T] · (permutation)      over K[G]^{W×W},

with N_i rank-one idempotents over the payload field K, g_i the paid frame changes and T the target. Cost-zero
factors absorb the free maps. Correctness for all column counts f is exactly this identity in the group ring.

Two facts govern any new mechanism:
- **Both sides must move.** κ ≤ min(a_b, a_c) up to the assembly factor. In particular κ ≤ a_c/(1+a_c), because the
  complex primitive's binary basis changes call chunk interchanges. A gain on one side alone is worth at most the
  gap to the other side, under 2% today.
- **Small m pays enormously.** a = ln G / ln m. Any saving at m ≤ 6 would be worth at least 50 times today's
  exponent. That is why much of the search below concentrates on small m.

## 1. The search of 9–10 October 2026

### 1.1 Complex primitive

**(a) m = 2: no saving for any frames. [cert]**
Model: frames are any matrices in GL₄(C) generated by Hadamards on the two qubits and free maps. Free maps are all
monomial matrices (any invertible diagonal, any permutation of the four basis states). Gate coefficients are any
complex numbers, and gates may join registers at different frames.
Certificate: the FO lemma with ρ(g) = conjugation by Λ²(g) on End(Λ²C⁴).
- Λ²C⁴ = P₁ ⊕ P₂ ⊕ P₃, where P_a = span{e_x ∧ e_{x⊕a}}, one plane per perfect matching of F₂².
- Y₀ = span{Π₁, Π₂, Π₃}, the coordinate projectors onto the P_a. Every monomial matrix permutes the three planes, so
  Y₀ is invariant under the free group.
- f(g) = dim(Y₀ + ρ(g)Y₀) − dim Y₀ is 0 on every monomial matrix, 1 for the Hadamard on each matching, and 2 for
  H⊗H. Hence s ≥ 2W = Wm.

This strengthens the earlier Clifford-only result. It covers Clifford+T, arbitrary phases, the exact "y-phase"
frames below and non-unitary diagonal scalings. The script is `checks/complex_m2_lambda2_certificate.py`.

**(b) m = 3: the natural certificates cannot exist; the case is open.**
- [proof] No torus-invariant FO certificate exists in characteristic ≠ 2. Suppose Y₀ lies in the weight-0 part of a
  representation, is AGL(3,2)-invariant, and gives a rank-linear bound. Then the fixed spaces of the coset block
  groups would give an n-multilinear representation of the Fano matroid. That is impossible when 2 is invertible,
  and gate coefficients contain 1/2.
- [exp] The best FO bound we found in the enlarged model is s ≥ 2W, from difference-class projectors in Λ², giving
  G ≤ 1.5. Searches over sums of irreducible pieces of Λ¹–Λ⁴ and Sym²–Sym³ found nothing better.
- In the Clifford model the earlier bound is s ≥ 50W/21. It leaves only W = 2 with s = 5 and W = 3 with s = 8 at
  small W.

**(b′) m = 3, W = 2: settled in the dilation normal form, open in the full register model.**
- [proof] *Window lemmas*, valid for any frame group because they use only subadditive cost, target cost 3 and
  cost ≥ 1 per paid move. Write t_j for the scalar mixing between moves j and j+1.
  - In a three-move window whose outside moves cost at most 2 in total, the identity coefficient forces
    t_j[1][1]·t_{j+1}[1][1] = 0, and the inverse network forces t_j[0][0]·t_{j+1}[0][0] = 0.
  - Two-move windows force antidiagonal mixing.
  - No single move can cost s − 2 or more.
- [cert] *Dilation normal form* (scalar idempotents, with free elements only inside paid moves): no W = 2 saving
  for any cost profile with s ≤ 5.
  - Five rank-one moves leave 169 zero patterns and 84 coefficient systems. Gröbner bases over Q decided each one,
    so the verdict holds over every field of characteristic 0.
  - The 6 consistent systems all force g₁ = 1 or g₅ = 1.
  - The other profiles with s = 4 or 5 leave no survivor.
  - A second, numerical code path agrees on all 84 systems.
  - Only the cost properties above are used, so this covers Clifford frames and Clifford plus 2^k-th-root phases.
- [cert] *Full register model*: W = 2 needs s ≥ 5. With one free B-element inserted anywhere (6 placements, about
  109,000 exact systems) there is still no saving; any W = 2 solution would need at least two such insertions
  between scalar mixings.
- [exp] *FO search*: nothing beats the Clifford bound 50W/21. 5,127 new functions from flag, isotropic-line and
  stabilizer-state modules gave at best 7/3. FO is also blind to the window constraints.
- Open: the full register model at W = 2, s = 5 with two or more inserted free elements, and W = 3, s = 8.

**(c) Non-Clifford frames and approximate arithmetic. [proof]/[arg]**
- [arg] Rounded arithmetic is legitimate in the cost model. Non-dyadic constants cost O(log p) per entry, which the
  leaf terms absorb. Error growth is polynomial in d, so O(log d) guard bits suffice, and dirty roles are restored to
  the same absolute error. But the network identity must still hold exactly in C[G]: a fixed per-level defect does
  not shrink to 2^{−p}.
- [proof] In the synthetic layout, records lie in C[y]/(y^r+1). A diagonal frame y^{θ(x)} is a controlled signed
  cyclic shift of the record, so it is free and exact. These frames reduce to the Clifford case:
  - with bounded exponents, setting y → 1 gives a Clifford network of equal cost;
  - with exponents k + l·r/2^s, one gets Clifford plus 2^{s+1}-th roots of unity, valid for every Galois conjugate;
  - phases algebraically independent of the rest specialise to 1.
- We found no mechanism by which non-Clifford frames enlarge the one-dimensional entrance label at large m.

### 1.2 Bit primitive: the abelian register model with per-role Levi targets

Each role may end at its own target C_w, any invertible lower-triangular matrix, because free scalings X_w on the H
fields and Z_w on the D fields at entry and exit turn the shear by C_w = X_w Z_w^{-1} into the shear by I. A common
target C is equivalent to I. Mixed targets break the common-target hypothesis behind the trace lemma (P1, §5) and
behind the parity cut.

**(a) Flag theorem. [proof]**
Setting: ∏_a (I + ([A_a] − 1) N_a) = diag([C_w]) over F[L], with every C_w invertible lower triangular (uniform or
not), any rational frames A_a and any payload field F. Call a frame *up* if its strictly upper part is nonzero.
Then

    W m − s  ≤  Σ_{a up} ( m/2 − rank A_a ).

In particular, **m = 2 never saves, for any assignment of Levi targets.**
Proof.
1. The strictly-upper part φ is a homomorphism from L to an abelian group. It kills every target and every low
   frame, so the up factors satisfy a target-zero identity in F[φ(L)].
2. At a generic character the up factors are proper pseudo-reflections with product I. In compact-WY form,
   X_up T Y_upᵀ = 0 with T invertible, and Sylvester's inequality gives rank X_up + rank Y_up ≤ #up.
3. The character ζ^{A_ii} kills no target, because invertible lower-triangular C_w have nonzero diagonal. The span
   condition (below) then gives span{x_a : (A_a)_ii ≠ 0} = F^W, so W ≤ rank X_up + #{low a : (A_a)_ii ≠ 0}.
4. Sum over i, using rank A ≥ #(nonzero diagonal entries) for lower-triangular A:
   mW ≤ m·rank X_up + Σ_low rank A_a. The same holds for Y. Add the two and use step 2 to get
   mW ≤ (m/2)·#up + Σ_low rank A_a. Subtract s.

With a common target, conjugate first; the bound then holds for every complete flag, with "up" meaning frames that
do not preserve it. Every inequality of the proof was checked on 18,200 exactly verified solutions from unpruned
solvers, and on 11,760 more from a pruned one.

**(b) Character and span conditions with Levi targets. [proof]**
For a character χ with kernel K, let t and u count the frames and targets in K, and let d = E − W. Then:
- |t − u| ≤ d;
- span{x_a : A_a ∉ K} ⊇ span{e_w : C_w ∉ K}, and the same for the y_a.

Targets that are all multiples of one matrix, for example a mix of I and 2I, look like a common target to every
characteristic-2 character. So "some roles at I, some at 2I" gives nothing at m = 2, and in general no more than a
common target does.

**(c) Small exact searches at m = 3, W = 3. [cert]/[exp]** (not packaged here)
Rank-one integer frames u vᵀ with u, v ∈ {0, ±1}³, and every lower-triangular subset-sum target, under the flag,
parabolic, character and span filters:
- E = 4: no candidate.
- E = 5: impossible by the flag theorem.
- E = 6: no candidate among 4.5·10⁹ leaves.
- E = 7, sparse pool: every candidate dies on a mod-3 kernel.
- E = 7, full pool: incomplete.
- E = 8 with Levi targets: not run.

**(d) Non-abelian frames with mid-network Levi maps (G₀ = shears ⋊ Levi pairs).**
Free Levi maps applied in the middle of a network act on each register's shear parts by n ↦ X n Z^{-1}. A single
paid shear can then act on different terms of one input with different effective shears. With X = 2I every shear
is a commutator, so all character-based bounds fail: doubled import, counting, span and flag. Simple FO certificates
from permutation modules reach a ratio of only 1.33, where 2 is needed. (e) settles the case anyway.

**(e) Theorem G₀: the flag theorem survives mid-network Levi maps. [proof]**
Setting: G₀ = N ⋊ L.
- N = M_m(Q) are the shears; L are the pairs (X, Z) of invertible lower-triangular matrices, acting by
  (X,Z)·M = X M Z^{-1}.
- The state lies in M_W(F[G₀]). Free operations are GL_W(F₂) mixing and left multiplication of any register by
  any l ∈ L, at any time. Paid operations are shears, at cost rank M.
- Targets are Π·diag([c_u l_u]), with c_u invertible lower triangular and l_u arbitrary.

Then W m − s ≤ Σ_{up} (m/2 − rank M_j), as in (a). So **m = 2 never saves in G₀ either.**

Why the earlier tools failed: with X = 2I every shear is a commutator, so every one-dimensional character is
trivial on shears. The proof uses a matrix rank function instead of characters.
1. Absorb the free operations into the idempotent vectors, which now have entries in F[L]. Projecting G₀ → L
   kills every paid factor, so the identity becomes ∏_j (I + a_j([n_{M_j}] − 1)b_jᵀ) = Π·diag([n_{c_u}])·Π^{-1}.
2. On the group rings of the solvable quotients G₀/N′, where N′ is spanned by the matrix units over a down-set
   (L-invariant, hence normal), use the Sylvester rank function. Its sources:
   - congruence subgroups are torsion-free;
   - torsion-free elementary amenable group rings are Ore domains (Kropholler–Linnell–Moody, Tamari);
   - rank([g] − 1) = 1 for g of infinite order;
   - matrices over F[L] have the same rank in every such quotient.
3. Two quotient families give the two halves of the flag argument:
   - dividing out the lower-triangular shears gives rk A_up + rk B_up ≤ #up;
   - projecting onto the rectangles {r ≤ i ≤ c} gives W ≤ rk A_up + #{low j : (M_j)_ii ≠ 0}.

The same proof allows free zero-in/garbage-out temporaries. A separate adversarial review found the proof correct.
Exact checks covered valid W = 2 networks that are genuinely non-abelian, including one where a single paid shear
acts on 7 terms of one input with 6 different effective shears.

Scope: G₀ omits the free lower-left updates D += K·H of the full machine, under which N is not normal. The full
non-abelian Borel model at m = 2 is therefore not covered by this theorem.

**(f) Abelian m = 3: s ≥ 2W + 1. [proof]**
- Tight-frame lemma: let k be a rank-one frame with A₀₂ ≠ 0, and let P be the set of frames whose strictly-upper
  parts are parallel to k's. Then 2W ≤ #up − |P| + 2·#{low frames with A₁₁ ≠ 0}. The proof uses a functional on the
  {0,1} × {1,2} block, which kills P and no target.
- At s = 2W, the flag theorem forces 2W up frames of rank one. The lemma then forces A₀₂ = 0 for every frame, and the
  coordinate kernels A₀₀, A₂₂ and {A₀₁ = A₀₂ = 0} contradict each other.
- So W = 3 needs s ≥ 7, and W = 4 needs s ≥ 9. The gain bound is still G < 3/2.
- [cert]/[exp] A type-level enumeration (the coordinate-character conditions depend only on support patterns) and
  {0,±1} box searches leave no candidate in the following cases; every candidate found was killed by the first-order
  mod-2 condition:
  - W = 3, E = 8, sparse box: 1,035,664 candidates;
  - W = 4, E = 8, full box: 7,176 candidates;
  - W = 3, E = 7, sparse box.
- Still open: W = 3 with s = 7 or 8, and W = 4 with s = 9 to 11.

### 1.3 Machine operations outside the frame model

Each operation below is linear-time on a multitape machine by the 02-streams lemmas.

| | Operation | Verdict | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| (a) | Address-dependent (masked) gates, with stream compression | Freezing every mask at one logical address gives an unmasked network of the same cost. A compressed stream of density ε needs at least ε(rank − log_Q(1/ε)) interchanges. So this reduces to (b) up to O(1/b). | [proof] |
| (b) | Clean ancillas (extra zero-initialised volume, garbage erased) | The first-order p-integral bound and the cut bounds survive; apply them to the data–data block. In the moment equation an ancilla of total rank R adds R to Σt_e and nothing to Wm, so it must raise the deficit by more than R. Nested dirty auxiliaries are deficit-neutral and dominate ancillas by more than 10× today. | [proof]/[arg] |
| (c) | Fourier duality (an odd-characteristic payload such as F₃ makes the WHT exact and growth-free) | An XOR-shear H ⊕= D equals WHT_H · diag((−1)^{ξ·D}) · WHT_H, and an interchange is 6 H-layers plus 3 free sign passes (checked exactly). It does not retire the bit primitive: the complex primitive's basis changes call chunk interchanges at width eK > e, so the recursion is circular, and a contiguous-slot layer network needs about 10 extra children per role against a deficit of about 0.2. Inside a network it is covered by the non-CSS-label row of P1. | [cert]/[arg] |
| (d) | Multiplicative index maps (multiplying an index by a fixed unit mod N is O(V)) | Transposition is k ↦ 2^e·k mod (2^{2e}−1). The CRT turns it into a free negation, but the CRT step is itself the expensive shear. *Valuation lemma:* any factorisation into fixed units of size at most C has length at least e/log₂C. BFS over the generators {−1, 2, 3, 5, 7} gives exactly e for e ≤ 10, the naive count. | [proof]/[cert] |
| (e) | Carry-free digitwise maps (XOR, F_q-linear maps, F_{q^k} arithmetic on whole fields) | Not linear-time by any known method. Free updates are cyclic shifts with carries plus fixed units, so cheap linear frames live in characteristic 0, where the trace lemma and the p-integral theorem apply. An escape from characteristic 0 must therefore be non-abelian, not cross-characteristic. | [arg] |
| (f) | Nonlinear controlled-shift frames (the offset may be any polynomial-time function of earlier fields) | The target still costs m per register: a backward-information function is subadditive, at most 1 per interchange and equal to m on the target. A nonlinear shear x += g(y) costs 2 children, not 1. Frames that are smooth at a common fixed point linearise through the Jacobian. At m = 1, Q = 3 the generated group is all of S₉, and the exact cost census matches these lemmas. Non-smooth frames evade every linear obstruction; permutation-module FO gives only s ≳ 2W. | [proof]/[cert]; open |
| (g) | Unequal or digit-class streams, multi-rate heads | Reduce to virtual registers, compression or copying, and to the P1 rows on unequal widths and mixed motifs. | [arg] |
| (h) | Sparse convolution and prefix-sum gates | Augmentation kills translations, so the derivative bound survives; the cut bounds survive. No mechanism found. | [arg] |

**Follow-up on (f), nonlinear frames at small size.**
- [arg] **Common-frame networks with s ≤ 8 never save, for any frames (nonlinear included) and any gates.**
  - A common-frame network is a one-shot code on the undirected graph of distinct gate frames, with at most s edges.
  - Coding equals routing for at most 6 coding nodes, for trees, for graphs whose terminals lie on one face
    (Okamura–Seymour), and for theta graphs (Liu–Que–Li–Li, arXiv:2608.06070, Theorems 5 and 7, and the earlier
    work cited there). So a saving needs at least 7 frames and cyclomatic number at least 3.
  - That settles m = 2 with W = 3 or 4, and m = 3 with W = 3, without search. The smallest open common-frame
    case is m = 2, W = 5, s = 9.
  - This assumes the cited coding model covers time-ordered gate circuits.
- [proof]/[cert] **At m = 2, nonlinear frames have no local advantage over linear flags.** In the machine's free
  group (controlled shifts by any polynomial-time offset, plus fixed units), the largest set of pairwise
  session-compatible unit-cost directions is exactly q, as for linear flags. It is 3 at q = 3 for every b, checked
  exhaustively and by SAT. Carry-free fibre maps would give 9 at N = 9, but they are not linear-time.
- [cert] **Register-model searches.** Exact F₂[Γ] arithmetic at N = 3, W = 2, m = 2, s = 3, over two structured
  classes (6.6·10⁶ and 7.95·10⁷ distinct states), found no saving.
- [exp] **FO bounds.** Permutation-module FO certificates, including an LP over all free-group-stable sets of
  point pairs, give only s ≥ 4W/3.
- **Open:** the register model beyond these classes (W = 2 with s = 3, W = 3 with s = 4 or 5). It has no useful
  first-order identity: the group is generated by involutions, so every characteristic-2 character is trivial.

### 1.4 Other primitive families, and embedding known coding-gap codes

The reduction used throughout is this. With free temporaries, a frame network is a frameless bijective circuit plus
a map of its gates into the frame space. The gain is therefore at most the coding gap of the frame space for sessions
at the target's relative position. The undirected unicast gap codes that refuted the Li–Li conjecture this month
(Braverman–He, arXiv:2610.10108; Zhang–Li–Li, arXiv:2610.09367) are the natural seeds.

**(a) Frame families.**
- Permutation and signed-permutation frames, finite subgroups of GL(Z), and lattice-preserving finite linear groups
  never save. FO on Λ/pΛ applies whenever the H and D parts split the lattice mod p. [proof]
- Qudit Clifford frames over Z/N give the same rank geometry for prime N, and are slot-additive across primes for
  composite N (coordinate isolation). [arg]
- Abelian or cyclic frame groups (units mod N, digit rotations, Z/N translations) have l₁-embeddable metrics, so
  their gap is 1. [arg]
- Tensor-power primitives A^{⊗e} are slot-additive without free carry-free mixing. [arg]
- Primitives defined through multiplication are circular. [arg]

**(b) Cayley-graph seeds. [proof]** If a finite frame group with cost-1 generators S is used directly, with
sessions along an involutive target z at distance D, the gap is at most D/|S|. A gain needs physical degree below
the session distance, which is the opposite of the Tanner seeds: Braverman–He has degree 10 at distance 5.

**(c) Full GL_{2m} flag model (non-abelian, same interchange primitive). [cert]** At m = 2, among the cost-1
neighbours of a flag, the largest pairwise session-compatible set has size 2 at q = 2 and 3 at q = 3. In the abelian
shear model it is m = 2. So the local "compatible directions ≤ m" obstruction, which rules out isometric Tanner-code
embeddings in the abelian model, is model-specific. On the bit side, m = 2 is closed for the abelian and G₀ models
(1.2(a), (e)), but not yet for the full non-abelian flag model. Script: `checks/flag_model_cliques.py`.

**(d) Braverman–He's PG(2,9) code with per-session sign gauges. [cert]**
- With a common target I, an isometric frame embedding at m = 5 with rank-one edges is impossible: on a geodesic,
  steps are orthogonal idempotents, and 731 edges are crossed in opposite directions by different sessions.
- With targets C_i = ±C₀, geodesic steps are C_i·P. Opposite traversals become consistent exactly when the two
  sessions have opposite signs, which turns the obstruction into a sign system on the 157 sessions × 735 edges.
- No session uses an edge in both directions, but the system is unbalanced: 23,025 violated constraints in one
  component. Sign gauges do not help.
- With general Levi targets the embedding is heavily overdetermined: about 11,760 rank-one conditions against 4,550
  unknowns.

Script: `checks/bh_sign_gauges.py`. The data file holds the deleted-edge list R and the vertex order π transcribed
from the paper's appendix.

### 2.1 In-class routes on the paired-cube design

| Approach | How it failed |
|---|---|
| **5-pair ports** (bigger cubes, less centre loss) | Each 32-port cube needs 171 decoder channels, so the cost per port is about 4× the triples', against a 1.2–1.4× budget. Even at p = 16 the complex saving would be about 0.4× the triples'. (Compare P2's weight-five row.) |
| **Register recycling** (#184-style reuse of retired registers) | The peak number of simultaneously live registers forces at least 4.9 per port (4.76 under fresh-rank liveness), against about 3.45 needed for 2×. Even with the recycling conditions relaxed, the gain is +3.7% (at most about +5.4%). |
| **Source-assisted words** (#193, original sources cancel values) | Even with every source available everywhere (not actually allowed), κ gains only +2.9%. The extra registers come from fan-out, not from the cancellable part. |
| **Nested-chain decoder modules** | They would need sources that targets cannot read inside side roots, or about 220 extra centres. Those centres cost more than the whole deficit. |
| **Better pair modules** (exact minimum register count) | The J0 module is within about 7% of the best found, worth about +0.5–1% κ. No construction gap. |
| **Smaller cover / cancelling modules** | The cover dimension is fixed because a three-shear rotation is minimal. Cancelling sums would put unreadable sources into a root's frame, which the checker rejects. |
| **Centre restructuring** (built from P, reordered, split) | −1% to −7%, because the centre sums are already co-optimised with P. |
| **Root-role permutations, merging 2-port roots, gauge parameter** | Exactly neutral. |
| **Non-uniform local circuits, parity-based cube totals** | No gain, or −1.3%. |
| **Five-stage "bridged" schedule** (#209's idea) | Our pricing at the time: about +6% on the complex side but only +1.9% on the bit side, which then sets κ, so about +1.9% overall. #234 later realised it with a five-stage bit supplier as well. |

### 2.2 New-primitive routes

| Route | Result | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Different-frame (DF) gates: Clifford operator-valued gate coefficients | No saving at m ≤ 2 for any W (exact rational certificate over Sp(4,2)). Gates without a (1+i) denominator never save, at any m. At m = 3 the best bound is s ≥ 50W/21; the only surviving W = 2 profile is five rank-1 children. DF cannot cut centre loss: summing the per-class decoder identities gives K + H + B = I as scalars. | [cert]/[proof] |
| Characteristic-0 Tanner codes on the dual polar graph DSp(2m,2) (small-instance reduction: free temporaries give a one-shot code over Q(i) with sessions between opposite Lagrangians) | Every m = 2 LP optimiser found has no code over any field (tight-cut propagation, corridor argument). Sp(2m,2)-invariant schemes cannot deliver opposite-pair sessions. Product Tanner codes have causal gap exactly 1. | [proof]/[cert] |
| Small instances | No saving at m = 1, W ≤ 2, ≤ 5 session labels, ≤ 6 coding labels, cyclomatic number ≤ 2, product label families, mW ≤ 9, m = 2 with W ≤ 5, m = 3 with W ≤ 3. | [cert] |
| Centre decoders | The paired-cube and bit star decoders sit at their invariant minimum rank, and the dense readable remainder is forced at rank about h. Overcomplete or coordinate-direction centres gain at most about 4–10%. | [cert] |
| More than one saved dimension per data role | k-dimensional source labels need degree ≥ 3 exact-match indicators, so the centre rank grows from about h to about h⁴. Two-sided labels split the readers of a centre copy into about 15 classes at p = 12, multiplying copy cost. Port-mixing shears break the Pauli structure. | [arg] |
| Cover and stage structure | Within one pair's three-transfer swap with common-frame scatter, the cover penalty (ln 3 per local rank unit) is intrinsic; padding loses (4h −11%, 5h −18%). #209's five-stage twins evade this by sharing helpers across pairs. | [arg] |
| Cross-level and recursive primitives | A self-similar k-level scheme flattens to one level at (W^k, m^k). Gauged or network-valued children gain at most ×1.03. Cross-level labels give at most ×1.11 before auxiliaries. | [proof]/[arg] |
| Gain mechanisms in the Clifford model | Fixed points of the output permutation never save. Swap partners that share a label save nothing. A dirty-role relay at best breaks even. Each saving role needs ≥ 3 coded transfers; #209 later showed 2 suffice with free gates between pairs. Aux swaps (×0.73) and k-cycles (×0.27) lose. | [proof]/[arg] |
| Breaking min(a_b, a_c) | The bit network appears in the complex recursion only as rank-zero adapters, which the a_c moment already counts, so κ ≤ a_c/(1+a_c). | [arg] |
| Assembly weighting | The cover assemblies pass m* = min(a_b, (1−β)a_c) into an unchanged structured assembly. A balanced prefix (margin 1 − ε) gives about +0.05%. | [cert] |

## 3. What remains open

None of these has a construction. They are listed in rough order of how directly a positive answer would bear on κ;
remember that both sides must move.

1. **Complex primitive, m = 3.**
   - The full register model with W = 2 and s = 5 needs two or more free elements inserted between scalar mixings.
   - W = 3 with s = 8 is open: the Clifford bound is s ≥ 50W/21, and the enlarged model has only s ≥ 2W.
   - Torus-invariant FO certificates cannot exist there (Fano obstruction), so a proof needs a different tool.
2. **Bit primitive, abelian, m = 3.** W = 3 with s = 7 or 8, and W = 4 with s = 9 to 11. Proven bounds allow
   G < 3/2.
3. **Bit primitive, full non-abelian model at m = 2.** Theorem G₀ does not cover the free lower-left updates
   D += K·H. With them the shears no longer form a normal subgroup.
4. **Non-smooth nonlinear frames, in the register (superposition) model.** W = 2 with s = 3, and W = 3 with s = 4 or
   5, beyond the searched classes. The first open common-frame case is m = 2, W = 5, s = 9.
5. **Large m.** No mechanism is known that saves more than one rank unit per data role at a decoder cost comparable
   to today's. The flag theorem and the FO bounds still allow gains up to about m/2. So the obstruction is the lack
   of a construction, not a proof.
