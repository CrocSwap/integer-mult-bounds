# The physical adjoint on one affine-quadratic chart

## One symbolic contraction for 256 labels

On the independent support $a=a_0+Cu$ of the supplied chart, $y(a)=\alpha i^{q(u)}$. All binary sums in addresses are XOR. The quadratic $q$ has arbitrary linear coefficients modulo four and even cross coefficients. Write $(r_i(u),c_i(u))$ for the two four-bit addresses at site $i$. For the whole fibre simultaneously the coefficient at $(x,z)$ is
$$
t_{S,16x+z}=\alpha\sum_u i^{q(u)}\,
 (-1)^{\sum_i z\cdot(r_i(u)+x)}
 \prod_{i=1}^{13}\overline{(U_S/4)_{r_i(u)+x,c_i(u)}}.
$$

The normalized Choi entries of $U_S$ form a single affine-quadratic chart. One way to see this is to prepare four Bell pairs and implement the local Clifford. The program constructs its 256 entries by the stated transvections. It finds a reduced basis of their affine support, takes the entry at the lexicographically least (binary integer order) support point as the absolute anchor, interpolates the phase on the coordinate basis and its pairs and verifies every matrix entry. This directly fixes the global phase and checks the chart extracted from the actual specified lift. Let $\beta$ be this anchor. For each site the program adds the binary constraints for membership in that support and subtracts its quadratic phase from the combined polynomial. The Weyl modulation contributes $2\sum_i z\cdot(r_i+x)$, again quadratic of the admissible type. Hence the result is $\alpha\bar\beta^{13}$ times a quadratic Gauss sum on an affine binary system in the eight label bits and at most 104 input parameters.

Eliminate the highest pivots, putting the eight parameter bits at low indices. If inconsistent, the answer vanishes. Otherwise the low free variables parametrize exactly the projected affine set of labels, injectively. Each pivot among the original label bits depends only on earlier free label bits. The remaining free variables index the slice to be summed. Substitution pulls the polynomial back into these coordinates and preserves the even-cross form because a binary affine form expanded modulo four has even mixed terms and twice a product only needs its expansion modulo two.

For a summed bit $w$, write the dependent part of the phase $aw+2w\ell$ with $\ell$ linear in the other bits. For $a=1,3$, summing $w$ gives $(1+i^a)i^{-a\ell}$, leaving a new admissible phase. For even $a$, summing gives twice the indicator $\ell=a/2$. When $\ell$ uses another summed variable, solve and substitute that variable. When it only uses retained variables, record the equation on those parameters. An impossible constant equation produces zero. Each step removes at least one summed bit and never enumerates its assignments. Solve the recorded equations after the last summed variable, restrict the quadratic phase and multiply the initial scalar by the resulting Gaussian integer. The label map stays injective on the final affine parameter space. The return is thus either zero or an eight-bit chart with $2^d$ equally large nonzero coefficients and a common absolute scalar, $0\le d\le8$. ## Sharp image of the single-fibre map

The 256 one-site normalized columns $b_p=\mathrm{vec}(W_pU_S)/4$ form an orthonormal stabilizer basis. The linear map $|p\rangle\mapsto b_p$ is Clifford. At $S=I$, Fourier-transform the four $z$ bits and add the resulting column bits into the four $x$ bits to obtain $4^{-1}\sum_c(-1)^{z\cdot c}|x+c,c\rangle$. For $S\ne I$, apply $U_S^{\mathsf T}$ on the column register. The transpose is again a Clifford, with the specified absolute lift. Four simultaneous real Hadamards have Gaussian-dyadic entries; alternatively each can be rephased by $e^{i\pi/4}$ and the common sign corrected after four. Copy the computational label thirteen times by binary CNOTs before applying this basis transformation sitewise. This is a Clifford isometry $E_S:|p\rangle\mapsto b_p^{\otimes13}$. Therefore $E_S^*E_S=I$; for any eight-bit affine-quadratic chart $w$, $E_S w$ is a single affine-quadratic observation (up to its supplied scalar) whose requested fibre is exactly $w$. The statement covers non-unit scalings because classical stabilizer-state closure applies after removing a common factor. The elementary generator routes or the same parametric elimination also work directly on unnormalized charts. In particular all nine nonzero support ranks occur. The test generates nine explicit large-support observations by Fourier synthesis at $S=I$ and checks all coefficients including their phases.

For the zero output, use any distinct one-site computational labels $a,b$ in the first two tensor positions. The difference of the two ordered basis strings with $a,b$ interchanged and the other sites fixed is a two-point affine-quadratic observation. Its pairing with every $v^{\otimes13}$ vanishes by site exchange, even though the observation itself is nonzero.

## Access and arithmetic

For rank $r$ input, storing the address basis takes at most $104r$ bits and the dense phase table has at most $1+r+\binom r2$ entries, in addition to the scalar. These data are shared across named targets. Each target uses at most 16 transvections on a 256-entry site table; repeated targets can reuse this site description. There are at most 52 affine membership equations because the support of any unitary has at least 16 entries. One binary solve on at most $D=8+r\le112$ variables and one elimination of at most $r$ internal bits suffice. The final constraint solve uses at most eight variables. A conservative bound for the explicit Python phase substitutions is $O(D^5)$ small coefficient operations per target: a substitution expands at most $O(D^2)$ terms into at most $O(D^2)$ terms apiece; it can recur at most $D$ times. Binary row arithmetic uses masks of at most $D+1$ bits. There is no $2^{104}$ amplitude table. The output has at most eight basis vectors, 36 nonconstant phase coefficients and one complex scalar, or 256 entries if expanded.

If the input scalar has component magnitude at most $2^B$ before its dyadic denominator, each dense lift numerator entry has magnitude at most $2^\ell$ for a word of length $\ell$. An unreduced output denominator is $2^{e+13(\ell+2)}$ for input denominator $2^e$. The accumulated Gauss factors have magnitude at most $2^{104}$ by the inner sum bound, and $|\alpha_{\rm num}|\le\sqrt2\,2^B$. Thus $B+13\ell+106$ magnitude bits per numerator component suffice. These are classical arithmetic and representation bounds. The separate price of the observation producer, physical movement and any complete multiplier depends on its interface.
