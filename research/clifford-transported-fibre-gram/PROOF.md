# Fibre coordinates and the width-13 normal operator

Let $G=\operatorname{Sp}(8,2)$ and $W_p=X^xZ^z$ for $p=16x+z$. Fix $U_S$ for each $S\in G$ using the transvection word returned by the #7800 binary reduction. For an axis $a$, use the Gaussian-dyadic unitary $R_a=(1+i)(I+iP_a)/2$, $P_a=i^{x_a\cdot z_a\bmod2}W_a$. A word in application order $a_1,\ldots,a_\ell$ gives $U_S=R_{a_\ell}\cdots R_{a_1}$. Each word has $\ell\le16$. Our coefficient indexed by $(S,p)$ refers to the absolute lift $V_{S,p}=W_p U_S$. Define $A$ with columns $(\operatorname{vec}V_{S,p}/4)^{\otimes13}$, using the computational row/column order. All inner products are conjugate-linear in the first entry. ## Read out a batch on one observation chart

Consider the #7800 Bell decoder $B$. At a raw address denote the twelve 4-bit shifts of its adjacent pairs by $s$, the twelve free second nibbles by $v$, and the two singleton nibbles by $y$. Compute the partial Walsh transform $T(s,w,y)=\sum_v(-1)^{w\cdot v}Y(s,v,y)$. For a supplied affine-quadratic chart, #7813 produces a common complex scalar, affine support equations and a quadratic phase function for $T$ on its support. Its restrictions to the singleton directions have the same kernel $U$ of dimension $e\le8$ at every choice of $(s,w)$. Each translated and character-modulated nonempty slice fits in one Fourier bin of a coset of $U$ after removing a common quadratic phase. These data depend only on the chart. [Predecessor elimination and bin construction](https://github.com/apoursh/integer-mult-bounds/blob/65573785774c6bbac12485a4d90cd82778d25e36/research/clifford-compact-pauli-fibre/PROOF.md)

Start with the 4,265 identity-fibre records $(I,J,n,\delta)$, where $I,J$ are input/output six-tuples, $n$ is the integer numerator and $i^\delta$ the compiler correction. On the output labels compute two tables using the fixed word for $S$. Write $U_S W_u U_S^*=i^{\gamma(u)}W_{Su}$ and $U_SW_tU_S^\mathsf{T}=i^{\theta(t)}W_{L(t)}$. The latter label action is affine. Transport replaces $J$ by $(L(t):t\in J)$ and changes the correction to

$$\delta' = \delta+\gamma(\mathop{\bigoplus} J)-\sum_{t\in J}\theta(t)\pmod4.$$

Indeed the six Bell phases multiply, and removing the ordinary Weyl phase on their sum gives the block transport phase. The correction undoes this phase. Six labels cancel the affine offset in their XOR. The ratio is independent of lifting the same symplectic map through a Pauli and a Gaussian unit: a Pauli contributes the sum of the six conjugation signs, which is the sign on their XOR, and the sixth power of its squared unit is one. Thus this traversal gives the same records as composing the output symplectic maps before independently choosing their lifts. The correctness extends to arbitrary chart amplitudes because the two linear functionals agree term by term.

Write $(x_o,z_o)=\bigoplus J'$ and $(x_i,z_i)=\bigoplus I$. The corrected singleton entry at $y+(x_o,x_i)$ gets the raw entry at $y$ with sign $(-1)^{(z_o,z_i)\cdot y}$. Both translations and the constant $i^{\delta'}$ are allowed by the shared bin calculation. After the per-coset transforms, chirp rotations and the common scalar, the compiler produces its corrected $16\times16$ target matrix. Right multiply by $U_S^*$ and take the sixteen length-16 Walsh transforms of its shifted diagonals to extract the trace against every $W_p U_S$. In the raw-sum convention of #7813 the final denominator is $4096$, from the Bell factor $4$, compiler denominator $1024$ and trace division by $16$, in addition to the supplied chart denominator and lift denominator. Tensoring each specified absolute lift thirteen times gives the corresponding one-hot complex coefficient. The procedure also evaluates $BY$ off the image of $A$. ## An exact scalar convolution on every matrix entry

For a coefficient vector $c$ write $M_T=\sum_b c_{T,b}V_{T,b}$. The inverse is $c_{T,b}=\operatorname{Tr}(V_{T,b}^*M_T)/16$, by orthogonality of the $W_b$. This is a scaled isometry with $\lVert M_T\rVert_F^2=16\sum_b|c_{T,b}|^2$.

For $D=V_{S,p}^*V_{T,b}$ put $\tau=\operatorname{Tr}D$, $g=S^{-1}T$, and $k=\dim\ker(g-I)$. The trace of conjugation by $D$ on all matrices equals $|\tau|^2$. In the Weyl basis it equals the sum of the signs on the fixed Weyl labels. Indeed a fixed label's conjugation phase squares to one since its square is scalar. These signs form a character on the fixed subspace because two Weyl labels multiply to their sum up to scalar. The sum is either zero or $2^k$. In the latter case $\tau\in\mathbb Z[i,1/2]$ has this norm and equals a Gaussian unit times $(1+i)^k$: clear powers of two and factor in $\mathbb Z[i]$, using $2=-i(1+i)^2$. Hence $\tau^4=(-4)^k$ if it is nonzero. In both cases $\tau^{13}=(-64)^k\tau$.

It follows that $K=A^*A$ has coefficient entry $(\tau/16)^{13}=16^{-12}(-64)^k\operatorname{Tr}(V_{S,p}^*V_{T,b})/16$. Substituting into the inverse coordinate formula gives, for **every** $c$,

$$M_{Kc}(S)=\sum_{T\in G} f(S^{-1}T)M_c(T),\qquad f(g)=16^{-12}(-64)^{\dim\operatorname{Fix}(g)}.$$

The matrix entries of $M_c$ therefore give 256 independent copies of a scalar central convolution. The group size is 47,377,612,800. The nonzero/zero Pauli-trace pattern, its complex phases and cancellations all survive the change of coordinates. Computing the single-step weight needs only an eight-column binary rank since $\ker(S^{-1}T-I)=\ker(T-S)$.

## Compile a complete filter before receiving its input

In an irreducible character $\chi$ of degree $d$, convolution by $f$ has eigenvalue $\lambda_\chi=\sum_g f(g)\chi(g)/d$. The real rational packet sums in the accompanying table suffice, since inversion preserves $f$, Galois-conjugate characters have the same rational eigenvalue here, and $\chi(g^{-1})=\overline{\chi(g)}$. For a function $H$ on the 52 distinct eigenvalues the scalar kernel of $H(K)$ is

$$h(g)=|G|^{-1}\sum_{\chi}d\,H(\lambda_\chi)\chi(g^{-1}).$$

For a packet of size $n$ use its character sum, whose value at the identity is $nd$, and keep the same constituent degree $d$ in the inverse transform. This gives at most 80 dot products of length 80. Concrete eight-column inputs are recognized in the same class order by the native certificate. The independent spectral multiplicity check uses $\sum_{\chi:\lambda_\chi=\lambda}d^2$ on the quotient, with an additional factor of 256 for the original Gram. ## Cost on named coefficient blocks

An input dictionary denotes complete support in its named fibres; omitted fibres are exactly zero. Count $m$ supplied 256-entry blocks across all input dictionaries, $u$ distinct named source fibres in their union and $r$ distinct requested output fibres. Synthesize the $m$ matrices once, using sixteen length-16 Walsh transforms plus a lift for each. Each output needs at most $u$ relative class queries (or $u$ binary ranks for a single Gram application), $256m$ real-weight/complex-entry multiply-adds, and one adjoint lift followed by sixteen length-16 transforms. Each block change thus costs $1024$ complex Walsh additions and $256\ell$ two-term lift combinations. A zero or shared input/output may reduce work. The input contains up to $256m$ complex values plus eight 8-bit columns per named target; output size is $256r$ complex values. The example programs store source matrices and output blocks, the fixed class inventory or Bell read list where applicable, and ordinary header/work arrays. A query has no loop over unlisted intermediate group elements, even when the mathematically intermediate vectors are dense. All targets can be named, incurring their output size. [Block representation and executable](gram.py)

Exact coefficient sizes are measurable. Over *all* 80 classes take the least common denominator across the kernels used by an action and use that denominator for every numerator. Its bit length and the maximum absolute numerator bit length are respectively $(145,145)$ for $K^{-1}$, $(11381,11381)$ for the 51-step pair, and $(7500,7500)$ for the 52-step pair. The Gram itself admits denominator $16^{12}$. Suppose supplied real and imaginary components have a shared positive denominator $D$ and numerator magnitude at most $2^B$. Let $L\le16$ be the largest lift length, $H$ the chosen common kernel denominator and $J$ the largest numerator bit length. An unreduced common denominator for a returned component is $D H 2^{2L+4}$. Its numerator magnitude is bounded by $\max(1,m)2^{B+8+4L+J}$: each Walsh bank contributes a factor of 16, each lift at most $4^L$ to each component of its numerator, and the scalar sum contributes at most $m2^J$. Shorter lift denominators can be padded to $2^L$ within the same bound. These are arithmetic/word counts on supplied blocks; $A^*y$ and $By$ for a general observation may both have dense support. Evaluating $By$ at requested targets does not certify it vanishes elsewhere. The cost of producing complete support for either schedule input, a full array of outputs, and a multiplication supplier are separate questions.
