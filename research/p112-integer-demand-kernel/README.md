# An exact 139-row integer P112 demand subfamily

The fixed 280-row, 14-variable P112 demand system has a **unique minimum
139-row original subfamily over nonnegative integers**, with no upper bounds.
This resolves [PR6455](https://github.com/CrocSwap/integer-mult-bounds/pull/6455)'s
integer-only question after its 237-row real-domain minimum.

The new work removes 98 additional rows by complete integer projections and
finds five new integer separating points. The 43 earlier real-domain deletions
and 134 existing integral points are authenticated, validated and credited reuse.
Thus all 103 previously unresolved labels are decided. Every one of the 139
retained rows has a full integer point satisfying all 279 other original rows
and violating that row; these force every equivalent original-row subfamily to
contain all 139. No minimum over arbitrary new inequalities is claimed.

## Why a finite test covers unrestricted integer multiplicities

For a violated target `(I,d)`, integrality forces the total of the target
coordinates to at most `d-1 <= 7`. Set every outside coordinate to 8: every row
with an outside bit is then satisfied. The remaining constraints have proper
subset supports. Exhausting the inside weak compositions therefore decides
whether a global counterexample exists, despite unrestricted multiplicities.
Support-cardinality induction makes simultaneous old/new deletions sound.
`PROOF.md` states the complete projection, enumeration and minimum arguments.

For example, half of source rows `0x5a` (demand 4), `0x1a98` (3) and `0x1ac2`
(2) gives exactly the coefficient vector of target `0x1ada` with RHS `9/2`.
Its integer LHS is therefore at least 5, the target's demand. The source's
real separating point has LHS `9/2`, showing why the row was needed over reals.
The new projection checks all 495 inside integer profiles for this target.

## Scope and limits

The result concerns exactly the original PR6425 masks and unrestricted
`Z^14 >= 0`. The inside total bound is derived from violating a particular row;
it is not a global cap on multiplicities. The all-orthant equivalence remains
valid if identical extra constraints are conjoined to both systems. The minimum
claim itself need not persist under a cost/COPY budget or multiplicity caps,
