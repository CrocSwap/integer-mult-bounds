## Exception precedence and traceback

Once a body/reference/freshness/publication/cleanup error P is active, close-error bookkeeping, status/error metadata, stage-stat metadata, failure-manifest encoding/writing and secondary-error recording are best effort. Their synchronous BaseException failures cannot escape the guarded diagnostic body in place of P. A managed close error C is primary only when there was no earlier body error. For nested final/incoming managers, P remains primary through both closes. The handlers re-raise with bare `raise`; they do not construct a replacement error or use `raise error` or explicit traceback replacement.

Future controls compare the exact injected exception object with `is` and retain a traceback node captured at its original raise; the caught exception must still contain that identical linked traceback suffix. Ordinary outward propagation adds caller/manager frames. The promise is preservation of P and its original traceback chain, not identical top-level traceback objects across distinct invocations. Natural receipt/freshness errors are captured inside the active failure handler before secondary reporting, then compared by identity and suffix. The fallback status-control captures the active freshness error inside the rejecting mapping setter before raising its diagnostic error.

These guards do not promise recovery if the interpreter cannot execute exception handling, is asynchronously terminated or uses unadmitted/concurrently mutated providers. A failure before a primary exists remains the primary failure. Repeated calls can leave `last_operation` from a previous invocation if entry validation fails before a new record. `last_operation` is supplemental, mutable and non-authoritative. If status bookkeeping fails, status may remain IN_PROGRESS or LITERAL_REFERENCE_FALLBACK; that cannot authorize success. The caller must observe actual return/exception and apply independent checks.
