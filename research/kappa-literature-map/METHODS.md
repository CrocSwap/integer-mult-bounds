Each followed primary-paper queries and citation chains, read relevant definitions or mechanisms where accessible, and recorded the actual depth in its SOURCES.json. Abstract-only and failed full-text fetches are visible. No search-engine snippet was promoted to an inspected proof.

The lane SEARCH.json files record 70 actual topic/search queries (17 root, 21 quantum, 19 algebra, 13 RAM), alongside local lookups and citation followups. They preserve actual topic queries, local queries, followed chains and limitations. Their combined coverage is broad but bounded: one session, selected languages and databases, finite citation depth, and uneven historical/full-text access. No saturation proof or absence-of-prior-work theorem is possible. Seventy-four source records include overlapping foundational Clifford papers. Normalized identifiers in BIBLIOGRAPHY.json prevent counting those shared references as independent discoveries. Relevant papers include recent 2026 work, historical FFT algorithms, and corrected preprint versions. One plausible arXiv identifier was checked and excluded because it was about Navier–Stokes rather than multiplication.

Each worker consulted relevant source files or indexed hits in the registered local DAG/Lean tree. Discovery was not restricted to Mathlib. Exact local module identifiers and SHA-256 pins are public; private raw retrieval packets, machine paths, personal source headers and unrelated files are not distributed. Evidence labels distinguish source inspection from fresh kernel checking. No competing live DAG was created or existing proof environment modified.
