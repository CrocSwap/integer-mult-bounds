Different ordered bases for one subspace carry the same mandatory grade under this check.

## A single chart

Take a trial dimension $d$. An incident neighbor of dimension $d$ makes strict containment impossible. Every smaller neighbor’s space must be contained in the candidate $X$. The candidate must be contained in every larger neighbor. For a productive edge the scan also requires its original increasing orientation. Set $A$ to the linear span of the smaller neighbors and $B$ to the intersection of the larger neighbors. Empty lower and upper lists give the zero space and the full nine-dimensional ambient space. The exact test is $A\subseteq B$ and $\dim A\le d\le\dim B$. The admissible choices correspond to subspaces of $B/A$ of dimension $d-\dim A$, hence number
$$
{\dim B-\dim A\brack d-\dim A}_2.
$$
Every choice at a fixed site and dimension has the same change of grade on every edge. The verifier sums the occurrence multiplicities before computing the tariff difference.

The same dimension at every individual chart forces its original subspace. Among the 78,707 nonidentical single internal assignments, 18 preserve the complete histogram. The other 78,689 strictly worsen it. The 62 helper-only sites give 8,793 site/subspace combinations. All 8,731 changes cost mass. For chart 1094 at dimension six the signed grade difference is $2[1]-2[2]-[3]+[4]-[5]+[6]$, where $[r]=r^t$. The independently recorded interval for its per-call change lies strictly below −0.00843834771094.

## Two incident charts

Fix two adjacent internal chart IDs, allocate each outside edge to its original endpoint, and count the shared MOVE multiplicity once. Equal trial dimensions are impossible by strict nesting. Orient the calculation so the trial ranks are $u<v$. From the frozen neighbors alone the candidate spaces must satisfy $A\subseteq X\subseteq B$ and $E\subseteq Y\subseteq F$, with $X\subset Y$.

Set $C=B\cap F$. If $A$ is not contained in $C$ or $\dim C<u$, there are no choices. Otherwise take the quotient $C/A$. Set
$$
 n=\dim C-\dim A,\quad k=u-\dim A,\quad
 d=\dim(A\cap E),\quad p=\dim(C\cap E)-d .
$$
The fixed subspace $(A+(C\cap E))/A$ has dimension $p$. Let $\ell$ be the dimension of its intersection with $X/A$. There are
$$
 {p\brack\ell}_2 {n-p\brack k-\ell}_2\, 2^{(p-\ell)(k-\ell)}
$$
such choices of $X$. Choose the intersection, the image of $X/A$ in the complementary quotient, and a linear map from that image into the fixed $p$-space modulo the chosen intersection. This gives all choices exactly once. Each has $\dim(X\cap E)=d+\ell$, so $X+E$ has dimension $g=\dim E+u-d-\ell$. The number of possible $Y$ is then $ {\dim F-g\brack v-g}_2$. A Gaussian coefficient outside its dimensional range is zero. Summing the product over $\ell$ accounts for both endpoints and every allowed orientation. A separate brute enumeration in all 16 subspaces of $\mathbb F_2^3$ verifies every one of 5,956 ordered lower/upper interval and trial-dimension cases against literal flags.

The 1,244 adjacent pairs have 29,995 legal trial-dimension combinations and 3,284,027 exact subspace-pair choices. Of these choices 3,282,715 lose mass and 1,312 tie. These raw totals repeat the baseline and the one-site changes for each incident pair. For a pair $(i,j)$ subtract its one baseline assignment, every change available at $i$ with $j$ fixed, and every change available at $j$ with $i$ fixed. The remaining 3,114,632 assignments change both actual subspaces and all strictly worsen the receipt. Subtracting by support matters because a mutable space can keep its old dimension even after its neighboring space changes.
