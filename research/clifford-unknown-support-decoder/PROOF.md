# Exact reconstruction from a tensor train

## Statement and conventions

Let d=2^n, D=d² and F=Q(i). A site has D physical symbols. Symbol q=dx+y denotes output y of input x. An open-boundary train supplies matrices G_t(q) over F, with sizes r_(t−1) by r_t and r_0=r_f=1. Its observation at a word is the scalar product of the corresponding matrices in order. Repeated sites can share a core. Arbitrary bond bases, nonminimal ranks and signed cancellation are allowed.

Choose one projective label for each n-qubit Clifford. Its signature w is its vector of matrix entries divided by the first nonzero entry. The output gives one nonzero Gaussian-rational coefficient for each distinct label in

    T = sum_j c_j A_j^(tensor f).

Here A_j is the unit-norm canonical column defined below. For n=4 the normalization is vec(U_j)/4. Zero has empty support. No support list is an input. Let k=floor((f−1)/2) and h'=f−1−k. Consider any support class for which the k-th powers of every permitted support are independent. The decoder decides exact membership in that class if its final label test recognizes the class. It recovers every label and coefficient on membership. The [support proof](SUPPORT.md) establishes the classes used by the program, including a complete-fibre test at four-qubit widths 25 and 26 and unrestricted support at width 27 and above.

The independence condition is sufficient. At narrower widths “outside_model” has the meaning given by the two limits and the full-span flag in the output. Two eligible decompositions cannot give different answers. Their coefficients lie in F even if one initially permits complex weights, since the full-rank coefficient solve has its matrix and right-hand side in F. A larger ineligible representation can exist at a dependent width. For example I=((1−i)/2)S+((1+i)/2)S† at width one. At four-qubit f≥13 the predecessor threshold gives uniqueness even against ineligible alternatives.

## Canonical Clifford coefficients

The image of the computational zero vector is a stabilizer state. Its Z-type stabilizers constrain its support to an affine space. The X projections of the stabilizer generate all translations on that space. Each maps a nonzero amplitude to another with a multiplier in {1,i,−1,−i}. More explicitly, if the X projections have dimension h, the kernel in the n-dimensional stabilizer has n−h independent Z constraints. Their solution is one coset of size 2^h. The X projections act transitively on it. Every other input column comes from the first by a conjugated X Pauli. Thus one global nonzero pivot suffices for the whole matrix. All nonzero entries of w are fourth roots, and each column has m=2^h nonzero entries. The first input column contains a pivot.

Set U=(1+i)^(−h)W, where W has entries w, and set e_n=2^floor(n/2) times (1+i)^(n mod 2). Then U is unitary, its entries are Gaussian dyadics and A=vec(U)/e_n has norm one. In particular e_4=4. Any two Gaussian-dyadic unitary representatives of this projective Clifford differ by a fourth root. To see this for a scalar in the dyadic ring, reduce a²+b²=2^(2j) modulo 4 and divide out all factors of two. For the ratio between representatives, it is the scalar in the dyadic product of one representative with the adjoint of the other. A change A' = uA changes the published coefficient to c'=c/u^f.

For the converse, check W*W=mI and require each conjugate of an X or Z generator by W, divided by m, to be a Pauli. Unitarity makes this a test for membership in the full Pauli normalizer. The implementation finds the single displacement and phase from column zero of each conjugate. It checks the entry on that displacement in every column against one binary character. Its modulus is one, so unitarity forces all other entries in that column to vanish. The unsigned displacement and character masks of the 2n images identify the symplectic fibre. The code tests X and Z for every bit. A diagonal CCZ passes flatness and the Z tests but fails an X test.

## A cut of the actual observation

Write T as a mixture of signature powers with weights α_j. A change from A_j to w_j only changes the nonzero weight. Independence of w_j^k implies independence at every larger exponent. There exists a linear form nonzero on each of the finitely many w_j over F. Contracting an extra factor with it multiplies each column by a nonzero scalar. In particular both flanks, of lengths k and h', are independent on the support.

For each prefix p compute its boundary row G(p) starting with the scalar 1. At every step keep an independent subset of actual rows and their words. They span all boundary rows by induction, since right multiplication by a fixed next slice is linear. Repeat from the right for suffix boundary columns. Use the resulting actual words p_i and u_j at the two ends of the middle site. The sampled slices are

    M_q[i,j] = T[p_i,q,u_j] = P diag(α_b w_b[q]) Q,
    P[i,b] = w_b^(tensor k)[p_i],  Q[b,j] = w_b^(tensor h')[u_j].

P has full column rank s, and Q has full row rank s, for a valid s-term observation. Indeed the full prefix flattening has rank s. Its opposite factors are the independent suffix powers tensored with individual nonzero middle columns. The selected boundary rows span every row of this full flattening. Its restriction therefore still has rank s and its left factor is P. Apply the same argument from the other end for Q. In particular s≤min(number of selected prefixes, number of selected suffixes). This reasoning permits a train with inaccessible or cancelling states. Taking the maximum of the ranks of *individual* middle slices would give a false cap. Distinct components may have disjoint pivots.

## Identify labels without an eigensolver

Try each possible first nonzero pivot a in the first input column. Require zero at earlier coordinates and set w[a]=1. At a partial choice of values v_q from {0,1,i,−1,−i}, intersect the kernels of M_q−v_q M_a. Denote this intersection by E. Keep the choice exactly when some y in E has M_a y nonzero. In program terms, appending the rows of M_a to the accumulated equations must increase their rank.

To prove the test, write z=Qy. Every z in F^s is available. P is injective and each α_j is nonzero. The equations force each coordinate z_j to zero whenever a chosen ratio disagrees with w_j[q]/w_j[a] and w_j[a] is nonzero. Labels with zero at a cannot contribute to M_a y. A surviving coordinate with w_j[a] nonzero gives a witness by surjectivity of Q. This also proves that two different surviving prefixes at the same depth require two different labels. The earlier zero tests assign each complete label to a unique pivot. The row test works when the observed tensor vanishes at the constant pivot word.

Zero slices force zero coordinates on all valid labels. Two slices related by a fourth root force the same relation on every label, by the ranks of P and Q. The implementation groups these slices and expands the signatures at the end. After each coordinate it bounds the live choices for this pivot together with already completed pivots by the cut cap and any requested size cap. A failed cap rejects an off-model input immediately. Counting completed labels alone could take exponential time on a malformed observation. Each surviving label must then pass the Clifford and support-class tests.

## Recover the coefficients and verify the whole tensor

From the actual prefix words, select s independent rows of P using the recovered signatures. For each distinct pivot a, contract the actual train against the constant suffix a^(f−k), including the middle site. Solving the selected square prefix system against these s values gives z_j=α_j w_j[a]^(f−k). The coordinate with pivot a has w_j[a]=1. Reading each coordinate at its own pivot gives all α_j. A singular system or a zero coefficient fails the test. The coefficient in the advertised convention is c_j=α_j((1+i)^h e_n)^f.

A separate exact equality test covers all D^f words. Start a boundary row with (1,α_1,…,α_s). At each step multiply its first block by the input slice and its remaining coordinates by the proposed signature entries. Keep independent actual rows as before. On the last step require the scalar in the first block to equal the sum of the remaining coordinates for *every extension of every retained row*. Linearity proves equality on every word. Conversely, any mismatch gives a nonzero linear functional on the span and fails on some tested row extension. This proves soundness on arbitrary off-model input. Completeness follows from the previous steps on any allowed decomposition. The zero tensor also passes with an empty list. Two eligible decompositions of the same train produce the same surviving paths and the same coefficients, proving uniqueness within the class.

## Work, storage and arithmetic

Let R≥1 be the largest packed bond dimension, D=d², and s the number of labels on success. The number of live labels and paths is at most R even on rejection. An integer declared as a huge unused bond does not cause a dense allocation. At each bond the reader intersects the coordinates appearing on its two sides, discards the others, and renumbers the intersection. A complete path can use only such coordinates. Parsing and sorting use the input record count, the sizes of indices and D. A list of f site references costs at least f units to read. The reader can expand the used cores by site. A dense observation must first be supplied in this interface at the producer's expense.

Prefix/suffix sampling and the full residual take O(fD(R+s)^3) field operations. The D cut matrices need O(DR³) operations. There are at most D pivots, D coordinates per pivot, five choices per coordinate and R live prefixes. Appending at most R rows to an echelon basis costs O(R³). Thus branch discovery takes O(D²R⁴) field operations, also on negative inputs. Clifford membership for s outputs takes O(s n d³). Coefficient solves add at most min(d,s) square systems of size s and that many constant-suffix contractions. These fit in the previous bounds. Copying partial words additionally takes O(f²DR+D³R) index operations. Stored arithmetic data fit in O(fDR²+R³+Ds) field elements, together with index and word storage O(fR+DR) at a cut and the input and output. Repeated cores can reduce actual storage. The algorithm reads the supplied local matrices, stores the live bases, and writes s explicit length-D signatures and their coefficients. It requires no externally supplied Gram application, inverse row, square root or polynomial factorization.

All operations use exact rational real and imaginary parts. Integer and fractional inputs use explicitly written decimal digits. For a bit bound, let B be the sum over **site occurrences** of the total bit lengths of their rational entries, plus f log(2R)+f. Products of all entry denominators in each site clear that site. A literal prefix, suffix or full entry has O(B) numerator and denominator bits by the matrix-product expansion. Rows saved as actual sampled evaluations have this bound. A reduced basis of width m on entries of L bits has entries that are ratios of at most m by m minors. Clearing per-row denominators and using the determinant expansion bounds their bit lengths by a polynomial in m and L. In the branch test the defining rows always come from the fixed cut matrices and the five units. The reduced equations for one group have polynomial bit length. Every accumulated reduced basis is the unique reduced basis of a subset of these fixed equations. Thus the bound does not compound once per coordinate. The coefficient solutions are minors of the sampled prefix system. The residual again saves actual evaluations with those fixed weights. This gives polynomial bit work and output size in D, f, the packed dimensions and the explicit input bits. The bounds specify classical arithmetic and memory access. A reversible simulation, movement on the project's tapes, noisy support selection and a source of the cores in a multiplication caller would each require a separate construction.

For the four-qubit normalization, suppose an external noisy reading y differs from A_fc by norm at most ε. For any proposed coefficients b with residual norm ρ=||A_fb−y||, the width-13 predecessor bound and its monotonic extension give ||b−c||≤(ε+ρ)/sqrt(470624175/4294967296) for f≥13. The sharper odd and Bell-sector bounds apply at their stated widths. This is a posterior error estimate for supplied coefficients. The rank decisions in this algorithm require exact input. The decoder uses general Q(i) divisions, including odd factors when needed. The canonical dyadic denominator obstruction remains intact.
