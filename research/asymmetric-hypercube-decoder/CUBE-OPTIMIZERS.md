# Ordinary-cube shortening and exceptional optimal decoders

**Exact structural results; no new κ or paid supplier.** For `q=p-k=2`
and `k>=4`, this note classifies every subcode attaining the third, fourth,
or fifth generalized weight of either signed proper-star code. It also
determines exactly which prescribed target sets attain eleven deletions.
The proof uses an exact shortening theorem valid for every `k>=2,q>=0`.

This extends the thresholds in [HIGHER-WEIGHTS.md](HIGHER-WEIGHTS.md).
All code arithmetic is over `F_2`. A port chooses one signed coordinate
from each of k distinct pairs among p pairs. The code `C+` is generated by
all proper stars, including the empty star; `C-` omits the empty star.
An ordinary cube consists of all sign choices on one fixed set of k pairs.
Shortening to a set means requiring a codeword to vanish everywhere else,
then restricting it to that set. Merely projecting a codeword is different.

The common factor

```text
F(k) = binom(k+2,k) * 2^(k-4) * GaussianBinomial(k,4;2)
```

counts affine four-dimensional flats inside all ordinary k-cubes. The
complete optimizer classification is:

| Rank | Minimum support | Optimal subcode on an affine four-flat Q | Count |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 3 | 14 | All affine functions on Q vanishing on one affine line L | `120 F(k)` |
| 4 | 15 | All affine functions on Q vanishing at one point P | `16 F(k)` |
| 5 | 16 | All affine functions on Q | `F(k)` |

Functions are extended by zero outside Q. For example, at `(p,k)=(6,4)`
the counts are `(1800,240,15)`; at `(7,5)` they are
`(156240,20832,1302)`. Each optimizer determines its Q and its zero set
uniquely.

## 1. Exact ordinary-cube shortening

Write `RM(d,k)` for evaluations of Boolean polynomials of degree at most d
on `F_2^k`, with `RM(d,k)=0` when `d<0`. For any ordinary k-cube Q,

```text
shorten_Q(C+) = RM(k-q-1,k).
```

For `C-`, the same equality holds when `k>=q+2`. At the boundary `k=q+1`,
the `C+` shortening is the repetition code; the `C-` shortening is zero
when k is a power of two and is the repetition code otherwise. For
`q>=k`, both shortenings are zero.

For necessity, generalized-cube parity forces the restriction f to have
even sum on every face fixing at most q signs. Indeed, replace each fixed
sign's alternative with a coordinate from a distinct pair outside Q;
every other branch is outside the support. These face indicators span
`RM(q,k)` when `q<k`; when `q>=k`, singleton faces force f to vanish.
The orthogonal complement of `RM(q,k)` is `RM(k-q-1,k)`: products of
monomials of combined degree at most `k-1` have even total evaluation,
giving containment, and the dimensions sum to `2^k`.

For sufficiency, let `z_r` indicate occupancy of an outside original pair
and put

```text
M = product_(r outside Q) (1+z_r).
```

Multiplying M by an inside sign polynomial of degree at most `k-q-1`
gives a proper-star polynomial supported exactly within Q. This proves
sufficiency in `C+`. To handle `C-` when `k>=q+2`, its only potentially
empty term is a constant multiple of M. On the port domain, `M=M z_i`
for any inside original pair i. The latter has degree `q+1<=k-1` and no
empty monomial. At `k=q+1`, M differs from the global empty star by
nonempty proper stars, so membership of M in `C-` is exactly the
empty-star question. PR #772 supplies the stated power-of-two distinction.

## 2. Two local parity facts

Suppose all support contains a fixed original-pair set K of size `k-2`.
At q=2 there are four remaining pairs, denoted a,b,c,d. An *edge block*
ab consists of all ports using K,a,b, with arbitrary signs. Ordinary-cube
parity says the vector column labels in each block sum to zero.

Each block also has even parity after fixing one core sign. Replace that
core slot's alternative by a coordinate from a pair outside the block.
Every added branch omits a universal core pair and therefore vanishes.
This applies to each scalar component of a subcode's column labels.

### A four-point slice on complementary blocks

Suppose a word is supported on just ab and cd, and its ab restriction h
has weight four. Then h consists of all four a,b sign choices at one
fixed signed core K.

First, every face of h fixing one sign has even parity. Core signs were
handled above. For sign a, replace its alternative by a coordinate from c
while keeping b ordinary. The added cb branch belongs to neither allowed
block; similarly for b. Thus the four sign vectors in h sum to zero and
form an affine two-flat P in `F_2^k`.

Every face fixing two signs also has even parity, except possibly the
pair `{a,b}`. For two core signs, use c and d as replacements. For a core
sign and a, use distinct replacements c and d; added branches either omit
the core sign or use a forbidden residual pair set. The case of b is the
same.

Parameterize P by two bits. Write `v_j in F_2^2` for the linear part of
coordinate j. The parity of the product of two coordinates on P is
`det(v_i,v_j)`. All these determinants vanish except possibly for a,b.
Since P is two-dimensional, some two coordinate linear parts are
independent; they must be a,b. Every core linear part is dependent with
both, so is zero. This proves the claim. Such four-point slices are
pairwise disjoint or equal.

## 3. A gap in the universal-pair count of weight-eight words

Every q=2 weight-eight word has at least `k-2` universal original pairs.
Avoiding a nonuniversal pair gives a nonzero word at q=1 of weight at
least four, so each such pair has degree at most four. If c pairs are
universal, counting occupied pair incidences gives

```text
8k <= 8c + 4(k+2-c), hence c>=k-2.
```

It cannot have exactly `k-1` universal pairs. To see this, split its
support over the three residual pairs. Each block has at most four
ports. Summing its two residual signs gives a core-sign function whose
faces fixing at most two signs have even parity, using the other two
residual pairs as replacements. A nonzero such function would have
weight at least eight, so it vanishes. Hence each block has paired signs
and corresponds to a core-sign set `F_r` of size at most two. Their
three sizes sum to four.

For distinct residual pairs r,s, `F_r+F_s` has even parity on every face
fixing at most one core sign: mix r,s in one slot and use the third pair
as a replacement. Its nonzero weight is therefore at least four. But the
only size distributions are `(2,2,0)` and `(2,1,1)`, up to order. In the
first, compare a size-two set with the empty set; in the second, compare
size two with size one. Each gives a nonzero difference of weight below
four, a contradiction. Thus the universal-pair count is k or `k-2`.

## 4. Global localization of every rank-three support-fourteen subcode

Let V be such a subcode. All seven nonzero words have weight eight, and
each nonzero `F_2^3` column label occurs twice. For an original pair i,
consider the kernel of restriction to ports avoiding i. It cannot have
dimension two: its support is at least eleven and its nonzero avoidance
image has support at least four, on disjoint coordinates. Thus its
dimension is zero, one, or three.

If c pairs are universal for V, count universal-pair incidences over its
seven nonzero words. A nonglobal pair can be universal for at most one
word, so

```text
7(k-2) <= 7c + (p-c) = 6c+k+2, hence c>=k-2.
```

The case c=k already gives one ordinary cube. If `c=k-1`, Section 3 makes
each word cube-supported. Words in different residual blocks would be
disjoint and sum to weight sixteen. Thus all words use one block, again
giving an ordinary cube. It remains to exclude `c=k-2`.

### Case A: some word lies in one edge block

Let f be supported in ab. Any other cube-supported word lies there too,
since it meets f in four points. A word g that is not cube-supported has
degree four at every residual pair. Its intersection with f already
accounts for four points in ab, saturating a and b; its remaining four
points must lie in cd.

Let `V_0` be the nonzero subspace supported in ab. If `V=V_0`, localization
holds. Otherwise every word outside `V_0` has a weight-four cd slice.
Section 2 makes these slices unsigned blocks at fixed core signs. Distinct
slices are disjoint, and their sum would have weight eight, so the image
on cd has dimension at most one. Thus `dim V_0>=2`.

Fix g outside `V_0` and independent `f_1,f_2 in V_0`. For every nonzero
f in `V_0`, the words g and g+f have weight-four ab slices while f has
weight eight there. Therefore the ab support of g is contained in the
support of each of `f_1,f_2,f_1+f_2`. Those three supports have empty
triple intersection, a contradiction.

### Case B: every word uses all four residual pairs

At each residual pair, all seven nonzero restrictions have weight four.
The restriction is therefore rank three on seven points, with each
nonzero column label occurring once. Each residual vertex has union
degree seven, with distinct incident labels.

Every edge block has label sum zero. A nonempty block cannot have one
point, or two points with distinct labels, so its size is at least three.
A weighted graph on four vertices with weighted degrees seven and every
positive edge weight at least three has only two forms: two complementary
edges of weight seven, or a four-cycle with alternating weights three and
four. A degree-one vertex forces its weight-seven edge to isolate both
endpoints; otherwise all vertices have ordinary degree two.

The complementary case is impossible by Section 2: all nonzero slices
would be weight-four unsigned blocks, which cannot span rank three while
keeping all nonzero weights four.

On a size-three block, the distinct nonzero labels sum to zero and give
the even-weight `[3,2]` code. On a size-four block they span rank three
and give the even-weight `[4,3]` code. In both cases every pair of positions
occurs as a weight-two restriction. The core-face parity from Section 2
then forces those two ports to agree on every core sign. Thus each block
has one fixed signed core, possibly different between blocks.

Adjacent blocks ab and bc must have the same core signs. If they differ
at core index i, choose the i-sign of ab, use the mixed slot
`{a^alpha,c^gamma}`, keep b ordinary, and replace i's alternative by a
coordinate from d. All replacement branches omit universal pair i;
the bc block has the wrong i-sign. Generalized-cube parity therefore
forces the labels in every a-sign slice of ab to sum to zero. A size-three
block has a singleton slice; a size-four block has two distinct labels
in each slice. Either is a contradiction. Connectivity makes all four
blocks share one signed core.

Shortening to this signed core gives `C+_(4,2)`, whose third generalized
weight is fifteen. It cannot contain V of support fourteen. The signed
shortening assertion follows directly from derivatives: for a word
supported on `i+`, its `i-` slice is zero, so its `i+` slice equals the
signed derivative, lowering degree, p and k by one. Conversely multiply
a smaller proper-star polynomial by the fixed signed coordinate. Iterate
over the core. A nonempty fixed core also makes every lifted monomial
nonempty, so this shortening is the same for `C+` and `C-`.

Both cases are excluded. Every optimal rank-three subcode lies in one
ordinary k-cube.

## 5. Affine classification at ranks three, four and five

The exact shortened code is now `RM(k-3,k)`. Its minimum words have
weight eight and are indicators of affine three-flats. Here is an
elementary proof of the standard minimum-word description. A nonzero
degree-r Boolean polynomial in m variables has weight at least
`d=2^(m-r)`, by the even-face lemma. If it attains d and t is a difference
of two support points, its translated derivative has degree at most
`r-1`. Were it nonzero, its weight would be at least 2d; the intersecting
supports of the two translates give strictly less than 2d. Therefore the
derivative vanishes. Translating one support point to zero makes the
support closed under addition, so it is a linear `(m-r)`-space. Conversely
an affine `(m-r)`-flat indicator is a product of r affine equations.
Constants and the degree-m boundary are immediate.

We also need exact shortening to an affine four-flat Q. An invertible
affine change of coordinates preserves degree and sends Q to
`x_5=...=x_k=0`. A function supported there has unique multilinear form

```text
product_(j=5..k)(1+x_j) * h(x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4).
```

For nonzero h its degree is `k-4+deg(h)`, so membership in `RM(k-3,k)`
is equivalent to `deg(h)<=1`. The shortening is exactly `RM(1,4)`.

For rank three, two independent support flats A,B meet in four points;
their affine hull is a unique four-flat Q. Their symmetric difference
is another three-flat inside Q. A third support flat C meets each of
`A,B,A+B` in four points. If C were not contained in Q, its intersection
with Q would have dimension at most two. Its intersections with A and B
would then be the same plane, leaving no intersection with their
symmetric difference. Hence all words lie in Q. Their affine functions
form a rank-three space with no nonzero constant, whose common zero set
is an affine line L. This is precisely the first family in the table.

For rank four of support fifteen, its fifteen rank-three hyperplanes
have supports summing to `14*15=210`. Each is at least fourteen, so every
one attains fourteen. Shared nonzero words force any two such hyperplanes
into the same ordinary cube. Every nonzero word has weight eight. Taking
two independent words in one hyperplane and any word outside it, the
preceding flat argument puts all words into one Q. The rank-four affine
space has one common zero P, giving the second family.

For rank five of support sixteen, its 31 rank-four hyperplanes have
supports fifteen or sixteen and total `30*16=480`. Exactly sixteen have
support fifteen. Two such hyperplanes share nonzero words and together
span V, putting V in one ordinary cube. One already has support `Q\{P}`.
The full support can add only one point. If that point c were outside Q,
an affine function vanishing on Q but equal to one at c would pair oddly
with a word using c. This contradicts orthogonality of `RM(k-3,k)` to
linear functions. Thus V is the full five-dimensional shortening on Q.

Finally, an ordinary k-cube has `2^(k-4) GaussianBinomial(k,4;2)` affine
four-flats. Each has 120 affine lines and sixteen points. Multiplying by
the `binom(k+2,k)` ordinary cubes proves the counts stated above.

## 6. Prescribed targets attaining eleven deletions

For `q=2,k>=4` and `r in {3,4,5}`, r distinct prescribed targets attain
`d_r-r=11` deletions if and only if they use the same k original pairs
and their sign vectors are affinely independent.

This is a statement for observation rows in the proper-star code. Applying
it to the original uniform overlap-corrected decoder retains the existing
power-of-two-k hypothesis, unless its observation model already absorbs
those corrections. Arbitrary independently weighted cap-readable sources
form a larger model; this deletion theorem does not bound that model.

Necessity follows from the optimizer classification. An eleven-deletion
decoder supplies an r-dimensional subcode supported within the targets
and deletions, with rank r on the target columns. It must attain `d_r`.
Its columns are affine evaluations on one Q, so an affine dependence
among targets would contradict rank r.

For sufficiency, their affine hull A has dimension `r-1`; extend it to a
four-flat Q inside the common k-cube. For r=3, take coordinates with
`A=<e_1,e_2>` and choose `L=e_4+<e_3>`. The three functions
`x_1,x_2,1+x_4` vanish on L and have full rank on any affine basis of A.
For r=4, choose P outside the three-flat A; functions vanishing at P
restrict isomorphically to all affine functions on A. For r=5, use all
affine functions on Q. In each case delete the support outside the
targets, and change row basis to obtain retained unit vectors.

Three distinct binary points are automatically affinely independent.
Four distinct points are independent exactly when their XOR is nonzero.
For five points use rank five of their augmented columns `(1,t)`.
Failure proves that more than eleven deletions are required; the larger
optimal budget is not determined here.
