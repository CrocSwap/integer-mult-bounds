# Minimum global-star words are rigid beyond one unused pair

**Uniform equality classification and a two-target consequence; no new κ.**
For every `k >= 2` and `p >= k+2`, the minimum-weight vectors in the global
proper-star evaluation code are exactly the individual `(k-1)`-star vectors.
Thus, at a power-of-two k, arbitrary off-target stars admit exactly **k**
minimum deletion patterns for a fixed retained target, and all k patterns
already have a target-local one-star decoder.

For every `p>=k+2`, the second generalized Hamming weight is exactly
**`4(p-k)+3`**. At a power-of-two k this forces at least **`4(p-k)+1`
deletions** to decode two distinct retained targets; explicit target pairs
attain this bound. The companion [equality classification](EQUALITY-PAIR.md)
determines the exact threshold for every prescribed target pair throughout
`p-k>=2`.

These statements classify evaluation vectors and supports. They do not claim
unique coefficient representations: dependent star generators can give many
expressions for the same vector. No paid supplier or multiplication-bound
improvement is inferred.

## Model and relation to concurrent work

Use the signed-port model in [README.md](README.md). A port in `P_{p,k}`
chooses k of p disjoint coordinate pairs and one coordinate from each chosen
pair. For a signed partial port L, write

```text
m_L(S) = 1[L subset S].
```

Let `C^+_{p,k}` be the binary evaluation code generated by all `m_L` with
`0 <= |L| < k`, including `m_empty=1`. Let `C^-_{p,k}` omit that empty
generator. We first prove the result for the larger space `C^+`; because all
attaining vectors are nonempty stars, the same equality classification then
holds for `C^-`.

Luke Mansoor's [PR #732](https://github.com/CrocSwap/integer-mult-bounds/pull/732),
inspected at `80431a74c31e807581f5c2258c87abe2084caacd`, proves the uniform
distance formula `d(C^+_{p,k})=2(p-k+1)` and the resulting global one-target
puncture threshold. Its generalized-cube parity and even-face arguments are
reproduced below as credited prerequisites, not new results here.

Sagar Thakrar's [PR #725](https://github.com/CrocSwap/integer-mult-bounds/pull/725),
at `6eadc4eb4ebc355b13fa36a3138d97a583ea7f37`, classifies the 67 minimum
target-containing supports at `(p,k)=(5,4)`. Alishan Ladhani's
[PR #747](https://github.com/CrocSwap/integer-mult-bounds/pull/747), at
`aeaf0899179a14644b412e838ede14db967c765e`, gives the finite `(6,4)`
classification: 160 minimum words and four optimal patterns per target.
The theorem below extends that equality classification uniformly to every
`p>=k+2`, rather than re-claiming the finite `(6,4)` result.

ellipsi2's [PR #772](https://github.com/CrocSwap/integer-mult-bounds/pull/772),
at `02b59e553649c333072b5dd4dfb97e43942efa14`, proves uniform proper-star ranks
and algebraic all-target puncture thresholds. Rank and all-target information
sets are a different question from the minimum one- or two-target supports
studied here. We do not re-prove or claim those rank results.

## Credited prerequisites: generalized cubes and distance

A **generalized cube** has k slots, each with two distinct coordinate choices.
The original pair indices used by different slots must be disjoint; the two
choices within a slot can belong to one original pair or to two different
pairs. All `2^k` choices of one coordinate per slot are then distinct valid
k-ports.

For any monomial `m_L` with `|L|<k`, its sum over a generalized cube is zero.
Either L is incompatible with the slots, giving zero occurrences, or it fixes
`|L|` slots and occurs `2^(k-|L|)` times. This count is even, also for the
empty monomial. Therefore every word of `C^+_{p,k}` has even parity on every
generalized cube. In particular, ordinary sign cubes on fixed k pair indices
partition the port family, so every codeword has even total weight.

We also use the following even-face fact. If a nonempty subset W of a binary
m-cube has even cardinality on every face fixing at most r coordinates, with
`0<=r<m`, then `|W|>=2^(r+1)`. If `r>=m`, no such nonempty W exists, since a
singleton face would violate parity. For the bound, induct on m. At r=0,
nonempty even cardinality is at least two. Otherwise split on one coordinate.
If both slices are nonempty, each inherits the property for r-1 further fixed
coordinates and has at least `2^r` points. If only one slice is nonempty, it
inherits the property for r coordinates on the remaining m-1 coordinates;
induction gives the asserted bound, or a singleton-face contradiction if
`r>=m-1`.

For completeness, here is #732's distance argument. Put `q=p-k` and induct
on q for fixed k. At q=0, ordinary-cube parity gives weight at least two,
attained by a `(k-1)`-star. For q>=1, suppose a nonzero word has weight
`w<2(q+1)`. Evenness gives `w<=2q`. Choose a support port T with pair set Q.
For each outside pair r, restricting to ports avoiding r gives a nonzero word
of `C^+_{p-1,k}`, still containing T, with weight at least `2q` by induction.
It follows that w=2q and no support uses r. Thus the entire support lies in
the ordinary cube on Q.

Any face of that cube fixing s signs, for `s<=min(q,k)`, is its intersection
with a generalized cube: replace the alternative at each fixed slot with a
coordinate of a distinct outside pair, and leave the other slots ordinary.
The word vanishes on the added ports, so each such face has even support.
For `q>=k` this contradicts a singleton face. For `q<k` the even-face fact
gives `w>=2^(q+1)>2q`, also a contradiction. Finally, a `(k-1)`-star has
`2(p-k+1)` extensions. Hence

```text
d(C^+_{p,k}) = d(C^-_{p,k}) = 2(p-k+1).                 (1)
```

The equality for `C^-` follows from containment and the same nonempty-star
witness. This distance formula is the inherited result of #732.

## Theorem: uniform classification of the minimum words

**For `k>=2` and `p>=k+2`, the minimum nonzero words in either `C^+_{p,k}`
or `C^-_{p,k}` are exactly `m_J`, where J ranges over the signed
`(k-1)`-ports.**

Put `q=p-k>=2`. Let W be the support of a minimum word in `C^+`, so
`w=|W|=2(q+1)`. Call an original coordinate pair **universal** if every port
of W uses that pair. Let c be the number of universal pairs, and let `d_r`
count support ports using pair r.

For a nonuniversal pair r, the restriction to ports avoiding r is nonzero
and belongs to `C^+_{p-1,k}`. By (1), its weight is at least `2q`. Consequently

```text
w-d_r >= 2q,    so d_r <= 2.
```

Count original-pair incidences in W:

```text
k w = sum_r d_r <= c w + 2(p-c).
```

Substitution of `w=2(q+1)` and `p=k+q` gives `q(k-c-1)<=0`, so `c>=k-1`.
Certainly `c<=k`.

If `c=k`, W lies in one ordinary sign cube. The generalized-face construction
from the distance proof again forces even parity on every face fixing at
most q signs. If `q>=k`, singleton faces give a contradiction. Otherwise

```text
|W| >= 2^(q+1) > 2(q+1),    since q>=2,
```

contradicting minimal weight. Thus `c=k-1`.

Let K be these k-1 universal pair indices. Every support port contains K and
one additional pair r. There are q+1 possible r, each with `d_r<=2`, and
their counts sum to `2(q+1)`. Hence each r occurs in exactly two support
ports. Write `W_r` for this two-point block in the cube on `K union {r}`.

**Each block is an edge.** Fix i in K and choose s outside `K union {r}`.
For any signs a,b, form a generalized cube with slot `{i^a,s^b}` and ordinary
two-sign slots on every other pair of `K union {r}`. The half using s omits
universal pair i, so contains no support. Thus the i=a face of `W_r` has even
cardinality. The two points of `W_r` must agree at i. Applying this to every
i in K shows that these distinct points agree on K and differ only at r.
Let `a_i(r)` denote their common sign at i.

**All edges have the same signs on K.** Choose distinct r,s outside K and a
third outside pair t; this is possible because `q+1>=3`. Fix i in K. Build
a generalized cube with slots `{r^b,s^c}` and `{i^a,t^d}`, and ordinary slots
on the pairs in `K\{i}`. Branches using t omit universal pair i, so contain
no support. The other branches meet `W_r` in one point precisely when
`a_i(r)=a`, and meet `W_s` in one point precisely when `a_i(s)=a`. Parity gives

```text
1[a_i(r)=a] + 1[a_i(s)=a] = 0.
```

Therefore `a_i(r)=a_i(s)`. This holds for all i,r,s. The common signed
coordinates on K form a `(k-1)`-port J, and W consists of exactly all its
extensions. Hence the evaluation vector is `m_J`. Every such star has
minimum weight, completing the classification for `C^+`. Containment and
the nonempty witnesses give precisely the same minima for `C^-`.

The support determines J by intersecting all its ports. Thus different J
give different words, and the exact total is

```text
number of minimum words = 2^(k-1) binom(p,k-1).          (2)
```

A fixed target T contains exactly k signed `(k-1)`-subsets, so it belongs to
exactly k minimum supports. This recovers #747's count of 160 words and four
patterns at `(p,k)=(6,4)` and extends it to all the stated parameters.

### Why the unused-pair hypothesis matters

At `p=k+1`, the strict inequality above becomes equality. For example, let m
be the missing pair of an ordinary k-cube, let `z_m` be the sum of its two
coordinate indicators, and fix k-2 signed coordinates J inside that cube.
Then `(1+z_m)m_J` has degree at most k-1 and is supported on exactly four
ports in that single cube. It is a minimum word, but is not a `(k-1)`-star.
For k>=3 its expansion uses only nonempty stars. In particular, at `(5,4)`
it supplies the kind of additional minimum pattern classified in #725.
For k=2 this particular expression uses the empty generator; no claim that
the two codes have identical q=1 classifications is being made.

## Consequence for a specified retained target

Suppose k is a power of two. For `0<j<k`, the overlap indicator has expansion

```text
1[|S intersect T|=j]
  = sum_{l=j}^k (binom(l,j) mod 2)
      sum_{L subset T, |L|=l} m_L(S).
```

At intersection size r the right side evaluates to
`binom(r,j)2^(r-j)`, which is one exactly at r=j. The top coefficient
`binom(k,j)` vanishes at a power-of-two k, so these corrections already lie
in `C^-`. This absorption identity is credited to #725/#732.

For a surviving family `F=P\D` containing T, a decoder gives a full-family
word f with `f(T)=1` and support contained in `{T} union D`. Therefore
`|D|>=2(p-k)+1` by (1). At equality, f is a minimum word, so the new theorem
forces it to equal `m_J` for one of the k subsets `J subset T`, `|J|=k-1`.
Its support has size `|D|+1`; thus D is exactly all other extensions of J.

There are consequently **exactly k optimal deletion sets**, all attained by
a target-local single star. Global stars do not add optimal deletion patterns
when `p>=k+2`. They may provide other coefficient expressions, and the
classification says nothing about patterns with more deletions.

All other ports remain in the surviving family as independent nuisance
inputs: this is one specified target with the stated D removed, not an
all-target information-set construction. For non-power-of-two k the minimum
codeword theorem still holds, but the decoder consequence does not: permitted
overlap corrections can then isolate a target on the unpunctured family.

## Two targets: uniform second generalized weight

For a binary code C, its second generalized Hamming weight `d_2(C)` is the
smallest size of the union of supports of a two-dimensional linear subcode.
This is a support-union invariant; it is not the second-smallest nonzero
codeword weight.

**For every `k>=2` and `q=p-k>=2`, both `C^+` and `C^-` have
`d_2=4q+3`.** In particular, the values at q=2 and q=3 are eleven and fifteen.

### The lower bound eleven at p=k+2

Here (1) gives minimum weight six, and the classification says every word of
weight six is a `(k-1)`-star. Two distinct such stars intersect in at most
one port: their defining sets have union of size at least k, and a common
extension exists only when that union is an admissible k-port, in which case
it is the unique extension.

Let U be the support union of a two-dimensional subcode. Its three nonzero
words f,g,f+g satisfy

```text
wt(f) + wt(g) + wt(f+g) = 2|U|,                         (3)
```

because every coordinate in U is one on exactly two of those three words.
All three weights are even and at least six. If `|U|<=10`, their sum is at
most twenty. There must then be at least two weight-six words: otherwise
the sum is at least `6+8+8=22`. But two distinct weight-six stars have union
at least `6+6-1=11`, a contradiction. Hence `d_2>=11`.

### Induction for every q>=3

Induct on q, using the q=2 lower bound just proved. Suppose a two-dimensional
subcode of `C^+_{p,k}` has support union U of size `n<=4q+2`. Its three
nonzero words have even weights at least `2q+2`. For any original pair r,
let `d_r` count ports of U using r. Whenever restriction to ports avoiding r
is injective on this subcode, its image is a two-dimensional subcode of
`C^+_{p-1,k}`. The induction hypothesis then gives

```text
n-d_r >= 4(q-1)+3 = 4q-1,    hence d_r <= 3.            (4)
```

There are two cases.

**Case 1: a nonzero word has minimum weight.** Two distinct minimum stars
already have union at least `2(2q+2)-1=4q+3`, so there is exactly one such
word `f=m_J`. Write A for its support and g,h=f+g for the other words.
Evenness implies `wt(g),wt(h)>=2q+4`. Because any two distinct nonzero words
of the subcode have support union U,

```text
|A intersect supp(g)| = wt(f)+wt(g)-n >= 4,
|A intersect supp(h)| = wt(f)+wt(h)-n >= 4.
```

There are q+1 residual pairs outside J's k-1 pair indices, and f uses each in
exactly two ports. After avoiding any such pair, f is still nonzero, and g
and h each retain at least two support points inside A. All three nonzero
words survive, so the restriction is injective and (4) applies. Since two of
the at most three ports using that pair already lie in A, at most one lies
in `B=U\A`.

On the other hand, (3) gives

```text
n >= ((2q+2)+(2q+4)+(2q+4))/2 = 3q+5,
|B| = n-(2q+2) >= q+3.
```

Every k-port of B uses at least one of the q+1 residual pairs. Their combined
capacity in B is at most q+1, contradicting `|B|>=q+3`.

**Case 2: there is no minimum-weight word.** All three nonzero weights are
at least `2q+4`, so (3) gives `n>=3q+6`.

Say that a pair is universal for a nonzero word when every port in its
support uses that pair. If pair r is universal for one word f but not for
another g, the restriction of g to ports avoiding r is nonzero and has
weight at least `2q` by (1). That support is disjoint from the support of f,
so

```text
n >= wt(f) + wt(g restricted away from r)
  >= (2q+4)+2q = 4q+4,
```

a contradiction. Therefore all three words have the same set K of universal
pair indices. Write `c=|K|`. For each pair outside K, all three nonzero words
survive restriction, so (4) applies. For each pair in K, all n support ports
use it. Counting original-pair incidences gives

```text
k n <= c n + 3(p-c),    or (k-c)(n-3) <= 3q.
```

But `n-3>=3q+3>3q`. Since `k-c` is a nonnegative integer, it must be zero.
Thus c=k and the entire support U lies in one ordinary sign cube. For each
nonzero word, generalized-cube parity forces even support on every face
fixing at most q signs, as in the distance proof. If `q>=k`, singleton faces
contradict nonzero support. If `q<k`, the even-face bound gives

```text
wt(f) >= 2^(q+1) > 4q+2 >= n,    for q>=3,
```

again a contradiction. Both cases are impossible. This completes the
inductive lower bound `d_2(C^+)>=4q+3`, also valid for the subcode `C^-`.

### A general upper witness and precise target-pair interpretation

Put `q=p-k>=1`. Take any admissible k-port W and omit two different
coordinates to form `(k-1)`-sets `J_1,J_2`. Their stars each have weight
`2(q+1)` and intersect exactly at W. They span a two-dimensional subcode
with support union

```text
2(q+1) + 2(q+1) - 1 = 4q+3.
```

This proves `d_2(C^+)<=d_2(C^-)<=4q+3`, attaining the lower bound for every
q>=2. The uniform exact formula therefore holds with or without the empty
generator. The same construction gives an upper bound at q=1, where no exact
second-weight statement is claimed here.

At a power-of-two k, if two distinct retained targets are decodable after
deleting D, their two unit coordinate vectors on F lift to independent
full-family codewords whose support union is contained in `D union {T_1,T_2}`.
It follows that **`|D|>=d_2-2=4q+1`** for every q>=2: nine deletions at q=2,
thirteen at q=3, and so on.

Here are attaining target pairs, making the sharpness quantifier explicit.
Write `W={w_1,...,w_k}`, choose a signed coordinate v from a pair outside W,
and set

```text
J_1 = W\{w_1},          T_1 = J_1 union {v},
J_2 = W\{w_2},          T_2 = J_2 union {v}.
```

Then `T_1` lies in the first star support but not the second, and conversely
for `T_2`. Delete the other `4q+1` ports in the union of the two star supports.
On the surviving family the two stars equal `x_{T_1}` and `x_{T_2}`. Every
other port outside that union is retained and contributes zero to both totals.
Thus `4q+1` deletions are attainable for these explicit pairs, sharply for
every q>=2. This generalized-weight argument alone does not establish the
optimum for every prescribed pair. [EQUALITY-PAIR.md](EQUALITY-PAIR.md) gives
that additional classification for every q>=2. These results do not supply
the all-target information sets of #747/#772.

## Nonlinear postprocessing of the same observations gives no escape

Fix the surviving family and collect the available binary linear totals as
rows of an observation matrix A. Inputs range over all independent binary
assignments. An arbitrary exact function g satisfying `g(Ax)=x_T` exists if
and only if the target coordinate row `e_T` belongs to `rowspace(A)`.

Indeed, for every `h in ker(A)`, x and x+h yield identical observations, so
exact recovery requires `h_T=0`. Thus `ker(A) subset ker(e_T)`, equivalently
`e_T in rowspace(A)` by finite-dimensional binary linear algebra. Conversely,
a row-space representation supplies a linear decoder. The same argument
applies to each coordinate of a multiple-target output.

Consequently arbitrary nonlinear postprocessing of these same linear star
and overlap observations cannot evade the threshold, equality classification,
or two-target lower bound. This does not include new nonlinear helper
observables, restricted input distributions, approximate recovery, or external
side information.

## Interpretation and limits

The new results are the uniform minimum-word equality classification and
its stated second-generalized-weight consequence. The inherited distance,
finite `(6,4)` classification, uniform ranks and all-target thresholds retain
the credits above. Finite checks can corroborate the arguments but do not
replace the all-parameter proofs.

Neither the number of generator terms nor existence of a scalar decoder
prices preparation, source reads, copies, dirty restoration, legal frames,
hosting, recursive calls, or analytic transfer. The results therefore do not
construct a paid supplier, change the selected construction, or improve κ.
