# The fifth generalized weight and the second-weight codewords

**A uniform five-weight prefix; no new κ.** For every `k>=2` and `q=p-k>=3`,
both signed proper-star codes have

```text
d_5(C^-_{p,k}) = d_5(C^+_{p,k}) = 10q.
```

Together with the preceding notes, this determines their first five weights
for the whole range:

```text
(d_1,d_2,d_3,d_4,d_5) = (2q+2, 4q+3, 6q+3, 8q+2, 10q).
```

The proof also classifies every word of the second nonzero weight `4q+2`: it
is exactly the sum of two adjacent minimum stars. These are scalar
linear-information statements. Five selectable targets therefore need
exactly `10q-5` deletions. No cost for acquiring the star totals, complete
paid supplier, or improvement to κ is asserted.

## Notation and dependencies

A port chooses k signed coordinates from distinct original pairs among p
pairs. A star `m_L` is the binary containment indicator of a signed partial
port L. The code `C^+_{p,k}` spans stars with `0<=|L|<k`, and `C^-_{p,k}`
omits the empty star. For a code C, `d_r(C)` is the minimum support union of
an r-dimensional subcode. All vector spaces are over `F_2`. Lower bounds are
proved in `C^+`; the attaining constructions lie in `C^-`.

We use the following results with their existing proofs and attribution:

- [MINIMUM-WORDS.md](MINIMUM-WORDS.md): minimum distance `2q+2` and
  minimum-star rigidity for `q>=2`; the `q=1` minimum-distance bound.
- [EQUALITY-PAIR.md](EQUALITY-PAIR.md): `d_2=4q+3`, with optimal planes
  generated by two adjacent minimum stars.
- [HIGHER-WEIGHTS.md](HIGHER-WEIGHTS.md): the full gap above minimum
  weight, `d_3=6q+3` for `q>=3`, and the `q=2` boundary values.
- [FOURTH-WEIGHT.md](FOURTH-WEIGHT.md): `d_4=8q+2` and classification
  of all optimal four-dimensional subcodes for `q>=3`.
- [CUBE-OPTIMIZERS.md](CUBE-OPTIMIZERS.md): at `q=2,k>=4`, every optimal
  rank-three support-fourteen and rank-four support-fifteen subcode is
  localized to one ordinary cube, and all its nonzero words have weight
  eight. The first five weights at this boundary are `(6,11,14,15,16)`.
- [HIGHER-GRAPH.md](HIGHER-GRAPH.md): the complete `k=2` hierarchy.

Generalized-cube and even-face parity are used in the form stated and
credited in [HIGHER-WEIGHTS.md](HIGHER-WEIGHTS.md#inputs-and-attribution).
An original pair is universal for a word or subcode if every occupied port
uses it. Its degree in a support union is the number of occupied ports using
it. Avoiding a pair decreases q by one; taking the sum of the two sign
slices at a universal pair decreases p and k by one and preserves q.
Shortening on a fixed signed face is exactly the lifted smaller proper-star
code, as proved in
[FOURTH-WEIGHT.md](FOURTH-WEIGHT.md#signed-face-shortening).

Every codeword has even weight. The weights of all nonzero words of an
r-dimensional subcode sum to `2^(r-1)` times its support size. An affine
coset of an r-dimensional subcode supported on A has total weight
`2^(r-1)|A|+2^r b`, where b is the weight of its common restriction outside
A. We use these averaging identities repeatedly.

## The attaining five-star construction

Fix a signed `(k-2)`-face K and five signed coordinates `a_1,...,a_5` from
distinct original pairs outside K. The five stars `m_(K+a_i)` have pairwise
intersections of size one, all distinct, and no triple intersections. Their
support union has size

```text
5(2q+2) - binom(5,2) = 10q.
```

They are independent. For each i, choose any j different from i and extend
`K+a_i` by the coordinate opposite to `a_j`. This port belongs to star i and
to none of the other four stars, giving an identity evaluation matrix. This
also works when `q=3`, where all five residual pairs are selected. All five
rows lie in `C^-`.

## An individual weight-eight lemma at q=2

For a word w of weight 8, every nonuniversal original pair has degree at
most 4: its avoidance is nonzero at q=1 and has minimum weight 4. If c
original pairs are universal and t=k-c, incidence gives

    8t <= 4(t+2),

so t<=2. Thus there are at least k-2 universal pairs.

There cannot be exactly k-1. In that case there are three residual-pair
blocks, each of weight at most 4. In the common-core notation, each residual
sign sum H_r has even parity on core faces fixing at most 2 signs, and
weight at most 4<8, hence vanishes. Collapse paired residual signs to scalar
functions F_r on core signs; each has support at most 2, and the three
support sizes sum 4. Every difference F_r+F_s has even parity on faces
fixing at most 1 sign, so if nonzero has weight at least 4.

If some F_r is zero, all other F_s have weight at most 2 and must also be
zero, contradiction. Otherwise the support sizes are 2,1,1. A size-one
function differs from any other on at most 3 positions, forcing all three
functions equal, again contradiction. Singleton-face cases are included in
the same even-face argument. Therefore the universal-pair count is exactly k
or exactly k-2.

If exactly k-2, fix the common original core K. The four residual pairs are
all nonuniversal and each has degree at most 4. Every occupied port uses two
of them, so their total degrees equal 16 and all four degrees are 4. Every
ordinary residual-edge block has even weight. Divide its weight by two and
form a weighted graph on the four residual pair indices. Every vertex has
degree 2. Consequently the graph is either two disjoint edges of weight 2,
or a four-cycle whose edges have weight 1.

The four-cycle is impossible. Write it r-s-t-u-r, each edge block having two
distinct occupied ports. In the r-s block these two ports agree on every
sign of K: fix a core sign by replacing its opposite choice in the
corresponding generalized-cube slot with a coordinate from one of the unused
residual pairs. The replacement branch omits a universal pair and vanishes,
so both fixed-sign parities are even.

Now take ordinary core slots, an ordinary r-slot, and the mixed slot
{s^a,t^b}. The r-t block is absent, so generalized-cube parity forces the
number of the two r-s ports with s-sign a to be even. Their s-signs are
therefore equal. An ordinary s-slot and mixed {r^a,u^b}, using the absent
s-u block, force equal r-signs too. They agree on all k coordinates and are
the same port, contradiction.

Thus an individual weight-eight word either lies in one ordinary cube, or
has exactly k-2 universal pairs and is supported on two complementary
residual-edge ordinary cubes, with four ports in each. We do not need any
further classification of those four-port slices here.

## Every second-weight word is an adjacent-star sum

For all k>=2 and q>=3, every word of weight 4q+2 is the sum of two adjacent
minimum stars. In particular its universal original-pair set has exactly k-2
elements, and all its ports fix the signs on that set.

First suppose avoiding some nonuniversal pair r leaves a minimum word of
weight 2q. Lift that minimum star to the full port set, obtaining a minimum
star g of weight d=2q+2. The lift has exactly two ports using r, and agrees
with w away from r. The word h=w+g is supported on r. The r-part of w has
size (4q+2)-2q=d, so

    wt(h)=d+2-2e,    e in {0,1,2}.

The full spectral gap excludes d+2<4q+2 and the minimum-distance bound
excludes d-2. Thus e=1 and h is a minimum star. Their intersection has size
1, since wt(w)=2d-2. Hence w is the claimed adjacent-star sum.

It remains to prove that such a minimum avoidance exists.

At q=3, suppose none exists. Every nonuniversal pair then has avoidance
weight at least 8, so degree at most 6. If c is the universal-pair count,
incidence gives

    (k-c)(14-6)<=6q=18,

forcing c>=k-2. The c=k case is impossible by the single-cube even-face
bound 16. If c=k-1, residual blocks have size at most 6. Common-core
sign-pairing and block equality make the weight a multiple of 2(q+1)=8, not
14. Thus c=k-2.

On the five residual pairs form the graph whose edge weights are half the
even ordinary-block weights. Its total edge weight is 7, and every vertex
degree is at most 3. The degree sequence must be (3,3,3,3,2). Delete any
degree-three vertex r. The remaining word has weight 8 at q=2, and contains
the same k-2 universal core pairs. By the weight-eight lemma it is either
one ordinary cube or two complementary four-port blocks. The former would
require an edge of graph weight 4, exceeding its endpoints' original degree
bound 3. Thus the latter holds, and every edge away from r has weight 0 or 2.
Every edge of the five-vertex graph avoids at least one of its four
degree-three vertices. All edge weights are consequently even, contradicting
the odd vertex degrees. This proves the q=3 base.

For q>=4 the existing gap theorem at q-1 suffices directly. If there is no
minimum avoidance, every nonuniversal pair has avoidance weight at least
4(q-1)+2=4q-2, and hence degree at most 4. Incidence gives

    (k-c)(4q-2)<=4q,

so c>=k-1. The one-cube case is excluded by its bound 2^(q+1)>4q+2. For
c=k-1, block sizes at most 4 give the common-core divisibility 2(q+1) |
(4q+2), which is impossible. The claim follows for all q>=3.

## The fifth-weight lower bound at q=3: pair kernels

Suppose V has dimension five and support U of size n<=29. We will derive a
contradiction. Minimum stars have weight 8; nonminimum words have weight at
least 14. Any five distinct minimum stars have union at least
5*8-binom(5,2)=30, so V contains at most four minimum words.

For a pair r, let L_r be the kernel of restriction to ports avoiding r.
Kernel support and avoidance support are disjoint. Kernel dimensions three
or four would give respectively

    d_3(q=3)+d_2(q=2)=21+11=32,
    d_4(q=3)+d_1(q=2)=26+6=32,

both impossible. A dimension-two kernel needs at least 15+14=29 ports. A
nonminimum dimension-one kernel needs at least 14+15=29. We now exclude
these equality cases using the q=2 optimizer classification.

### Excluding a two-dimensional kernel

Attaining rank-three support 14 or rank-four support 15 at q=2 forces k>=4:
the established k=2,3 third weight is 15 and generalized weights are
strictly increasing. Thus every invocation of the q=2 optimizer
classification below is inside its stated k>=4 domain.

Such a kernel can occur only when n=29. Its support A has size 15 and is an
optimal plane generated by two adjacent minimum stars f,g. The avoidance
image has support B of size 14 and dimension three; its seven nonzero words
all have weight 8, and its support is contained in an ordinary cube on a set
J of k original pairs, excluding r. Every occupied port using r belongs to
A.

Every global minimum word of V lies wholly in A or wholly in B: its
restriction to B is zero or has weight 8, its entire minimum weight. Fix i
in J and suppose i is not universal for V. The avoidance-i image is
supported inside A, so has support at most 15.

Its kernel cannot have dimension zero: a rank-five q=2 image needs at least
16 ports. It cannot have dimension two either. Such a kernel would be an
optimal plane with two adjacent minimum stars. They cannot lie one in A and
one in B because those supports are disjoint. They cannot both lie in B
because their union would have size 15>14. If both lie in A, their span is
L_r; then A uses i, as does B, making i universal after all.

A nonminimum kernel line would have weight 14. Equality in the support bound
forces all occupied i-ports to be precisely its support. Since B consists of
14 such ports, that word is the indicator of B. Its image under avoidance of
r would have weight 14, contradicting the constant weight 8 of the q=2 image
on B.

The remaining possibility is a minimum-star kernel line. Its rank-four q=2
image must have support at least 15, and hence exactly A; in particular all
of A avoids i. By the q=2 rank-four optimizer classification, every nonzero
word of that image has weight 8. But f+g, of weight 14, survives unchanged
on A, contradiction.

Therefore every i in J is universal for V. A port in A would then have to
use those k pairs and also r, impossible. This excludes every
two-dimensional pair kernel.

### Excluding a nonminimum kernel line

Again equality is required: n=29, its word has weight 14 on A, and its
rank-four q=2 image has support B of size 15. That optimizer is localized to
an ordinary cube on k pair indices J excluding r. For i in J, the
avoidance-i image is supported in A and has size at most 14. If i were
nonuniversal, a zero kernel would leave rank-five and need at least 16
ports; a one-dimensional kernel would leave rank-four and need at least 15.
Kernel dimension two was just excluded, as were three and four. Thus i is
universal for every i in J, incompatible with A's ports using r.

Consequently every original-pair avoidance kernel is zero, a line generated
by a minimum star, or all V.

## The fifth-weight lower bound at q=3: low-weight words

There is a word of weight 14 in V. Otherwise all nonzero weights are 8 or at
least 16. With m<=4 minimum words, their sum is at least 496-8m>=464. Since
it equals 16n<=464, equality requires n=29, m=4, and all other words of
weight 16. But the four minimum stars have total individual size 32 and
union at most 29, so some two intersect. Their sum has weight 14 after all.

By the second-weight classification a weight 14 word fixes a signed core K
of size k-2, which is exactly its universal original-pair set. A pair in K
has an avoidance kernel containing this nonminimum word; by the pair-kernel
exclusions that kernel must be all V. Thus K is universal for V. It is
exactly V's universal pair set, since no additional pair is universal for
the weight 14 word.

Every minimum star in V contains K in its defining face. Otherwise one could
extend that face using an available pair different from the omitted core
pair, contradicting universality. It therefore fixes the signs on K. The
second-weight classification likewise shows that every weight 14 word of V
fixes all K signs. Shortening on any fixed signed assignment to K is exactly
the lift of the graph code C+_(5,2). Hence the subcode W spanned by all
weight 8 and weight 14 words lies in the direct sum, over core-sign
assignments, of those lifted graph codes. Put a=dim W.

We first prove a>=4. If a<=3 there are at most 7 nonzero words in W. Also
m<=3: any four distinct minimum stars are independent, since a sum of three
has weight at least 3*8-2*3=18>8. Every nonzero word outside W has weight at
least 16. Consequently the total word weight is at least

    8m+14(7-m)+24*16 = 482-6m >= 464.

Using seven slots for W can only lower this bound when a<3. Since the actual
sum is 16n<=464, equality forces n=29, m=3, a=3, and all four remaining
nonzero words of W to have weight 14. The three minimum stars span W, but
their sum has weight at least 18. Contradiction.

If a=5, then V itself lies in that direct sum of graph codes. Let r_sigma be
the ranks of its projections into nonzero sign sectors. Their sum is at
least five; choose smaller nonnegative ranks summing exactly five. The first
five graph weights at q=3 are

    d_r(C+_(5,2))=8r-binom(r,2),    1<=r<=5.

The support in the direct sum is at least the sum of these weights. For any
partition sum r_sigma=5 this sum equals

    (85-sum r_sigma^2)/2 >= (85-25)/2 = 30,

contradicting n<=29. Thus a=4.

## The fifth-weight lower bound at q=3: the final coset

Let A be the support of W and B=U\A. The scalar fourth-weight theorem gives
|A|>=26. All sixteen words in the other coset of W have weight at least 16.
Their total weight is 8|A|+16|B|=16n-8|A|. Therefore

    256 <= 16n-8|A|,    so |A| <= 2n-32 <= 26.

Every inequality is an equality: n=29, |A|=26, |B|=3, and all sixteen coset
words have weight 16. By the fourth-weight equality classification, W is a
four-star clique. Its four private column labels each occur 5 times, and its
six pair-intersection column labels each occur once.

Choose a representative h of the other coset. Since W vanishes on B and B is
the union's outside support, h is one at all three B-ports. Thus h+w has
weight 13 on A for every w in W. Label A by its nonzero evaluation vectors x
in F_2^4. For each label x define

    c_x = sum over ports with label x of (-1)^(h(port)).

The equality wt((h+w)|A)=13 for every w says

    sum_x c_x (-1)^(w.x) = |A|-2*13 = 0

for every w in F_2^4. Fourier inversion forces every c_x=0. Thus every
nonzero label occurs an even number of times, half with each h-value. This
contradicts the multiplicity-one pair-intersection labels (and also the
multiplicity-five private labels) of the four-star clique.

All rank-five supports of size at most 29 are excluded. The five-star
construction gives support 30, proving `d_5=30` at `q=3` for every `k>=2`.

## Induction to every q>=4

For q>=4 put d=2q+2, s=4q+2, and suppose a rank-five subcode V has union U
of size n<=10q-1. Assume d_5(q-1)=10q-10. Pair avoidance kernels of ranks
2,3,4 would give respectively

    d_2(q)+d_3(q-1)=10q,
    d_3(q)+d_2(q-1)=10q+2,
    d_4(q)+d_1(q-1)=10q+2.

A rank-one nonminimum kernel gives at least s+d_4(q-1)=12q-4>n. Thus every
kernel is zero, a minimum-star line, or all V. Injective avoidance gives
pair degree at most 9; a minimum-star kernel gives at most n-d_4(q-1)<=d+3.
A pair in two minimum-star cores is therefore universal for V. There are at
most four minimum words, since any five minimum-star supports have union at
least 5d-10=10q.

Exactly k-1 universal original pairs are impossible. Each residual block has
degree at most 9 (none of these pairs lies in a minimum-star core; any
minimum word in its kernel would have its full core among the universal
pairs, making the residual pair nonuniversal for that word). Sign-sum parity
forces even blocks, hence degree at most 8. Collapsing residual signs gives
vector blocks of size at most 4; pairwise differences have weight at most
8<2^q, so are zero. All q+1 blocks are identical, hence n>=5d>10q-1.

Let m be the number of minimum stars in V.

### m=0

Let c be the global universal-pair count and t=k-c. Incidence gives

    t(n-9)<=9q.

All 31 nonzero words have weight at least s, so n>=ceil(31s/16). For q>=4,
2(n-9)>9q, forcing t<=1. The t=1 case is the common-core contradiction. If
t=0, ordinary-cube even-face parity gives every nonzero word weight at least
2^(q+1), or zero if singleton faces apply. Averaging forces
n>=31*2^(q-3)>10q-1. Contradiction.

### m=1

Let A be the minimum star's support and B=U\A. The outside image has rank 4.
Each nonzero image word comes from two nonminimum words, so has weight at
least s-d/2=3q+1. Averaging gives

    |B|>=ceil(15(3q+1)/8).

For global-core deficit t, the t-1 nonglobal defining pairs have B-degree at
most 3, and the q+1 other pairs at most 9-2=7. Thus

    t(|B|-3)<=7q+4.

Twice the left factor's lower bound exceeds 7q+4 for q>=4, so t<=1. The
common-core contradiction applies.

### m=2

Let the two stars meet in e in {0,1}; their union A has size 2d-e. The full
kernel outside A is their span since |A|<d_3(q). The image has rank 3. Each
nonzero image word comes from four nonminimum words, so has weight at least
ceil(s-|A|/2)=2q+e. Therefore

    4|B|>=7(2q+e).

Coincident original cores give the common-core contradiction. Otherwise
write their intersection size k-1-u, u>=1. The 2u private core pairs have
B-degree at most 1+e; the q+1-u other pairs have B-degree at most 9-4=5.
Every B-port uses u+1 slots outside the universal intersection, yielding

    (u+1)|B|<=2u(1+e)+5(q+1-u)=5q+5+u(2e-3).

For e=0, the left is at least 7q while the right is at most 5q+2,
impossible. For e=1, adjacency gives u=1; the left is at least 7q+4 while
the right is 5q+4, also impossible.

### m=3

Pairs occur in zero, one, or all three cores. Let the common core be K and
each private core have size u. If u=0, common-core contradiction applies. A
private pair has A-degree d+4 minus its star's number of intersections with
the other two. Since its union degree is at most d+3, each star has an
adjacent neighbor. Disjoint private cores force u=1, and intersection is
equality of the signs on K, so the three-star adjacency graph is a union of
cliques with no isolated vertex: it is a triangle.

Thus A has size 6q+3. The outside kernel is exactly this rank 3 span, since
|A|<d_4(q), and the image has rank 2. Each of its three nonzero words comes
from eight nonminimum words, so has weight at least ceil(s-|A|/2)=q+1. Hence

    2|B|>=3(q+1).

The three selected residual pairs have B-degree at most 1, and the other q-1
residual pairs have B-degree at most 9-6=3. Incidence gives

    2|B|<=3+3(q-1)=3q,

contradiction.

### m=4

Pairs occur in zero, one, or all four minimum-star cores. Write their common
core K and private sets of equal size u. If u=0, common-core contradiction
applies. A private pair has union degree at least d+6-j, where j is that
star's number of neighbors among the other three; those other stars cannot
intersect on this private pair. The bound d+3 forces j=3 for each star. Thus
u=1 and the four stars share one signed (k-2)-core, with four distinct
residual coordinates. Their union A has size 8q+2.

Their span has rank 4; the other sixteen words are nonminimum. The coset
identity gives

    |B|>=s-|A|/2=1.

Every selected residual pair already has A-degree d+3, so B avoids all four.
Each of the other q-2 residual pairs has A-degree 8 and union degree at most
9, so its B-degree is at most 1. Each B-port uses two of these pairs. Any
ordinary cube containing such a port has exactly one occupied point, since
the degree bound permits no other B-port on either residual pair and A uses
a selected pair. This contradicts vector ordinary-cube parity. The q=3 base
is proved above, so this completes the induction.

## All rank-five optimizers for q>=4

For every k>=2,q>=4, an optimal rank-five subcode is exactly the span of
five minimum stars with a common signed (k-2)-core and five residual signed
coordinates from distinct original pairs. The count is

    2^(k+3) binom(p,k-2) binom(q+2,5).

Prescribed five targets attain 10q-5 deletions exactly when some such
five-star matrix has rank five on those targets.

### Pair kernels

Put N=10q, d=2q+2, s=4q+2. A pair-avoidance kernel of rank three or four would
give at least 10q+2 ports. A nonminimum line would give s+d_4(q-1)=12q-4>N
for q>=4. Thus proper kernels have rank one, generated by a minimum star, or
rank two.

If a pair has rank two kernel L, the kernel and image bounds sum exactly N:

    d_2(q)+d_3(q-1) = (4q+3)+(6q-3)=10q.

Both are optimal: L contains two minimum stars, and the image is a
three-star triangle with three independent minimum words of weight 2q. Each
such image word has four lifts. Their sum of weights is

    2(4q+3)+4(2q)=16q+6 < 4s.

Thus one lift is a minimum star. Choosing one for each of the three
independent image minima gives five independent minimum stars in V.

It remains to rule out fewer minima when no rank two pair kernel exists.
Every injective avoidance has pair degree at most N-d_5(q-1)=10. A
minimum-star kernel gives degree at most N-d_4(q-1)=d+4. A pair lying in at
least two minimum-star defining cores is universal for all V. There are at
most five minimum stars, since six would have union at least 6d-15=12q-3>N.

### Common-core exclusion

If exactly k-1 original pairs are universal for V, every other pair has
injective avoidance: a minimum star supported on the common original core is
not universal on any residual pair. Every residual block has size at most 10.
Its sign sum vanishes by even-face weight bound 2^(q+1)>10. The collapsed
blocks have size at most 5; their pairwise differences have weight at most
10<2^q and vanish. All q+1 blocks are identical. Rank five therefore needs at
least five distinct nonzero labels and N>=5d=10q+10, contradiction.

### Zero or one minimum word

With no minima, let t=k-c, where c is the global universal-pair count.
Incidence gives t(N-10)<=10q, forcing t<=1. The t=1 case is the preceding
common-core contradiction. For t=0, the single-cube even-face bound and
averaging give N>=31*2^(q-3)>10q, also impossible.

With one minimum star, its support A has size d, and B=U\A has size
N-d=8q-2. If t=k-c, the t-1 nonglobal defining pairs have B-degree at most 4,
and the q+1 other pairs at most 8. Thus

    t(|B|-4)<=8q+4.

For q>=4 this forces t<=1, again the common-core contradiction.

### Two minimum words

Let their intersection size be e in {0,1}. Their union A has size 2d-e, so
|B|=6q-4+e. Coincident original-pair cores are excluded by the common-core
argument. Otherwise write the common core size as k-1-u, u>=1. The 2u private
pairs have B-degree at most 2+e; the q+1-u pairs outside both cores have
B-degree at most 6. Therefore

    (u+1)(6q-4+e) <= 2u(2+e)+6(q+1-u)
                       = 6q+6+2u(e-1).

For e=0 the left is at least 12q-8 and the right at most 6q+4. For e=1,
adjacency gives u=1, and the left is 12q-6 while the right is 6q+6. Both are
impossible for q>=4.

### Three minimum words

Every original pair belongs to zero, one, or all three defining cores. Write
the common core as K and the three disjoint private core sets as having
equal size u. The case u=0 is the common-core contradiction.

If u>=2, no two stars can intersect. Their union A has size 3d=6q+6, and
|B|=4q-6. A private pair already has A-degree d+4, its full allowance, so B
avoids all 3u private pairs. There are q+1-2u original pairs outside all
three cores. Each has A-degree 6 and union degree at most 10, so its
B-degree is at most 4. Every B-port uses u+1 such pairs. Hence

    (u+1)(4q-6)<=4(q+1-2u),

impossible for q>=4,u>=2.

If u=1, let e be the number of pairwise star intersections. Their
intersection graph is a union of cliques indexed by agreement of the signs
on K, so e is 0, 1, or 3. The three pair intersections, when present, are
distinct, and no triple intersection occurs. Thus

    |A|=3d-e,    |B|=4q-6+e.

For a selected residual pair, its A-degree is d+4 minus its star's number of
neighbors. Its B-degree is consequently at most that number of neighbors.
These three capacities sum 2e. The q-1 other residual pairs have B-degree at
most 4. Counting two residual slots per B-port gives

    2(4q-6+e)<=2e+4(q-1),

which would require q<=2. Contradiction.

### Four minimum words

Pairs again occur in zero, one, or all four defining cores. Write their
common core as K, with disjoint private sets of size u. The case u=0 was
excluded. A private pair of a star with j neighbors among the other three
has A-degree d+6-j. Since its union degree is at most d+4, j>=2. An
intersection forces u=1, and the intersection graph is a union of cliques
indexed by the signs on K. A graph on four vertices whose components are
cliques and whose minimum degree is at least 2 must be K4. Thus the four
stars form a clique with

    |A|=8q+2,    |B|=2q-2.

Each of the four selected residual pairs has A-degree d+3 and B-degree at
most 1. Each of the q-2 other residual pairs has A-degree 8 and B-degree at
most 2. Incidence gives

    2|B|<=4+2(q-2)=2q,

contradicting 2|B|=4q-4 for q>=4.

### Geometry, uniqueness, and targets

All cases with fewer than five minimum stars are excluded. The five are
independent: any sum of j of them,2<=j<=4, has weight at least
jd-2*binom(j,2)>d, so cannot be another minimum star. Their union bound
5d-binom(5,2)=N is attained. Hence every pair intersects in one port, and no
three share a port. As in the fourth-weight classification, these faces have
a common signed (k-2)-core and five distinct residual signed coordinates
from distinct original pairs.

A sum of j of these five stars has weight j(2q+3-j),1<=j<=5. Only j=1 is
minimum. Thus the five generators, their core, and their unordered residual
coordinates are recovered from the subcode. The count is

    2^(k-2) binom(p,k-2) * 2^5 binom(q+2,5),

as claimed. For prescribed targets `T_1,...,T_5`, form

```text
E_ij = 1[K union {a_i} is contained in T_j].
```

Exactly `10q-5` deletions suffice if and only if some such E is invertible.
For sufficiency, left-multiply the column of star rows by `E^(-1)` and
delete their union outside the target set. For necessity, five unit
decoders at that budget span an optimal subcode and thus have the stated
five-star form. Their restriction to the targets must have full rank.
Failure of the criterion gives a lower bound of `10q-4` deletions; no exact
larger budget is claimed.

## Simultaneous targets and the scalar scope

An r-dimensional subcode with support W has r independent evaluation
columns. Selecting those coordinates as targets and changing the row basis
makes their evaluation matrix the identity. Deleting the other `|W|-r`
coordinates then gives simultaneous unit decoders. Conversely, r unit
decoders supported on the targets and deletions are independent, so their
combined support has size at least `d_r`.

Consequently the five-star witness and lower bound give exactly `10q-5`
deletions for five selectable targets. The private ports in the construction
give an explicit information set. At power-of-two k, the original
overlap-class corrections are already in the nonempty proper-star span, so
the same statement applies to that corrected scalar model.
