E8 section kernels and the two compiler interfaces
==================================================

This note distinguishes the local certificate in code/e8_sections.py from
an end-to-end exponent claim. Counts and center bills are read from that
certificate; this note does not replace the complete cost receipt.

1. What the new complex-side pair supplies

For squared-norm-four lattice representatives v,w, put

    K(v,w) = ((v.w)^3 - 4(v.w))/48,
    B(v,w) = 1 + (v.w mod 2).

Both diagonal entries equal one. At distinct lines, K can be nonzero only
when v.w is odd, so B is then zero. The certified E8^2 section has 30,960
ports, rank_Q(K)=816 and rank_F2(B)=17. A binary Gram realization of B gives
the norm-one source lines and target caps used by the complex Clifford
frame interface. The symmetric dyadic factorizations in e8_sections.py
supply an exact scalar center and certified binary source-support widths.
Those widths are dimensions over F2, not over Q.

The use of rational dyadic coefficients is compatible with the retained
complex scalar primitives. The general Clifford frame lemma also admits
degenerate binary intermediate subspaces; their dimensions alone do not
impose an orthonormal-basis condition. It still requires exact frame
representatives, common labels on every scalar gate, and paid residual
recipes. In particular, a matrix factorization by itself is not a local
transparent word, a helper allocation, or a complete recursive instance.

The relative-sign pair (u,v),(u,-v) has equal binary labels. Consequently
its rational Walsh change H2 on one side and H2/2 on the inverse side can
be performed within an equal-label group. It preserves the local common
frame. This observation removes no helper, exit, restoration or lifetime
obligation unless an emitted word actually uses that change of basis.

2. Why the bit dimension is not automatically 17

The bit interchange interface uses rational address projectors. Its scalar
payload can be F2, or any fixed finite alphabet, but this is independent of
the address-label field. For example, the existing E8 bit word has rational
address dimension eight, while the E8 complex word has binary address
label dimension nine. Its bit checker requires exact rational
orthogonality of each source line to the target cap.

An F2 scalar product being zero says that an integer lift has even scalar
product; it does not say that its rational scalar product is zero. Neither
a dyadic scalar factorization nor reduction of its coefficients modulo an
odd prime converts a binary orthogonality certificate into a rational one.
Thus the h=17 complex center widths cannot be inserted into the bit cost
model without an additional rational address construction.

The fixed finite-alphabet transfer does permit the scalar dyadic word to
be reduced modulo an odd prime. That permits finite pointwise gates on
bit inputs encoded as the symbols zero and one, with all scratch restored
at the boundary. The address prime is chosen separately to preserve the
finite rational matrix and minor tables. This transfer changes neither
the rational address geometry nor its charged ranks.

3. A concrete dual bit construction, with a larger frame

The same pair can exchange its characteristic-zero and characteristic-two
roles. Take the rational cubic feature space of K as the address space,
and use B as the scalar matrix over F2. The exact symmetric diagonal
factorization gives a nondegenerate rational form with signature (800,16)
in dimension 816. Each port has norm one in this form. Whenever a distinct
pair has B(v,w)=1, its integer scalar product is even, hence K(v,w)=0.
Therefore all entries of the off-diagonal matrix A=B+I can be delivered
along rationally orthogonal source and target lines.

A finite, deliberately uncompressed local implementation is available:
use the rank-17 factorization of B for 17 full-frame center totals, and a
separate helper for every ordered nonzero entry of A. Copy the associated
source through that helper's norm-one line and inject it into its target
cap. A source line is contained in that cap. Both are nondegenerate, even
though the ambient form is indefinite; nested orthogonal-projector
transitions are therefore legitimate. Apply the standard old-value/new-
value transparent wrapper to restore arbitrary helper and center data.
The target contribution is B+A=I over F2.

This proves the existence of a local bit scalar/frame construction with
rational frame dimension 816 and a full-frame center bill at most
17*816=13,872. It does not prove a useful bit saving: the direct helper
count can be quadratic in the number of ports. A useful instance needs a
much smaller reversible aggregation circuit, exact nondegeneracy for all
of its rational intermediate frames, and its own complete paid profile.
No minimality assertion is made about the rational frame dimension or
the center bill.

The complex free Walsh step does not transfer unchanged. For roots u,v
of squared norm two, (u,v).(u,-v)=0, so these two ports have K-pairing zero
and norm one individually. They are distinct orthogonal rational address
lines in this dual construction. A Walsh gate touching both cannot be
placed at their separate one-dimensional source frames without a new
common-frame schedule and its rank charges.

4. What remains before an exponent claim

A useful local word must specify both orientations, every scalar operation
and common frame, all scratch and source cleanup on arbitrary input
columns, and actual center-copy lifetimes. Its entire physical recursive
list must then include helper entry and exit residuals, all-role endpoints,
data corrections, adapters, remainder children, and any hosting owners.
The finite scalar/precision, tape, row and stopping inequalities and the
appropriate exact moment engines must be rerun on that list. Finally the
complex and bit receipts must be composed through the retained outer
inequalities. Improving only one rail can leave the other rail as the
limiting quantity. No numerical kappa follows from the kernel ranks or
center bill alone.

  result; not a record of every more recent open research PR.
