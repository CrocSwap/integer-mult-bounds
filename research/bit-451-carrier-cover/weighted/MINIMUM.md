The integer logarithm power 32 is sufficient at every compiler height. Every smaller positive integer power fails at height 5 for the **same rational envelope**. This is an exact decision for q=3/4, scale h=1804, numerator shift 7/6, denominator shift 319/100 and slope 23/20. **All heights, all integer powers.** For height G≥0 and X∼Binomial(G+1,3/4), put U=(7/6+X)/(319/100+23G/20). Every possible U is strictly between zero and one: its numerator is at most 13/6+G, strictly below the denominator at every nonnegative G. Thus E[U^p] is strictly decreasing in p at *each* G. A common bound λ<η verified uniformly for p=32 holds for all larger powers. One counterexample above η at p=31 also excludes all smaller positive integer powers at that same height. This argument uses monotonicity of the actual fixed-envelope expectation, not monotonicity of its sufficient coefficient tests.

The independent engine evaluates N(G)=E[(7/6+X)^p] and D(G)=(319/100+23G/20)^p. In particular N(G) is the integer sum Σ_i binom(G+1,i)·3^i·(7+6i)^p divided by 4^(G+1)·6^p. As functions of G both N and D are polynomials of degree ≤p, since every integer binomial moment is a linear combination of falling factorials of its trial count. For any nonnegative shift K, their jth forward differences at K are exactly j! times the coefficients of (G−K)_j. These falling factorials are nonnegative at every integer G≥K. Consequently the maximum of the forward-difference ratios bounds the entire tail if the denominator differences are positive and the numerator differences nonnegative. A finite prefix can be evaluated directly for G<K. This is a finite, exact rational all-height certificate in the setting of zxv’s weighted theorem and Ali Romman’s fixed-law all-height comparison.
