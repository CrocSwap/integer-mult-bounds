Rectangular prescribed-basis compatibility: dimension-free A1 argument and A3 transfer

Status: written candidate proof for independent review. Exact witnesses and
combinatorial conditions accompany it; no theorem/PR is claimed by this worker.
Credits: icekylinx's PR18 prescribed family and Zhihao Chen's PR21 translated
auxiliary exit. The construction below isolates their dimension dependencies.

Let a,b,k be integers, k=2a+s with 0<s<a, H=ab, n=a+k, 2n<=H, H>2k.
Set I=(0,...,a-1), S=(0,...,s-1), R=[I,S,I,I], C=[I,I,S,I]. Require:
(1) For each beta modulo b the first-n row prescriptions and last-n inverse-
column prescriptions define a partial permutation of a coordinates, and their
unspecified blocks admit an invertible completion.
(2) The graph with edges (R_i,a+(i mod b)), 0<=i<a+b-1, is a tree; the graph
with edges (C_j,a+((H-n+j) mod b)), n-(a+b-1)<=j<n, is a tree.
(3) a mod b and k mod b are nonzero.
Use K=T2(I_a tensor L2), J_beta=M_beta L1, and the matched outer family
S_outer=T(I_k tensor K), exactly as in PR18. A fixed permutation completion
of every M_beta is sufficient for the following structural identities; the
remaining free bases and outer completions form irreducible rational families.
One may fix the M_beta at these permutations, rather than argue that a union
of unrelated completions is irreducible.

A1: Structural block identities.
Write the two orthogonal inner lines as u,eta and v,nu with eta*u=nu*v=1,
eta*v=nu*u=0. Let p,xi be the first-factor line pair and t,phi the outer
line pair. The first-n/last-n corner of the rank-n null projector is
 Q_ij = [o_i=o'_j] p_(R_i) xi_(C_j) v_(beta_i) nu_(beta'_j)
       + [R_i=C_j] t_(o_i) phi_(o'_j) u_(beta_i) eta_(beta'_j),
where o=[0,...,k-1,0,...,a-1], o'=[0,...,a-1,0,...,k-1],
beta_i=i mod b, beta'_j=(H-n+j) mod b.
This is the actual factorized projector corner, not an arbitrary sparse matrix.
For these R,C, equality o_i=o'_j implies R_i=C_j. Therefore Q splits by
coefficient label r. For r<s there are four rows and four columns; for r>=s
there are three of each. No entry coupling different r can appear after any
elimination, because the starting matrix and all elimination steps respect
this direct sum. This proves the diagonal-block structural zeros identically.

Within one coefficient r the first summand has a rank-one 2-by-2 block on the
duplicated outer coordinate r, plus one (or two) nonzero scalar blocks on the
other outer coordinates. The second summand is rank one. The full 3-by-3
or 4-by-4 block is nonsingular iff its rank-one row vector escapes the first
summand's column space and its column vector escapes its row space. These
conditions compare u_beta/v_beta on the duplicated rows, whose beta positions
differ by k mod b, and eta_beta/nu_beta on the duplicated columns, whose
positions differ by a mod b. Condition(3) makes each a comparison of distinct
coordinates. On the rational variety of a pair of dual independent lines,
these inequalities are nonzero rational conditions. Orthogonality imposes
pairings, not equality of distinct coordinate ratios. Their finite product
with nonzero coordinate conditions is nonzero, giving nonsingular restrictions
for all finitely many actual motif-line pairs.

Physical ordered elimination first uses the rightmost a columns against the
first a rows. They form a diagonal block consisting only of the second
summand, so this is an a-block. The next s rows pivot against the preceding
s columns. The corresponding pivot is the nonzero scalar block of the first
summand, since the first block subtraction cancels the rank-one term there.
The remaining corner is a two-by-two matrix of diagonal a-blocks. Its top-right
entries are nonzero rational functions: after clearing the first pivot, each
contains the product of two nonzero first-summand scalar entries, plus terms
in outer rank-one coordinates. That product is not identically cancelled:
set the relevant outer line coordinates to zero and normalize the outer line
on an unused coordinate, leaving the product nonzero as a cleared polynomial.
For a four-by-four block, set its extra outer row coordinate to zero, reducing
this nonvanishing test to the three-by-three case while keeping its separate
scalar pivot nonzero. Such specializations need not themselves satisfy every
other open condition; they establish nonzero polynomials, whose finite product
does. The final pivot follows from full block nonsingularity. The physical A1
profile is therefore [a,s,a,a,m-2n], by the retained large-projector identity.

A5.
Its nullspace restriction, after nonzero diagonal rescaling, evaluates
x_(R_i)+y_beta. The unique constant redundancy has dimension one. Each tree
in condition(2) consequently has rank a+b-1 by leaf elimination. Thus the
ordinary A5 profile consists of a+b-1 singleton pivots and the unchanged
middle block H-2(a+b-1). Any additional A5 subblock must have its own ordered
Schur-complement proof; the trees alone do not establish such subblocks.

A3 transfer and precise remaining review obligation.
The PR18 A3 argument uses only: the matched first-k/last-k outer L0/L0-inverse
prescription; nonzero inner line coordinates; disjoint first/last k multiplicity
intervals (H>2k); and the large-projector cancellation identity. It does not use
25,23 or57 arithmetically elsewhere. In the H-coordinate corner the disjointness
removes the diagonal tensor term. The k-square perturbation is a diagonal
rescaling of an ordinary rank-(k-1) projector, giving one singleton and a
(k-2)-block. Exhausting this perturbation leaves H-2k+2 matching diagonal
pivots. The remaining finite nullity corner has 2k-2 singleton pivots; the
large-projector middle block has width m-2H-2k+2. Hence the same proof gives
2k-1 singletons and blocks [k-2,H-2k+2,m-2H-2k+2]. This is a dimension-free
restatement of the inherited A3 argument, not an independent dense-projector
verification. Independent review must confirm that PR18's untouched matching
interval and finite-nullity corner are established within the actual prescribed
completion family; a finite A1 or A5 witness cannot substitute for this step.

Translated auxiliary exit and local profiles.
The PR21 exit has null projector (outer line) tensor (first-factor line)
tensor I_b. In the same nested family its first-b/last-b corner is diagonal,
with generically nonzero products of transformed line coordinates. It has
blocks[b,m-2b]. The ordinary a,b,k local profiles retain their own matched
first/last factor prescriptions. Products of their finitely many nonzero
minor polynomials with the A1/A3/A5 conditions give a common rational choice,
provided each inherited minor assertion is valid in this same family.
