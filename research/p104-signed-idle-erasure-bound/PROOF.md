# One-volume residual rank on the stage-eight signed idle pair

On each old lane the active field has 512 coordinates and the same signed map $D=C^{\otimes 8}\otimes C^{-1}$, up to placement of the central factor. The two old lanes are arbitrary and independent. The normalized two-point matrix $C$ has Walsh eigenvalues $(1,i)$. The replay pins the literal caller and reuses the binary primitive from the signed-child source. The source also documents an older 18-stage layout. The actual tested geometry comes from the current 17-stage caller.

Grant an independently chosen $E_j=a_j I+b_jD^2$ on each lane, with arbitrary complex coefficients and zero cost. The two terms are monomials because $C^2$ is the bit flip. Define the residual on the two original dirty lanes as $T=\operatorname{diag}(D-E_0,D-E_1)$. The tested architecture requires $T=L(U-I)R$, where $U$ is a normalized rank-nine tensor of signed $C$ factors on one lane volume, optionally multiplied by a fourth root of unity. $R$ maps the two old lanes into this one core and $L$ maps its displacement back to the two old lanes. Both are arbitrary linear maps for the necessary test. They do not provide independent end-to-end contributions to $T$. The chargeable mechanism has one core and an unchanged fixed parked fibre.

The four ordered phase counts of $D$ are $(136,120,120,136)$. Multiplying $(1+t)^8(1+t^{-1})$ modulo $t^4-1$ gives these exact counts. On phases $i^p$, $D^2$ has eigenvalue $(-1)^p$. Each $E_j$ is constant on the two even phases and on the two odd phases. At most $136+136=272$ eigenvectors can be matched per lane. Two independent lanes therefore require rank at least $2(512-272)=480$. The independent replay also checks that all four even/odd match choices can be interpolated and that the best one leaves rank 240 per lane.

The nine positive factors have phase counts $(136,136,120,120)$. Replacing a factor $1+t$ by $1+t^{-1}=t^{-1}(1+t)$ modulo $t^4-1$ only rotates the counts. The global phase also rotates them. Thus the rank of $U-I$ is at most $512-120=392$. Since a factorization through $U-I$ cannot increase rank, 392 cannot realize 480. A negative control drops one old lane and the rank obstruction disappears, exercising the two-return requirement.

## Width interpretation

The finite case alone excludes a uniform supplier using this architecture at every $f\ge1$. If the same architecture has exactly $9f$ tensor bits in each old lane and in the same-volume core at every $f$, the rank gap also holds individually at each width. Put $n=9f$. Filtering $(1+t)^n$ at the fourth roots of unity gives a relative phase-count error at most $2^{-n/2-1}\le1/32$ around $1/4$ for $n\ge8$. Sign and fourth-root factors only rotate these counts. Hence $T$ needs rank at least $2[1-2(1/4+1/32)]=7/8$ of one old lane, while the one core supplies at most $1-(1/4-1/32)=25/32$. This analytic extension uses the same volume, tensor and erased-action premises. The finite replay exercises width one only.
