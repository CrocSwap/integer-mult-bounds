# Terminal coefficient conversion on an adjacent-site tape

## Source and consumer

For an input sign and magnitude $N\ge0$, odd $D\ge1$ and $e,p\ge0$, the source value is $\pm N/(D2^e)$ and the returned fixed-point grid is $2^{-p}$. A source supplies the magnitude **already** scaled to the chosen common denominator. Set $s=\max(e-p,0)$, $a=\max(p-e,0)$ and divide
$$
2^aN=(D2^s)q+R_0,\qquad 0\le R_0<D2^s.
$$
For $s>0$ write $N=2^s A+B$. Divide only $A$ by $D$ and retain $B$ intact. The concatenation of the high remainder $r$ with these $s$ bits is $R_0=2^sr+B$. For $s=0$ append $a$ zeros to the input and use the ordinary remainder. The fraction $R_0/(D2^s)$ certifies the precise fractional part in output-grid units. Truncation is toward zero after the sign is reapplied.

The source check reconstructs $D$ independently from the cited data. On a rational Galois packet, compute constituent dimension $d$ from the character norm and the Gram scalar from the fixed-space weighted character sum. Apply $1/x$ or the two schedule functions $P(x)=\prod(1-h_i x)$ and $(1-P(x))/x$, then invert the character transform on all 80 classes, multiplying each orbit-summed character by $d/|G|$. Taking the LCM of the reduced entries proves minimality of the reported *shared* kernel denominator. At widths 15, 16, 18, 20 and 22, the checker also applies the grouped inverse-projector formula to the five-qubit corner rows. Their exact odd moduli have bit lengths 381, 727, 997, 2073 and 1829, respectively. The physical program receives the modulus as data. The source-precomputation cost is separate. ## Tape protocol and reversibility

Each site has eight binary tracks. A primitive is NOT, CNOT or Toffoli on at most two adjacent sites; distinct wires act as controls and target. Three CNOTs exchange two wires. A single head accesses its site and the site immediately to its right. We charge unit travel per site between the leftmost sites of consecutive gates, beginning and ending at site zero. Scratch is zero on entry and exit. The magnitude result, exact Euclidean remainder and (for rounding) the one-bit decision are outputs. The sign tag bypasses unsigned arithmetic. Negatives that round to zero read as the integer zero. The program is a uniform family of schedules indexed by public lengths. Generating control instructions, building input numerators, ferrying external data, and counting a universal machine's program counter require their own model.

The modulus uses $k=\operatorname{bitlength}(D)$ bits. Choose input width $n\ge\max(1,s+1,\operatorname{bitlength}(N))$, with explicit leading-zero padding if needed. Add *one further zero* so rounding up also fits when $D=1$. The number of quotient digits is $m=1+n+p-e$. There are $S=m+k+\max(s,1)+2$ sites. Initially the data track holds $k$ zeros for the residue, the additional zero, the input and any appended zeros. The separate modulus track holds $D$ in sites 1 through $k$. A converter may reserve and re-use these $8S$ binary wires over any number of calls.

At the start of a digit, the active data window contains a $k$-bit residue $r<D$ followed by input bit $b$. Put $w=2r+b$, so $0\le w<2D$. An unsigned compare obtains $c=[w\ge D]$. Subtract $cD$ in place. The high bit of the result is zero and receives $c$, leaving the next residue in the following $k$ sites. Thus every digit advances the residue to the right and deposits a final quotient digit to the left. The bijection $w\leftrightarrow(c,w-cD)$ on the $2D$ legal values proves both exactness and the feasibility of uncomputation without saving previous input digits.

For comparison the carry of $w+\overline D+c_0$ has final value $[w>D]$ for $c_0=0$, $[w\ge D]$ for $c_0=1$. A majority is the XOR of its three pairwise ANDs. Complement one wire temporarily with two NOTs to implement the needed complement convention using three Toffolis. The low-to-high pass computes all carries; copy out the final carry and reverse the pass. For controlled subtraction, copy $c$ along the window and compute the addend bit $cD_i$ at each site. The borrow out is $\mathrm{maj}(\neg w_i,cD_i,b_i)$ with incoming borrow $b_i$. On the return pass erase that site's borrow *before* changing $w_i$ by $cD_i\mathbin\oplus b_i$. The incoming borrow still exists when needed. Uncompute the addend, copied controls and compare flag and shift the modulus to align with the next window. At the end all $m$ modulus shifts are reversed on the modulus track. Gating carries and advancing the modulus use no nonlocal fan-out. [Uniform gate generator](tape.py)

## Correct rounding and exact tape cost

For $s=0$, increment the magnitude if $2r>D$. An odd $D$ precludes equality. For $s>0$, let $b$ be the high bit of $B$ and let sticky be the OR of its remaining bits. Compare $2r+b$ with $D$. On equality increment if sticky or the truncated quotient's low bit is one. This is precisely round-to-nearest with ties to even. A reversible OR chain on the tail, plus propagation of quotient parity across the residue, supplies that carry-in to the comparator. All these temporary bits are restored before modifying the quotient. A controlled increment propagates carries from the least significant bit and erases them using the updated zero-run. Keeping the increment decision $u$ along with the original Euclidean remainder makes the conversion reversible: the magnitude of the rounded readout is $q+u$. At genuine ties, keeping only rounded magnitude and Euclidean remainder can conflate $q=1$ and $q=2$ at the same midpoint remainder. One bit of side information is both sufficient and necessary on this output convention when both inputs are admitted.

For $s=0$, the maximum scalar error in nearest conversion is $(D-1)/(2D)$ output units; the two nearest nonzero residues to the midpoint attain it for $D>1$ with unrestricted magnitudes because 2 is a unit modulo $D$. For $s>0$, half an output unit is attainable at $R_0=D2^{s-1}$. Each real or imaginary component is converted independently. The worst complex Euclidean error for independent components is $\sqrt2$ times that scalar bound, multiplied by $2^{-p}$.

Write $w=k+1$ in this invoice. Each ordinary digit uses $(8w+2,7w+1,14w)$ gates in order (NOT, CNOT, Toffoli). Restoring the initial modulus position costs an additional $3mw$ CNOTs. The nearest-rounding pass for $s=0$ adds $(4w+2m,m+3,6w+2m)$. For $s>0$ also add $(0,2w+4s+2,2s)$ for parity transport and sticky. For one digit entered and exited at its left boundary the movement is $10k+4$. Moving to the next window adds one. Each reversed modulus shift walks its $k+1$ adjacent swaps and repositions the head by at most $k+1$ between shifts. The rounding, head setup and final return use fewer than 20 full-tape sweeps (including the scratch reversals). In particular total head moves are at most $m(12k+10)+20S$. The generator/simulator computes exact moves for each choice of lengths. These upper bounds include stored input, quotient, local gate scratch and the scale-dependent remainder. ## Optimal between-digit state and sharp prefix latency

Consider a deterministic most-significant-first transducer at a fixed input position. It must emit the digit of the exact Euclidean quotient on each read and cannot revisit input or output. All stored information usable at the next digit, including any information in addressing or control, counts as state. Reading the $k$-bit binary expansion of $0\le r<D$ reaches residue $r$ after the same length and outputs zeros. Reading a further $k$ zeros then emits the $k$-bit word for $\lfloor2^k r/D\rfloor$, strictly increasing in $r$. Hence these $D$ prefix states must be distinct. The elementary update achieves equality. The same common suffix distinguishes all $D^L$ vectors of residues on $L$ synchronously read, independent scalar lanes. Their minimal information width is $\lceil L\log_2 D\rceil$ bits. Knowing $D$ on every lane does not supply their independent residues. On supplied independent Gaussian components one can instead time-share the converter at sequential input cost. This state lower bound concerns fixed-position, no-revisit interfaces; a tape with published output available to reread has another way to store information.

For $n$-bit integers $0\le N<2^n$ and odd $D>1$, put $h=(D-1)/2$. The nearest integer is $\lfloor(N+h)/D\rfloor$. For output bit $j$ its value flips precisely at the integer boundaries $t=kD2^j-h$ in $1\le t<2^n$, for positive integers $k$. After $d$ input bits every prefix is a dyadic interval of length $2^{n-d}$. Every such prefix determines that bit if and only if every flip occurs on an interval boundary. Consequently the exact worst-case determination time is
$$
d_j=n-\min_t v_2(t)
$$
when there is at least one flip, and zero otherwise. The two adjacent inputs $t-1,t$ for a minimizing boundary witness necessity. Aligned prefixes prove sufficiency. For $j>v_2(h)$, each boundary has valuation $v_2(h)$, giving $d_j=n-v_2(h)$. Floor division has boundaries $kD2^j$ and hence $d_j=n-j$ whenever the bit can flip. The high-bit delay caused by rounding can grow with $j$ even for a fixed supplied denominator. Allowing a backward correction pass gives the constructive tape scheme above. All claims in this paragraph concern an integer input and rounding at the integer grid; the program separately implements coarser/finer dyadic grids and signed inputs.
