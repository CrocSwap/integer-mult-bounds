# Zero-one entries, with the inverse taken over the rationals

The word “binary” in Gonzalo Mier's #3990 refers to **coefficients restricted to zero and one**. Its inverse is taken over the rationals. In four ports this gives 391 signatures, between the 355 attained in the two-element field and the 439 attained over unrestricted rationals. The analogous six-port counts are **30,547, 32,287 and 33,727**, respectively. The middle quantity is a finite coefficient census and does not extend the arbitrary-size sufficiency theorem to a restricted alphabet. ## How the enumeration covers every matrix

1. For each triple of distinct nonzero zero-one rows, compute its 20 maximal minors. A nonzero minor vector identifies its rational row space up to a common scalar. Dividing by the gcd and fixing the sign gives a unique key. One way to see uniqueness is to choose a nonzero pivot minor. Ratios of the minors that replace one pivot column recover the entries in that pivot chart by Cramer's rule. Every independent unordered triple contributes six ordered row bases to its space. This gives 11,445 spaces spanned by an admissible basis.
2. A coordinate is absent from that space if no nonzero pivot minor uses it. Its coordinate vector lies in the space if every nonzero pivot minor uses it, because deletion of that column then drops the row rank. Thus the two incidences needed for the signature are read from the minor key.
3. Two spaces are complementary exactly when the signed sum of products of their minors on complementary column sets is nonzero. This is Laplace expansion of the stacked determinant over the integers. Coordinate permutations preserve the signature profile and number of zero-one row bases. Their action on the first space gives 72 orbits. Test every second space against one representative and multiply by the orbit size. For each complementary pair, multiply by the numbers of ordered zero-one bases in its two spaces to count actual matrices.
4. The result is 81,939,900 ordered pairs of admissible complementary spaces and 28,836,612,000 invertible matrices. There are 179 message profiles or 32,287 labeled signatures. Dividing each profile's matrix count by its multinomial multiplicity gives the exact count per label. A first representative is relabeled canonically and inverted over the rationals before publication. [Matrices and per-label counts](ZERO-ONE.json) · [Executable census](zero_one_census.py)

As a separate control, literal integer cofactors on all 65,536 four-port matrices reproduce the entire four-port profile histogram, including all 22,560 invertible matrices and all 391 labeled codes. The six-port total of invertible matrices is a previously published figure. The distinct question here is their block source/reader distribution. The zero-one graph witnesses for all feasible two-element-field signatures have odd determinant. In those pivot charts the masks depend on which rows and columns are zero, so the integer matrices retain their signatures over the rationals. The six-port restricted alphabet thus adds 1,740 labeled signatures to the binary-field set and leaves 1,440 ordinary signatures unrealized. The strict positive-tariff example in the main proof is attained with zero-one entries over the rationals.
