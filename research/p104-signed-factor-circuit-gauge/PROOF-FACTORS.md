# Rank-minimal rational transvection factorization

Let T be a determinant-one linear map over Q. Write D=T−I, F=ker D and k=rank D. A transvection has the form S=I+u f with u and f nonzero and f(u)=0. The exact minimum number of transvections for T is k when T is the identity or T²≠I, and k+1 when T is a nonidentity involution.

A product of k−1 transvections has displacement rank at most k−1. If T=S U has rank k and U has displacement rank at most k−1, the decomposition D=(S−I)+S(U−I) has summands with disjoint image. Each therefore vanishes on F. Choose x fixed by U outside F. Then Dx=u f(x) is nonzero, f(x)≠0, and f(Dx)=0. If the action of T on V/F is λI for λ≠1, then f(Dx)=(λ−1)f(x) is nonzero, a contradiction. The determinant on V/F is one, so a rational nonidentity scalar quotient can only be −I. The condition on this quotient is equivalent to D²=−2D, hence to T²=I.

For the construction, choose x outside F and f vanishing on F with f(x)=1 and f(Dx)=0. Set u=Dx and S=I+u f. The residual S⁻¹T fixes F and x. Rank subadditivity shows it drops exactly one. When T acts as identity on V/F, any x and a covector with f(x)=1 work because Dx is in F. The residual quotient also acts as identity. When T is nonscalar on V/F, choose x whose class and the class of Tx are independent. If the residual quotient has dimension at most one, its determinant-one action cannot be obstructed. Otherwise take a nonzero g vanishing on F, x and Tx. The two residual quotient maps from f and f+g differ by the nonzero rank-one operator −[Dx]gT. On a space of dimension at least two, two scalar operators cannot differ by rank one. At least one choice avoids the obstruction. Repeat to reach identity. All choices are rational row operations. If T is a nonidentity involution, split V into F and the −1 eigenspace. The latter has even positive dimension. Left-peel any nontrivial transvection supported there. The new residual still has displacement rank k and has a nonscalar action on V/F. The construction then uses k more factors.
