# Rank-8 rigidity on every P108 unit

Notation, (E), equations (2), (E0), (E2), and Lemmas 2–8 are those of PR1212
(`research/complex-rank8-decoder-rigidity/PROOF.md` at head `57099cb5`). Lemma 9 and "Step 1" are PR1399's
(`research/complex-rank8-rigidity-p104-admissible/PROOF.md` at head `4e1ba3a1`). Lemma 10 and the coverage rule
are PR1419's (`research/complex-rank8-rigidity-extension/PROOF.md` at head `81b8997b`). The per-unit argument is
PR1425's (`research/complex-rank8-rigidity-p110-odd/PROOF.md` at head `4b707c22`), applied to 168 new units.
Finite facts marked **[X]** are recomputed by this package's `verify.py` (check id `X`; `u` is one of `u001`, …,
`u168`).

**Theorem.** Let X be a 4-subset of P112 and L = P112 \ X. Let K be an L × L matrix over a field of
characteristic 0 with rank K ≤ 8 that satisfies (E). Then K = D (2 G_L) D⁻¹ for an invertible diagonal D.

**Corollary.** In the E-graph model, every decoder with exactly eight costly terms on a P108 unit has
cst ≥ 60 = 72 − 12. This is PR968's Theorem 4(b) with p = 12, reached through PR1212 §8 verbatim.

## Reduction to 168 units, without a census of quadruples

Let H be the group generated by `verify.py`'s 8 generators. Each is a reflection or a product of two reflections
fixing FRAME0 **[model.generators]**, so H maps frames inside P112 to frames inside P112. H splits the 227920
triples of P112 into 24 orbits **[census.triples]**. Let t_1, …, t_24 be their first members.

**Candidate lemma.** Every 4-subset Y of P112 is H-equivalent to a *candidate* t_k ∪ {x} with x ∉ t_k.
*Proof.* Pick a triple s ⊂ Y. Its orbit contains some t_k, so h(s) = t_k for some h ∈ H, and h(Y) = t_k ∪ {h(y)}
where y is the fourth line. ∎

There are 2517 distinct candidates **[census.cover]**:

- **49 lie inside a P112 frame.** Since H preserves that property, so does every Y equivalent to one. These units
  are rigid by PR1419's Theorem, which cites the P104 units of PR1212, PR1389 and PR1399.
- **The other 2468 are covered by the units.** `COVER.json` gives each one a unit and a word in the generators,
  of length at most 10. `verify.py` applies the word to the unit's quadruple and checks that the result is the
  candidate. None of the 168 units lies inside a P112 frame. That they lie in distinct orbits, one per open
  orbit, comes from `make_proof.py`'s grouping and the independent census. `verify.py` does not check it, and the
  argument does not need it, since a redundant unit would only add work.

So every 4-subset of P112 is either inside a P112 frame or transported from one of the 168 units. PR1212 §10
transport needs only some Weyl element carrying one unit onto another. The orbits were grouped with numpy in
`make_proof.py`, which is not trusted. The full quadruple census, 180 orbits with 12 inside a frame and 168 open,
is consistent with an independently written count (see README) but is not needed by the argument.

## Per-unit argument

For each unit **[u.rigid]** checks the same conditions as PR1425 and the P109 package:

- PR1212 Lemma 2's D4 facts relative to the pinned pivot F ⊂ L;
- (I3) with live witnesses on every complete class;
- (I4), all 14 blocks, and gluing through every shared pivot pair;
- Lemma 9 connectivity and its sign identity for every other non-pivot live line;
- (I2) zero exclusion: the zero-pattern CNF plus "some pattern variable is true" is refuted by the DRUP proof
  in `PROOFS.json.gz`, which is replayed by `verify.py`'s own RUP checker and fails without that clause;
- exact rational checks of Lemma 9's rank-3 systems and of the §7 assembly on all 108 live lines;
- the gauge pattern satisfies the CNF.

The proof is then PR1425's:

- **Step 1** (PR1399): Lemmas 5–6 on the complete classes;
- **Step 2:** Lemma 9 on the rest;
- **Assembly:** PR1212 §7, then transport. ∎
