# Exact inverse input lattice

This contribution was produced by Nick Petalas. Apache-2.0.

## Statement

Let D = Z[i,1/2], f >= 1 and n = 8^f. Use the literal letters of
lee-phys's #4688 and Yiyang's #4710, as fixed in Cinna Davis's #4998:

- U e_(x0,x1,x2) = e_(x0,x1,x2+x0).
- V e_(x0,x1,x2) = e_(x1,x0,x2).
- S e_x = i^popcount(x) e_x, P = SUS, Q = SVS.
- C = ((1+i)/2) I + ((1-i)/2) X_0.

A subscript f means the tensor power of that individual letter. Define

    F_f = C_f (U_f + 2 V_f) C_f,
    F'_f = C_f (P_f + 2 Q_f) C_f.

These are the two prescribed first outputs of #4998. Its second output is
the inverse applied to an independent input. We cite its operator definition
and inverse construction as premises; we do not audit that construction.

For either G = F_f or F'_f, put

    a_f = (4^f + 2^f)/2,
    b_f = (8^f - 4^f)/4,
    h_f = a_f + b_f = (8^f + 4^f + 2*2^f)/4.

The new claim is the following D-module isomorphism:

    D^n / G D^n  =  (D/(3))^a_f  direct-sum  (D/(15))^b_f.       (1)

Thus the exact index is 9^h_f * 25^b_f. The independent inverse input y
has G^-1 y in D^n precisely when y belongs to G D^n. Its fraction in every
complete residue period modulo 15, on any fixed dyadic Gaussian grid, is

    9^(-h_f) * 25^(-b_f).                                      (2)

At f=1 this is 1/164025. At every f>=1 the least positive odd integer m
such that m G^-1 D^n is contained in D^n is exactly 15. This is a statement
about the odd denominator; no fixed bound on powers of two is claimed.

## Reduction to cycle relations

C^2 = X_0, so C^-1 = X_0 C has entries in D. Consequently C_f is in
GL_n(D). The same holds for the permutation letters and the phase S_f.
For R = U^-1 V = UV, let H_f = I + 2 R_f. Then

    F_f  = (C_f U_f) H_f C_f,
    F'_f = (C_f S_f U_f) H_f (S_f C_f).                         (3)

The repaired identity follows from P_f + 2 Q_f = S_f (U_f+2V_f) S_f.
Both sides of H_f in (3) are unimodular over D. Right multiplication does
not change its image module; left multiplication carries that module by an
ambient automorphism. Thus both cokernels are isomorphic to coker H_f.
This reduction preserves the actual phase-repaired interface, rather than
replacing it with an unsigned permutation matrix.

On the eight base addresses, the cycles of R are

    (0), (4), (1,2,5,6), (3,7).

The fixed-point counts of R and R^2 are 2 and 4. Independent tensor digits
therefore give 2^f fixed points of R_f and 4^f fixed points of R_f^2.
Since R_f has order four, its cycle counts are

    c1 = 2^f,
    c2 = (4^f - 2^f)/2,
    c4 = (8^f - 4^f)/4.

Here c1+c2=a_f and c4=b_f. In particular b_f>0 for every positive f.

## One cycle, with an explicit quotient map

Order a cycle of length ell in {1,2,4} as e_0,...,e_(ell-1), with
R_f e_j = e_(j+1 mod ell). The image relations of H_f are

    e_j + 2 e_(j+1) = 0.

Because 2 is a unit in D, these relations express every generator using
e_0, and the closing relation is (1-(-2)^ell)e_0=0. There are no further
relations. Explicitly, let d_ell=abs(1-(-2)^ell), and set

    phi_ell(z) = sum_(j=0)^(ell-1) (-2)^(ell-j) z_j mod d_ell.   (4)

Each column e_j+2e_(j+1) maps to zero, including the closing column.
The coefficient at z_0 is (-2)^ell, which is a unit modulo d_ell, so phi
is onto. Conversely the generator elimination just given shows that the
quotient is generated by one element killed by d_ell. Its surjection onto
D/(d_ell) is therefore an isomorphism. Hence

    coker(I+2 cycle_ell) = D/(d_ell),
    d_1=3, d_2=3, d_4=15.

This proves (1) by the direct sum over disjoint cycles. It is also an
invariant-factor decomposition over D. Unit diagonal entries are omitted.
No finite-field rank measurement is used to infer higher prime powers.

## Actual input membership and exact density

For an actual input y, first compute z = L^-1 y, where the left factor L
in (3) is C_f U_f or C_f S_f U_f. Thus

    z = U_f C_f^-1 y                    for the original prefix,
    z = U_f S_f^-1 C_f^-1 y             for the repaired prefix.

Apply (4) separately on every R_f cycle. The inverse output is dyadic
Gaussian if and only if every resulting congruence is zero. On a four-cycle
one can use the weights (1,7,4,13) modulo 15. Replacing those weights by a
plain sum is incorrect.

The quotient D/(d) has d^2 elements for odd positive d. Indeed reducing the
real and imaginary integer coordinates modulo d gives d^2 possibilities,
and inverting 2 does not change them. Therefore the independent conditions
have total index 9^a_f * 225^b_f = 9^h_f * 25^b_f. Also 15D^n is contained
in G D^n by (1). Reduction modulo 15D^n makes the membership condition a
kernel of that exact index. Any dyadic grid 2^-q Z[i]^n maps bijectively
onto all residue coordinates modulo 15D^n when its integer numerators run
over a complete period of length 15 in each real coordinate. Every kernel
fiber has equal size. This proves (2), without assuming a probability
measure on the infinite set D^n. Expanding boxes in any such fixed grid
have the same limiting natural density.

The first independent input x of #4998 always has Gx in D^n. Thus the same
fraction describes pairs (x,y) with both prescribed outputs in D^n.
A caller may deliberately restrict y to G D^n, in which case every input
it supplies is safe. The complete-period fraction is not a claim about
that caller's distribution.

## Sharp denominator and implementation consequence

For a positive odd integer m,

    m G^-1 D^n subset D^n  iff  m D^n subset G D^n.

Equivalently m annihilates the cokernel in (1). Since b_f>0, the summand
D/(15) forces m to lie in 15D. For an ordinary odd integer this means
15 divides m. Conversely 15 kills every summand. Thus the least such m
is exactly 15 at every width. Both odd primes are necessary; a larger
dyadic precision alone cannot remove either one for unrestricted inputs.

Any exact implementation whose input, intermediate and literal output
coordinates all lie in D cannot deliver the prescribed pair on all of D^n.
This follows already from its output set, regardless of how much D-valued
workspace it has. A dyadic-linear program is one example. The statement
allows exact odd-denominator arithmetic, rational encodings with a decoded
output contract, rounded outputs and restricted safe inputs as changed
premises. It does not rule out any of those alternatives or price them.

## Evidence and limits

The universal theorem is the written module argument above. The self-contained
verifier enumerates the new cycle counts at widths 1 through 5, exhausts all
50,865 real residue vectors on the three cycle types, and checks the literal
Gaussian realifications at widths 1 and 2 modulo 3 and 5. Its separate rational
matrix inversion at width 1 checks both prefixes' sharp odd denominator and
128 input-membership probes, plus 32 forward-image columns. These are finite
corroboration, not a proof-kernel certificate or an audit of predecessor words.

No complete multiplication theorem, new kappa, physical realization, precision
budget, Gaussian-compatible consumer or supplier admission follows. The proof
uses the elementary cyclic presentation computation and makes no claim of
literature priority for Smith normal form or finite-module counting.
