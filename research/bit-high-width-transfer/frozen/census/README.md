# Five calls, two relations: an exhaustive census at one and two dirty auxiliaries

**Independent finite corroboration; no new κ.** JoyboyBrian's merged
[PR #5481](https://github.com/CrocSwap/integer-mult-bounds/pull/5481)
([`research/bit-fivecall-dirty-block`](../bit-fivecall-dirty-block/README.md))
proves in writing that three invertible rational 2×2 targets need six rank-one
calls in the formal abelian BIT model, with any number of dirty auxiliary
registers. That closes open question 1 of unpaidmoon's
[`research/bit-span4-auxiliary-structure`](../bit-span4-auxiliary-structure/README.md)
(five calls at frame span 3), and so the eight-call span-four case. Its README
says that no five-call word enumeration was run and that the inherited
low-span, one-relation and three-register lemmas were cited, not re-proved.

This package runs that enumeration for **W = 4** scalar registers (three data
registers and one dirty auxiliary) and **W = 5** (two auxiliaries), and for
W = 2, 3. It covers every relation lattice of rank at most 2, that is, every
five-call word with frame span at least 3. It does not use the conic argument,
the one-relation theorem or the three-register theorem. The 120^5 search that
the span-four note called blocked becomes small after one reduction (step 2
below), which extends seed04's derivative step to two relations.

**Result.** For W ≤ 5 and every relation lattice of rank ≤ 2, no diagonal
five-call word has three registers whose endpoint class differs from `0` and
from every `±A_j` modulo the relations. Two such registers do occur. An invertible 2×2
target is not `0` or a rank-one frame, so no five-call word reaches three
invertible targets at W ≤ 5. This agrees with PR #5481 for one and two dirty
auxiliaries. The census does not cover three or more auxiliaries or frame span ≤ 2.
Span ≤ 2 is Lemma 2 of
[`research/bit-dirty-auxiliary-registers`](../bit-dirty-auxiliary-registers/README.md).

Base: `721c03822c421b7f7fcbb2598993b947c4d88419` (priority revision 8). Card
`bit-new-register-model`, drawn with seed `df1ea4ba466d07a5fa28dab974fcdd78`.

## Model

This is the model of `bit-span4-auxiliary-structure` and PR #5481, at five calls.
Paid factors are `F_j = I + ([A_j] - 1) N_j` on `W` binary scalar registers. Here `A_j`
is a nonzero rational 2×2 frame of rank one and `N_j = x y^T` is a binary rank-one
idempotent. The product must equal `diag([C_1], ..., [C_W])` in `F2[(M_2(Q), +)]`.
The data endpoints must be invertible; auxiliary endpoints are arbitrary. Let
`L = {n in Z^5 : sum n_j A_j = 0}`, a saturated lattice. Then
`H = Z^5 / L` embeds in `M_2(Q)`, and the product can be computed in `F2[H]`.

## Reduction

**Step 1 (only the cube part of L matters).** The product is multilinear in the
`t_j`: `P = sum_S c_S t^S` over subsets `S` of the calls. Two subsets
`S`, `T` have the same address exactly when `1_S - 1_T` lies in `L`, so in
`D = L ∩ {-1,0,1}^5`. Let `L'` be the saturation of the span of `D`. Then
`L' ⊆ L` and `L' ∩ {-1,0,1}^5 = D`. So diagonality is the same over `L` and `L'`.
A class that is `0` or `±A_j` modulo `L'` is also so modulo `L`.
Hence it suffices to enumerate the finitely many `L'`:

- rank 2: the Q-spans of two independent cube vectors (4,250 lattices; 4,060 after
  removing those that contain a unit vector, because frames are nonzero);
- rank ≤ 1: the zero lattice and the 116 lines spanned by a cube vector
  with at least two nonzero entries.

**Step 2 (derivative condition).** Let `b_1, b_2` be a Z-basis of `L'` (rank 2).
The kernel of `F2[Z^5] -> F2[Z^5 / L']` is generated by `t^{b_1} - 1` and
`t^{b_2} - 1`, so every off-diagonal entry of `P` is `(t^{b_1} - 1) g_1 + (t^{b_2} - 1) g_2`.
At `t = 1`, `d/dt_j P = N_j`, and so

```
offdiag(N_j) = (b_1)_j M_1 + (b_2)_j M_2   (mod 2)
```

for two fixed off-diagonal binary patterns `M_1`, `M_2`. A call whose basis
column is `0 mod 2` is a coordinate projector. The other calls take at most
three patterns, `M_1`, `M_2` and `M_1 + M_2`, each fixed by its column class. Over F2 this is
seed04's step 1 with one more relation. It is the "pattern plane" of the
[`two-relation-pattern-census-seed52`](../two-relation-pattern-census-seed52/README.md) census.
Each pattern has at most two idempotent lifts, except the zero pattern, which has `W`.
So the census enumerates, for each lattice, every admissible `(M_1, M_2)`
and every lift. Rank 1 is the same with one pattern.

**Step 3 (orbit reduction).** Register permutations act on `(M_1, M_2)`.
The census evaluates one representative per orbit and weights it by the orbit
size. The weighted counts equal the unreduced enumeration at W = 2.

For each word the exact product is formed over all 32 subsets. Coefficients are
merged by address class, and the word is accepted only if every class is diagonal
and each register has exactly one class with coefficient 1. A register is
**high** if its endpoint class is not `0` and not `±e_j` modulo the lattice.
Lattice membership is exact rational rank arithmetic.

Controls: six coordinate calls, two per data register, give three high
registers, so the detector can fire. A cancelling pair `A_1 + A_2 = 0` makes its
register low. A non-coordinate projector without a relation is rejected, and
`D_N(B) D_N(-B)` is accepted. Every listed lattice basis is saturated and spans its cube part,
and 190 of the 4,250 planes contain a unit vector (a zero frame) and are dropped.

## Scope

This is a finite census in a formal model. It corroborates PR #5481 for at most two
dirty auxiliaries. It is not a new proof for any number of auxiliaries, and it
does not re-check the conic argument. It is independent of the one-relation and
three-register theorems for these W. The only cited ingredient is Lemma 2 (frame span ≤ 2).
The written steps 1 and 2 are short; the unpruned cross-checks test step 2 at small sizes,
not at W = 4, 5. The "high" test is a necessary condition for an invertible target, so the
census is stronger than needed, and it says nothing about rank realizability of the words
it does find. No physical supplier, pricing, compiler or recursive-bill obligation is addressed.
The selected bit profile and κ are unchanged.

## Open next questions

1. **Why at most two high registers?** At W ≤ 5 every diagonal five-call word with
   relation rank ≤ 2 has at most two high registers, and two is attained. A written proof
   for every W would give a second, conic-free proof of PR #5481. A first step: classify the
   lattices and pattern planes that attain two.
2. **Spans 5 and 6 at eight calls** (PR #5481's open question). Step 2 applies to any relation
   rank: offdiag `N_j` is the `j`-th column of the relation basis mod 2 applied to fixed
   patterns. At `k = 8` the cube-lattice count and the pattern space grow, so an enumeration
   needs stronger pruning (for example a prefix test or second derivatives).
