# Inverse 5-cycle pair in the four-generator S5 model

**Scoped exact minimum: eight paid factors.** The complete radius-seven search finds no shorter word in the stated two-register group-algebra model. No selected supplier or κ improvement is claimed.

## Model and target

Use the full group algebra `F2[S5]`, with standard right-to-left permutation composition. The group is generated by the four adjacent transpositions

```text
s1=(12), s2=(23), s3=(34), s4=(45).
```

For each generator `si` and every rank-one idempotent `N` in `M2(F2)`, a paid factor is

```text
D_N(si) = I + ([si] + [1]) N.
```

There are six such idempotents and 24 paid factors. Each factor costs one call. The target is `diag([c],[c^-1])`, where `c=s1*s2*s3*s4=(1 2 3 4 5)`. Independent coordinate projectors give a direct eight-call word: four factors on `E11` for `c` and four on `E22` for `c^-1`.

The exact result is that no word of length at most seven over the complete alphabet realizes this target. Since the direct eight-call word is replayed exactly, the minimum is eight in this model. Equality in the full `F2[S5]` algebra gives the arbitrary-dirty-state statement for the two registers.

## Exact search and completeness

The verifier enumerates the full 120-element permutation group, checks that the four transpositions generate it, enumerates all 16 binary 2x2 matrices and retains all six rank-one idempotents, then verifies that each of the 24 paid factors is an involution.

It builds exact matrix-state balls through length four. Each group-algebra coefficient vector is a 120-bit integer; addition is XOR and right multiplication by a group element permutes those bits. Equal complete matrices have identical continuations, so the exact-depth state layers safely deduplicate words while retaining one witness per state.

Every word of length at most seven splits as `P S` with `|P|<=3` and `|S|<=4`. Because each factor is self-inverse, `P S = T` exactly when `S=P^-1 T`. The verifier stores all states in the prefix and suffix balls and checks each inverse-prefix target in the suffix map. This covers all 4,785,883,225 raw words of lengths 0 through 7 without walking them individually. The complete balls contain 6,505 unique prefix states and 93,121 unique suffix states; per-depth counts and SHA-256 digests are in `CERTIFICATE.json`.

The search's orientation is checked on a known three-call diagonal target, which is recovered by a 1+2 split. The eight-call direct construction, reverse target orientation, dropped-factor mutation, and wrong-generator mutation are also checked.

## Cost and limits

The direct formal cost is eight calls. A shorter witness through seven would save at least one fully charged call (12.5% of this word). This alphabet has no helper operations, recursive children, frame changes, copies, restorations, or hosting costs; every legal factor is charged once. The exact cost model is only the finite group-algebra word model.

This is not a native integer-multiplication circuit or a selected bit-supplier mapping. The result does not change the selected record or κ. A physical address-map realization and its implementation, precision, hosting, and assembly costs remain outside the scope.

## Predecessors and overlap

The closest two-register inverse-pair work is the free-group claim in Discussion #1997. It was handed off without a PR because the sharp two-label `D_infinity` theorem in merged [PR #1911](https://github.com/CrocSwap/integer-mult-bounds/pull/1911) implies that free-group endpoint. That theorem uses two paid reflections and its proof does not descend to finite quotients. This package instead charges four adjacent-transposition labels in `S5`; the four-label finite group and 5-cycle target are not covered by the two-label theorem.

Other nearby results use a different object or endpoint: [PR #2116](https://github.com/CrocSwap/integer-mult-bounds/pull/2116) studies a four-role `A4` target with rank-one idempotents in `M4(F2)`; [PR #2187](https://github.com/CrocSwap/integer-mult-bounds/pull/2187) studies four order-two outputs over `C4`; [PR #2186](https://github.com/CrocSwap/integer-mult-bounds/pull/2186) uses two reflection labels in `D_infinity`; PR #2135 studies a three-role `D8` inverse pair. Discussion #2012 uses three registers over `S3`, while #1740 studies four registers over `S4`. The refreshed checks and the exact distinctions are recorded in `TASK.json`.
