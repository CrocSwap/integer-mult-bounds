# Exact reachability of square-zero radical right-vector targets

This contribution was produced by Nick Petalas. It answers the singular-target
question in [#4601](https://github.com/CrocSwap/integer-mult-bounds/pull/4601),
starting with its suggested algebra F₂[x,y]/(x,y)². The main result is a
**scoped proof**, with exact finite corroboration. No new κ is claimed.

Let K be any field and R = K ⊕ J a unital K-algebra with K central and J² = 0.
The ideal J may have any dimension. A paid factor is I + u yᵀ with u ∈ R²
and y ∈ K². Arbitrary GL₂(K) maps are free anywhere in a word. Factors may
be singular. These are the formal alphabet and free-map convention of
Wentao Li's #4099 and Nick Petalas's #4243/#4276.

For every radical target M ∈ M₂(J), the exact answer is

```text
cost(M) = 2       if some nonzero φ ∈ (K²)* and v ∈ K² satisfy φ M v = 0;
cost(M) = ∞       otherwise.
```

In particular, a third or later factor never enlarges the reachable radical
set. This is a theorem for arbitrary finite words, not a bounded search failure.
It does not classify all singular targets outside M₂(J), nonsplit extensions,
higher nilpotency or more than two roles.

For K = F₂ and finite d = dim J, write z = 2^d. The exact number of reachable
radical targets is

```text
R_d = 9 z³ − 30 z² + 42 z − 20.
U_d = z⁴ − R_d.
```

The exact pairs (reachable, unreachable) for d = 0,1,2,3 are
(1,0), (16,0), (244,12), (3004,1092). The d = 2 value agrees numerically
with the 12 unreachable targets reported by #4276; it is a calibration point,
not new credit. The quantified criterion and counting law are the additions.

## Proof of the criterion

All products below use algebraic order; execution on column vectors is from
right to left. A bar denotes scalar residue modulo J. Every paid factor has
scalar residue of rank at least one, since it fixes the kernel of its
base-field right row. Consequently a radical target needs at least two paid
factors, even when M = 0. Free factors alone have invertible residue.

**Necessity.** Read any realizing word in execution order, including its free
maps. Start at I. There is a first event where the accumulated scalar residue
becomes zero. The preceding product P has residue P₀ of rank one. The event
cannot be a free map. Write its paid factor as

```text
Q = Q₀ + u₁ yᵀ,      Q₀ = I + u₀ yᵀ,
P = P₀ + P₁,          P₁ ∈ M₂(J),  u₁ ∈ J × J.
```

Since Q₀ P₀ = 0 and neither residue is zero, Q₀ has rank one.
Choose a nonzero row φ annihilating Q₀. Choose a nonzero column v in the
kernel of the one-row matrix yᵀP₀. The square-zero relation gives

```text
QP = Q₀P₁ + u₁ yᵀP₀,
φ(QP)v = 0.
```

Thus the first radical product has a common bilinear zero. Once the residue
is zero, later multiplication by Q₀ + Q₁ acts as Q₀ on that radical product,
because Q₁ times a radical matrix is zero. If Q₀ is invertible, transport φ
by Q₀⁻¹. If it is rank one, take φ to annihilate its image. A later free map
is the invertible case. This preserves the existence of a nonzero pair
(φ,v) at every remaining event, and proves necessity for the final M.

**Sufficiency.** Given φMv = 0, choose G,H ∈ GL₂(K) with first row of G equal
to φ and second column of H equal to v. Then

```text
N = GMH = [[a,0],[c,d]],
C₁ = [[a,0],[c,1]] = I + (a−1,c)ᵀ e₁ᵀ,
C₂ = [[1,0],[0,d]] = I + (0,d−1)ᵀ e₂ᵀ,
M = G⁻¹ C₁ C₂ H⁻¹.
```

Both C₁,C₂ are paid factors. This is the triangular construction behind
#4276's two-factor criterion. Together with the lower bound it gives cost
exactly two. The new necessity argument excludes every longer word when no
common zero exists.

## Proof of the binary counting law

Choose a basis of J and write M = Σ_j A_j ε_j, with A_j ∈ M₂(F₂). Use the
nondegenerate entrywise pairing on the four-dimensional matrix space. Let
W = span(A₁,...,A_d) and S = W-perpendicular. The common-zero criterion is
that S contains a nonzero rank-one tensor φᵀvᵀ, with entries φ_i v_j.
Thus M is unreachable exactly when every nonzero element of S is invertible.

Such a subspace S has dimension at most two: evaluation T ↦ T e₁ is injective
on S and takes values in F₂². There is one zero subspace and six allowable
one-dimensional subspaces, generated by the six invertible 2×2 matrices.
There are exactly two allowable planes. To count them, normalize any nonzero
A ∈ S to I by left multiplication. The other generator B must have B and
I+B invertible, so its characteristic polynomial is x²+x+1. There are exactly
two such binary matrices: their diagonal entries differ and both off-diagonal
entries are one. Together with I they form the same normalized plane.
Therefore each invertible A determines one allowable plane, and each plane
is counted by its three nonzero elements, giving 6/3 = 2 planes.

For a fixed S of dimension s, the ordered d coefficient matrices must span
the fixed (4−s)-space W. There are Π_{i=0}^{3−s}(2^d−2^i) such tuples, by
counting full-row-rank (4−s)-by-d binary matrices. The product is zero when
d < 4−s. Hence

```text
U_d = (z−1)(z−2)(z−4)(z−8)
    + 6(z−1)(z−2)(z−4) + 2(z−1)(z−2).
```

Expanding and subtracting from z⁴ proves the stated R_d. The rank-metric and
finite-vector-space counting tools are classical; their application to this
formal-alphabet reachability question is the scoped contribution.

Another CPython 3.12 interpreter may replace the absolute local path. The

It compares the entire literal two-factor/free-endpoint image with the
criterion on all radical targets for d = 0,1,2,3, and reconstructs every
accepted target by the displayed word. The alphabet enumeration does not use
the criterion. It independently derives the count polynomial by
inclusion-exclusion over all 511 nonempty subsets of the nine rank-one

controls for obstruction, separate coefficient charts, reversed/dropped paid

active under optimized Python. This finite evidence corroborates the written
proof; it is not proof-kernel verification of its quantifiers.

## Limitations and implementation decisions

The algebra must be split with residue exactly K, two roles and J² = 0.
Free maps and right vectors are common across all radical coordinates. Separate
charts for each coordinate would wrongly accept the irreducible-pencil control.
Every paid factor has its full formal price. Native preparation, routing,
precision, restoration, stock and a complete supplier bill are unknown. No
physical caller, admission, complete multiplication theorem or selected-frontier
change is supplied. Independent human and formal proof-kernel review are
unclaimed. No Lean, Docker or selected-construction replay was run.

The verifier has fixed dimension at most three, at most 766 distinct paid
factors and at most 4096 radical targets. Functions remain below 60 lines.
Power-of-ten rule 3 has a bounded managed-runtime deviation: Python allocates
sets, tuples and integer objects during enumeration. Fixed loop domains bound
the stored matrix sets by 4096 entries; this offline standard-library checker
has no steady-state service or persistent resources. A future larger census
must specify fresh memory/runtime bounds instead of increasing MAX_DIM silently.
Warning-strict ordinary and optimized replays supply the available diagnostics;
no separate static analyzer is required by the package environment.

## Open next questions

1. Does the common-zero obstruction lift through a local algebra with J³ = 0,
   and what additional second-order condition is needed? Start with
   F₂[x,y]/(x,y)³ and the same residue-first-zero expansion. Products of radical
   entries no longer vanish, so the present invariant alone does not suffice.
2. What is the exact finite-field counting law over F_q for q > 2? The criterion
   already holds. Count subspaces of M₂(F_q) containing no rank-one matrix,
   including the two-dimensional field-spread case, before any larger census.

These follow-ups are not claimed or investigated here. Structured questions
and blockers are in TASK.json.
