# Dirty auxiliary registers do not save in the two-register m = 3 bit model

**Theorem.** In the abelian bit model of
[PR #468](../bit-three-register-lower-bound/README.md), add any number of
*dirty auxiliary* scalar registers: registers with arbitrary input that may end
at any diagonal monomial, in particular restored exactly. Two arbitrary
invertible rational `3×3` data targets still need six nonzero rank-one paid
calls. Six suffice.

The general statement behind it holds for every address dimension `m`, every
number `W` of data registers and every number of auxiliary registers. Suppose a
word with nonzero rank-one frames reaches invertible data targets in fewer than
`mW` calls. Then its rational frame span `d` satisfies `m+1 ≤ d ≤ s−2`.
For `(m,W)=(3,2)` and `s ≤ 5`, no such `d` exists.

This is a scoped lower bound for a formal register model. It does not change
κ or the selected record. It does not cover clean (zero-initialized) ancillas,
other paid primitives, or arbitrary free address maps between calls. Three data
registers with an auxiliary register remain open; see section 6.

The changed hypothesis is the register count: PR #468 and the open
[PR #720](https://github.com/CrocSwap/integer-mult-bounds/pull/720) state

that PR #288 §1.3(b) records as deficit-neutral, so they are the economically
relevant extra registers in this model. No public claim was posted.

## 1. Model

Let `G=(Q^{m×m},+)`, written multiplicatively as `[A][B]=[A+B]`, and work in
`M_n(F₂[G])`. There are `n=W+k` scalar registers: data registers `0,…,W−1` and
auxiliary registers `W,…,n−1`. A paid call is

$$
D_N([A])=I+([A]-1)N,\qquad \operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Q}A=1,\qquad
N=xy^T,\quad x,y\in\mathbb F_2^n,\quad y^Tx=1 .
$$

For `n=3` there are 28 such idempotents `N`; for `n=4` there are 120. Free
scalar mixings are absorbed into the choice of `N`, as in PR #468 §1. A word
`D_{N_1}([A_1])⋯D_{N_s}([A_s])` succeeds when

$$
\prod_{j=1}^{s}D_{N_j}([A_j])=\operatorname{diag}([C_0],\ldots,[C_{n-1}]),
\qquad C_0,\ldots,C_{W-1}\ \text{invertible}. \tag{1}
$$

Every formal coefficient must agree. The auxiliary endpoints
`C_W,…,C_{n−1}` are unconstrained; exact restoration is `C_i=0`. A dirty
auxiliary may hold any input, so (1) is an equality of whole matrices, not of
their action on a zero-initialized vector. No triangularity is imposed on the
data targets.

Zero frames give identity factors and can be deleted. Splitting
`D_N([A])=D_N([A/2])D_N([A/2])` turns any shorter successful word into one of
any larger length. It therefore suffices to exclude exactly `s` nonzero calls.

## 2. Disjoint subsets with at most one relation, for any register count

**Lemma 1.** Let `A_1,…,A_k` lie in a torsion-free abelian group, and let
`N_1,…,N_k` be binary rank-one idempotents in `M_n(F₂)`, for any `n`. Suppose
the product of the factors `I+([A_j]−1)N_j` is a diagonal monomial matrix
`diag([C_0],…,[C_{n−1}])`. If the integral relation lattice of the `A_j` has
rank at most one, there are pairwise disjoint sets `S_0,…,S_{n−1}` with

$$
C_i=\sum_{j\in S_i}A_j\qquad(0\le i<n). \tag{2}
$$

For `n=3` this is the zero-or-one-relation step of PR #468 §4, also isolated in
§2 of PR #720. The new content is the treatment of a common off-diagonal
pattern for arbitrary `n`, which replaces the three-register `J` argument.

*Proof.* Delete zero frames; this keeps the hypothesis. The generated group is
free abelian, so its group algebra is a Laurent polynomial domain. Put
`P(t)=∏_j(I+(t_j−1)N_j)`; its entries are multilinear polynomials and
`∂P/∂t_j` at `t=(1,…,1)` equals `N_j`.

*No relation, or a generator with an entry of absolute value at least two.*
Differences of subset indicators lie in `{−1,0,1}^k`. A primitive generator
`r` with some `|r_j|≥2` has no nonzero rational multiple in this cube, so
distinct subset monomials map to distinct group elements. Every off-diagonal
polynomial of `P` vanishes identically. Its derivatives show that each `N_j` is
diagonal, hence a coordinate idempotent `E_{ll}`. The product is then the
diagonal of coordinate factors, and register `l` receives the sum of the frames
with `N_j=E_{ll}`. This gives (2).

*A cube relation.* Otherwise the primitive generator is `r∈{−1,0,1}^k`, with
support `S` of size at least two. The substitution `t_j↦[A_j]` has kernel
generated by `g=∏_{r_j=1}t_j−∏_{r_j=−1}t_j`: the generated group is
`Z^k/Zr`, because the relation lattice is saturated. Each off-diagonal entry is
`P_{ab}=h_{ab}g`. Differentiating at one gives

$$
(N_j)_{ab}=h_{ab}(1)\,(r_j\bmod 2).
$$

So the projectors outside `S` are coordinate idempotents, and all projectors on
`S` share one off-diagonal pattern `Π=(h_{ab}(1))_{a≠b}`.

For `N=xy^T` with `y^Tx=1`, the supports of `x` and `y` meet in an odd number of
coordinates. If they meet in one coordinate, the directed graph of `N`'s
off-diagonal entries has no cycle: each vertex of a cycle lies in both
supports. If they meet in at least three coordinates, two of them form a
two-cycle. Since the projectors on `S` share `Π`, either all of them meet in one
coordinate or all meet in at least three.

*Acyclic case.* `Π` is acyclic. A topological order of its vertices makes every
projector on `S` upper triangular, and the coordinate projectors are diagonal.
The diagonal of a product of triangular matrices is the product of their
diagonals. Each projector has exactly one diagonal one, so diagonal entry `l`
of `P` is the monomial in the frames whose projector has its diagonal one at
`l`. Equality of group monomials gives (2).

*Common projector case.* Every projector on `S` has
`|supp x∩supp y|≥3`, so `|supp x|,|supp y|≥2`. Then `supp x` is the set of
nonzero rows of `Π` and `supp y` the set of its nonzero columns. All projectors
on `S` therefore equal one `N₀=xy^T`. Since `N₀` is idempotent,
`(I+(a−1)N₀)(I+(b−1)N₀)=I+(ab−1)N₀`.

First set every variable outside `S` to one. This is a ring map modulo `g`,
because `g` involves only variables in `S`. The product becomes
`I+(t^{1_S}−1)N₀`. `N₀` has a nonzero off-diagonal entry, so
`t^{1_S}−1∈(g)`. In `F₂[Z^S/Zr]` this means `1_S∈Zr`, so `r=±1_S` and
`∑_{j∈S}A_j=0`.

Now keep one outside factor, with projector `E_{ll}` and variable `t_o`, and set
the other outside variables to one. Let `z` be the monomial of the factors of
`S` before it. Modulo `g` the product is
`I+(t_o−1)U(z)E_{ll}U(z^{−1})`, where `U(z)=I+(z−1)N₀`. Since `t_o` is a free
variable, `t_o−1` is a nonzerodivisor in the domain `F₂[Z^S/Zr][t_o^{±1}]`. So
every off-diagonal entry of `uw^T` vanishes, where

$$
u=e_l+(z-1)y_l\,x,\qquad w=e_l+(z^{-1}-1)x_l\,y .
$$

If `x_l=y_l=1`, take `a∈supp x` and `b∈supp y` different from `l`, and with
`a≠b`; three common coordinates make this possible. The entry `u_aw_b` is
`(z−1)(z^{−1}−1)`. If only `x_l=1`, choose `b∈supp y∖{l}`; the entry `u_lw_b`
is `z^{−1}−1`. If only `y_l=1`, choose `a∈supp x∖{l}`; the entry `u_aw_l` is
`z−1`. In each case the domain property gives `z=1`. The prefix of `S` before
the outside factor is then a zero-sum subset of `S`. The frames on `S` have
span `|S|−1` and the single relation `1_S`, so the prefix is empty or all of
`S`.

If `x_l=y_l=0`, then `E_{ll}N₀=N₀E_{ll}=0`, so the two factors commute.
Commute these factors out of the `S` region. The remaining outside factors lie
before or after all of `S`. The `S` factors are now contiguous, and their block
is `I+([∑_S A_j]−1)N₀=I`. Delete it. The remaining coordinate factors give (2),
with every `S_i` disjoint from `S`. ∎

**Corollary.** In (1) with rank-one frames and at most one relation,
`∑_{w<W}rank C_w ≤ s`. In particular `mW ≤ s` for invertible `m×m` data targets.
This uses rank subadditivity on the disjoint sets `S_0,…,S_{W−1}`; auxiliary
endpoints only consume frames.

## 3. Low span, for any register count

**Lemma 2 (PR #468 §3, with auxiliary registers).** If the nonzero rank-one
frames of a successful word span a rational space of dimension at most `m`,
then `s≥mW`.

*Proof.* Every data target is the exponent of a monomial in the expanded
product, hence a sum of frames, and lies in the span. A span of dimension
below `m` contains no rank-`m` matrix. At dimension `m`, choose a basis of
paid frames `B_i=u_iv_i^T`. One invertible data target is
`X diag(c_i) Y^T` with `X=(u_i)`, `Y=(v_i)` invertible and all `c_i≠0`. In these
row and column bases the span is diagonal. Each paid rank-one frame lies on
one coordinate line. Project onto the coefficient of one line. This is a group
homomorphism to `Q`; factors on other lines become `I`. Every invertible data
target has a nonzero coefficient on every line. The projected endpoint minus
`I` therefore has rank at least `W`, whatever the auxiliary entries are. A
product of `q` factors has the form `I+XTY^T` with `T` of size `q`, so that line
carries at least `W` calls. The lines are disjoint. ∎

## 4. Proof of the theorem

Let `s<mW` nonzero calls succeed, with frame span `d`. Lemma 2 gives `d≥m+1`.
The relation lattice has rank `s−d`; the corollary of Lemma 1 gives `s−d≥2`.
This proves the general statement.

For `m=3`, `W=2` and `s=5` it requires `4≤d≤3`. Hence five calls fail, and
by splitting so does every shorter word. Six calls succeed without touching the
auxiliaries: write `C_w=∑_i(C_we_i)e_i^T` and use projector `E_{ww}` for each of
these three frames. ∎

The data targets need not be lower triangular or share a flag. Without
auxiliaries this recovers PR #468's two-register lemma.

## 5. Independent exhaustive check for one and two auxiliary registers

The engine [`engine.cpp`](engine.cpp) checks the `m=3`, `W=2`, `s=5` case
without Lemma 1. It only assumes Lemma 2, which is a frame-space argument.
For five nonzero frames that lemma leaves span four or five, hence a relation
lattice of rank at most one.

For each ordered word, the engine expands
`P(t)=∏_j((I+N_j)+t_jN_j)=∑_a M_a t^a` over all `a∈{0,1}^5`. It then tests
every possible collision pattern of subset monomials:

- `R=0` covers no relation and every generator with an entry of absolute value
  at least two; subset sums do not collide.
- For each cube vector `r` with support at least two, up to sign, subsets
  `a,b` collide exactly when `a−b=±r`.

This gives 117 options. For each, the grouped coefficients must have zero
off-diagonal entries and exactly one odd monomial on each diagonal. Such a
terminal fixes a subset representative `c_w` for each data target. If
`c_w+λr` has support at most two for some rational `λ`, that target is a
combination of two rank-one frames and has rank at most two. Only
`λ∈{−1,0,1}` can lower the support. A terminal whose two data targets both
pass would be reported as a candidate, not as a construction.

| Registers | Words | Diagonal terminals | Touching an auxiliary | Candidates |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `n=2` (no auxiliary) | 7,776 | 4,384 | 0 | 0 |
| `n=3` (one auxiliary) | 17,210,368 | 36,757 | 32,373 | 0 |
| `n=4` (two auxiliaries) | 24,883,200,000 | 183,432 | 179,048 | 0 |

Every ordered word is enumerated; no symmetry reduction is used. Zero
candidates exclude every rational frame realization for these register counts.
This is a second, computational proof for `n≤4`, independent of the pattern
analysis in Lemma 1. The `n=4` run took 80 minutes on one core.

Controls, all run by [`verify.py`](verify.py):

- **Positive length control.** With six calls and one auxiliary, the same
  engine reports 4,852 candidates, including the six-call construction. With
  `W=1` and three calls it reports the expected candidate.
- **Alphabet failure control.** Adding the rank-two scalar idempotent
  `E₀₀+E₁₁`, outside the model, yields 13,067 candidates at five calls. The
  rank-one scalar hypothesis is what the search tests.
- **Symbolic cross-check.** [`controls.py`](controls.py) recomputes terminals
  from Laurent exponent vectors reduced modulo `Zr`, without the engine's
  pairing tables. It matches all 7,776 words for `n=2`; all 614,656 words for
  `n=3`, `s=4` in full mode (every 31st in quick mode); and 35,080 words for
  `n=3`, `s=5`: every 500th word in enumeration order plus the word of every
  20th terminal.
- **Physical realization.** Sampled engine terminals are realized with exact
  rational rank-one frames having exactly the stated relation lattice. The
  formal product in `F₂[(Q^{3×3},+)]` is checked to be the reported diagonal
  monomial, and some data target has rank at most two (300 terminals).
- **Checker tampering.** Dropping one terminal line, altering one reported
  class representative, or flagging a terminal as a candidate is rejected.
- **Formal identities.** The six-call construction with a restored auxiliary
  passes, and a one-frame mutation fails. A `J` block over all three registers
  with frames `B,−B` is the identity; separating its two factors by a data call
  breaks diagonality.

## 6. Scope and handoff

What is proved: for `m=3`, two data registers and any number of dirty
auxiliary registers, six calls are necessary and sufficient. For general
`(m,W)`, any saving word has relation corank at least two and span above `m`.
The proof is written; the finite engine independently checks one and two
auxiliaries. Neither is a proof-assistant verification.

What is not covered:

- **Clean ancillas.** Requiring correctness only on zero-initialized
  auxiliaries weakens (1). The derivative step no longer forces coordinate
  projectors outside `S`. PR #288 §1.3(b) prices an ancilla of rank `R` at `R`
  extra children; this note makes no claim about them.
- **Three data registers, eight calls, one dirty auxiliary.** Lemmas 1 and 2
  exclude frame spans at most three and at least seven. Spans four, five and
  six remain. The next step is an `n`-register version of the two-relation
  argument (PR #468 §5 and PR #720 §§3–4). With four registers there are 120
  projectors instead of 28, so its pattern-plane classification and common-`J`
  calculation must be redone. Then span four, as in PR #720, and the span-five
  scalar catalogue of PR #468 §7 would remain. This engine cannot reach eight
  calls directly: `120^8` ordered words is infeasible without those reductions.
- Other paid primitives, non-abelian frames and free address maps between
  calls, as in PR #468.

## 7. Reproduction

From the repository root:

```sh
python3 -B research/bit-dirty-auxiliary-registers/verify.py
```

The quick mode compiles the engine, reruns `n=2,3` and all controls, and
checks counts and terminal-file digests against [`EXPECTED.json`](EXPECTED.json)
in about one minute. `--full` adds the six-call positive control (about three
minutes), the complete `n=3`, `s=4` symbolic comparison (about four minutes)
and the `n=4` exhaustive run (about 80 minutes). It requires Python 3.10+ and a
C++17 compiler; `CXX` selects the compiler. [`RESULT.json`](RESULT.json) records
the full run used for this note.
