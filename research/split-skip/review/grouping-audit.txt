General validity of singleton/pair grouping

Let V be the current weighted complete graph, with vertex weights w_a and
edge values e_ab, all representing disjoint families of original inputs.
For S contained in V, write

  F(S) = sum_{a in S} w_a + sum_{{a,b} contained in S} e_ab.

Partition V into disjoint groups G_i of size one or two. Their order and
choice of partners are arbitrary. The coarse vertex weight is

  W_i = sum_{a in G_i} w_a + sum_{{a,b} contained in G_i} e_ab,

and the coarse edge E_ij is the sum of edges from G_i to G_j. Therefore the
coarse weighted graph's induced sum on any set of groups equals F on their
union. By induction the recursive outputs give total F(V), outside[i] =
F(V minus G_i), and far[i,j] = F(V minus (G_i union G_j)).

For a in G_i let H_i = G_i minus {a}. Since |H_i| is at most one, it has no
internal edge. The carry and value list in the implementation give

  sums[a] = sum_{u in H_i} w_u + sum_{j != i} sum_{u in H_i,v in G_j} e_uv,

  strips[a][j] = sum_{u in H_i} w_u
                + sum_{k != i,j} sum_{u in H_i,v in G_k} e_uv.

Hence outside[i] + sums[a] = F(V minus {a}). If a,b belong to one group,
that group is exactly {a,b}, so outside[i] is the required leave-two-out
value. If a is in G_i and b is in G_j, the required value is the disjoint
sum of four terms: far[i,j], strips[a][j], strips[b][i], and the edges
joining G_i minus {a} to G_j minus {b}. These are exactly the four terms
in the current producer. No vertex weight or edge is duplicated or lost.

For a singleton group H_i is empty: its carry, strips, sums, and relevant
remaining cross edges are zero. The inherited zero-filtering code handles
this case correctly. Thus introducing singleton groups does not require a
new scalar identity or a new asymptotic transfer theorem.

Termination must also be explicit: at each nonbase step there must be at
least one size-two group, so the number of groups is strictly less than
the number of vertices. The current split-skip one-pair-split code applies
only when len(points)>3 and guarantees that condition. More general
multiple-split searches must forbid splitting every remaining pair.
Groups of size three or larger are NOT covered by this argument or the
unmodified recurrence: H_i can then contain internal edges, and deleting
two vertices within one group can leave part of that group behind.

Structural search results

The baseline h=23 PR53 graph has 40,329 additions, 12,719 matched donors,
and 32,946 physical roles. All following role counts use exhaustive
maximum-cardinality matching through the inherited admissibility graph;
they are screening results, not independently completed fixed-profile
certificates.

1. Longer skip distances 2 through 6 were tested with sequential and
   aggregate near-prefix construction. All were worse than baseline. The
   best among those was skip distance 3 with aggregate near prefix,
   R=35,727. Ordinary prefix/suffix gave R=36,432.

2. Balanced leave-one-out recursion gave R=37,814. Chunk sizes 2 through 6,
   ordinary/skip inner layout and skip/balanced outer layout, all lost.
   The best chunked result was R=36,146.

3. All 256 top-strip mixed left-skip/right-skip masks at h=23 were checked.
   None improved R=32,946. All 256 masks selecting original skip versus
   ordinary prefix/suffix at interior positions were also checked; none
   improved it. Deeper strip layouts remained the PR53 baseline in these
   enumerations. These are bounded negative results, not global lower
   bounds or a proof that skip-prefix is optimal.

4. Ordered singleton/pair compositions were explored beyond splitting
   preexisting pairs, allowing singleton positions that shift the pairing
   of later vertices. The h=23 search completed 962 configurations; its
   best R was 32,667. The h=25 scan was stopped after reaching R=42,969,
   since the root/order-agent combined searches had already produced
   stronger candidates. The best h=25 third-level group sizes were
   (1,2,1,2,2), which are not expressible solely by splitting original
   consecutive pairs. This family is a possible future search direction,
   although the present result is not competitive with the final winner.

5. An independent exact-support enumeration looked for alternative
   non-original source partitions for PR54 paid clones. It indexes all
   scalar supports, restricts to parents with at least two unmatched
   continuation chains, and requires each partition child to be an actual
   operand of an unused nested provider. It found no eligible alternative
   partition on the original PR53 h=23 or h=25 graphs, and no eligible job
   on the final order-search h=23 weighted graph. This says nothing about
   possibilities after additional clone rounds or with other matchings.
   The reusable discover_alternative_candidates(c, links) returns complete
   job dictionaries and also checks provider times and the whole chain.

Implementation audit

Reviewed research/split-skip/graph.py, producer.py and witness.py as they
stood during construction, together with the inherited verifier,
producer, balanced assembly and timeline interfaces. No concrete
arithmetic or replay error was found in that version.

The producer redirects the original audit's record directory and graph
factory to the new construction, while preserving the inherited exactness
and source checks. It regenerates scalar supports, envelope labels,
admissible pinned matching uses, independent role/rank recount, ordered
fixed-basis profiles with CRT checks, physical timelines, every dirty
basis vector in both orientations, and the unchanged data-corner pairs.

The arithmetic witness alone does not perform all that physical replay.
It checks fresh scalar graph identity, pinned hashes, stored timeline and
profile summary consistency, exact characteristic bounds, and the 47
assembly inequalities/seven margins. Therefore a complete reproduction
must report successful runs of BOTH the full producer and the arithmetic
witness, and retain the producer receipt. Hash-pinning a profile is not
equivalent to regenerating it.

Explicit checks that each configured summand order and common-point order
is a permutation of the proper indices were recommended. They make a
malformed configuration fail at its source, even though the full dense
support/output audit already supplies the essential finite correctness
check. The root agent accepted this hardening recommendation.

Remaining proof dependencies and review caveat

The grouping argument establishes the new scalar recurrence for arbitrary
singleton/pair partitions. The finite replay establishes the particular
graphs and selected carrier/clone actions only if its exact checks finish
successfully. Neither settles the inherited general transfer obligations:
the stated base theorem, residual compiler, fixed-alphabet multitape
recursion, analytic/semantic/routing/recovery interfaces, and eventual
threshold arguments remain dependencies. This is an independent agent
audit of a conditional finite witness, not formal verification or
independent external mathematical peer review. A lower role count alone
is not sufficient to assert a better final exponent; the recomputed fixed
profiles, exact characteristic bound, and full assembly must all pass.
