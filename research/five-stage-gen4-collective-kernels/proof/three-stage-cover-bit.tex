\subsection{Atom contract and common endpoint}
Fix an odd prime $q$, chosen below, and put $R_w=\mathbb Z/q^w\mathbb Z$.
An atom is a complete consecutive $w$-digit address field. Write $C_n$ for
reversal of the $n$ atom positions, without reversing digits inside an atom.
The recursive contract is the involution
\[
 F_A(H,D)=(AC_nD,C_nA^{-1}H),
\]
where $A$ is a diagonal array of units on the head atoms. Its entries may
depend on exposed ancestor row fields and the atom index, but not on the
fresh role fields of this invocation or on the head/data values. Only the
polynomial-time descriptors generated by the construction are required;
no constant-cost oracle for arbitrary functions of a prefix is assumed.
Every call preserves all complete spectator fields and restores its row
coordinate. The original unweighted call has $A=I$.

For a split idempotent $P$ on the head coordinates set
\[
 D_P=\begin{pmatrix}I-P&PC_n\\C_nP&I-C_nPC_n\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad F=D_I.
\]
These are involutions, and $D_P-I$ has split rank $\operatorname{rank}P$.
Nested commuting-projector identities are polynomial identities and require
no symmetry of $P$. For $n=mf$ and $G=g\otimes I_f$ define
\[
 Q_g=\operatorname{diag}(G,C_nGC_n).
\]
Then $Q_gF=FQ_g$ and $Q_gD_PQ_g^{-1}=D_{GPG^{-1}}$.
Consequently conjugating every base frame by any $g\in\operatorname{GL}_m(R_w)$
leaves the same full physical endpoint $F$.

Put $Q_A=\operatorname{diag}(A,I)$. Conjugate the entire completed scalar
word by $Q_A$. Scalar gates mix roles having the same address map, so they
commute with $Q_A$. A current role routing also commutes with $Q_A$, since
it changes only fresh fields. Thus only address residuals change, and the
completed endpoint is $Q_AFQ_A^{-1}=F_A$. More explicitly, when a port
changes charts from $g$ to $gR_s$, its local label changes from $M$ to
$R_s^{-1}MR_s$, and
\[
 (gR_s)(R_s^{-1}MR_s)(gR_s)^{-1}=gMg^{-1}.
\]
This routing is a change of row/chart, not an uncharged dense address map.
The adapted chart $Q_AQ_gQ_A^{-1}$ commutes with $F_A$. None of these
conjugations is executed as an extra normalization pass.

\subsection{Explicit rational three-stage geometry}
Use the retained PR97 local producer with
\[
 h=23,\qquad v=1771,\qquad R_{\rm loc}=28866,\qquad \ell=506.
\]
Its physical projector ranks and scalar word are unchanged. On
$\mathcal A=\mathbb Q^{23}$ take the form $H_0=(I-J/9)/2$.
For a triple $S$ let $q_S=\chi_S$; then $q_S^tH_0q_S=1$.
Fix $q_0=\chi_{\{1,2,3\}}$ and $K=q_0^\perp$. A rational basis is
\[
 k_i=e_i-(e_i^tH_0q_0)q_0,\qquad 2\le i\le23.
\]
Projection along $q_0$ is injective on the span of $e_2,\ldots,e_{23}$,
since $q_0$ has nonzero first coordinate. Thus this is indeed a basis.
Let $\mathcal B,\mathcal C$ be orthogonal copies of $K$ and put
$\mathcal E=\mathcal A\perp\mathcal B\perp\mathcal C$, of dimension $m=67$.

Choose a coordinate permutation $\pi_S$ taking $\{1,2,3\}$ to $S$.
It preserves $H_0$. The rational isometry $R_{12,S}$ fixes $q_S$ and
$\mathcal C$, and exchanges $\pi_Sk_i\in q_S^\perp\subset\mathcal A$
with $k_i\in\mathcal B$. Define $R_{23,S}$ by exchanging the same vectors
with their copies in $\mathcal C$, fixing $q_S$ and $\mathcal B$.
Both maps are involutions. A hyperedge $(g,S)$ uses the invocations
$gR_{12,S},g,gR_{23,S}$, with active spaces
\[
 g(\mathcal B+\mathbb Qq_S),\qquad g\mathcal A,
 \qquad g(\mathcal C+\mathbb Qq_S).
\]
Their projectors commute; their pairwise and common intersection is the
projector $P_{gq_S}$, and their sum is $I+2P_{gq_S}$.
This follows from the orthogonal decomposition
$\mathbb Qq_S\perp q_S^\perp\perp\mathcal B\perp\mathcal C$ before
conjugation. These are the cumulative-projector three-stage transfer
identities: the three scalar shears complete the interchange, with zero
interstage connectors and no rank-one endpoint correction. Each port
family visits the full cover bijectively, by right multiplication with
its displayed involution. The physical ledger is therefore three copies
of the local auxiliary, data and center histograms, and three copies of
the exterior histogram $m-\text{nullity}$.

\subsection{One weighted child on a generic residue class}
Work first on one atom column. Let $g$ be its weighted residual involution,
of split rank $r$ relative to $I$. If the cross block has generic northeast
rank profile, unit lower row and column elimination, without normalizing
pivots, gives
\[
 Lg_{XY}R=ED_pF_0^t,
 \quad E=(e_1,\ldots,e_r),\quad
 F_0=(e_m,\ldots,e_{m-r+1}),
\]
with invertible diagonal $D_p$. Conjugate by $\operatorname{diag}(L,R^{-1})$.
Split rank and the unit pivot minor give a factorization
\[
 g-I=\binom EB(A_0,D_pF_0^t),\qquad
 A_0E+D_pF_0^tB=-2I_r.
\]
For clarity, if $g-I=UV$, then $g^2=I$ gives
$U(VU+2I)V=0$. The unit minor supplies a left inverse of $U$ and a right
inverse of $V$, proving the identity over the local ring itself.
Set
\[
 K=-(B+F_0D_p^{-1})E^t+F_0D_p^{-1}A_0(I-EE^t),
 \qquad S=\begin{pmatrix}I&0\\K&I\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The identities $KE=-B-F_0D_p^{-1}$ and
$D_pF_0^tK=A_0+E^t$ give
\[
 SgS^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}
 I-EE^t&ED_pF_0^t\\F_0D_p^{-1}E^t&I-F_0F_0^t
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
This is one weighted reversed interchange on the first $r$ head and last
$r$ tail coordinates. All other factors are unit lower intrabank maps or
lower cross-bank additive maps. Thus variable diagonal units remain in
the child's weight array rather than becoming unsupported routing passes.

For $n=mf$, group atoms by coarse coordinate $i$ and column $j$.
If $L_P,R_P,D_P$ are unweighted normalizers for the current projector and
$A_j=\operatorname{diag}(A[if+j])$, then
$L_j=A_jL_PA_j^{-1}$ and $R_j=R_P$ are unit lower normalizers for
$A_jPC_m$. The child's exact contiguous index rule is
\[
 A_{\rm child}[kf+j]=A[i_kf+j]D_P[k].
\]
The cross-bank atom pairing is reversed, and $C_r\otimes C_f=C_{rf}$.
Hence the middle operation is exactly one $rf$-atom call of the same
contract, with no interleaving or gathering. Its coefficients depend only
on parent prefix fields, which become ancestor spectators at the child;
the child's fresh roles come from disjoint unused row stock.

All unit lower adapters require $O(n)$ atom passes. The retained ordered
affine lemma implements each later-target shear in $O(V)$, including
prefix-controlled offsets. The generated descriptors have polynomial cost
in $w$: good-class evaluation follows one ancestral index per level;
fallback evaluation branches by a constant per level. Since depth is
$O(\log e)$ and $w=\lceil e^{1/1000}\rceil$, this costs $e^{O(1)}=w^{O(1)}$.
Stored ancestor data use $O(w\log e)$ bits. The proof of the ordered affine
lemma still applies with these controls: polynomial preparation per prefix
is absorbed by the complete $q^w$ target fiber. Unrelated spectator counters
are incremented by ripple counting rather than read in their entirety.

\subsection{Weighted leaves and a genuine local-ring fallback}
At one atom write $J_a=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&a\\a^{-1}&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$,
$U(a)=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&a\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ and
$L(b)=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\b&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$.
Then
\[
 J_a=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)U(a)L(-a^{-1})U(a),
 \qquad U(a)=J_1L(a)J_1.
\]
Thus a weighted leaf uses four old ordinary atom interchanges, three
prefix-controlled shifts and one fixed sign. With
$a_{\rm old}=384599/10^{10}$ it costs $O(Vw^{1-a_{\rm old}})$.
This constant occurs only at stopped leaves.

A bad residue class cannot be treated by field Bruhat elimination:
an entry zero modulo $q$ need not vanish modulo $q^w$. Instead Gaussian
eliminate the full invertible residual of size $N_0=2m$ with unit pivots.
Each invertible Schur complement has a unit in its next pivot column;
row swaps and unnormalized additions clear that column. There are at most
$N_0(N_0-1)$ additions and $N_0$ swaps. Pivot choices are determined by
the unweighted low residue class: diagonal conjugation scales every
Schur-complement row and column by units and preserves its zero pattern.
The final diagonal has product a fixed sign, since the residual has
determinant $(-1)^r$ and row swaps change only the sign.

A lower addition is a paid additive adapter. An upper addition is
conjugated into a lower addition by two ordinary coarse-field swaps.
For a field of $f$ atoms, let $R_{\rm tail}$ reverse the later field.
Then the ordinary weighted swap is
\[
 J_A=R_{\rm tail}F_AR_{\rm tail},\qquad
 J_A(H,D)=(AD,A^{-1}H).
\]
Each $R_{\rm tail}$ is one unweighted reversed call on the first and last
$\lfloor f/2\rfloor$ atoms, fixing a possible middle atom. Thus $J_A$
costs at most three children of widths $f,\lfloor f/2\rfloor,
\lfloor f/2\rfloor$. A paired diagonal scaling is $J_aJ_1$, at most six
children. The full diagonal is a product of $N_0-1$ such pairs and a fixed
sign. Across the two banks the reversed pairing already makes the swaps
and diagonal pairs directly reversed calls; the preceding ordinary bounds
remain conservative. Reindexing a tail weight by $j\mapsto f-1-j$ changes
only its descriptor. Charging every addition as upper therefore gives
\[
 6N_0(N_0-1)+3N_0+6(N_0-1)<32m^2.
\]
Every child has width at most $f$, hence ratio at most $1/m$. Coefficients
are computed from constant-sized local-ring matrices and parent weight
descriptors; only units are inverted. This proves the fallback cost,
including its actual reversals, rather than assuming nonunit pivots away.

\subsection{Finite covers, batching, and the strict moment}
Take $q>2^{80}$ avoiding all fixed rational denominators and rank witnesses.
At atom width $w$ use the finite cover $\operatorname{GL}_m(R_w)$ and its
right-multiplication edges $g\mapsto gR_s$. Every vertex has the unique chart
\[
 g=g_0+qG_1,\quad g_0\in\operatorname{GL}_m(\mathbb F_q),
 \quad G_1\in(\mathbb Z/q^{w-1}\mathbb Z)^{m^2},
\]
using fixed representatives for $g_0$. Right multiplication permutes $g_0$
and acts affinely on $G_1$, with a carry determined by $g_0$. Its fixed
dimensional rational linear part factors into fixed scalings, ordered
shifts and a constant number of old width-$O(w)$ interchanges. Thus each
fixed port routing costs $O(Vw^{1-a_{\rm old}})$.

Let $W_0=|\operatorname{GL}_m(\mathbb F_q)|(2v+3R_{\rm loc})$.
The full role count is $W(w)=W_0q^{m^2(w-1)}$. Split preceding rows into
low role type, high matrix and unused stock. Batch \emph{all} high matrices
for each low type in one stream. A frame edge is one child on this whole
batch, of volume $V/W_0$, and routing is executed once per fixed operation
type. Consequently neither the number of tapes nor the number of child
programs grows with $w$. High cardinality cancels in the normalized moment,
but its $m^2(w-1)$ digits are charged in the row reserve.

For each fixed projector require the leading minors of sizes $1$ through
$r$ to be units. Each cleared minor polynomial in $g_0$ is nonzero:
similarity to $\operatorname{diag}(I_r,0)$ supplies a witness after the
fixed rank is preserved modulo $q$. Their total degree is at most $m^3$.
The polynomial zero bound and conditioning on invertibility (a factor at
most two) give bad fraction at most $2m^3/q<10^{-16}$ for each edge type.
No simultaneous union bound over edge types is needed, since their moments
are averaged separately. Good classes use the whole-rank child; bad
classes use the full $32m^2$-child fallback just proved.

Let $H_r$ be the three-stage histogram described above and
$W=2v+3R_{\rm loc}=90140$. The retained ledger gives
\[
 \sum_r rH_r=6037356,\qquad Wm-\sum_r rH_r=2024=2v-3\ell,
 \qquad \max\{r:H_r>0\}=66.
\]
For $a=747/2000000$, $\tau=1-a$, and $b=10^{-16}$ the conservative moment is
\[
 \mu_\tau\le
 \sum_r\frac{H_r}{W}(r/m)^\tau+
 b\frac{32m^2\sum_rH_r}{W}(1/m)^\tau<1.
\]
The entire fallback is added; no displaced good contribution is subtracted.
The exact script \path{scripts/three_stage_cover_network.py} and certificate
\path{certificates/three-stage-cover-network.json}, from the retained axis
\path{certificates/three-stage-cover-bit-input.json}, give
$1-\mu_\tau>6.3397366396\cdot10^{-7}$.
The certificate uses the 32-term positive atanh logarithm series with its
geometric tail, $\exp(u)\le1+u+u^2/(2(1-u/3))$, and upward rounding to
the grid $2^{-120}$. All inputs have $0\le u<3$; these are rational upper
bounds, not floating-point root estimates. Since every child ratio is at
most one and $\tau<1$, this same strict bound implies $\mu_1<1$.

With $T(n,w)$ denoting time divided by logical volume, the fixed low-type
profile $\{c_r\}$, including fallback classes, satisfies
\[
 T(n,w)\le\sum_rc_rT(r\lfloor n/m\rfloor,w)+Cn+Cw^{1-a_{\rm old}}
 \quad(n\ge m),\qquad
 T(n,w)\le Cw^{1-a_{\rm old}}\quad(n<m).
\]
Peeling fewer than $m$ leftover atoms uses the weighted leaf algorithm.
The constants are independent of $w,n$ and ancestor values. Induction,
using $\mu_1<1$ for the linear term and $\mu_\tau<1$ for the stopped term,
gives $T(n,w)=O(n+n^\tau w^{1-a_{\rm old}})$. The maximum proper ratio
$66/67$ has halving degree $47$, so recursion depth is $O(\log n)$.
