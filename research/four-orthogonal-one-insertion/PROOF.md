# Four orthogonal demands with one insertion

This contribution was produced by Nick Petalas. Apache-2.0.

## Contract and claim type

Let R be a commutative unital Boolean ring. Thus every x in R satisfies
x squared = x, the characteristic is two, and every elementary row addition
is its own inverse. Let lambda_0,...,lambda_3 be nonzero, pairwise orthogonal
elements, and put g = lambda_0+...+lambda_3. There are exactly four arbitrary
dirty rows a_0,...,a_3 and a disjoint preserved source p. The required map is

    a_j -> a_j + lambda_j p,   j = 0,...,3.

The gates are P_i(beta), adding beta p to row i, and E_ij(alpha), adding
alpha times row j to row i for distinct i,j. Every coefficient belongs to R.
A word is fixed and finite. All four original dirty columns must return
exactly. There are no extra rows, free scalar maps, erasures, clean fields,
intermediate readouts or input-dependent choices.

We restrict to exactly one nonzero P gate. Each E has a common price c > 0;
an insertion at row i has price mu_i > 0, independent of its coefficient.

**Result.** For each insertion row i, the exact minimum is twelve ADDs and
one insertion, costing mu_i+12c. Across insertion rows it is min_i(mu_i)+12c.

The six-ADD column lower bound is an exact finite certificate over F2^4.
The argument below lifts it to every R in the stated contract. The upper
word is a written identity. This combination is a computer-assisted scoped
proof, not a proof-kernel verification or a multiplication theorem.

## 1. The insertion coefficient is forced

Write M for the ADD-only prefix matrix and L for the ADD-only suffix matrix.
The full dirty endpoint requires LM=I, so M=L inverse. The source endpoint
requires L(beta e_i)=lambda. Consequently every lambda_j is a multiple of
beta, and beta is a linear combination of the lambda_j, by applying L inverse.
In a Boolean ring, g generates exactly the ideal generated by the lambda_j:
g lambda_j=lambda_j and g is their sum. Thus (beta)=(g).

An idempotent has a unique generator of its principal ideal. Indeed, if
beta=xg and g=y beta, then beta g=beta and beta g=g, respectively. Hence
beta=g. This is the same ideal invariant used in lee-phys's #5514 triple
contract. The number of rows does not change this argument.

Both the suffix L and the inverse prefix M inverse therefore send g e_i
to lambda. Inverting an ADD word preserves its number of gates. A column
lower bound of six gives six ADDs on each side of the insertion.

## 2. Finite component quotient

For a proposed finite word, take the Boolean subring generated by its finitely
many coefficients, the lambdas and 1. This subring is finite: if generated by
m elements, every element is a sum of the at most 2^m products formed by
choosing each generator or its complement. The nonzero such products are
pairwise orthogonal atoms summing to 1. Every generator, and hence every
subring element, is constant zero or one on each atom.

Each nonzero lambda_j contains at least one atom. Choose an atom under each
lambda_j; the four are distinct by orthogonality. Restricting to these four
components gives a ring homomorphism into F2^4, sending the lambdas to
(1,0,0,0),...,(0,0,0,1) and g to (1,1,1,1). A word with fewer gates would
give a word with at most that many gates after projection. Projected zero
coefficients are harmless no-ops.

It therefore suffices to exclude a column word of length at most five over
F2^4. This reduction uses only the finite coefficient subring of the proposed
word. It does not assume that the whole Boolean ring is finite or that
lambda_0+...+lambda_3 equals 1.

## 3. Complete finite distance certificate

Represent a source column over F2^4 by four row masks v_0,...,v_3 in
{0,...,15}. Every atomic component must have a nonzero binary column:
elementary row additions are invertible on each component. Equivalently,

    v_0 OR v_1 OR v_2 OR v_3 = 15.

There are exactly (2^4-1)^4 = 50,625 such states, choosing independently
one nonzero four-bit column for each of the four components. There are
65,536 possible packed four-nibble indices including invalid states.

For root zero the starting state is (15,0,0,0), and the target is (1,2,4,8).
An ADD from row j to distinct row i replaces

    v_i by v_i XOR (alpha AND v_j),   alpha = 0,...,15.

All other row masks stay fixed. This is exactly the stated gate alphabet.

The verifier first computes distances by breadth-first search, enumerating
nonempty subsets of the donor mask. These are all distinct nontrivial changes;
no-op gates cannot improve a shortest path. The queue has capacity 50,625,
and a state is enqueued only once.

It then certifies the labels in a different representation. The four nibbles
now store the four independent binary component columns. For each of the
50,625 vertices, each of the twelve ordered distinct row pairs and each of
the sixteen component coefficient patterns, it applies the binary row update
component by component. For all 9,720,000 transitions, including no-ops,
it checks that the successor stays in the domain and that

    abs(potential(successor)-potential(vertex)) <= 1.

The starting potential is zero, and the target potential is six. Telescoping
along any finite gate word gives length at least six. This local inequality
certificate does not require trusting the discovery search's shortest-path
claim. Its separately encoded transition scan covers every coefficient and
every state in the exact finite quotient. The verifier also checks the domain
of every one of the 65,536 indices and the coordinate conversion.

Permuting row indices gives the same lower bound for every insertion root;
the target permutes by relabeling its four atomic demands. The verifier checks
an attaining word for each root. The distance array is generated deterministically,
and its SHA256 is printed in the receipt. Use --write-distances PATH to export
its 65,536 bytes, with 255 marking invalid states. No distance labels or
receipts are handwritten.

## 4. Six-ADD column attainer and complete restoration

Fix the insertion row i. In any Boolean ring, execute the column word C:

    E_ji(lambda_j), for each j != i in increasing order;
    E_ij(lambda_j), for each j != i in increasing order.

It has six ADDs. On g e_i, the first three give lambda_j in each other row,
because lambda_j g=lambda_j. The last three change row i to

    g + sum_(j != i) lambda_j squared = lambda_i.

Thus C(g e_i)=lambda. The complete chronological word is

    C inverse; P_i(g); C.

It has one insertion and twelve ADDs. Its dirty matrix is C C inverse=I,
and its source column is C(g e_i)=lambda. Every other source column is
unchanged, including the preserved p. This proves the upper bound and the
claimed exact single-insertion price.

Formal endpoint checks use the universal five-atom Boolean ring F2^5, with
lambda_j encoded by 1<<j and the unused complement encoded by 16. Identity
coefficients are 31, whereas g is 15. This explicitly retains arbitrary dirty
data on the complement, which a test restricted to sum lambda_j=1 would miss.
A separate binary interpreter checks every five-bit input on each atom,
forward and inverse, for all four roots.

## 5. Boundaries and credit

The new result is the four-demand column minimum, its finite certificate,
and sharpness of the twelve-ADD single-insertion restoration bill.
lee-phys's #5514 supplies the three-row Boolean contract, its ideal invariant
and the prefix/suffix method. Its exposed triple price envelope is not checked
here. Retain the #5304/#5259 lineage, Ali Romman's insertion feasibility,
Krzysztof Wojt's support classification, Ross Richardson's restoration
arguments, Nick Petalas's older ring-valued fanout contracts and the earlier
contributors credited in that source.

Nick Petalas's #5948 classifies three-row Pareto vectors, and #6663 prices
a three-row directed-cycle family. They are background, not the construction,
identity or finite certificate used here. No predecessor executable is imported
or replayed. The six-ADD column construction is an elementary mask routing
identity. Its complete twelve-ADD word specializes Ali Romman's #5310 general
4(k-1) restoring upper bound. That upper bound is credited prior work; this
package adds its sharpness for the stated four-demand contract.

Exactly four rows and exactly one insertion are essential scope restrictions.
This result does not classify extra workspace, multiple insertions, unequal ADD
prices, non-Boolean rings, restricted coefficients or a physical native caller.
Scalar gate counts are not recursive child ranks. Routing, frame changes,
owned storage, stock, precision and the full supplier invoice remain unpriced.
All five selected framework assumptions remain. No new kappa or selected-frontier
change is claimed.
