Three active targets and one returned register
=============================================

The target is diag([C_1],[C_2],[C_3],1). Each C_i is an invertible
lower-triangular rational 3 by 3 matrix. The fourth input is arbitrary.
The complete fourth row and column must be those of the identity. An
eight-call solution would use four scalar registers. The three-register
lower bound in PR468 does not by itself exclude this model.

The span-four lemma extends to zero target frames. In its proof, replace
W in each target-minus-identity rank bound by the number of active
invertible targets. Select an active C_1 for the common-ratio flag
argument. A zero target remains zero in every projected block. All
active targets retain invertible projected blocks. Thus a frame span
of dimension at most four needs at least three calls per active target.
An eight-call solution with three active targets has frame span at
least five and relation-space dimension at most three.

Two active targets need at least six calls even with any number of
returned registers. For a putative word with at most five calls, a
frame span of dimension at most four is covered by the preceding
lemma. The only remaining case has five independent frames. Distinct
subset sums then cannot cancel. Each off-diagonal polynomial coefficient
is zero, and differentiation at one makes every scalar projector
diagonal. The calls are disjoint by target role. Rank subadditivity
then requires three calls for each of the two active targets.

One active target needs at least three calls. Its frame belongs to the
span of all paid frames, by projection with rational functionals. Fewer
than three rank-one frames cannot span an invertible 3 by 3 matrix.

A three-active, one-returned eight-call word must therefore be scalar
irreducible. A common proper binary invariant subspace splits the
target characters between smaller blocks. If both blocks have active
targets, the counts are one and two and require at least 3+6 calls.
If one block has all three active targets, it has scalar size three,
so PR468 requires at least nine calls. Repeated target characters do
not cause a problem: split the invariant binary subspace within each
repeated-character space before taking the diagonal blocks.

For a possible returned coordinate w, each nonzero binary row r other
than e_w must meet at least three x vectors. The same condition holds
for y vectors. Otherwise a rational functional can kill the at most
two frames seen by r while remaining nonzero on an active target in
its support. The projected circuit fixes r, but its target does not.

There is one more first-order condition. Delete the three active
diagonal entries from each binary projector N_j. The resulting pattern
vectors span a binary space of dimension at most three. The omitted
entries may have nonzero target derivatives. All other entries,
including the returned diagonal entry, have zero target derivatives.
Their derivative rows lie in the reduction modulo two of the saturated
integer relation lattice, which has dimension at most three.

The dirty_ancilla.py checker uses exact rational collision branching.
At a diagonal terminal it tries each returned role w and adds the
relation C_w=0. It then checks that each of the other three target
frames is not in the span of any two paid frames. An accepted terminal
would still need a rank-one rational matrix realization and a complete
formal identity check.
