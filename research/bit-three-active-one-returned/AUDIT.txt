Three active targets and one returned register: independent audit

Model and relation bound

The exact target is diag([C1],[C2],[C3],1), up to a permutation of
coordinates. The C_i are invertible lower-triangular rational 3-by-3
frames. The fourth input is arbitrary and must be returned unchanged.
The paid frames are nonzero rational rank-one matrices. There are eight
binary rank-one idempotent scalar calls.

The span-four lemma continues to hold with zero targets: replace the
number of scalar registers in each projected target-rank count by the
number of active targets. Choose an active invertible target when the
proof forms target ratios. Zero targets stay zero in each block; active
targets have invertible diagonal blocks. Thus frame span at most four
requires at least nine calls. Any eight-call circuit needs frame span
at least five and rational relation dimension at most three.

A candidate must be scalar irreducible. Let V be a common binary invariant subspace. The product preserves
V after extension to the group algebra. Distinct group monomials are
linearly independent. Thus each coordinate projection onto one equal-
character space preserves V. This includes the zero character of the
returned register, which differs from every active target character.
V is the direct sum of its intersections with those character spaces.
Choose a basis in each equal-character space adapted to V. This keeps
the endpoint diagonal and puts every paid scalar projector in block
upper-triangular form. This argument does not assume that V is a
coordinate subspace or that the three active characters are distinct.
A rank-one idempotent in this block form has exactly one nonzero
diagonal block, and that block is a rank-one idempotent. Thus the calls
partition between the two diagonal blocks. A block with one active
target needs at least three calls by rank subadditivity. Two active
targets need at least six, even with returned coordinates: at at most
five calls, span at most four is covered by the preceding lemma; the
only other case has five independent frames. No distinct subset sums
then collide, and differentiation of the diagonal polynomial at one
forces all scalar projectors to be coordinate projectors. Each active
target still needs three frames. A proper block with all three active
targets has size three and is covered by PR468. Every split therefore
needs at least nine calls. These arguments are also in DIRTY-MODEL.txt
and were reviewed separately by bit8_algebra and the root agent.

Visible scalar distance is a rational-rank argument

Fix a possible returned coordinate a. For a binary row v, let J be
the calls with v*x_j=1. If a rational functional kills all frames in
J, each projected factor fixes v. The projected target can fix v only
if it kills each active target selected by v. Thus each such target is
in the rational span of the frames in J. Fewer than three rank-one
frames cannot span an invertible 3-by-3 target. Every nonzero v other
than e_a must therefore select at least three calls. The argument for
y_j and columns is the dual statement. It uses no reduction of address
frames modulo two.

The code correctly takes the union over all four possible returned
roles. A row or column of weight greater than one with fewer than three
visible calls rejects every role. A failing singleton leaves only its
own role. Two different failing singletons reject every role.

The exact returned-register condition

At a rational diagonal terminal, every diagonal entry is one monomial,
by the determinant/unit argument in PROOF-AUDIT.txt. A returned entry
must equal [0]=1 in the group algebra of rational frames. This requires
its exponent to be exactly zero, not merely even or zero modulo two.

dirty_ancilla.py implements this correctly: for each possible returned
coordinate, it adds that exponent as an exact rational relation using
Fraction RREF. It rejects zero paid frames and relation rank above
three. It then checks every active target against the exact rational
span of each pair of paid-frame units. This test does not use a short
representative in a binary quotient. Each rejected active target is
therefore a rational sum of at most two rank-one matrices.

Adding a relation after reaching a diagonal terminal cannot create an
off-diagonal term. There is no missing collision branch at this step.
Trying every coordinate covers every possible returned role.

The finite binary quotient is only a necessary identity check

Let K be the actual saturated integer relation lattice in Z^8. Its
modulo-two image has dimension at most three. The off-diagonal
augmentation derivative rows belong to this image. The returned
diagonal derivative row belongs to it too, since the derivative of 1
is zero. The active diagonal rows need not belong to it.

If the selected derivative rows have rank greater than three, the
candidate is impossible. If their rank is exactly three, their span
is the full modulo-two image of K. Map the formal polynomial to the
finite group algebra of F2^8 modulo that span. Every off-diagonal
entry must still vanish. If the returned diagonal is included, it must
equal one, and the code subtracts that constant before checking it.

The H-alphabet filter uses only off-diagonal rows. The later rank-two
and generic rank-three filters also use a candidate returned diagonal.
When the selected rank is below three, the rank-two filter retains the
word because an additional unknown relation may still occur. It takes
the union of the results for all admissible returned coordinates.

This check is sound because every valid rational identity maps to the
finite quotient identity. The converse is false. In particular, a short
binary representative of an active target does not imply a rational
rank bound. None of the inspected filters makes that false inference.

Independent H-alphabet census

The alphabet consists of four coordinate projectors and the four
projectors from H=I+J_4. An independent script enumerates multiplicities
as weak compositions, not as the producer's combinations with
replacement. It checks irreducibility by closing the orbit span of each
nonzero vector, instead of enumerating invariant subspaces.

It groups orders by their subsequences on every pair of noncommuting
letters. These dependence signatures identify trace classes. An
independent explicit orbit calculation then applies coordinate
permutations and reversal. This does not reuse the producer's greedy
trace-normalization procedure to establish class coverage.

For each class, direct dense matrix multiplication in the finite group
algebra checks the binary quotient condition. This differs from the
producer's formal polynomial expansion and remainder grouping.

The complete independent census agrees:

  20 distance-compatible multiplicity classes;
  14 scalar-irreducible classes;
  216720 distinct raw orders within those representative classes;
  2771 classes after the valid order symmetries;
  1115 words rejected by the binary quotient identity.

The other 1656 representative words use the separately audited rational
collision solver. Its saved result has zero candidates. The finite
census does not claim an independent full rational collision replay.

Reversal remains valid for nonsymmetric projectors

For any idempotent N, D_N(z)^(-1)=D_N(z^(-1)). Invert a valid diagonal
word, then apply the address-group map A -> -A. This reverses factor
order and preserves the original diagonal target. Thus generic order
searches may use reversal without transposing the individual N_j.
Commuting adjacent factors is also valid for every assignment of frames.

Independent coverage of the generic off-pattern catalogue

There are 120 binary rank-one idempotents in four registers. Their
nonzero off-diagonal parts have 104 distinct patterns. The derivative
bound implies that the actual off-pattern span has dimension at most
three: the row rank and column rank of the derivative matrix agree.
Its basis can be chosen from the actual patterns. Thus direct spans of
all one-, two-, and three-element pattern subsets cover every case.

Each catalogue pool contains every projector with pattern in its space,
including all four coordinate projectors. If that full pool is
reducible, every subset is reducible. A subspace invariant under all
coordinate projectors must be a coordinate subspace. Hence the full
pool is irreducible exactly when its union support graph is strongly
connected. It is valid to discard non-strong spaces. Individual
multisets in a strong pool still need their own irreducibility check.

The independent generator uses direct pattern combinations, a compact
twelve-bit encoding, Boolean matrix transitive closure, and explicit
coordinate-permutation orbits. It reproduces every representative and
every complete projector pool in the producer's catalogue:

  rank two:  4964 spaces,   634 strong spaces,   45 classes;

Review of the generic C++ multiset filter

rank3_multisets.cpp enumerates every nonnegative count vector with sum
eight in each pool. It requires actual off-pattern rank three, applies
the visible-distance condition, and tries every possible returned role.
For each role it appends the actual N_aa bit to the off-pattern vector
and requires augmented binary rank at most three. All of these are
necessary conditions. Multiplicity does not change the column-span
rank, but the distance count correctly includes multiplicities.

Its invariant-subspace check closes the orbit span of each nonzero
vector under the used projectors. A fixed proper orbit span is an
invariant subspace. If a proper invariant subspace exists, an orbit
started inside it cannot reach the whole space. The check is exact.

The integer fields called off must be a linear coordinate map for the
catalogue's off-pattern space. The separate input-binding check verifies
all projector labels and all pairwise XOR identities for these labels.
The source review does not alone certify an arbitrary unbound input file.

Exact collision search

At a state with rational relation space R, reduce the formal polynomial
modulo R. Choose a term in a nonzero off-diagonal entry. In any valid
extension of R, that term must collide with another surviving term in
the same entry. Branch on every such difference. Terms already removed
in pairs stay removed under every extension. Thus no necessary branch
is lost. Each new relation increases rank, and rank is at most three.
A diagonal terminal can add the exact returned exponent as a relation.
Adding this relation cannot create an off-diagonal entry.

The determinant of each paid factor is its frame monomial. A diagonal
terminal therefore has monomial product determinant. The frame group
is finitely generated and torsion-free, so its group algebra is a
Laurent polynomial ring over F2. Its units are monomials. Each diagonal
entry is consequently a single monomial. The exact rational pair-span
test rejects a target in the span of two paid rank-one frames. This is
a necessary rank-three condition, not a sufficient construction test.

Two engines complete the rank-three input: the primitive-integer
engine and the finite-field engine with exact rational reconstruction.
Both process all 1901600 words, return the same 538 terminal word IDs,
and have zero surviving target candidates. A Fraction implementation
also replays all 538 terminal words and rejects all of them. The rank-
two primitive-integer run processes all 27156 canonical words before
any finite-quotient pruning, and has zero diagonal terminals.

Why reduction modulo 101 is exact here

Every state has at most three independent original relation rows.
Each original row is a difference of two subset indicators, or the
returned subset indicator. Its entries are in {-1,0,1}. A rank or
membership test adds at most one further such row. Pair-span tests
also append two coordinate unit rows; expansion along those rows
removes them from the relevant minors. Every remaining minor uses at
most four bounded nonunit rows. Hadamard's bound gives absolute value
at most 16, which is less than 101. A nonzero rational rank witness
therefore cannot vanish modulo 101.

Each completed state RREF entry is a ratio of minors of order at most
three. Numerator and denominator have absolute value at most five.
Two such reduced fractions cannot have the same residue modulo 101:
their cross difference has absolute value at most 50. Thus the code's
bounded rational reconstruction is unique. The parity prune uses the
primitive integer lift of this rational row, and hence uses a member
of the actual saturated integer kernel. It does not assume that a
chosen set of nonprimitive integral generators is already saturated.

Bounds for the primitive-integer implementation

Completed primitive state rows have entries of absolute value at most
five, by the same minor bound. Their positive pivots are at most five.
A raw bounded row reduced against three such rows has intermediate
entries at most 1000. The separate pair-span helper can append two
unit rows: a conservative bound is 1000 for the first reduced row,
2000000 for the second, and 8000000000000 while reducing the target.
All are below the signed 64-bit limit. The common quotient denominator
is at most lcm(1,2,3,4,5)=60, and quotient-key entries are at most 960.
The reviewed arithmetic does not depend on signed overflow.

Scope of completion

The analytic reductions, the generic off-pattern catalogue, the full
rank-three multiset and order front end, and the exact collision tests
have passed independent mathematical review. The finite-field test
has an explicit rational-equivalence proof; it is not a probabilistic
prime test. The two final engines use different arithmetic but share
an input file, whose complete generation has a separate independent replay for both
rank two and rank three. Both the catalogue and the role union are
covered by these replays.

Subject to the proved span-four and three-register lemmas in PR468,
the exact eight-call classification excludes three active invertible
lower-triangular rational 3-by-3 targets with one arbitrary returned
register. Any positive shorter word can be padded to eight calls by
splitting a nonzero rank-one frame A into two consecutive calls with
frame A/2 and the same projector. Hence eight-call impossibility also
excludes shorter words. Nine coordinate calls realize the three
identity 3-by-3 targets and return the fourth register. The minimum
number of calls in this specified model is therefore nine.

This conclusion concerns four scalar registers, rank-one rational
paid frames, binary rank-one idempotent scalar projectors, and a
formal identity for all inputs. It is not a lower bound for every bit
construction in the repository. It gives no direct increase in kappa.
This audit is mathematical and computational; it is not a Lean proof.
