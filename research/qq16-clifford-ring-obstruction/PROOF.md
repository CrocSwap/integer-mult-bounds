# The retained QQ16 obstruction over normalized Clifford+T coefficients

Let `D=Z[1/2]`, let `zeta=exp(pi*i/4)`, and set
`R=D[zeta]=Z[zeta,1/2]`. This note extends the **coefficient-ring boundary**
of Nick Petalas's [PR2546](https://github.com/CrocSwap/integer-mult-bounds/pull/2546),
pinned at `3aff4ccbfaa1005e5093e95535e4d99aa3813e54`. It reuses that work's
explicit witness. It does not supply a new unread-minor census or read lower bound.

## Statement and fixed observations

Use the unchanged conserved `F0/B_1_2` pair and exactly the two-cut observation
contract of starship-s's PR2443, with its unread set changed to
`H=[1,25,3,4,5,6,8,12]`. Grant these complete owner/payload arrays:

1. `x1_A`, `x1_T`, `y1_A`, `y1_T` immediately after complete F0;
2. the 88 arrays `x1_(U\H)` at that same cut;
3. `y2_A` and `C=x2_U` immediately after B1, with no intervening modification.

There are 224 granted arrays. Reading and retaining these observations may even
be free. There is no observation of `y2_U`, original selected inputs, or an
additional ordinary query. The selected sets A,T, ordinary set U and integer
query matrix R in the source are fixed. In this note the ring is also named R;
where a distinction is needed the query matrix is written `R_query`.

**Theorem.** No fixed finite affine-linear circuit whose linear coefficients lie
in the ring `Z[zeta,1/2]` can recover all 224 original port arrays from these observations
for arbitrary original inputs. Arbitrary finite auxiliary workspace and affine
constants do not change the conclusion, when their initial values are the same
for the two inputs compared. Input-dependent advice or additional observations
are not part of this contract.

This covers the explicit normalized gate alphabet H, S, T, X, Z, CNOT and SWAP,
their inverses, permutations, tensor products, direct sums, and scalar linear
ADDs/dyadic scalings. Here H is the normalized Hadamard, `S=diag(1,i)` and
`T=diag(1,zeta)`. Fixed phases generated by zeta are included; arbitrary global
phases are not asserted to belong to this ring. The theorem excludes an even
larger class of finite R-linear circuits, so no gate synthesis theorem is needed.

## A proper ideal after adjoining the normalizations

The cyclotomic relation is `zeta^4+1=0`; the polynomial is irreducible over Q
(substitute `x+1` and apply Eisenstein at 2). Consequently every ring element
has a unique expression `c0+c1*zeta+c2*zeta^2+c3*zeta^3`, with each `cj` dyadic.
Equivalently the ring is `D[x]/(x^4+1)`.

Let `F25=F5[t]/(t^2-2)`. Two is not a square modulo five (the squares are
0,1,4), so this quotient is a field. There is a unital ring homomorphism

```text
phi: D[zeta] -> F25,
phi(2^(-1))=3,  phi(zeta)=4t=3/t.
```

Indeed `(4t)^2=2` and `(4t)^4=4=-1`, so the defining polynomial vanishes.
The integer map extends to D because two maps to a unit. In the unique basis,

```text
phi(c0+c1*zeta+c2*zeta^2+c3*zeta^3)
 = (c0+2*c2) + (4*c1+3*c3)*t  (coefficients reduced modulo five).
```

In particular `phi(1)=1` and `phi(5)=0`. Thus `5R` is proper. We do not claim
that `5R` is the entire kernel of this particular specialization.

The actual normalizing coefficients lie in the ring:

```text
i=zeta^2, sqrt(2)=zeta-zeta^3,
1/sqrt(2)=(zeta-zeta^3)/2, zeta^(-1)=-zeta^3.
```

Their images are respectively `2,t,3t,2t`. In particular adjoining normalized
Hadamards has not made five invertible. The coefficient `1/sqrt(2)` has nonzero
odd basis coefficients, so it is outside the old Gaussian-dyadic subring D[i].
This is a strict extension of the explicitly excluded class in PR2546.

The specialization is **not assumed to preserve complex conjugation**. For
example, i maps to 2, while its complex conjugate -i maps to 3; every field
automorphism fixes these prime-field elements. Gate inverses are instead exact
ring identities before specialization: `H^2=I`, `S*S^(-1)=I`,
`T*T^(-1)=I`, and the named permutation gates are involutions. These identities
remain true under any ring homomorphism. No reduced-unitarity claim is used.

## Source witness, with complete arbitrary-input boundary

For this paragraph write the query matrix as `R_query`. The authenticated packet
gives `R_A R_A^T=R_T R_T^T=4I`. Put `P_A=R_A^T/4`,
`z=(1,2,1,1,1,1,3,-2)`, and `q=10e_1` (zero-indexed centre coordinate).
Direct multiplication gives `(R_query)_H z=q`.

Choose every owner and payload entry of each port array constant, and initialize

```text
x_U=iota_H z, x_A=-P_A q, x_T=0,
y_U=0,       y_A= P_A q, y_T=0.
```

These are legitimate dyadic inputs, including nonzero selected dirt. The actual
cover maps are permutations of owner/payload addresses; hence they fix these
constant arrays. This is an input subspace of the original full-array contract,
not an assertion that arbitrary frames or arbitrary gates preserve constants.

The full conserved F0 equations now give a zero first query and

```text
x1_U=y1_U=iota_H z,
x1_A=x1_T=y1_A=y1_T=0.
```

The completed B1 future logger gives `y2_A=P_A q`, and its ordinary target
subtraction gives `C=x1_U-y1_U=0`. All 88 read coordinates vanish. In A order,

```text
y2_A=(5/2,-5/2,0,5/2,0,0,-5/2,0).
```

Therefore every entry of every granted observation is in `5D`, hence in `5R`.
The original port `x_1` is the constant array one. These source witness equations
are credited to PR2546 and recomputed from the pinned packet; they are not new.

Compare this input with the all-zero original input, using identical auxiliary
workspace and affine constants. The observation difference lies entrywise in
`5R`, and workspace differences are zero. Any R-linear gate, at any finite width,
maps vectors in this ideal to vectors in this ideal. This follows directly from
closure under finite sums and multiplication by R; induction proves it for the
whole circuit. Injecting/forgetting coordinates or permuting addresses also
preserves it. Constants cancel in the two executions, regardless of their values.
The required output difference at every coordinate of original `x_1` is one,
which cannot lie in `5R` because phi sends it to one. This contradiction proves
the theorem, without assuming that intermediate values remain constant arrays.

## Positive and failure boundaries

The old unread set `H0=[1,2,3,4,5,6,8,12]` still has a dyadic inverse. Its two
inverse orders are checked; this note does not obstruct that existing decoder.
The alternative H is invertible over Q with inverse entry `(0,1)=1/10`. Thus
the result is a coefficient restriction, not information loss over Q. Allowing
`1/5` invalidates the proper-ideal argument: `(1/5)*5=1`.

The witness does not obstruct recovery of only the original selected inputs:
those inputs in this family are already determined dyadically by `y2_A`.
Nonlinear division, measurement/postselection and normalization, input-dependent
branching, additional observations, or a changed first pair require separate
analysis. Merely adjoining these explicit normalized Clifford+T coefficients
does not repair complete-input recovery at the fixed cut.

No claim binds every available native frame or child operation to this alphabet.
Physical access, chronological retention, precision, native kernel costs,
all-size supplier admission and a full controller invoice remain open.
