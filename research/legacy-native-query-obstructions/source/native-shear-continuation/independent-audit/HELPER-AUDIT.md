# Audit of the dirty-helper extension

The proposed extension is valid under the stated model. Let R=F2[G],
H_i=I+N_i(g_i-1), and N_i=a_i b_i^T, with b_i^T a_i=1. Require
g_i!=1 and z outside <g_i> for each query. Let D be a constant binary
idempotent of rank three. The exact endpoint is

    P=I+(z-1)D.

This is equality in Mat_r(R) on every input. It restores every dirty
helper. The argument does not use an orthogonal projection D.

## Coloops

If a_i is an image coloop, choose a binary row alpha that kills every
other a_j and has alpha a_i=1. Suffix telescoping gives

    (z-1)alpha D=(g_i-1)b_i^T S,

where S is the suffix after H_i. If alpha D!=0, a coordinate has left
side z-1. Sums on the sets Hx, with H=<g_i>, kill the right side and
give a contradiction. If alpha D=0, multiply by S^-1 on the right.
This gives (g_i-1)b_i^T=0. A coordinate of the nonzero binary row b_i^T
is one, so g_i=1. This is also a contradiction. The inverse exists
because H_i^-1=I+N_i(g_i^-1-1).

For a covector coloop, choose a binary column v that all other b_j^T
kill and for which b_i^T v=1. Prefix telescoping gives

    (z-1)Dv=L a_i(g_i-1).

If Dv!=0, use the right multiplication orbits xH. If Dv=0, multiply
by L^-1 on the left. The nonzero binary column a_i again gives g_i=1.
Thus neither query list has a coloop. For a rational rank-one update,
every power fixes its nonzero rational fixed line. The map z=-I2
fixes no such vector, so the required cyclic exclusion holds.

## Query spans

Let U span all a_i and let W be the row span of all b_i^T. Suffix
telescoping puts each binary coefficient column of P-I in U. Prefix
telescoping puts each binary coefficient row in W. The coefficient at
z of P-I is D. Therefore

    im D is contained in U; rowspace D is contained in W.

Both query spans have dimension at least three.

With two negative queries, the sign character gives equal projectors.
Each span then has dimension at most four. Dimension four would make
each of the three positive images a coloop. The same statement holds
for their covectors. Thus both spans have dimension three and equal
the corresponding spaces of D.

With four active projectors whose sum is zero, write their sum as AB=0.
Their image and covector ranks have sum at most four. One remaining
query and the span bounds force both active ranks to equal two and
both full ranks to equal three. The remaining query is then a coloop,
so this case is impossible directly.

## Exact reduction and positive words

When U=im D and W=rowspace D, each query satisfies

    DN_i=N_i=N_iD.

Use a binary basis of im D and ker D. In this basis D=diag(I3,0), and
each N_i has only a three-dimensional productive block. The query is
identity on the helper block. This is an exact reduction to three roles.

The two positive unipotent branch arguments also work for arbitrary r.
The target coefficient D has rank three. Three rank-one target terms
must all be nonzero in the first form. Their four scalar couplings
force the unique full selected coefficient to be nonzero. Its ADDRESS
map C^-1 differs from both 1 and z, so the endpoint requires zero there.
In the second form the target coefficient factors into a sum of two
terms of rank at most one. Its rank is at most two, which excludes D.

For nonunit positive determinants, the ordinary determinant is used
only after the ADDRESS determinant map gives a commutative group
algebra. The endpoint then maps to I_r and has determinant one. The
four-active argument above applies to the cyclic dual-number character.
Define that character on the group generated by the query maps, whose
determinant image is <lambda>. It need not extend to all Q_(>0).

No initialized, discarded, or partial helper endpoint is covered here.
No statement about three-dimensional ADDRESS maps or eight queries is
made. This note is a theoretical audit; no helper search was run.
