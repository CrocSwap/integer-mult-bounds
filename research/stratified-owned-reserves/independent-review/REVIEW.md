## Least certificate, including mixed self/lower paths

The possible concern is a long self-chain followed by an expanding lower
edge. It does not create an omitted term. At one stage, let c be its local
cost, rho its self-contraction and y=c+a M_j the cost of exiting immediately
to lower stage j. After m self-edges and then that exit, the supremal path
cost is

    c(1-rho^m)/(1-rho) + rho^m y
      = (1-rho^m) c/(1-rho) + rho^m y.

It is a convex combination of the pure self-chain limit and immediate-exit
cost. Thus some endpoint is at least as large. Applying this elimination
successively leaves a strictly descending path whose final vertex carries
its full geometric self-chain reward. There are only finitely many such
descending paths in the fixed stage graph. Their maximum equals the stated
Bellman formula.

This also gives a lower certificate. For any maximizing descending path,
replace the terminal infinite chain by m terms. Its value increases to the
claimed certificate, with exact missing tail

    product(lower-edge factors) * c_terminal * rho_terminal^m/(1-rho_terminal).

Therefore no smaller coefficient bounds every finite abstract path. This is
sharpness for the stated real-volume relaxation, not an assertion that a
particular integer-sized supplier attains the bound. Nonnegative local terms,
strictly descending nonself edges, and rho<1 are essential.

The independent script enumerates complete **descending paths with exact
terminal geometric rewards**, rather than bounded-depth active paths. It
checks 1,068 coordinate certificates over 48 seven-stage rational graphs,
three source-shaped six-stage examples and the unit example. It evaluates
4,920 descending paths, gives an exact finite dual witness plus its remainder
for each certificate, and checks every Bellman inequality and tightness.
The source unit example is exactly 6,572. A one-unit reduction fails the
root's lower-exit inequality.

depth is at most 2+ceil(log_N E). Increasing both the stock base and exponent,

The logarithm of the newly added factor is O(E^rho log(2E))=o(E) for fixed
