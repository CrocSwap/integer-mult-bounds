# A four-call exact target in an integer shear alphabet

**Status: scoped exact minimum. No new kappa or supplier claim.**

## Question and model

Use the nonabelian address group

$$
G=\langle X,Y\rangle\leq \operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb Z),\qquad
X=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\quad
Y=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\1&1\end{pmatrix}.
$$

The paid address alphabet is exactly
$$
\mathcal A=\{X,X^{-1},Y,Y^{-1}\}.
$$
These are native reversible integer coordinate shears on an address pair
$(a,b)\in\mathbb Z^2$: $X$ adds $b$ to $a$, and $Y$ adds $a$ to $b$.
Their inverses subtract. Each label is unimodular and each difference from
the identity has rational rank one:

$$
X-I=E_{12},\quad X^{-1}-I=-E_{12},\quad
Y-I=E_{21},\quad Y^{-1}-I=-E_{21}.
$$

The scalar data has two roles, both arbitrary elements of
$R=\mathbb F_2[G]$. One paid factor is
$$
D_N([g])=I_2+([g]+[e])N,
$$
where $g\in\mathcal A$ and $N$ is any rank-one idempotent in
$M_2(\mathbb F_2)$. Every factor costs one call. There are no free address
updates between calls. The lower bound below also survives any intervening
constant scalar matrices over $\mathbb F_2$, since they add no group labels.

The target is
$$
B=X^2Y^2=\begin{pmatrix}5&2\\2&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\operatorname{diag}([B],[e]).
$$
Here $\det B=1$ and $\det(B-I)=-4$, so $B-I$ has rank two. Thus $B$ is not
one of the legal rank-one address labels.

**Question.** What is the minimum paid-call count for this target, over all
legal idempotent frames and all arbitrary dirty inputs?

## Result

The exact minimum is **four calls**. With
$P=E_{11}$, which is a legal rank-one idempotent,

$$
D_P([g])=\begin{pmatrix}[g]&0\\0&[e]\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
D_P([X])^2D_P([Y])^2
=\begin{pmatrix}[X^2Y^2]&0\\0&[e]\end{pmatrix}.
$$

This is a full formal group-algebra equality. On arbitrary dirty
$(u,v)\in R^2$, the endpoint is $(Bu,v)$. The second role is unchanged.

## Why fewer calls cannot work

In a product of $k$ paid factors, each factor contributes either the
identity group label or its one alphabet label in each expanded term.
Therefore every group element in every matrix-entry support is represented
by a word of at most $k$ letters from $\mathcal A$. This remains true for
arbitrary rank-one idempotents and for interleaved constant scalar matrices.
The coefficient $[B]$ in the target's first diagonal entry requires
$B$ to occur in that radius-$k$ word ball.

An exact enumeration of all $1+4+4^2+4^3=85$ words of length at most three
in $\mathcal A$ contains no $B$. Thus no word of at most three paid calls
can reach the target, regardless of the idempotent frames. The four-call
word above attains the bound. The verifier reproduces the 85-word check and
checks the complete $2\times2$ formal operator.

## Complete bill

| Item | Count | End state |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Paid factors, with labels $X,X,Y,Y$ | 4 | First role receives $B$ |
| Other dirty scalar roles | 1 | Second role is unchanged |
| Scratch roles | 0 | None borrowed |
| Copy, reset, inverse cleanup, or restore calls | 0 | No cleanup required |
| Uncharged address updates | 0 | Every label use is a paid factor |

The count is complete in this stated group-algebra model. It does not price a
larger bit-supplier interface or show that these labels improve the selected
recursive cost profile. The label operations themselves are the specified
native integer shears; a separate low-level hardware or compiler invoice is
outside this result.

## Relation to prior work

The three-register lower-bound package uses the additive group of
$M_3(\mathbb Q)$ and explicitly excludes extra roles and arbitrary free
address maps between calls. This result keeps exact formal coefficients and
rank-one paid updates, but refines the card to a specified multiplicative
nonabelian address family generated by opposite integer shears. It uses two
scalar roles and makes no claim about the original abelian theorem.

The model and factor notation follow the prior register-model line cited in
the [three-register bound](../bit-three-register-lower-bound/README.md),
which credits eumemic's PR #288 and huxint's PR #203. The [GL3 mixed-target
review](../bit-gl3-mixed-targets-length10/README.md) records a distinct
finite GL3 alphabet and a three-role probe through length seven. We do not
rerun either historical computation.

The overlap check rejected an S5 route because a prior result gives the
exact adjacent-transposition tariff for all symmetric groups. Other nearby
work includes the free-group commutator-pair claim and a four-role UT5(Z)
claim. This package uses a concrete integer matrix group and the different
target $X^2Y^2$; it does not claim novelty for the general factor formalism.
The current UT3(F4) central-triple PRs also use a different field, alphabet,
target and role count. See [OVERLAP.md](OVERLAP.md) for exact heads, URLs and
the live searches made for this pass.

## Reproduce

From the repository root:

~~~sh
python3 -B research/sl2z-four-call-native-target/verify.py
python3 scripts/research_package.py --package research/sl2z-four-call-native-target --check-only
~~~

The verifier uses only the Python standard library and exact integer and
$\mathbb F_2$ arithmetic. Its finite enumeration is limited to the 85 words
needed for the length-three lower bound; the operator identity itself is
checked in the full formal group algebra.

## Limits

This proves a minimum for one endpoint in one declared abstract address
model. It does not certify a physical bit implementation of the group-algebra
registers, a supplier invoice, a favorable recursive moment, or a change to
the selected result. No larger target search was run.

## Open next questions

Can $B=X^2Y^2$ and $B^{-1}$ share paid factors in the two-role endpoint
$\operatorname{diag}([B],[B^{-1}])$, whose coordinate-projector word costs
eight calls? This matters because a one-target word has no slack below its
four-call minimum; only cross-role sharing could lower the combined count.
The first step is a complete 4+3 meet-in-the-middle over the 24 legal
$(N,g)$ factors, covering every word through length seven with exact
group-algebra joins and an eight-call positive control. The blocker is proving
a lower bound if the split finds no seven-call word; the one-target support
argument alone does not settle cross-role cancellation.
