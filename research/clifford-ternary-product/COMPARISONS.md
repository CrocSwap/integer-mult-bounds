# Comparisons on the same ordinary binary inputs

Let N=3^m and φ(x)=Σxⱼ3ʲ for x∈{0,1}^m. An input defect has its bit q(x) at φ(x) and zeros elsewhere. This section uses the same two defects in the signed dense-template caller. The baseline receives the resulting two ordinary binary strings, including their declared length. The strong specialized tests below also give the comparator the recovered defects for free.

## An output-size obstruction for explicit coefficient methods

Choose two arbitrary, independent quadratic Boolean forms Q₁,Q₂ on m−1 bits. Use the additional low bit as a selector, qᵢ(x₀,x′)=x₀⊕Qᵢ(x′). Each defect has exactly 2^(m−1) input ones. A direct support-pair algorithm executes exactly 4^(m−1) positive contributions.

Write c(v,t′) for the ordinary uncarried product coefficient, with low output trit v∈{0,1,2} and upper output word t′∈{0,1,2}^(m−1). There is no index carry in ternary addition of two binary digits. For any fixed t′ let w(t′) be its number of ones. There are exactly 2^w upper source pairs, since a trit one chooses 01 or 10. Each pair has one low bit in each input that makes its selected value one. Its low sum is assigned to exactly one of the three coefficients. Consequently

    c(0,t′)+c(1,t′)+c(2,t′)=2^w(t′).

The largest coefficient in each triple is at least 2^w/3. Its literal binary magnitude takes at least w−1 bits, also as a lower bound when w=0. Across all upper words the sum of w is (m−1)3^(m−2), by counting each position separately. Therefore the total number of bits in an explicit list of the nonzero coefficient magnitudes is at least

    3^(m−1) ((m−1)/3 − 1) = Ω(Nm).

The count uses only the isolated low selector. The functions on the other m−1 bits may also choose independent quadratics at each high prefix. This gives the same lower bound with a growing number of distinct required chart labels. A high bit can switch a low quadratic cross term on and off, which excludes a single chart on the resulting full affine hull. On this family the sector consumer uses O(N(log(m+2))^0.831) time, including high-block accumulation. [Literal multi-chart instance](check_sectors.py)

At least 3^(m−1) coefficient addresses occur. This argument needs no randomness, no cost per address and no assumption about internal arithmetic. It applies to sparse output lists and to dense arrays of ordinary binary magnitudes. Ce Jin and Yinzhan Xu give a Las Vegas O(t log t) algorithm for nonnegative convolution on a word RAM, where t is output support. Here t=Θ(N), and that coefficient-list interface incurs the displayed binary payload before ordinary carry. Pascal Giorgi, Bruno Grenet and Armelle Perret du Cray measure the bit cost of sparse polynomial multiplication against the explicit input and output sizes. The displayed lower bound applies to the size of such an explicit integer output. An exponent-coded, circuit-described or directly carried output has a different size. Supplying a compact circuit for a product still leaves the cost of emitting its binary value. These results give no lower bound for all algorithms on the represented family. [Jin and Xu](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.20326) · [Giorgi, Grenet and Perret du Cray](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.11959)

The usual per-coordinate rank-three algorithm evaluates a linear polynomial at zero, one and infinity, multiplies, then recovers (p₀,p₁−p₀−p∞,p∞). At m coordinates, literal recursion uses 3^m scalar products, 2(3^m−2^m) forward additions across the two inputs, and 2m3^(m−1) interpolation subtractions. Grouping ℓ≥2 low coordinates into b=3^ℓ-bit blocks reduces the number of leaf products to 3^h where h=m−ℓ. In each high input cell there is a one at exponent ≥3^(ℓ−1)=b/3 inside its low block. Set its most significant low digit to one and choose the free selector digit. Throughout evaluation every word is a positive sum of such words. At every completed interpolation level each resulting value is a nonempty sum of products of the evaluated low words, including the middle cross. Each has at least 2b/3 bits. The algorithm that materializes the 3^h records at each of its h interpolation levels thus writes Ω(hb3^h)=Ω(Nh) bits, even with exact variable-width integers and free pointwise multiplication. Precomputed leaf tables leave this cost in that schedule. This comparison permits alternatives that fuse the levels or change representation.

## Dense interactions under every ordering

For a quadratic form Q on d bits, let B be its alternating binary matrix of cross coefficients. On a cut U|V the sign matrix of (−1)^Q differs from the binary character table (−1)^(xᵀ B[U,V]y) by invertible diagonal signs. If the binary cross rank is k, exactly 2^k distinct mutually orthogonal real character rows occur. Its rank over the rationals is exactly 2^k. The Boolean indicator is half the difference of the all-ones matrix and the sign matrix, so its rational rank is at least 2^k−1.

There are explicit input descriptions with this obstruction on *every* balanced cut. To see existence, choose each upper-triangular edge bit independently and uniformly. A fixed a-by-(d−a) cross block has independent fair bits. The number of matrices of rank ≤k is at most 2^(kd), by factoring each through a k-dimensional space. For d/3≤a≤2d/3 and k=floor(d/12), failure probability for some cut is at most 2^(d+kd−2d²/9), which tends to zero. The number of edges concentrates at half of all possible pairs, by the variance bound for independent bits. Two independent connected dense graphs with these cut strengths therefore exist. For connectivity, a disconnection has probability at most Σ_(1≤j≤d/2) binom(d,j)2^(−j(d−j))=o(1). To include the isolated selector, cuts of m=d+1 bits can leave d/3−1 vertices on the smaller side of the core. Using that lower endpoint in the same count changes the exponent by O(d). The cross rank over the full cut equals that of its core. One can test all cuts for any supplied graph in 2^m poly(m)=o(N) operations. The multiplication algorithm works for every input in the class without testing these lower bounds.

There is also a deterministic infinite family. Take d=a² and use the complement of the square a-by-a grid as the graph on the nonselector bits. Each balanced cut of the core, allowing a loss of one for the selector, crosses at least a/6 grid edges for all sufficiently large a. If at least a/6 rows are mixed, each gives a horizontal crossing. Otherwise there must be one pure row of each color, since the mixed rows contain fewer than d/6 vertices. Each vertical column between the two pure rows then contains a crossing. The bipartite crossing graph has maximum degree four. Choose one crossing edge and discard all crossing edges incident to a neighbor of either chosen endpoint. This discards at most 32 edges per choice. The chosen edges give an identity submatrix, so cross rank is Ω(a). Taking the complement adds the all-ones cross matrix over F₂ and lowers rank by at most one. This deterministic dense family forces ranks 2^Ω(√m) for the signed value table under every balanced cut.

The random-graph count gives (1−o(1))2^binom(d,2) admissible hard cross-term descriptions already at fixed period. They encode different bit words, and multiplication by a fixed nonzero word is injective. An explicit complete answer table has more than O(N) entries. Small lookup tables or shared descriptions can still help a different algorithm. Leaf queries with a free random-access address also use a different primitive from a fixed number of sequential tapes. The coefficient-output lower bound remains valid with any internal lookup method that still writes the stated output.

In particular every bit ordering in the probabilistic family has an exponentially large middle bond for an exact tensor train of the Boolean defect values. A binary tensor tree has an edge giving a balanced cut, and the same rank restriction applies to that edge. A single product over disjoint bounded-size blocks has rank one across a balanced union of its blocks. A matching graph has a separate direct linear-time ordinary algorithm. Factor its signed truth-table integer into one sum of at most four signed powers of two per edge, with singleton factors for unpaired bits. Multiply these factors from both operands in increasing largest-bit order. Each shift/add at index j processes O(3^j) bits, with at most two constant-size factors per j, hence O(N) bit cost. For a Boolean indicator take half the Cantor support template minus half the signed table. The support template itself is a product of singleton factors. The same finite expansion prices the product of two indicators. The rank argument excludes a fixed bounded-width decomposition on the interacting family. Sums using different partitions may have different ranks.

The unknown-support decoder in PR #7815 assumes a supplied train on the appropriate matrix alphabet and tensor class. A cut-rank lower bound does not alone give a running-time lower bound for arbitrary uses of that decoder. The overlap here is the algebra of quadratic phases and exact elimination. The named canonical dictionary inverse has no role in computing the product. ## Two incumbent complete executions

Let n=Θ(N) be the ordinary input extent for the dense templates, p=6 ceil(log₂ n) and s=ceil(log₂(p+2)). The two pinned complete proposals perform fixed full-array operations that do not depend on operand values. These bounds count actual required movement in those schedules. They are independent of the proposals' upper bounds on multiplication time.

* In PR #7849 the complete external field has Tp=Θ(n) bits. Its cofinal controller has Θ(s³) different axis widths ℓ, each Θ(s³). Distinct width classes run serially. In any class choose one original axis of length r, with ceil(log₂ r)=Θ(ℓ). Its exact Fourier mean sends a basis impulse on that axis to r values of modulus 1/r. The local rational circuit has operator error 2^(−P)t^O(1)<1/(2r) eventually. Its impulse thus has at least r nonzero outputs. A diagonal cannot enlarge support and one H on a chosen local bit can increase it by a factor of at most two. At least ceil(log₂ r) local H stages are therefore present in that class. The actual schedule executes a full tensor diagonal traversal for each such stage before its H call. Each traversal of the materialized fixed-width field costs Ω(Tp) bit movements. Already one transform in the fast branch spends Ω(n s⁶)=Ω(n(log log n)⁶) on every input, including these dense examples. This support argument applies to the local rational word before its separately priced data rounding. [Controller and local guarantee](https://github.com/aliromman/integer-mult-bounds/blob/3615fd982f2f4dc7172bef6f0dc9a4f77d5ab6dd/research/direct-circulant-fourier/COMPOSITE-OUTER.md#L5-L13) · [Actual pack and data loops](https://github.com/aliromman/integer-mult-bounds/blob/3615fd982f2f4dc7172bef6f0dc9a4f77d5ab6dd/research/direct-circulant-fourier/tensor_word.py#L119-L167)
* In the Gaussian construction of PR #7846, write g=10⁻¹⁴. The cofinal fast branch has d=Θ(p^(1−g)) axes and groups of M=Θ(p^(1−3g)). The complete field again contains Θ(n) fixed-width bits. The prescribed broad transform keeps the whole dyadic cube through every group. It exposes and restores one group core, uses the full-spectator H and coarse-dependent diagonal scans on all cores, and supplies the materialized result to the next group. One such traversal per group already costs Ω(n d/M)=Ω(n p^(2g)) on these ordinary input strings. This is movement in the written grouped execution. No physical small-size demonstration of that asymptotic schedule is needed for the count. A dense hard subfamily uses independently chosen u-bit periods with high bit one and between u/3 and 2u/3 ones, u=2^floor(m/2). Repeat each through K=floor(N/u) full units. Add one shifted defect to the first repetition stream and subtract the other from the second. The defect has highest position ≤(N−1)/2 before the u-bit shift. Above that range plus one full period, the high complete periods are untouched, since a single carry or borrow meets a terminating bit in at most u positions. Both actual binary words have Θ(n) ones and zeros and canonical length n=Ku. The low u bits retain the literal period. Distinct legal images are ordinary binary integers, and no period or graph description is furnished to the caller.

## Executed witness

The finite replay combines both independent dense graphs, both isolated selectors and the signed dense-template input in one call to the multiplication entry point. The 6,560-bit operands contain 4,116 and 3,642 ones. Both seven-vertex cores have full-cut witnesses on the eight input digits with minimum rank two over all 91 distinct balanced cuts. The two folded sparse defects give 5,153 nonzero ordinary coefficients among 6,561, with 11,999 literal bits including single zero placeholders. The signed defect delivery issued 15,449 deposits, 9,242 ripple steps and 82,054 head moves, using 6,563 positions. These counts cover that accumulator only. The public product has 13,119 bits.
