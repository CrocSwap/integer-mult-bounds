# Higher-rank transported-response cohorts

The sufficient synthesis target is an actual changed scalar word, nested address frames, all clean source spans, all signed target columns, all five-stage binary formal columns, complete bank tiling, fixed finite invoice, two exact moment engines and all47 outer inequalities. No unconstructed gadget or free recovery is assumed.

Let E of dimension k be a common nondegenerate subframe of each selected helper first physical frame, with a common cold cut and exact transported signed response equations. For a pivot whose original first frame has dimension f, its initial gauge changes0 to E and its first child changes f to f-k. For a distinct donor, setup moves0 to E once and the first child changes f to f-k. Hence the local child changes are

- pivot: e_(f-k) - e_f;
- distinct donor: e_k + e_(f-k) - e_f,

omitting zero-rank calls. Scalar setup and restoration remain fully charged in the finite invoice. The exact address compiler and all-column checks must admit the literal word; this ledger alone does not prove its endpoint correctness.

If E is the whole first frame of every member, k=f, the donor histogram is unchanged and each pivot removes one rank-f child. Any admitted signed dependency then strictly decreases the paid recurrence numerator and reduces the residual stock consistently. This is a concrete sufficient construction, not a claim that a dependency exists on the current data.

For rank3 firstframes, a rank2 cap gives donor splitting2+1, exactly the same rank histogram as the old rank1 split1+2. It reduces each pivot child from3 to1 instead of3 to2. Therefore any common-cap signed dependency supported by the larger cap can yield a stronger construction, provided the larger frame is nondegenerate and its complete primitive/chart/bank costs pass. Our native scan should first use identical first frames, then registered rank2 intersections of first frames, rather than guessing scalar gains.

Novelty: linear dependency compression and frame retiming are established ideas, and rank-k parameterizations may already exist upstream. The finite higher-rank cohort search described here is a proposed application. Neither a first-ever technique claim nor an achieved kappa follows from this note.
