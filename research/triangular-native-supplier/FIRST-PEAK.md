# All occupied tape at the first connected precision

Use canonical signed input intervals and the fixed-head bit machine, including the input, output, installation, compiler, saved parent data, scratch, padding, cleaning and the historical maximum reached on each reusable physical head. Start with fresh or reset work tapes and a distinct output interval. The input consists of the two canonical signed binary intervals. Charge all output bytes that this call produces. Use the source's standard scalar arithmetic or its named bounded primitive. An arbitrary caller-supplied callback and unrelated bytes on externally owned files are outside this theorem. This is a bound on this tape representation. Host filesystem internals and process RSS are different measurements.

At $A=355763540965264$, the first public precision covered by the connected theorem is $p=p_*=6\lceil2^A/6\rceil=2^A+2$. Set $s=A+1$ and $T=2^L$. Every canonical input at this precision has $L\le p/6+3$. Its **complete historical tape uses fewer than $2^p$ bits**. This compares complete absolute storage across different operating scales. The smaller same-input asymptotic estimate has its own onset.

## Recursion and historical high-water space

The numerical recursion divides $h\le p$ by $M\ge2$, giving at most $s+1$ dynamic heights. At the first connected precision the direct cutoff gives at most eight same-stage positive divisions by $N_i$ and a stopped call. Six successive stages add at most 54 such heights. The finite compiled scopes, per-scalar Karatsuba, and the private Beneš recursion have already been included in the local bills. Thus $s+128$ bounds the number of dynamic levels through the complete multiplier.

Bound the size of each local bank and the next child's input inductively, starting with $K(T+1)$ cells and allowing $K^2$ per level for both parent and child. Siblings and the three transform invocations run serially. Reuse scratch by routine, physical role and depth. The existing data stacks use the top-only frame and return convention. The machine retires or reuses procedure banks after returns. The implementation uses the fixed stack and spool heads. For an upper bound, count a virtual interval for each role, slot and depth. There are fewer than $K^{2(s+129)}$ such intervals. If a physical head concatenates suspended intervals, its high-water extent is bounded by the sum of their individual maxima. At most $s+128$ active levels contribute to the earlier $K^2$ induction. Charging the largest interval at every possible role and depth bounds even maxima reached in different phases. In particular the complete nonobserver backing is at most

    X = (T+1) K^(8(s+130)).

The stated extent formulas determine payload lengths and slot numbers before the machine assigns head positions. The resulting global head position is less than $X<2^{p/5}$, so it fits the $p$-bit global address allowance in the control bill. This avoids any circular assumption on the width of an allocated payload.

Movement totals, past peaks, H-call counts and CRT statistics can also be included without estimating $C_*$. They are write-only observers as far as control decisions and payload outputs are concerned. Project them out. A terminating run of the resulting deterministic fixed binary tape machine never repeats a full configuration. With at most $X$ occupied bits, $X$ control/head names and $X+1$ positions per head, a configuration including the finite program, input and output has an encoding of fewer than $4X^2$ bits. There are fewer than $2^{4X^2}$ visits. One recorded move or transfer adds at most $2X$ to its meter. A CRT group count per event is at most $X$. Collecting an instrumented stage can sum at most $X$ such counters. Even squaring the generous allowance $2X^2\,2^{4X^2}$ takes fewer than $16X^2$ bits per reported integer. There are at most $X$ statically or dynamically allocated meter slots. Allowing another factor $X$ for distinct historical tapes, copies for arithmetic and intermediate aggregates gives complete occupied backing below $32X^4$.
