\begin{abstract}
We show that the discrete Fourier transform of every length $n$ can be computed
exactly in
\[
 O\bigl(n(\log n)^{\theta}(\log\log n)^{4-\theta}\bigr),\qquad
 \theta=1-\frac{607}{1250000}=1-4.856\times10^{-4},
\]
operations, in the exact complex-arithmetic model of OpenAI's
\emph{An explicit power saving for the exact discrete Fourier transform}
(25 September 2026). There the saving $1-\theta$ is about $2.1\times10^{-13}$.
Two changes give the improvement. First, a frame transition of residual
dimension $\rho$ is executed as one recursive call to $C^{\otimes\rho f}$
instead of $\rho$ separate directional steps. Second, the fixed finite network
is replaced by the complex supplier of the community repository
\texttt{CrocSwap/integer-mult-bounds}. That network was built by many
contributors, credited in Section~\ref{sec:credits}. The rest of OpenAI's
argument is used unchanged. The same bound holds for exact complex convolution
as a bilinear algorithm, with general multiplication allowed for the pointwise
products as in OpenAI's Corollary~1.3. The theorem depends on the correctness of that network. Its
proofs are written arguments, checked in part by scripts, and have had no
independent human review (Section~\ref{sec:scope}).
\end{abstract}

\section{Result}
We use the model of \cite[\S1.1]{oai-dft} without change: exact complex
arithmetic at unit cost, input-independent scalars, a specified Fourier root,
and unit-cost integer operations on $O(\log(n+2))$-bit words. Scalar
preparation, schedule construction, index work and data movement are charged.
Throughout,
\[
 C=\frac12\begin{pmatrix}1+i&1-i\\1-i&1+i\end{pmatrix}
\]
is the matrix of \cite[(2.1)]{oai-dft}.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
There is a deterministic algorithm that, for every $n\ge1$ and every
$x\in\mathbb C^n$, computes $F_nx$ exactly in
$O\bigl(n(\log n)^{\theta}(\log\log n)^{4-\theta}\bigr)$ operations,
where $\theta=1-607/1250000$. The same bound holds for the linear convolution
of two vectors in $\mathbb C^n$ by a bilinear algorithm (not an input-linear
circuit), with general field multiplication allowed for the pointwise products,
as in \cite[Corollary~1.3]{oai-dft}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}
For every fixed $\kappa<607/1250000$, both problems take
$O\bigl(n(\log n)^{1-\kappa}\bigr)$ operations; for example,
$\kappa=4.85\times10^{-4}>2^{-11.01}$.
\end{corollary}

In \cite{oai-dft} the exponent comes from one fixed network through
\cite[Theorem~2.6]{oai-dft}: $C^{\otimes k}$ costs $O(2^k(k+1)^{\theta_0})$
with $\theta_0=\log_m\lambda$ and $\lambda=m-\Delta/W_*$. In that recurrence every
unit of residual dimension is a separate directional step on a fiber of $f$
bits, so it pays one full level of recursion. Here a residual of dimension
$\rho$ is a single call on $\rho f$ bits (Theorem~\ref{thm:batched}). The
critical exponent then solves the moment equation
$\sum_\rho N_\rho(\rho/m)^\theta=W$. The same batched accounting is used for
integer multiplication in \cite{cms}, where it was introduced by icekylinx
(``projector batching'' and ``whole-residual complex recursion'').
Section~\ref{sec:proof} explains why the remaining sections of \cite{oai-dft}
apply unchanged.

\section{Batched networks}
Write an array address as $f$ columns of $m$ bits, followed by spectator bits.
A \emph{columnwise} map applies the same map to every column.

\begin{definition}\label{def:network}
A \emph{batched network} of width $m$ on $W$ roles is a finite list of
operations on $W$ role arrays and finitely many temporary arrays. It must have
the following property for every $f\ge1$ and every spectator set: for arbitrary
initial role arrays $A_1,\dots,A_W$, with temporaries starting at zero, the list
leaves every role array equal to $C^{\otimes mf}A_w$, tensored with the
identity on spectators. Each operation is one of the following.
\begin{enumerate}
\item A \emph{gate}: a fixed linear map with coefficients in
  $\mathbb Q(i)$, applied pointwise across a fixed set of arrays (the same
  address in each array). Copying an array, erasing a temporary and renaming
  arrays are gates.
\item An \emph{adapter} on one array: a columnwise invertible affine binary
  address map, followed by multiplication by $\omega^f i^{q(x)}$, where $q$ is a
  sum over columns of one fixed function $\F_2^m\to\mathbb Z/4$ and
  $\omega\in\mathbb Q(i)$ is a fixed constant (a per-column scalar).
\item A \emph{child} of width $\rho\in\{1,\dots,m\}$ on one array: $C^{\otimes\rho}$
  or $C^{-\otimes\rho}$ on $\rho$ fixed bit positions of every column, that is,
  $C^{\pm\otimes\rho f}$ along those $\rho f$ bits.
\end{enumerate}
Let $N_\rho$ be the number of children of width $\rho$.
\end{definition}

Children on temporaries are counted in $N_\rho$, but temporaries are not roles.
This is the usual rank deficit: $\sum_\rho\rho N_\rho<Wm$ is necessary for a
saving, and copies of shared values spend part of it.

The property for every $f$ is not automatic. A list that is correct for $f=1$
can fail for larger $f$. For example, copy $A$ to $T$, apply a child $P$ to $T$,
and subtract $T$ from $A$. This gives $I-P^{\otimes f}$, not $(I-P)^{\otimes f}$.
The following condition rules this out.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:columns}
Give every array a \emph{frame}, an invertible operator on
$\mathbb C^{\F_2^m}$ with entries in $\mathbb Q(i)$.
\begin{itemize}
\item Roles start at the identity, and a temporary created by a copy takes the
  frame of its source.
\item An adapter or child replaces the frame $R$ of its array by $XR$, where $X$
  is its one-column operator. An adapter's operator includes its per-column
  scalar $\omega$.
\end{itemize}
Suppose every gate touches only arrays whose current frames are equal as
operators, and every temporary ends at zero. Let $S$ be the matrix of the
scalar network obtained by deleting all adapters and children, and let $R_w$ be
the final frame of role $w$. Then for every $f$ the list maps
$A_w\mapsto\sum_{w'}S_{ww'}R_w^{\otimes f}A_{w'}$. If for $f=1$ it maps
$A_w\mapsto C^{\otimes m}A_w$ for every role, then for every $f$ it maps
$A_w\mapsto S_{ww}^{1-f}C^{\otimes mf}A_w$. A final adapter with $\omega=S_{ww}$
(identity address map, $q=0$) followed by the fixed gate $S_{ww}^{-1}$ multiplies
role $w$ by $S_{ww}^{f-1}$, which gives the property of
Definition~\ref{def:network} for every $f$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By induction over the list, each array $a$ holds
$R_a^{\otimes f}\sum_{w'}S^{(t)}_{aw'}A_{w'}$, where $S^{(t)}$ is the scalar
network so far. An adapter or child applies $X^{\otimes f}$ and so multiplies the
frame. A gate acts on arrays that hold $R^{\otimes f}v_j$ with the same $R$. It
maps them to $R^{\otimes f}\sum_jg_{ij}v_j$, because a pointwise scalar map
commutes with an operator applied identically to every touched array. A copy
duplicates $R^{\otimes f}v$. For $f=1$ the hypothesis gives
$S_{ww'}R_w=\delta_{ww'}C^{\otimes m}$. Since $R_w$ is invertible, $S_{ww'}=0$ for
$w'\ne w$ and $R_w=S_{ww}^{-1}C^{\otimes m}$. Hence
$S_{ww}R_w^{\otimes f}=S_{ww}^{1-f}C^{\otimes mf}$. Temporaries are handled in the
same way.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:address}
Let $k=mf+r$ with $0\le r<m$. Fix an adapter as in
Definition~\ref{def:network} and a set of $\rho$ bit positions in a column.
Applying the adapter to an array of length $2^k$, and then moving the $\rho$
selected bits of every column into one contiguous least-significant field of
$\rho f$ bits, takes $O(2^k)$ time. This includes index preparation and data
movement. The constant depends on $m$ and the fixed maps, but not on $f$ or $k$.
The inverse movement has the same bound.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
This is the proof of \cite[Lemma~2.5]{oai-dft} with two additions. For every
column digit, the finite table also records the selected $\rho$ bits and the
value of the phase function on that column. The prefix traversal carries a
third partial value, the accumulated phase in $\{\pm1,\pm i\}$. Extending a
prefix by a digit multiplies it by the tabulated phase in constant time. At a
leaf the value is copied between the two computed addresses and multiplied by
the accumulated phase and by $\omega^f$, which is prepared once in $O(f)$
operations. The selected field has $\rho f$ bits, so each
fiber of the following child is contiguous, and $W$ consecutive fibers form a
contiguous block of $W2^{\rho f}$ entries.
\end{proof}

\section{The batched recursion}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:batched}
Let $\mathcal N$ be a batched network of width $m$ on $W$ roles whose children
all have width at most $m-1$. If $0<\theta<1$ and
\[
 \mu:=\frac1W\sum_{\rho}N_\rho\Bigl(\frac\rho m\Bigr)^{\theta}<1,
\]
then $C^{\otimes k}$ can be applied exactly to an arbitrary complex array of
length $2^k$ in $O\bigl(2^k(k+1)^\theta\bigr)$ time, for every $k\ge0$. The
bound includes fixed-table construction, all scalar arithmetic, index
preparation and data movement. Only constants in $\mathbb Q(i)$ are used.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
We describe a procedure $\mathcal A(k)$ that applies $C^{\otimes k}$ to each of
$W$ arbitrary arrays of length $2^k$, and bound its cost $T(k)$. Put
$t(k)=T(k)/(W2^k)$. Let $\rho_{\max}\le m-1$ be the largest child width
of $\mathcal N$.

For $k<K$, with $K$ fixed below, apply the $k$ factors of $C$ one at a time
along their fibers. This costs $O(kW2^k)$, so $t(k)\le c_1K$.

For $k\ge K$ write $k=mf+r$ with $0\le r<m$. Apply the $r$ spectator factors
directly, at cost $O(rW2^k)$. Then execute $\mathcal N$ on the $W$ arrays with
$f$ columns. Gates cost $O(2^k)$ per array touched. Adapters cost $O(2^k)$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:address}. A child of width $\rho$ on one array first moves its
selected bits into the low field (Lemma~\ref{lem:address}), so that the array
consists of $2^{k-\rho f}$ contiguous fibers of length $2^{\rho f}$. Group the
fibers into $\lceil2^{k-\rho f}/W\rceil$ batches of $W$ fibers, padding the last
batch with zero arrays, and run $\mathcal A(\rho f)$ on each batch. Then undo the
movement. An inverse child is a forward child followed by a translation
\cite[Lemma~2.2]{oai-dft}. Correctness follows by induction on $k$, because
$\rho f<k$ and Definition~\ref{def:network} holds for arbitrary role values.
Zero padding is mapped to zero. Each call allocates and zeroes its own
temporaries, at cost $O(2^k)$ per temporary.

There is a constant $A_0$, depending only on $\mathcal N$ and $m$, with
\[
 T(k)\le A_0W2^k+\sum_{\text{children }c}
   \Bigl(\frac{2^{k-\rho_cf}}{W}+1\Bigr)T(\rho_cf).
\]
Since $k-\rho_cf\ge(m-\rho_{\max})f\ge f$, dividing by $W2^k$ gives
\[
 t(k)\le A_0+\bigl(1+W2^{-f}\bigr)\sum_\rho\frac{N_\rho}{W}\,t(\rho f).
\]
Suppose $t(j)\le B(j+1)^\theta$ for all $j<k$. Since
$\rho f+1\le(\rho/m)(k+1)+1\le(\rho/m)(k+1)(1+m/(k+1))$,
\[
 t(k)\le A_0+B(k+1)^\theta\,\mu\,\bigl(1+W2^{-f}\bigr)\Bigl(1+\frac m{k+1}\Bigr).
\]
Choose $K$ so large that the product of $\mu$ and the last two factors is at most
$\mu'=(1+\mu)/2<1$ for all $k\ge K$, and put
$B=\max\{c_1K,\,A_0/(1-\mu')\}$. Then $t(k)\le B(k+1)^\theta$ for every $k$.
Finally, place the input in one role, set the others to zero, and run
$\mathcal A(k)$. Since $W$ is a constant, the cost is $O(2^k(k+1)^\theta)$.
Index widths, the recursion stack and workspace are bounded exactly as in the
proof of \cite[Theorem~2.6]{oai-dft}: the array lengths shrink geometrically,
and addresses have $k+O(1)$ bits.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
\cite[Theorem~2.6]{oai-dft} corresponds to the case where every child has width
one, up to the strict inequality: then $\mu=S/(Wm^\theta)$ and the condition
reads $\theta>\log_m(S/W)$, whereas \cite[Theorem~2.6]{oai-dft} reaches
$\theta=\log_m(S/W_*)$ exactly with a padded role count $W_*$. In
Theorem~\ref{thm:batched} a child of width $\rho$ costs $(\rho/m)^\theta$ of a
role, not $\rho(1/m)^\theta$. To first order in $1-\theta$, the critical saving
is the rank deficit $Wm-\sum_\rho\rho N_\rho$ divided by
$\sum_\rho N_\rho\,\rho\ln(m/\rho)$, rather than by $\ln m\sum_\rho\rho N_\rho$.
Power-of-two padding of the role count is not needed, because partial batches
are lower order. Theorem~\ref{thm:batched} also requires the network property for
arbitrary role values, for the reason given in \cite[Remark~2.7]{oai-dft}.
\end{remark}

\section{The network}\label{sec:network}
We first record why the child histogram of a network depends only on binary
frame data. Write $\mathcal P$ for the Pauli group generated by translations
$X_a f(x)=f(x+a)$ and signs $Z_bf(x)=(-1)^{b\cdot x}f(x)$ on arrays indexed by
$\F_2^m$. An invertible operator $U$ that normalizes $\mathcal P$ induces a
binary symplectic map $g_U$ on Pauli labels. Let $\rho(U)$ be the rank of its
block that maps sign labels to translation labels. As in
\path{notes/general-clifford-frames.tex} of \cite{cms}, this is the Fourier rank of
a frame transition.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:normal}
Let $U$ normalize $\mathcal P$ and have entries in $\mathbb Q(i)$, and put
$\rho=\rho(U)$. Then $U=u\,A_1\,(C^{\otimes\rho}\otimes I)\,A_2$. Here $A_1,A_2$
are adapters in the sense of Definition~\ref{def:network} with $f=1$ and
$\omega=1$, $C^{\otimes\rho}$ acts on $\rho$ coordinate bits, and
$u\in\mathbb Q(i)$ is a scalar. The width $\rho$ is determined by $g_U$ alone.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $P\subset\operatorname{Sp}(2m,\F_2)$ be the stabilizer of the sign labels.
By the Bruhat decomposition for this parabolic, $\operatorname{Sp}(2m,\F_2)$ is
the disjoint union of the double cosets $Pw_\rho P$, $0\le\rho\le m$, where
$w_\rho$ exchanges translation and sign labels on $\rho$ coordinates. The rank
$\rho(\cdot)$ is constant on each double coset. Every element of $P$ is induced
by an adapter: invertible address maps $x\mapsto Mx$ induce the Levi part, and
diagonal phases $i^{x^{\mathsf T}\tilde Sx}$ with $\tilde S$ an integer
symmetric lift induce the symmetric shears. The operator $C^{\otimes\rho}$ on
$\rho$ coordinates induces an element of $Pw_\rho P$, since $C$ exchanges the
two labels of its coordinate up to a shear. Hence $g_U=p_1\,g_{C^{\otimes\rho}}\,p_2$
with $p_1,p_2\in P$. Choosing adapters $A_1,A_2$ that induce $p_1,p_2$, the
operator $V=A_1C^{\otimes\rho}A_2$ induces $g_U$. Then $UV^{-1}$ maps every Pauli
to itself up to sign, so it is a scalar multiple of a Pauli. A Pauli is an
adapter, so it is absorbed into $A_1$. The scalar lies in $\mathbb Q(i)$
because $U$ and $V$ have entries in $\mathbb Q(i)$.
\end{proof}

Consequently, errors in the exact phases or Pauli corrections of frame
representatives can only change adapters and scalars. They cannot change the
child histogram. For a columnwise operator $U_0^{\otimes f}$ the lemma applies
to $U_0$, which gives columnwise adapters and the scalar $u^f$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:network}
The paired-cube complex supplier of \cite{cms} at commit \texttt{d1d6c07} is a
batched network of width $m=72$ on $W=|G|\cdot29937$ roles, with $G=O(72,2)$
and $N_\rho=|G|\cdot n_\rho$. Here $n_\rho$ is the per-vertex child histogram in
\path{certificates/network-children.json}. Every child has width at most $60$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof (by reference)]
The construction and its proofs are in \cite{cms}, pinned at commit
\texttt{d1d6c07} (full hash in the bibliography). The entry point is
\path{notes/paired-cube-note.tex} and the files it includes. We use four facts.
\begin{enumerate}
\item The completed three-stage word with shared completed cores restores the
  logical value of every auxiliary role and exchanges the two data banks. Each
  role therefore physically undergoes the exact $C^{\otimes m}$, up to fixed
  adapters, signs, a fixed Pauli translation and the bank exchange. Sources:
  \path{notes/paired-cube-construction.tex} (the identity $K+H+B=I$, the
  dirty-word contract, copied centers), \path{notes/paired-cube-sharing.tex}
  (sequential completed-core sharing: no role is used by two invocations at
  once; raw operator contract; final gauge child), and
  \path{notes/three-stage-cover-complex.tex} (chronology and phase endpoints).
  These notes in turn rely on the positive producer of PR \#117, the carrier
  compiler, the copied-center and deferred old-value interface, and frozen
  gauge-witness files, all recorded in \cite{cms}.
\item Every frame transition is an exact operator with entries in
  $\mathbb Q(i)$ that normalizes $\mathcal P$, so Lemma~\ref{lem:normal} writes
  it as adapters around one child of width equal to its Fourier rank. The
  frame representatives and their ranks are given in
  \path{notes/general-clifford-frames.tex}. Nondegenerate residuals use the
  normal form of \path{notes/endpoint-gauge-complex.tex}.
\item Copied centers are temporary streams: copy, transform, read, erase
  (\path{notes/copied-centers-lemma.tex}). Their transforms are children.
\item The complete child multiset per cover vertex is the ledger of
  \path{notes/paired-cube-sharing.tex}, \S``The complete paid ledger'',
  instantiated in \path{certificates/paired-cube-complex-input.json}:
  $\sum_\rho\rho\,n_\rho=2153528=72\cdot29937-1936$, with largest child $60$.
\end{enumerate}
All gate coefficients are rational or $i$, with denominators dividing $6$.
The star-center scatter, for example, uses the prepared rational scalar $-1/6$,
which the model of \cite[\S1.1]{oai-dft} allows.

The network is frame-consistent in the sense of Lemma~\ref{lem:columns}: at every
common scalar gate it uses one identical exact representative on all incident
registers (\path{notes/general-clifford-frames.tex}, \S``Exact representatives
and nested children''). Its frames are the representatives $T_U$ of that note,
which are defined for every binary subspace $U$, including the degenerate star
frames $U_i$. We do not use the frames $\Phi_U$ of \cite[(2.5)]{oai-dft}, which
need $U$ nondegenerate. Fact~1 is the identity for one column, so
Lemma~\ref{lem:columns} gives it for every $f$. Upstream also states the
$f$-column form of each residual directly: one $C^{\otimes rf}$ child, the scalar
raised to the power $f$, and columnwise quadratic phases modulo four
(\path{notes/endpoint-gauge-complex.tex}). Routing between invocations
renames arrays. Every multiplicity is $|G|$ times the per-vertex count, so
$|G|$ cancels from $\mu$. The data fronts, rank-two complements, width-$60$ gauge
children, copied-center transforms, endpoint Paulis and bank exchange all
fit Definition~\ref{def:network}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:moment}
For the network of Proposition~\ref{prop:network} and $\theta=1-607/1250000$,
\[
 \sum_\rho n_\rho\Bigl(\frac\rho{72}\Bigr)^{\theta}<29937-0.00315,
\]
so $\mu<1$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write $(\rho/m)^\theta=(\rho/m)\exp(a\ln(m/\rho))$ with $a=607/1250000$. The
script \path{scripts/verify_moment.py} bounds $\ln(m/\rho)$ above by the
series $2\sum y^{2j+1}/(2j+1)$, $y=(x-1)/(x+1)$, with every term rounded up to a
$10^{-60}$ grid and an explicit geometric tail. It also uses
$e^x\le1+x+x^2/(2(1-x/3))$ for $0\le x<3$. The sum is evaluated in exact
rational arithmetic, and the certified margin is recorded in
\path{certificates/moment.json}. The critical saving of this histogram is
$4.85657\ldots\times10^{-4}$. As a control, the script fails at
$a=4.8566\times10^{-4}$.
\end{proof}

This is an independent re-check, and $607/1250000$ is the upstream saving
rounded down. The repository \cite{cms} certifies the same moment at
$a_c=4856569/10^{10}$ (\path{notes/paired-cube-assembly.tex}, with normalized gap
about $2.0\times10^{-10}$ in \path{scripts/paired_cube_network.py}). Our script
also passes at that value, with margin $6.0\times10^{-6}$, and either value may
be used in Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.

\section{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}}\label{sec:proof}
By Propositions~\ref{prop:network} and~\ref{prop:moment},
Theorem~\ref{thm:batched} gives $C^{\otimes k}$ in $O(2^k(k+1)^\theta)$ with
$\theta=1-607/1250000$, using only rational constants and $i$, which \cite[\S5.3]{oai-dft} relies on.

The remaining sections of \cite{oai-dft} use their Theorem~2.6 through this
bound and through two further properties: only constants in $\mathbb Q(i)$ are
needed, which fixes the root order $D_*$ in their \S5, and the word-size
accounting of the recursion. Both hold for Theorem~\ref{thm:batched}. The
exact-width Fourier words of \S3, the sector synchronization of \S4
(\cite[Proposition~4.2]{oai-dft}, where each width-$2^k$ sector carries
$C^{\otimes k}$), and
the small-prime working lengths and chirp convolution of \S5 are used
unchanged. Their final estimate also uses $\theta>0$. With our $\theta$ their
proof of Theorem~1.1 gives $O\bigl(n\ell^\theta(\log\ell)^4\bigr)$, where
$\ell=\Theta(\log n/\log\log n)$ is the axis count. This is
$O\bigl(n(\log n)^\theta(\log\log n)^{4-\theta}\bigr)$. Their proof of
Corollary~1.3 uses three transforms of length $2n-1$, which gives the
convolution bound. Their Corollary~1.2 uses their specific constants
$m,W_*,\Delta$; our Corollary~1.2 replaces it. Their Appendix~A is not needed.

The fixed network enters only through finite tables and constants. Its role
count $W\approx2^{2570}$ ($2571$ bits) and the base range $K$ are absolute
constants. Because $1-\mu\approx10^{-7}$, $K$ is of order $10^9$. These
constants are astronomically large: this is an asymptotic existence and
explicitness result, with no practical crossover.

\section{Scope}\label{sec:scope}
This note is a paper proof plus an exact finite certificate. It is not formally
verified. The commit of openai/math pinned for \cite{oai-dft}
(\texttt{adc7f12}) has no Lean formalization of its uniform statements. A
formalization of the uniform $10^{-13}$ DFT and convolution statements was
added later, at commit \texttt{3014888} (8 October 2026), in
\path{lean/ComparatorChallenges/UniformFourier.lean} and
\path{lean/OAI/Computability/FourierTransform/}, with scope in
\path{lean/docs/130.md}. It does not cover the changes here.

Theorem~\ref{thm:main} depends on Proposition~\ref{prop:network}, whose proof
is the construction of \cite{cms}. That construction has had maintainer review
assisted by OpenAI Codex, but no independent human peer review. Its records
state what is checked by script and what is a written argument:
\begin{itemize}
\item checked by script: the local scalar identities, including the local
  scalar map on all $1760^2=3{,}097{,}600$ source/target entries, binary frame
  and rank data, and integer replays of the mixer;
\item frame checks cover binary geometry, not exact complex phases;
\item the exact complex phases, the Cayley cover and completed-core sharing are
  written proofs, and the cover is not materialized by the verifier.
\end{itemize}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:normal}, the child histogram depends only on the binary frame
data.

In \cite{cms} the network is one input to an integer-multiplication bound.
That bound is conditional on the interfaces stated in
\path{notes/paired-cube-note.tex} there. Each one is either replaced here or not
needed:
\begin{itemize}
\item Uniform recursion is replaced by Theorem~\ref{thm:batched}.
\item Ordered-affine streaming of the address work is replaced by
  Lemma~\ref{lem:address}.
\item Analytic reduction, semantic precision and exact recovery concern
  finite-precision Gaussian resampling and recovery. In exact arithmetic they
  are not needed, and the reduction from every length to tensor powers of $C$ is
  that of \cite[\S\S3--5]{oai-dft}.
\item Fixed-tape setup concerns the multitape Turing machine and is not needed
  in the model of \cite[\S1.1]{oai-dft}.
\item The opposite-bank primitive, ordinary leaves and prime packing belong to
  the bit interchange and the multiplication assembly, which are not used.
\end{itemize}
