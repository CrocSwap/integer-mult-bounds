# A 2-adic corollary for the full-Borel superposition register model

**What this is.** This is the `p = 2` instance of PR288's first-order (FO) lemma on `Λ/pΛ` (§1.1(a), §1.4(a)),
written out for the full lower-Borel free group. PR288 §3 lists that case as open item 3. The functional is P1's
`ν(g) = len(im g_HD)`, read through the mod-2 natural representation. Following PR288 §1.3(b) and PR2110, it is
applied to the data–data block.

The note adds two small things:
- **Free maps singular mod 2.** The applied free maps need only be 2-integral, so maps singular mod 2 such as
  `diag(2I, I)` are allowed. The argument runs over the monoid algebra.
- **Narrowing item 3.** On **unit targets** PR288's open item 3 is closed for 2-integral data. What remains open is
  everything that, once targets are normalised to `n_I`, needs data outside `Z_(2)`:
  - dyadic paid frames;
  - applied free maps with even denominators, such as the natively free scaling `diag(½I, I)`;
  - 2-integral words on targets that are not units mod 2, such as `n_{2I} = d·n_I·d⁻¹` with `d = diag(2I, I)`, which
    reach `n_I` only through the dyadic `d⁻¹`.

PR288 §1.3(e) argued that an escape from characteristic 0 must be non-abelian. This note checks the natural
non-abelian free class, the lower Borel, at `p = 2`.

## Setting

This is PR288's general register (superposition) model (§0), in which gates may join registers at different frames.
The free group is enlarged from `G₀`'s Levi pairs to the full lower Borel.

Fix a width `m ≥ 1`, `W` data registers and any number of auxiliary registers; `R` registers in all. `G ≤ GL_2m(Q)`
acts on `H ⊕ D = Q^m ⊕ Q^m`, with H coordinates ordered before D coordinates; vectors are columns. Each register
holds an element of the group algebra `F2[G]`. The state is `X ∈ M_R(F2[G])`, initially `I`, and every move
multiplies the state on the left or on the right.

- **(F1) Mixing.** `P ⊗ [1]` with `P ∈ GL_R(F2)`. Free.
- **(F2) Register map.** `diag(1, …, [l], …, 1)` with `l` invertible and lower triangular in the H-before-D order.
  This covers Levi maps (lower-triangular `X` on H, `Z` on D) and the lower-left updates `D += K·H`. These maps
  are free natively:
  - upstream Lemma `ordered-affine-streams` (`02-streams.tex`) updates a later field from earlier controls in
    `O(V)`;
  - the proof of Lemma `matrix-shear` (`04-swap.tex`) applies lower-triangular transformations of H or D in
    descending order.

  The proof below uses only that `l` maps the D part into itself. It therefore covers the whole block-lower
  parabolic `[[X, 0], [K, Z]]`, which is P1's Interface I free class ("preserving `G_D`").
- **(P) Paid call.** `I + x yᵀ ([n_A] − 1)` with `x, y ∈ F2^R` and `n_A = [[I, A], [0, I]]` (the shear
  `H += A·D`, upstream `Φ_A`). It costs `rank_Q A` interchanges (Lemma `matrix-shear`). PR288 requires `yᵀx = 1`;
  the proof does not use it.
- **Target.** Only the data-to-data block `X_final[data, data]` matters. Write `g_w` for its entry in data row
  `π_perm(w)` and column `w`. This is a single group element, for instance `l'_w n_{C_w} l_w` with free factors; the
  other entries of the block are 0.
  - Auxiliary rows and columns are unconstrained.
  - So the target condition covers restored dirty auxiliaries, and clean auxiliaries that start at zero and end as
    garbage (as in PR288 §1.3(b) and PR2110).
  - It also covers permuted outputs and free factors on targets (as in `G₀`'s target class).

**2-integral hypothesis.** Every matrix actually applied (each `A` and each free map) has entries in `Z_(2)`, the
rationals with odd denominator. Inverses need not be 2-integral: `diag(2I, I)` is allowed as long as its inverse is
never applied.

## Corollary

Under the 2-integral hypothesis,

    Σ_paid rank_Q(A_j)  ≥  Σ_w rank_F2( (ḡ_w)_HD ),

where `(ḡ_w)_HD` is the upper-right `m × m` block of `g_w mod 2`. Each `g_w` is a product of applied matrices, so it
is 2-integral and its reduction is defined.

**Unit targets.** Call `g_w` a unit target when `(ḡ_w)_HD` is invertible. For `g_w = l'_w n_{C_w} l_w`, the block is
`X'_w C_w Z_w`. So `g_w` is a unit target when `C_w`, `X'_w` and `Z_w` are invertible mod 2; for instance `C_w = I`
with free factors that are invertible mod 2.

Targets that are not units mod 2, such as `n_{2I}`, get only the weaker bound `Σ rank (ḡ_w)_HD < mW`. They are
natively equivalent to `n_I` only through free maps with even denominators, so they belong to the open dyadic case.

On unit targets the right side is `mW`, the direct cost. Let `n_r` be the number of paid calls of rational rank `r`.
Then for every `a ∈ [0, 1]`,

    (1/W) Σ_r n_r (r/m)^{1−a}  ≥  (1/W) Σ_r n_r r/m  ≥  1,

so the model gives no saving.

## Proof

1. **The reduction.** The state lies in `M_R(F2[S])`, where `S` is the monoid generated by the applied matrices.
   `S` lies inside `M_2m(Z_(2))`, and reduction mod 2 is a unital monoid homomorphism `S → M_2m(F2)`.
   - By the universal property of monoid algebras, its F2-linear extension is a ring homomorphism. `S` need not be a
     group.
   - Applied entrywise, `π: M_R(F2[S]) → M_{2mR}(F2)` is a ring homomorphism.
2. **The functional.** Let `H*` and `D*` be the unions of all registers' H and D coordinates, and set
   `ν_π(Y) = rank_F2 π(Y)[H*, D*]`. Two readings of `ν_π`:
   - it is PR288's FO functional `f(g) = dim(Y₀ + σ(g)Y₀) − dim Y₀` on `Λ/2Λ`, where `σ` is the representation,
     `Λ = Z_(2)^{2m}` and `Y₀` is the D part;
   - it is P1's `ν` read through `π`.
3. **Subadditivity.** `π(YZ)[H*, D*] = π(Y)[H*, H*] π(Z)[H*, D*] + π(Y)[H*, D*] π(Z)[D*, D*]`. Hence
   `ν_π(YZ) ≤ ν_π(Y) + ν_π(Z)`, and `ν_π(I) = 0`.
4. **Free moves.** `P ⊗ I_2m` has no `[H*, D*]` entries. A block-lower `l̄` has zero upper-right block, even when
   `l̄` is singular. So `ν_π = 0` for every free move.
5. **Paid calls.** The `[H*, D*]` block of `I + (x yᵀ) ⊗ (n̄_A − I)` is `(x yᵀ) ⊗ Ā`. Its rank is at most
   `rank Ā ≤ rank_Q A`.
6. **The data block.** `ν_π(X_final)` is at least the rank of its data-row, data-column sub-block. That sub-block is a
   block-permutation matrix with blocks `(ḡ_w)_HD`, of rank `Σ_w rank (ḡ_w)_HD`.
7. **Conclusion.** Combine steps 3–6. ∎

## Remark: width one, any rational data

At `m = 1`, for any rational data, every word with targets whose HD entries `h_w` are nonzero uses at least `W` paid
calls.

- **Previous cases.** For the abelian and `G₀` models this already follows from PR288's flag theorem (§1.2(a), (e)),
  since there are no up frames at `m = 1`.
- **New here:** the lower-left update `D += k·H`.
- **Content.** Every call at `m = 1` is full width, so this is a sanity bound with no exponent content.

**Proof.**
1. Choose a prime `p` that divides no denominator of any applied matrix and no numerator of any `h_w`. It must be
   odd, because the data may be dyadic.
2. Let `ψ_p` send `[g]` to the map `e_v ↦ e_{ḡ v}` on `V = F2^{F_p²}`. This is multiplicative even when `ḡ` is
   singular.
3. Take the rank of the block from the D-axis points to the points with `h ≠ 0`. It is subadditive, and it is 0 on
   lower-triangular maps and on mixing.
4. It is at most `p − 1` on a paid call, and `W(p − 1)` on the data block.

This is a permutation-module FO certificate, of the kind PR288 uses in §1.2(d) and §1.3(f).

For `m ≥ 2`, take targets whose HD blocks are invertible mod `p` (so `p` also avoids those determinants). Then the same
module gives `Σ_j (p^m − p^{m − rank_p A_j}) ≥ W(p^m − 1)`. By concavity this yields

    Σ rank_Q A_j ≥ W(1 + 1/p + … + 1/p^{m−1}),

for example `4W/3` at `m = 2` when `p = 3` is admissible. That is PR288 §1.2(d)'s ratio 1.33, not `mW`.

  P1's Theorem A gives the same `Σ t ≥ rank` per role stream with this free class and *no* integrality hypothesis.
  What needs 2-integrality is the superposition model's free cross-frame XOR. P1's motif model instead charges
  cross-frame copy edges (Thm. alg-samechar).

  From PR288 (eumemic) come:
  - the superposition register model (§0);
  - the FO lemma (§1.1(a)) and its `Λ/pΛ` form (§1.4(a));
  - the first-order `p`-integral bound applied to the data–data block (§1.3(b)), with the `p`-integral
    (mixed-characteristic) theorem credited to P1/P2 in PR288 §4;
  - the observation that an escape must be non-abelian (§1.3(e)).

  Other related work:
  - entrywise representations of superposition words also appear in P1 Prop. alg-origin and in merged PR1333 (the
    binary-affine register bound);
  - the abelian floor with dirty auxiliaries is open PR2110 (Cinna Davis), whose square-zero map equals `π` on
    shears;
  - philippeVV's 2-integral bound for masked plane decoders (Discussion 1587, comment 18927273) is an analogous mod-2
    subadditivity floor in a different model.
- **Status of PR288.** PR288 is closed and unmerged. Its §1.2 theorems are written arguments, incorporated by the
  ledger and not independently verified. This proof uses PR288 only for its definitions and context.
- **Is the hypothesis needed?** In general, yes. Upstream Proposition `bit-motif-interface` (`03-motifs.tex`) is a
  network of pointwise XOR gates and rational shear frames `Φ_M` (projections `P_U`), with unit targets `I_m` and
  `s_b < W_b m`, which is a saving. Read as a word in this model, it must therefore apply a matrix with an even
  denominator. P1 Cor. alg-mixed(ii) states the same conclusion for XOR motifs.

  This does not decide whether dyadic data can save at **small** `m` in this model. The abelian model and `G₀` never
  save at `m = 2` (PR288 §1.2(a), (e)), so `m = 2` with `D += K·H` and data outside `Z_(2)` is the first open case.
- **Scope controls.** Outside each hypothesis the invariant is undefined or weaker:
  - a dyadic frame has no reduction;
  - PR288 §1.2(d)'s commutator `d n_A d⁻¹ n_A⁻¹ = n_A` applies `d⁻¹ = diag(½I, I)`;
  - a target factor singular mod 2 lowers `(ḡ_w)_HD`.

  Among these controls, only a free upper map exhibits a cheaper word: it would reach the target `n_I` with no paid
  call. It is not natively free.
- **Outside the model:**
  - affine or carry-shift address maps;
  - nonlinear frames;
  - the complex primitive;
  - native words and their hosting.
