## Denominator proof from independent observables

For every word, the eight C0 queries immediately before /13 pull back
to Gaussian-dyadic coefficients on all formal inputs. In the free-word
representation checked here the scalar coefficients are rational dyadic,
and every warp letter has a Gaussian-dyadic physical endpoint. Thus these
completed queries have no new factor 13 in their denominator.

All other scalar and native operation endpoints are Gaussian dyadic.
Start with denominator height zero relative to the word input. A dyadic
operation cannot increase the largest 13-denominator height. Each odd
division acts on one of the proved height-zero queries and gives height
at most one. Induction over all literal events proves height at most one
at every operation boundary. This proof includes arbitrary helper payloads
and the private lifetimes; it is not a test on chosen numeric values.

The result also applies relative to an incoming odd height h: all local
prefixes have height at most h+1. It does not apply to arbitrary unscaled
numerators on a finest grid. The separate common-grid theorem must provide
divisibility. The internals of recursively implemented children need that
theorem's exact endpoint and precision conditions.

Consequently a future parent may charge one new 13-denominator level per
complete occurrence of one of these local words. This is stronger than
charging all eight complex division events separately. It remains
necessary to prove that each completed recursive provider and the outer
endpoint have the required dyadic full-state maps. E and G individually
are not such providers.
