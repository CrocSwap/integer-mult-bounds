# Cached chirp and finite synthetic polynomial convolution

The routine `chirp_middle` takes a signed dyadic field on the original padded rectangular tensor, with axis zero fastest. For source sides s, target sides t, T=prod(t), r=t[0], M=T/r, it computes an approximation to the normalized map with matrix entry

    exp(+2πi sum_i s_i x_i z_i/t_i) / T.

The inverse-sign call conjugates its entrance and exit. Both directions divide by T. Each internal real and imaginary component is on a 2^-f grid. The output is on the supplied 2^-q grid. Each drop truncates toward zero. The entrance check verifies the complex half disk.

## Polynomial-ring factorization

Let R=C[Y]/(Y^r+1). For every dyadic target extent t_i dividing r, Y^(2r/t_i) is principal of order t_i. Indeed evaluation at every complex root Y=e^((2k+1)πi/r) gives a primitive t_i-th root, since the exponent 2k+1 is odd. The evaluation map is injective on degree <r. Thus the usual Fourier product and inversion identities hold in R. This argument permits arbitrary odd roots in the different spectral components.

Twist the coefficient at the fastest physical coordinate j by e^(πij/r). The sign of every negacyclic wrap then cancels when the sign twist is undone. Along the other coordinates use a normalized forward DIF FFT without rearranging its frequencies. One butterfly averages the corresponding coordinates of the two ring records. Its second output is multiplied by Y^(-2r j/m) when the local block size is m and j is the within-half index. The signs and rotations use the actual padded original axes. Independent simultaneous butterflies give the sum of these rotation exponents. Inverse DIT first applies the opposite rotation and reverses the stages; the exact common frequency permutation cancels. Input lift by log2 M bits permits all normalized butterflies without fractional loss. The final synthetic FFT drop is the only rounding in that call.

A normalized within-frequency polynomial product divides by r. The signed Kronecker radix has a byte-aligned digit width at least `2f+bitlength(r)+5`. Its raw complex coefficients have modulus at most `2r*2^(2f)`, so centered extraction recovers every signed coefficient uniquely. For each input component the codec emits successive low limbs of `x_j+carry`; its signed carry is `0` or `-1`. On output, each unsigned limb plus its carry yields the unique centered digit and the next carry `0` or `1`. Both codecs traverse each packed word once. Subtracting the upper half folds `Y^r=-1`. The implementation divides by `r*2^f` before producing its signed integers, performing the r normalization and the f-grid product drop at once. The two forward ring FFTs, one ring product, opposite FFT and exact multiplication by M therefore give the scalar cyclic convolution divided by T. There is exactly one M restoration.

If a(x)=prod_i exp(-πi s_i x_i²/t_i), the exact middle is conj(a) times the normalized scalar cyclic convolution of a with conj(a)*x. Expanding a coefficient gives exp(+2πi sum_i s_i x_i z_i/t_i)/T. All slopes are odd and all target extents even, so the phases have the required target periods. No odd-order Fourier transform computes a payload value.

## Error for every admitted input

Write h=2^-f and measure complex modulus per coordinate. Each scalar truncation causes error <2h and cannot increase modulus. Each dyadic table root lies in the unit disk and differs from its exact unit root by at most 3h. This follows from the actual component enclosure check of at most 2h per component and sqrt(8)<3. The source generates cos and sin by dyadic outward square roots, from the exact four quadrant values and verified interval endpoints. The root table contributes at most 3Dh to a length-D chirp; the D rounded prefix products contribute less than 2Dh. These errors depend on the depth of one prefix. Reuse across input records does not accumulate extra error.

Fix the rounded chirp A first. Then |A|≤1 and |trunc(conj(A)*x)|≤1/2. Twisting those two operands has errors below 5h,4h, respectively (root error times each entrance radius plus <2h). The ring FFTs are contractions in maximum complex coefficient modulus; every component is a sum of M signed coefficient permutations divided by M. Their output drops give errors below 7h,6h relative to the exact transforms. The normalized ring product is a bilinear contraction in that same norm. In a telescoping difference, the first perturbation multiplies a factor of norm at most 1/2, and the second a factor of norm at most one. The ring product error including its drop is therefore <12h. The opposite ring FFT and its drop give <14h. Exact multiplication by M now gives <14Mh. The correct twisted cyclic result has modulus ≤1/2. Untwisting with the rounded root, of modulus ≤1, and dropping adds <4h. Two conjugate-A endpoint drops add <4h through the two contraction factors. Relative to the ideal exact map using the supplied A throughout, the defect is thus <(14M+8)h.

Replace each of the three A occurrences by a in the exact map. The difference of the three terms is at most (3/2)5Dh, since ||x||≤1/2. Multiplication by sqrt(2) bounds component-l1 from modulus, and the output q projection contributes less than 2*2^-q. The bound

    (24M+16D+16) 2^-f + 2*2^-q

holds for all accepted inputs, independently of oracle outputs. The signed working width f+(D-1)+4 includes temporary sums in the elementary simultaneous-butterfly simulator. The near-one input radii, plus 14M*2^-f, ensure the final genuinely M-scaled result fits.

## Physical boundary

Let `D` count the axes, `ell=log2 r`, `T=product(t_i)`, and `V=Theta(Tp)` for the fine family with `f=Theta(p)`. A phase magnitude table for one axis has at most `r` entries. The four principal quadrants are exact. At each nontrivial square root in a half angle, the exact result is at least `sin(2pi / n)>=2/n` for current order at most `n=2r`. Comparison with the exact root via the difference of squares bounds the new endpoint error by `(n+1)` times its old error plus one final arithmetic rounding unit. Induction over at most `log2 n` levels gives endpoint error at most `2^-B (2n+2)^(log2 n+1)`. Once the two endpoints span at most one `f` grid unit, inward projection has defect at most two units per component. The doubling loop thus finishes at private precision `B=O(f+ell^2)`. All coefficients remain in their closed quadrants. The `D*r` table entries take `D*r*poly(p+ell)` bit work and the same coarse upper bound on private storage. On this family `ell=Theta(p^.003)`, so this is `exp(o(p))`. Root preparation never raises precision on the external field.

The tape interpreter walks complete fixed-width records. Splits, binary joins, resets and merging all use sequential tapes. A signed cyclic rotation of one ring changes the head direction at most a constant number of times per row. Computing its exponent from all axis descriptors once costs `poly(p)` per row, absorbed by its `r=2^ell` records eventually. The chirp uses one shared axis table tape and a tape of suffix products. Axis `i` changes `O(T/product_{j<i} t_j)` times. Ordinary axis-zero advances move one entry; the reset costs `O(r)` once per `r` advances. Charge the table trip of length `O((i+1)r)` to each slower-axis carry. All sides lie between `r/2` and `r`, so for `i>=1` the denominator is at least `r*2^(i-1)`. The summed trip lengths and number of rounded prefix updates are therefore `O(T)`. The same count pays the growing odometer's moves, prefix-cache rewinds and the complete final erasure. No table is indexed at unit random-access cost in this bill.

The four real Kronecker products per frequency have word length `L=O(rp)`. `word_multiply.py` uses explicit shift-add up to a fixed 8192-bit boundary. Above it the program converts both magnitudes into canonical bit tapes and calls the actual pinned `FftWord.run`; it reads and consumes the complete `2L`-bit return. Its four source module hashes are checked and the retained child authenticates its own export. Program loading and the cold process are fixed supplier setup. This is the repository's acyclic word primitive. Its all-size `O(L log^2(L+2))` bit cost and `O(L)` space give `O(V(ell+log p)^2)` over all frequency products. Ordinary scalar products on `f`-bit words cost `O(V log^2 p)` per pass. Signed codecs, folded halves, power-of-two scaling and cleanup cost `O(V)` per pass.
