# Global boundary rows on sequential tapes

Let the padded axis have length t. Assume that Q has cyclic bandwidth W in target order. The active set E contains complete blocks with a hole, and the first and last W positions of every other block. Each block of E-complement is a contiguous interval I. Distinct such intervals have no coupling in either direction. A column or row coupling I to E can meet only the two adjacent length-W strips. With E ordered cyclically, the union of those two strips occupies at most 2W consecutive positions. Consequently the principal Schur complement on E has cyclic bandwidth at most 2W. In a long exceptional block the original Q entries still have range W. This bound applies to arbitrarily many consecutive exceptional blocks. If E is the entire axis, the original bandwidth suffices. A short axis can use the smaller radius floor(|E|/2), with only one copy of the antipodal coefficient.

Writing Q in I,E order as [A B; C D] gives F=D−CA⁻¹B. Then the restriction of Q⁻¹ to E rows and E columns is F⁻¹. For the truncated wrap-corrected circulant construction, R is the restriction of Q−J to E; J has wrap couplings only between two end strips of a regular block. They become adjacent in E order. Thus R has cyclic width O(W). Define all matching interior coefficients of the one rounded Q reference by the same exact quantized gauge/kernel/gauge factors as J. Independent rounding of each final entry of Q would destroy exact support of R.

When the scalar or truncated narrow Q has strict row diagonal dominance gap δ, exact elimination preserves at least δ in every remaining row. For row i≠k, triangle bounds give the old gap plus |Qik/Qkk| times the old row-k gap as a lower bound. Thus every principal block is invertible. If ||Q||∞≤2 then ||F||∞≤K=2+4/δ and every further Schur row norm is at most K'=K+K²/δ. The same statements can use a larger certified bound in a finite instance. The broad direction only applies its R and never solves with its F.

## Layout and cost

Put a=|E|. The structural radius is at most 2W. The emitted pages pay the conservative width w=min(4W+2,floor(a/2)). Defer the last w pivots as a border. The other m=a−w coordinates then have ordinary bandwidth w. Standard in-order elimination adds no entries farther away within this leading block. L has a lower band in the leading block and at most w dense last rows. U has an upper band there and at most w dense last columns. The offline generator computes these rational factors exactly. A minor bound for a polynomial-size rational input gives polynomial(t,p) coefficient bit length for the unquantized pages. The pages placed at online entry contain dyadic approximations to the needed multipliers and diagonal reciprocals.

Serialize forward pages by pivot. Store each row's at most w subdiagonal coefficients and the column of up to w coefficients for the last rows. Store the final triangular border afterward. When the solver emits z_i it updates a reusable vector of w border contributions. The original border right hand sides arrive at the end of the same input scan. For backward solution store U coefficients in reverse-pivot order. The w border variables are known first. A band row can then reread the w border variables and at most w already emitted near successors. Each dot product uses a short contiguous region. The complete forward vector is written once and traversed backward once. The corrected results are written in descending order and returned in ascending order. These scans, marker returns and clearing take O(a(w+1)) record movements. The serialized coefficient pages have O(a(w+1)) words. With online word width P, one RHS uses O(a(w+1)(M(P)+P)) tape operations under a paid word multiplication M. It uses O(aP+wP) data workspace, in addition to the reusable input-independent pages. The complex operation runs the same real coefficient against both components. The multiplication pass reads each cyclic w-neighborhood with modular seeks. Moving to the next window costs O(w+1) records apart from crossings of the physical cut. At most 2w windows cross that cut. Charging O(a) additional records to each of them preserves the O(a(w+1)) tariff, including w=floor(a/2).

Copy the compact E rows from the full array in its natural address order. The stream is a concatenation of a-by-b rectangles, with b equal to the inner spectator count. At each doubling level, two child groups of rows are written to separate temporary tapes. Interleave their left/right chunks for each of the b suffixes. The inverse writes the two chunks to their respective tapes and concatenates them. A possibly shorter last child is treated at its actual length, with no padding. Each level reads or writes the complete compact stream a constant number of times. At most ceil(log₂ a) levels expose or restore one axis. Reread the inclusion mask or compute its predicate on the final dense scan to merge with the retained baseline. For E membership in block [l,h), the exact number of selected q-images is c(h)−c(l) with c(x)=clip(ceil(s(2x−1)/(2t)),0,s). Long runs of holes and row heads require no stored coordinate lookup. The per-axis full cost is O(V) for scans, plus O(Vμ(ell+(w+1)M(P)/P)) for compact movement and arithmetic, where V measures bit volume. Predicate setup during one complete scan is O(T·poly(ell,log p)) bit operations with T records; the stated binary integer predicate costs O(ell²+poly(log p)) ordinarily, hence fits O(V) in the retuned family. Dense baseline cell transposes expose only the low log₂B bits and therefore cost O(V log B), independently of ell or the inner spectator count.
