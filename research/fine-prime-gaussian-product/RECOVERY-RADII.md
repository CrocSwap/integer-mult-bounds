# Track the size of an input-dependent page error

Suppose the normalized broad and narrow exact reference factors and their normalized ideal source factors have infinity norm at most one. Let η sum their certified operator-norm differences. The exact middle Fourier factor also contracts. Telescoping the product of factors gives an error at most η times the maximum complex modulus of the input. This error scales with the actual input even if the chosen digital working grid uses many more bits than the Gaussian tail accuracy. Suppose the separate digital evaluation, including all trigonometric tables, word truncations, grids and final conversions, differs from that exact reference at every admissible half-disk input by at most e. Let K=2^γ be the product of the normalization divisors. Then the delivered complete transform A, in either direction, satisfies

    ||A(x) − F(x)/K|| ≤ η ||x|| + e,

where F is the required normalized source Fourier transform with its index permutation. An arithmetic theorem for the actual implementation must supply e. Finite operator checks alone only test that theorem.

Put h=2^-q. Both inputs have norm at most z=1/4. Each computed forward transform, multiplied conceptually by K, differs from its exact source Fourier value by at most d=K(ηz+e) and has norm at most f=z+d. Form the product on the q grid. In the conceptual scale K² its norm is at most w=f²+2K²h. Its error from the product of the exact source Fourier values is at most d(f+z)+2K²h. The term 2h covers both component drops. Require f<1/2 and w/K²<1/2 for the entrances. The final opposite call acts on a vector of norm at most w/K². Multiplying its error by K³ gives at most Kη w+K³e. The exact opposite Fourier map contracts prior errors. Thus for the unscaled integer coefficients the sufficient strict bound is

    S² 2^(2b+4) [d(f+z) + 2K²h + Kηw + K³e] < 1/2.

This includes the two normalized source-size factors S, both b+2 digit scales, all three K divisors and the final grid. The term K³e remains present even for zero exact input. Only the certified *linear operator* differences receive the smaller actual-vector multiplier. The integer recovery and inverse CRT then use the original coordinate/carry identities. Fixed-size integer and rational arithmetic can evaluate every admission comparison exactly.

For `(31,29)→(32,32)`, α=2, b=4, B=16, W=7 and q=96, the original quantized factors give γ=12. Their directly certified broad defects are less than 5.368e−19 and 9.622e−17, and their narrow defects are below 2.311e−100 and 1.627e−101. With e=(64d+64)2^-q the above radius is less than 0.000247 for all data in the stated input range. The two genuine interior coordinates lie on the 31→32 axis, whose 30 remaining coordinates are on its boundary. The finite physical execution and the validity of the all-size digital premise e have separate evidence.

## Digital part of the bound

Here is a direct way to separate this e from every geometric defect. Let G=2^guard. The constructor gives G≥4, bounds t and B by G, bounds each relevant gauge, inverse gauge and kernel l¹ norm by G, and also bounds ||R||∞, ||F||∞, ||Q⁻¹||∞, δ⁻¹ and ||D||∞ by G where applicable. Consequently ||F⁻¹||∞≤G, since F⁻¹ is a principal block of Q⁻¹. Use complex modulus and h=2⁻q. At work precision the error quantum is u=2⁻³²h G⁻⁸⁰. Let X≥1 bound a kernel's input modulus.

Each local FFT consists of log₂ B≤G normalized butterfly levels and unit twiddles. Inward storage of a unit root has modulus at most one and error below 3u. A level's two coordinate drops and its twiddle error are below 8u(1+X). The ideal stage contracts. The implemented butterfly also contracts before its single two-component drop; an inward drop cannot increase complex modulus. Thus an entire normalized FFT has error at most 8Gu(1+X), including its signed component roundings. For a spectral diagonal, multiplying the enclosed roots by the exact real kernel coefficients in setup and taking the final grid integers gives coefficient error at most 3Gu+2u≤4Gu. The before gauge has norm at most G, the spectral diagonal at most G, and the B-compensated after gauge at most G². Scalar diagonal product drops have norm below 2u. A circle therefore has exact norm at most G⁴. Each of its five groups has prefix and suffix norm at most 2G⁴ after accounting for finite coefficient errors. Direct addition of the five one-group bounds gives a local error below 2¹³ G⁹ X u and output magnitude at most 2G⁴X. The latter follows also from its exact norm and the error bound.
