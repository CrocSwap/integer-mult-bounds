# Rounded leaves and borrowed ports

## 1. Result and scope

PR1057 proves that three dirty arrays are sufficient at every echo depth
when each child is an exact additive shear. PR985 and PR1054 prove a
different interface. Their rounded leaf restores its source, and its
literal inverse reverses the call. Its increment can depend on its target.
Those statements do not give the PR1057 child contract.

This package gives three results for that gap.

1. A borrowed-port subword has a necessary and sufficient translation
   condition. With identity neighbors, it requires additivity. A wrapper
   on the same unrestricted grid cannot meet that condition and approximate
   the admissible scalar inverse `x -> 4x/3` to error below `1/3` on
   the full physical interval `[-1,1]`. This statement permits arbitrary
   computation, restored workspace, source reads, and inverse executions.
2. For componentwise nearest rounding of `Pz/q`, the safe translations
   are exactly `H={h: Ph belongs to q Z^m}`. A correction that has access
   only to a saved summary of the old helper needs at least
   `[Z^d:H]` summary states. The residue `Pz mod q` attains this count.
   A diagonal coprime scalar needs `q^L` states for an array of length L
   when the admitted source and displacement ranges contain a complete
   residue domain. Smaller finite domains can need fewer states.
3. In the specified fixed-offset averaging class, a nonzero additive
   result needs at least q signed calls. Exactly q calls give the
   q-scaled integer map. They do not give an additive fractional map.

The second result is an interface tradeoff, not a lower bound for all
algorithms. Section 6 pays for access to an old helper and explains this
escape. A different recursive contract, changed grids, or clean precision
extensions can also escape. No impossibility result for arbitrary rounded
programs, no new supplier, and no new kappa is claimed.

All data arithmetic below has enough signed headroom. There is no wrap,
saturation, or overflow. All rounding is to nearest with ties toward
positive infinity, as in PR985's `rnd`. PR985's separate `trunc` function
prepares coefficients; it does not round the diagonal data output.

## 2. The exact restoration condition at a borrowed boundary

Let V be an abelian group. Let `A,B,C: V -> V` be deterministic maps.
On distinct rows x, y, z, put

    g_x(y) = B(y+A(x)) - B(y).

The four leaf calls

    y += A(x); z += B(y); y -= A(x); z -= B(y)

implement `U: z += g_x(y)` and restore y. Now use y as the output of
`W: y += C(z)`. The full outer word is `U W U^-1 W^-1`. At entry
write the rows as x, y, z, and put

    z1 = z + g_x(y),
    y1 = y + C(z1),
    z2 = z1 - g_x(y1).

The final W inverse does not change z2. Thus the helper z restores for
every x, y, z if and only if

    g_x(y+c) = g_x(y)  for every c in the image of C.       (1)

Necessity follows because z1 runs through all V when z does. Sufficiency
follows by substitution. Invariance under these translations is equivalent
to invariance under the subgroup generated by the image of C. This is an
exact condition; a small nonzero defect still violates restoration.

If `A=C=identity`, (1) says that `B(y+x)-B(y)` is independent of y.
If `B(0)=0`, set y=0 to get

    B(y+x) = B(y) + B(x).                                 (2)

Conversely, (2) restores z and gives the usual composed shear. A constant
offset in B cancels, so the general statement applies to `B-B(0)`.

This subword occurs inside the PR1057 allocator. In the ordered tree
`(0,((1,2),3))`, the root uses row 2 as a connector. Its right child
uses row 3. The child's `(1,2)` grandchild borrows row 1, which is the
right child's output. Set factors 1 and 3 to identity and factor 2 to B.
The displayed word is precisely this completed right-child boundary.
The external row 0 stays read-only. The probe tests restoration of an
actual lent ancestor port, not an unrelated equation for roundoff.

The condition is necessary for this universal substitution interface.
It is not necessary for every fixed factor list. Some nonadditive factors
have safe translation subgroups that happen to contain all later updates.
Section 4 describes those subgroups. A schedule that avoids this boundary
must prove its own child interface.

## 3. A physical-range obstruction for every same-grid wrapper

Let `delta=2^-W`, with integer `W>=0`. Suppose a wrapper implements

    target += F(source),

on arbitrary source and target data in `delta Z^L`. It restores all other
data and has no dependence on the incoming target or private dirt in its
completed increment. Suppose its child contract gives (2) whenever the
arguments and their sum are in `[-1,1]^L`.

For a coordinate vector e_j, write `F(delta e_j)=delta v_j`, where v_j
has integer entries. Repeated addition inside the interval gives

    F(e_j) = 2^W F(delta e_j) = v_j.                        (3)

Thus F acts as an integer matrix on the grid in this interval. If its
ideal map is the real matrix M, its error at some e_j is at least

    max_i distance(M_ij, Z).

In particular, `M=(4/3) I` forces infinity-norm error at least `1/3` at
a physical unit input. More generally, on `[-D,D]` with D a positive
grid point, the scalar lower bound is

    D * distance(alpha, Z).

This is not the smaller error `delta/3` at one grid step. The complete
physical input range is used in (3). Increasing W does not remove the
bound when arbitrary dirty sources can use every bit of the same grid.
The result also excludes a wrapper whose roundoff is uniformly bounded
by `C(D+1) delta` once that bound is below the displayed positive gap.

The map `4x/3` is an actual PR985 case: use a real coefficient `b=1/4`,
stride zero, multiplier one, and `beta=1/2`. Its diagonal call returns
`rnd(4x/3,W)`, with source restoration and an exact inverse. At W=12
the original admission `48*2^-W<=beta^3` holds. The scalar source grid
can be as fine as the working grid. Identity neighbors are also admissible
PR985 cases, with b=0. Thus this obstruction applies to a proposed generic
same-grid wrapper of the pinned leaf, not only to an arbitrary nonlinear
oracle unrelated to that implementation.

The wrapper can use unlimited computation and restored workspace. Those
resources cannot change (3). The conclusion is narrower than saying that
rounded recursion is impossible: it excludes a wrapper that retains the
same arbitrary-grid domain, the exact additive contract, and a small
uniform approximation error at the same time.

Ordinary guarded precision is not contradicted. If source data is restricted
to the coarser lattice `Delta Z` and output uses `delta Z`, a slope can be
an integer multiple of `delta/Delta`. Such slopes can approximate 4/3.
However, a borrowed ancestor port with arbitrary incoming fine-grid dirt
does not satisfy that coarser-source restriction. An implementation must
preserve or create that restriction and pay for it. Section 7 gives one
finite exact extension with explicit width costs.

## 4. The complete safe-translation lattice

Let P be an integer `m by d` matrix and let `q>=1`. Define

    R(t) = floor(t+1/2),
    F(z) = R(Pz/q),  z in Z^d,

where R acts on each component. A translation h is safe when
`F(z+h)-F(z)` is independent of z for every z in Z^d.

Then

    h is safe  <=>  Ph belongs to q Z^m.                    (4)

For sufficiency, use `R(t+k)=R(t)+k` for integer k. For necessity, let
the constant difference be c. Iteration gives `F(kh)=F(0)+kc=kc` for
every positive integer k. The rounding error in `F(kh)-kPh/q` has
each component at most 1/2 in absolute value. Divide by k and let k
increase. This gives `c=Ph/q`. Since c has integer entries, (4) follows.

For a reduced scalar `p/q`, the safe translations are exactly qZ. For
the diagonal map on L independent coordinates, they are `q Z^L`.
The same proof applies to floor, ceiling, or any bounded rounder with
integer-translation covariance and F(0)=0. It does not cover nearest-even
ties without a separate period analysis.

For the scalar nearest rounder, a finite complete test is sufficient:
the differences at z=0,...,q-1 are constant if and only if q divides h.
They repeat with period q. Their sum is ph, because `F(z+q)=F(z)+p`.
If they are constant, that integer constant must be ph/q. Coprimality
then gives q dividing h. This justifies the finite census in the checker.

## 5. Exact residual summaries and their sharp state count

Specify the following correction interface. The entry helper is z. Before
that value becomes unavailable, an encoder stores E(z). Later the adapter
receives E(z), a displacement h, and the immutable rounding descriptor. It
must return `F(z+h)-F(z)` exactly for every z and h. It cannot reread z or
an equivalent value from which z can be recovered. This restriction is
part of the theorem, not a property of every borrowing program.

Two helpers z,z' have the same complete response function if and only if
`z'-z` is safe. To prove the forward direction, rearrange equality of the
responses to get

    F(z'+h)-F(z+h) = F(z')-F(z)  for every h.

The variable `z+h` runs through the whole domain. This is exactly the
safe-translation condition. The reverse direction follows from the same
identity. Therefore E must distinguish all cosets of H. At least
`[Z^d:H]` states, or `ceil(log2 [Z^d:H])` bits, are necessary.

They are sufficient. Store the componentwise residue `r=Pz mod q`, with
`0<=r_i<q`. Then use

    F(z+h)-F(z) = R((r+Ph)/q) - R(r/q).                   (5)

The possible residue vectors form the image of P modulo q. The number
of these vectors equals `[Z^d:H]`. Redundant components need not be
stored. This is an exact group-index result, including composite q and
matrices with a nontrivial kernel.

For coprime p/q on L independent entries, the minimum is q^L states.
Equation (5) uses L residues with `ceil(log2 q)` bits each. An optimal
packed encoding uses `ceil(L log2 q)` bits. It also pays L source reads,
L products and reductions at capture, L residue reads, L products, two
rounded divisions per entry at correction, and all required output writes.
On an untrusted dirty register these residues need a reversible encoding
or separately initialized storage. They are not free clean scalars.

For PR985 take `b=2^-B`, `B>=2`. The diagonal slope is the reduced ratio
`2^B/(2^B-1)`, and `beta=1/2` is valid. The exact residual summary needs
`(2^B-1)^L` states, hence at least `ceil(L log2(2^B-1))` bits. A constant
number of B-bit scalar registers cannot hold this summary for arbitrary L.
This gives a concrete price for one proposed repair; it does not require
the entire P-bit original helper when B is much smaller than P.

For finite physical data, require a complete residue system in the allowed
range. For example, take `W>=max(B,9)` and physical grid `delta=2^-W`.
The integers `z,h=0,...,q-1` represent physical old helpers and shifts
below one; the evaluated sums are below two. All q scalar response
classes are distinct on these finite sets. Thus the q^L count already
holds in that bounded physical range. At a fixed W whose admitted source
set has fewer than q entries, do not claim q states. The correct lower
bound is the number of distinct response functions on the specified
source and displacement sets.

For several coprime scalar ratios `p_i/q_i`, one summary that supports all
their responses has safe subgroup `lcm(q_i) Z` per coordinate. Its exact
minimum is `lcm(q_i)^L` states. This can exceed each separate q_i count.

## 6. Reaccess, replay, and fixed-offset averaging

The summary lower bound does not apply when z+h and h are both readable:
one subtraction recovers z. The checker includes this zero-summary escape.
At the actual boundary of Section 2, the state after W is `(x,y1,z1)`.
The old y is exactly `y1-C(z1)`. A full `W^-1` recovers it with one
negative C execution. If a coordinate evaluator for C is available, one
can instead pay for that evaluator and each source read. A dense scalar
map can make this cheap. There is no claimed general lower bound on that
evaluation cost.

After `W^-1 U^-1`, however, both y and z are back at entry. The desired
output change is gone. Keeping `C(z1)` in a new array uses L values. A
replay can compute it again, but must also retain the old/new state needed
for the subtraction and prove restoration at the next parent. Such calls,
reads, arrays, and clean scalar work must be in its new recurrence. This
package does not assert that no cheaper changed schedule exists.

Even a successful local correction cannot make an accurate same-grid
fractional increment additive: Section 3 still applies. Thus rereading
the helper can escape the summary bound, but cannot validate an unchanged
use of the PR1057 additive induction. It needs a different child contract.

One concrete changed schedule removes base dependence by fixed offsets.
Consider the class

    H(z) = sum_j epsilon_j [F(z+t_j)-F(t_j)],

where each `epsilon_j` is +1 or -1 and each integer offset t_j is fixed
independently of z. Count each signed occurrence as one variable call;
constant preparation and source movement are additional costs. Let
`S=sum_j epsilon_j`. Since the roundoff is bounded, `H(z)-(Sp/q)z`
is bounded on all integers. If H is additive, it has an integer slope a.
It follows that `a=Sp/q`. For coprime p,q this requires q to divide S.
If a is nonzero, the number of variable calls is at least `|S|>=q`.
In particular, S=1 cannot produce an additive fractional approximation.

The q-call bound is attained by offsets 0,...,q-1, all with positive
sign. The residues `pr mod q` form a permutation. Direct summation of
the q floors gives

    sum_(r=0)^(q-1) [F(z+r)-F(r)] = pz.                    (6)

This is the q-scaled integer map. Dividing a dirty target by q is not a
free final step; it changes its arbitrary old contents and reintroduces
a fractional/residual contract. For `b=2^-B`, (6) needs `2^B-1` calls.
It is exponential in the descriptor width B before source scans, inverse
passes, constant preparation, head travel, or scalar cleanup are charged.
It cannot serve as a polynomial-in-B repair within this averaging class.

The bound does not cover adaptive offsets, extra nonlinear oracles, or
arbitrary programs. It does cover arbitrary integer signed combinations
of a fixed finite collection of translated rounded evaluations.

## 7. A finite exact-lift escape and its width bill

Exact rational arithmetic of unbounded height is not an implementation.
The following bounded construction states what an exact lift would cost
for repeated scalar p/q leaves. It is an interface comparison, not a fast
cyclic inverse or a supplier.

Fix a finite PR1057 word with N leaf records. Regard its linear state as
polynomials in noncommuting factor symbols, with each initial row a
separate symbol. Let D be the largest factor degree at any prefix. Each
record increases degree by at most one, so `D<=N`. D can exceed the
number n of factors at the root; do not assume `D=n` for borrowed ports.

Represent each row by an integer numerator with common denominator
`Q=q^D`. Initialize a coarse-grid integer entry z as zQ. For a leaf,
perform

    target_numerator += sign * p * source_numerator / q.

Every indicated division is exact. Before a multiplication by p/q, each
source monomial has degree at most D-1; its numerator contains a factor
q. This proves the assertion at every record. PR1057's exact additive
proof then restores every helper and gives the exact product. A literal
inverse executes the reversed records with opposite signs.

This construction restricts every incoming array to the embedded coarse
lattice. It pays for extra precision in all `L(S+2)` data entries, where
S is the dirty-array count. If the original integer entries have norm at
most M and `K>=max(1,|p/q|)`, use PR1057's prefix bound H. Numerator data
need signed width

    1 + bit_length(ceil(M H q^D)).

The scalar product needs a further `ceil(log2(max(1,|p|)))` bits. Store
q and Q, and pay for preparation of Q. Initialization requires one source
read, one multiplication by Q, and one write per data entry. Each leaf
requires L source reads, L target reads, L writes, L products by p, L
exact divisions by q, and L additions. Add control, coefficient reads,
source routing, tape movement, and scalar initialization/restoration for
the chosen backend. These are not supplied as unit-cost pointer actions.

The final output can be rounded once. Its initial coarse-grid target is
an integer, so nearest rounding commutes with adding it. Discarding the
output residue is a paid irreversible operation; a bit-reversible
implementation must retain or uncompute it. The helper extensions are
already restored to their initial embedded values.

Even the endpoint gives a lower bound for this exact-lift class. With
source 1 and n repeated coprime p/q factors, the exact output is
`p^n/q^n`. A common-denominator representation must have Q divisible by
q^n. Its fractional resolution thus costs at least `n log2 q` bits. With
arbitrary integer target data, each numerator word must also hold the
target times Q. The finite prefix can require more. This is a width bound
for the specified representation, not for compressed symbolic values. It does not
prove the same bound for approximate arithmetic or for a special kernel
whose factors cancel denominators.

The checker executes this finite-width integer construction and compares
it with a separate Fraction oracle. It records the actual D, widths,
leaf counts, division count, and accesses. This is exact-grid evidence
for the stated class, not a fixed-tape compiler or an unbounded rational
substitution for a rounded algorithm.

## 8. Kappa and remaining work

The selected source at the experiment base has conditional
`kappa=1661960547713947/10^18`. Its complex supplier binds. No selected
record or source changes here. The three-array saving of PR1057 changes
storage; it does not change that word's child multiplicities. Its inherited
direct all-role moment obstruction remains.

During this work, main advanced to
`ba82555c64e1879e4b7decce28186f208fcb8262`. Its selected record names
PR1042 and `kappa=1672188119205421/10^18`. The package preserves both
selected records as immutable context. The experiment still starts at
`abdc6a7002abdbcd9e21bfc5e1a795784deacfdf`; it does not claim that the
older selected record remains current.

The new result closes the generic same-grid exact-additive wrapper route
for nonintegral rounded factors. A residue repair has an explicit array
cost under the summary interface. Fixed-offset averaging has a sharp
denominator-dependent call cost. An exact lift has explicit growing
precision and initialization costs. None supplies a useful complete
supplier recurrence. PR1054's cubic precision proof applies to its retained
excluded-ancestor word, not automatically to one of these changed words.

A future contribution can escape through a proved restricted translation
lattice, different input/output grids with paid initialization, or a new
nonadditive restoration invariant and complete work/accuracy recurrence.
The present proof does not exclude those routes. Finite checks below
support the uniform arguments; they are not a formal proof of the full
multiplication theorem.
