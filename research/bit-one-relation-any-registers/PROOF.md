# At most one frame relation cannot amortize rank, for any number of roles

Work in the group algebra F2[M_m(Q)] of the additive group of rational matrices.
There are W scalar registers. Every paid factor is
D_j=I+([A_j]-1)N_j, with nonzero rank-one rational A_j and binary rank-one
idempotent N_j. Arbitrary scalar GL_W(F2) changes of basis are free. The target
is diag([C_1],...,[C_W]); C_i may be zero, so restored arbitrary dirty roles
are included. No triangularity of target frames is assumed.

**Theorem.** If the rational span of the k paid frames has dimension at least
k-1, then sum_i rank_Q(C_i)<=k. When every active target has rank m, a saving
below m times the active role count requires at least two independent rational
frame relations, regardless
of the number of restored dirty registers.

The source's Section4 proves its eight-call/three-register instance. We retain
its Laurent-kernel/derivative argument and generalize the scalar-pattern step.
This is not a general arbitrary-register lower bound when relation nullity>=2.

## 1. Normalize and identify the only possible collision

Move every free scalar basis map through adjacent paid factors by conjugating
N_j. All conjugates are still rank-one idempotents. Under augmentation [A]->1,
the target is I, so the final scalar map is I. We may assume only paid factors.
Only the finitely generated subgroup H of rational frames is used. It is free
abelian; F2[H] is a Laurent polynomial domain.

Introduce independent variables t_j and the multilinear polynomial matrix
P(t)=product_j(I+(t_j-1)N_j). If the frames are independent, all subset sums
are distinct. A diagonal formal target then makes every off-diagonal polynomial
identically zero. Differentiating at t=1 makes every N_j diagonal. Each such
rank-one projector selects exactly one coordinate. Its A_j contributes to that
coordinate target, and disjoint rank subadditivity proves the assertion.

With one rational relation, the saturated integer kernel of Z^k->H has a
primitive generator r. If any |r_j|>=2, no two subset sums can coincide: their
difference has all coordinates in{-1,0,1} and cannot be a nonzero integer
multiple of r. The preceding argument applies.

Otherwise r is ternary. Put S={j:r_j!=0}, T its complement. The Laurent kernel
is generated by g=product_{r_j=1}t_j-product_{r_j=-1}t_j. This follows by
extending primitive r to an integer lattice basis, in which the map sets one
Laurent variable to1. Every off-diagonal entry of P is divisible by g.
Differentiation at1 shows that all N_j for j in S have the same off-diagonal
pattern B, while every N_j for j in T is diagonal.

## 2. Classify a common rank-one off-diagonal pattern in arbitrary dimension

Write N=x y^T. Its diagonal support is supp(x) intersect supp(y), which has
odd size because y^T x=1. If the directed off-diagonal graph is acyclic, the
intersection has size1: any two vertices in it create a directed2-cycle.
Every projector with this same graph, along with all coordinate projectors,
is triangular in a common topological order and has exactly one diagonal1.
Their product diagonals allocate the A_j to disjoint target coordinates.
The same rank-subadditivity proof settles this case, including B=0.

If the graph is cyclic, choose distinct a,b in the intersection. In particular
x_a=y_a=1. For every i!=a, the off-diagonal entries N_ia=x_i and N_ai=y_i
determine both vectors; their a entries are known. Thus a cyclic off-diagonal
pattern determines **one unique projector N**, in every dimension. This replaces
the special three-register all-ones J case in the source. All S factors use N.

## 3. A diagonal idempotent argument controls every outside gate

The generated group splits H=H_S direct-sum Z^T, because the sole relation is
supported on S. Set all T variables to1. The S factors combine to
I+([sum_{j in S}A_j]-1)N. It is diagonal, and N has a nonzero off-diagonal
entry, so sum_{j in S}A_j=0. The unique primitive relation is therefore the
all-plus vector on S, up to sign. No proper nonempty subset of S sums to zero.

Keep just one outside factor with scalar projector E_aa, set other T variables
to1, and let B be the sum of S frames preceding it. The S factors after it
sum to -B. With z=[B] and independent t=[A_j], the product is
I+(t-1)M, where M=D_N(z) E_aa D_N(z^-1).
Since t-1 is nonzero in the Laurent domain and the target is diagonal, M is
diagonal. It is an idempotent because it is conjugate to E_aa. Every diagonal
entry of an idempotent over a domain is0 or1. Augmentation sends M to E_aa,
so M=E_aa exactly. Consequently D_N(z) commutes with E_aa, giving
(z-1)(N E_aa-E_aa N)=0.

If N commutes with E_aa, that outside factor may be moved past every S factor.
Otherwise z=1, hence B=0, forcing this outside factor to lie before all S
factors or after all of them. Move the commuting outside factors away; the
whole S block becomes consecutive and multiplies to I. Only coordinate
projectors remain. Their frames are assigned disjointly to targets, proving
sum rank(C_i)<=|T|<=k and completing the theorem.

All arguments concern formal coefficients in a torsion-free group algebra.
A finite torsion representation cannot replace that hypothesis. The supplied
finite tests corroborate the pattern and conjugation lemmas but do not replace
this written arbitrary-W proof. No proof-assistant or full multiplication claim.
