# Full-cover volume bound for two smaller BIT children

## Exact interface

The two fields are independent at the replacement boundary. Each is a binary function on the same complete cover rows C_w and physical address lattice Z_w over the admitted odd q-power digits. Extra common spectators can be included in Z_w. Write V_w = |C_w| |Z_w| for one field's volume. In each cover row g, the required difference-address map is T_g = g(I−2P)g⁻¹ with rank(P)=a, leaving the common sum address and spectators fixed. Each paid child is a fixed rational projector Q_i of proper positive rank r_i<a, acting at its current cover row by h(I−2Q_i)h⁻¹. The allowed digit primes make the rational chart and route denominators units.

The proposed replacement has at most two chronological paid positions. It permits arbitrary pointwise binary mixing within a cover row, copying and scratch, and a finite sequence of fixed rational right cover routes, including routes that visit different cover labels for different roles or paths. These ordinary operations preserve the full physical address, although they may change the cover label or normalized coordinates. The finite path graph and rational route multipliers are width-independent. Pull back the common permissible external weight. There are no paid or ordinary address-changing operations besides the declared children in this replacement. The scope includes variable bank occupancies when every occupied cell is charged. It does not assume an actual lowered replacement in the current compiler.

## Uniform bound on bypasses

A chronological path ending at output cover g and using zero or one paid child acts on the physical difference address by g B_j g⁻¹. To see this, walk the fixed right multipliers backwards from g. The cover at the single child is g S_j for a fixed product S_j. In the zero-child case B_j is the identity. In the single-child case it is a rational conjugate of the corresponding child map. Its rank defect from identity is at most r_i<a. In particular B_j differs from T=I−2P. The index j ranges over the entire finite set of bypass paths, including those beginning in a different cover row. Pointwise scalar factors can suppress paths, which only reduces this set.

For a fixed output cover g and a path j, the coincidence T_g z = g B_j g⁻¹ z has relative size at most q^(v_j−w) among all physical addresses, for all sufficiently large w. Multiply a nonzero rational row of T−B_j by a fixed denominator, and let v_j be the minimum q-adic valuation of its nonzero entries. A coefficient of valuation v_j gives the stated bound by counting that coordinate after fixing all others. The bijection z↦g⁻¹z makes the bound uniform in g. If there are no bypass paths use zero as the bound. The finite union bound ε_w tends to zero. The other physical coordinates are unchanged and can be counted as spectators.

The same argument applied to all paths shows that a successful replacement needs a path with rank sum at least a. Indeed, the rank defect of a product is at most the sum of its factors' defects, from I−AB=(I−A)+A(I−B). If all sums were below a, a generic physical address could never reach its required output coordinate. This also excludes any single proper child, regardless of volume.

## Count distinct cells across all cover labels

Fix a single **physical** input address z common to all cover rows, rather than a common normalized address. Call g good at z when every bypass misses the output address T_g z. Restrict inputs to both roles at physical z on those good rows, and restrict outputs to the corresponding two target cells at each good row. The required matrix is a permutation of an identity of rank 2 times the number of good rows. Because all bypasses to those outputs were excluded from **every** input cover, the restricted actual matrix factors through the first paid child's input cells at physical z. All ordinary operations before that child preserve z. Paths may use different cover labels, but one bank cell at a given physical z and label contributes at most one to the rank, even if many paths reuse it. Thus the first child needs at least 2 times the number of good rows at z in distinct charged cells. This rank argument permits arbitrary cancellations among paths that use the child.

Sum over z. The slices are disjoint within the first bank. The uniform bound says that the number of good (g,z) pairs is at least (1−ε_w)V_w. Hence the first child's charged volume is at least 2(1−ε_w)V_w. For the second child apply the same argument to the transposed chronological network, with roles of input and output exchanged. Right routes invert, ordinary transposes still preserve physical address, and the child and target projector permutations invert to themselves. Rectangular scratch maps transpose as well. This gives the same volume bound for the second bank. For one complete bank field per cover row, even a single fixed physical slice with more than half the rows good is impossible. With variable bank volume, the limiting coefficient of each child is at least two full fields.

The argument also applies to m simultaneous independent fields on this domain when both child ranks are below every target rank by summing the identity ranks for the good field/cover cells and applying the finite union bound uniformly. This supports aggregation of genuinely simultaneous field instances. It does not justify grouping different caller times with intervening paid work into a two-child replacement. A shared bank may not be billed twice as though independently available at disjoint chronological replacements.
