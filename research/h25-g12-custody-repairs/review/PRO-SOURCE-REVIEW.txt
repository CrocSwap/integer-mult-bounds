## New counterexample S-ALLOC-01

The broader claim that every known close is attempted and the first primary is preserved is false if allocation failure is admitted. Parent `_close_known_descriptors` allocates a 4096-slot `_RawG12Errors` at line 214, then allocates `tuple(descriptors)` at line 215. These operations execute within the finally path after the body may already have raised E. Let three owned descriptors be live, let the body raise E at 1437, and let the ledger allocation at 214 raise MemoryError M. Then no close is attempted. M replaces E as the propagating exception and the outer handler at 1748 records M as primary. E may remain in M's exception context, but that is not preservation of E as the first-primary channel. The preflight `fds` list is a local and is not included in `_g12_finish_failure`'s cleanup domains at 1731-1737. The outer handler therefore does not repair the skipped attempts.

The analogous counterexample exists at line 219 when construction of `(error, error.__traceback__)` or recording that pair fails after the first close error. Later descriptors are skipped and M replaces E or the earlier close error. If all closes have been attempted but `list(failures)[1:]` at 233 fails, C1 is replaced by M in the success-body branch. If copying records into `secondaries` at 234 or 237 fails, the pending first error can likewise be displaced before the outer handler sees it. The guarded bookkeeping note at 1752-1759 concerns failures in `_g12_finish_failure`, not these earlier failures in preflight finally.

This does not require arbitrary hostile callbacks, signals, threads, or process interference. The original CP14 explicitly excludes unrelated callbacks/finalisers/signals only under the original premises. The inspected operating premise at parent 1040-1044 and DESIGN-v1 lines 218-225 describes trusted sources/OS and cooperating ownership, not guaranteed successful allocation. ## Concrete counterexample and narrow repair

In P1182 to 1205, the pidfd close loop is reachable only after `stop_known`, `process.poll`, `observe`, and `_children` successfully establish an empty process domain. Choose an admitted trace in which the chain already owns two pidfds and a later `/proc` observation raises `PermissionError`. The main call exits with that error. P1695 to 1699 makes a final failure-disposal attempt, but the same denial raises again before the pidfd loop. P1700 to 1737 closes other domains but contains no chain-pidfd release. Neither known pidfd has received a close attempt. Publication remains closed, but the every-known-cleanup-attempt property is false.

`pidfd_release.patch` is a proposed minimal successor against the exact S parent. It introduces a separate known-pidfd release phase, calls it on the original successful process-empty path, and calls it in a P09 `finally` after the last process-disposal attempt. It retires each integer descriptor from the owned table before the close call so an ambiguous close cannot be retried blindly. A failure or unknown process state keeps `chain.unknown` true and does not set `chain.closed` merely because the descriptor table is empty. It preserves the existing successful cleanup record shape and all selection None values.

The narrow proof assumes creation of the descriptor snapshot, execution of exception handling, and raw secondary storage succeed. Under those assumptions each remaining known descriptor is visited once, failed observation cannot bypass the final descriptor phase, and an ambiguous close is never retried. The patch does not solve allocation exhaustion, failing raw-secondary storage, process disposal after lost observation, or earlier failed acquisition registration. It has only been AST-parsed as inert text. It must not be described as integrated acceptance or a complete cleanup repair.

`publication_size.patch` is a separate one-line successor for C1002. It adds `_uint(publication_size)` before publication consumption. Without that check, an equal-valued float passes the equality at C1017 and is retained in provenance at C1069. A genuine parent emits an exact integer, so the additional predicate preserves every genuine parent handoff while excluding that larger domain. This is a narrower exact-domain repair, not evidence that a foreign caller can create source provenance, and it leaves the remaining nested-schema gaps open. The proposed consumer was AST-parsed only.

An additional allocation/ownership gap remains at P1134 to 1144. A pidfd is inserted in `self.fds` before `_proc` and before `self.rows[key] = dict(current)`. If the latter allocation fails with the child still live, a later `observe` can call `add` again because the duplicate test consults only `self.rows`. That can open and overwrite the `self.fds` entry for the same key, losing the first descriptor. Final descriptor release alone cannot repair this acquisition-state defect.

## Allocation hoists

In S parent `_directory_chain`, lines 149-177, the new allocation precedes the first `os.open`. In S parent `_read_file`, lines 242-280, and `_exclusive_directory`, lines 1226-1248, it precedes the call that returns the held directory chain. In `_copy_file`, lines 1267-1282, it precedes the created file descriptor. The consumer hoists have the same relationship at lines 133-161 and 185-223, and put the consumer's final recorder before the retained directory acquisition at lines 1011-1013. The ordinary success path and subsequent original file/schema checks remain in the same order once the allocation succeeds.

This does not make acquisition atomic. An `os.open` can still succeed before a subsequent `fds.append` or dictionary publication fails. Existing primary tuple creation, close-list materialisation, raw error tuples, and recorder count updates remain fallible. Those failures need their own source disposition. The hoists also do not protect descriptors already owned by a caller when it invokes the function. They establish only that the specific initial recorder allocation is earlier than each function's new local acquisitions.

There is a real resource consequence. `_directory_chain` originally allocated its recorder only on failure; the hoist allocates the 4,096-slot recorder on successful directory-chain calls as well. The other moved recorders were already allocated on their successful paths. This extra allocation must be charged, and may cause an earlier source refusal under memory pressure. The patch must not be described as universal allocation-equivalence or a measured performance improvement. A non-vacuous clean trace remains compatible when the allocation and original operations succeed.

## Pidfd release

S `_KnownG12Chain.finish`, lines 1182-1212, reaches its close loop only after observations report the owned processes, occupied groups, and current children absent. A failure of observation or signalling prevents that loop. S `_g12_finish_failure`, lines 1695-1699, catches the final `finish` failure but has no subsequent pidfd close loop. The proposed `finally` therefore addresses a genuine reachable ownership gap.

The new `release_known_pidfds` snapshots the currently recorded owners, removes each numeric descriptor from `self.fds`, then attempts `os.close`. Under the original serial ownership premise and functioning bookkeeping, each recorded descriptor is attempted once in the snapshot's insertion order. On an ambiguous close error, retaining the number as active ownership would permit an unsafe later retry; deleting it before the attempt prevents that particular repeated-close error. The failure sets `unknown=True`, retains the raw exception and traceback in order, and does not infer process termination.

The normal `finish` path recomputes `closed` as `not self.fds and not self.unknown`. The final exceptional cleanup path deliberately sets `unknown=True` before releasing remaining pidfds. Thus an empty descriptor dictionary cannot produce success when either process cleanup or a close remains uncertain. No later process-disposal retry follows this final release site. Under original cooperating-source/no-external-alias premises, I found no newly introduced normal-path ownership or ordering defect in these edits.

The added method is included in the parent's existing initial class/function graph capture. S523-550 traverses class dictionaries and captures exact function fields, and S554-577 checks those captured members and fields. A future selected parent must nevertheless receive an updated hash and coherent source selection. The patch itself does not select it.

The limits are material. `tuple(self.fds.items())`, raw error tuple construction, and recorder updates can still fail. A recording failure can stop later pidfd attempts and abort the rest of `_g12_finish_failure`, including streams, reserved outputs, the lease, and directory chains. The enclosing S1749-1759 protects an already recorded parent primary, but does not preserve every additional raw error or ensure every known cleanup attempt after such an abort. Acquisition at S1140-1142 can also succeed before the pidfd is published in `self.fds`. This partial patch cannot justify the claim that every acquired pidfd is always closed, or that all 18 branches are now accepted.

## Explicit preflight outcome

The current helper at S223-237 consults `sys.exc_info()[1]` to decide whether a body failure is pending. Python's handled-exception state can instead come from an enclosing caller's `except`, even when the local preflight body succeeded. In that case, the helper appends a close error as a secondary and permits preflight to return instead of raising the close error at that boundary. The final parent may still refuse later, but the original requirement that body success plus close failure stops preflight is not proved by ambient exception state.

Initialising `body_failed=True` immediately before the protected `try`, and setting it to `False` only at the end of the successful body, gives the helper the right local outcome under the original non-asynchronous-interference premises. It keeps the pending body error propagating on local failure, and makes a first close error primary on body success. It does not rely on cross-process or newly fabricated identity. It changes no scientific code, oracle, or selection.

The signature change makes S `verify.py` calls at lines 203, 223, and 234 incompatible. The corresponding proposed controls should pass `True` for the test with a pending body error and `False` for the body-success and clean-close tests. That verifier imports target modules and is expressly prohibited from execution in this consultation. Its previously reported PASS cannot be reused after any of these source changes. The exact corrected-source hashes in the verifier and package metadata would also require regeneration if a new package were authored.

This optional patch retains the unresolved allocation sites in S214-219 and S230-237. Initial recorder/snapshot allocation can still mask a pending body error and skip all closes. Failure while retaining one close error can still skip later descriptors. `body_failed` solves outcome classification only.

## Publication-size domain

S consumer line 1001 unpacks `publication_size`. The next check validates the three digest fields, but there is no exact-type check for the publication size. At line 1017, `publication_record['bytes'] == publication_size` allows a finite float `N.0` equal to the genuine integer byte count `N`. Lines 1068-1071 then preserve that float in the returned provenance. A future source-selected handoff containing that float is not rejected at this schema boundary when the rest of its evidence is genuine. This is a type-domain counterexample, not a demonstrated way to forge source or custody.

The one-line `_uint(publication_size)` fix uses the existing exact `type(value) is int` and nonnegative-domain guard at lines 89-90. It rejects booleans, floats, and foreign equality domains before the later size comparison, while accepting every genuine parent-emitted integer size. All byte, hash, identity, currentness, and schema checks remain in place. A separate positive-domain check is unnecessary here because the subsequent exact byte comparison and JSON/publication schema already reject an empty file. No new ownership or order defect was found in this line.

## Combined result

The allocation hoists, pidfd release, and publication-size guard address disjoint local defects and can be composed without altering the independent uncached reference producer or P07/N22 scientific protocol. The parent has elected to exclude the optional helper-ABI change from the concrete three-part proposal. The ambient-exception classification issue therefore remains open. The combined proposal remains a partial successor. Its source inventory and hashes must identify the exact composed bytes, preserve predecessor provenance and the original `None` selections, and keep every unresolved cleanup, allocation, authority, and custody obligation explicit. Proposed regression controls and passive byte/AST checks must be labelled separately from target executions, of which there were none.
