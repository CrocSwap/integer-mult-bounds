General identity (no finite test is needed for this algebra)
Let F_Q denote the invertible address frame and let the original terminal
hold F_U w. The vector w is the entire arbitrary scalar value, including
all old scratch contributions. For forward schedule U -> 0 -> 1, the old
scatter source is F_0 w and the old cleanup input is F_1 w. The new schedule
copies F_U w, applies F_0 F_U^-1 to the copy, uses it for every read-only
scatter, and discards it. On the retained original it applies F_1 F_U^-1.
Both vectors are exactly the old ones. Hence any later cleanup gates,
source-copy cancellation and endpoint operator receive identical inputs.

For reverse complementary schedule 0 -> 1 -> Uperp, first advance the
original from F_0 w to F_Uperp w; copy that and apply F_1 F_Uperp^-1.
The scatter sees F_1 w and cleanup sees F_Uperp w, exactly as before.
This does not require w to be a fresh producer output or the scratch to be
zero. A subsequent arbitrary linear mixer cannot distinguish the schedules.
It also holds with spectator fields and suppressed tensor factors untouched.

In the orthogonal decomposition D0 plus active local F_h, the old ranks
are (r,h) forward and (h,r) reverse. The new copied change has rank r
and the direct original change rank h-r. Partial swaps are involutions.
For phases, forward copied change is C_U^-1, reverse copied change C_U,
and direct original change is C_Uperp. These are separately PAID recursive
calls, so the new local profile is z^r+z^(h-r), with rank saving r.
The inverse phase must not be silently replaced by a forward phase.

Cost and new rank mass
Every temporary duplicates precisely one original role stream at volume V/W.
Paureel's retained interface pays copy/read/discard O(V/W), parks originals,
and processes one extra stream sequentially on the same depth-first tapes;
see references/copied-centers/pr29/paureel-direct-transfer.tex:78-88 and
notes/copied-centers-lemma.tex:29-35. The r-child on the temporary is included;
there is no uncharged undo or extra independently varying row coordinate.

H'_1=H_1+h and H'_h=H_h-h, with H'_(h-1)=H_(h-1) unchanged. Applying both
axis banks saves L=v_b ell_a+v_a ell_b, so s=Wm-N+L. All N rank-one data
endpoint-copy calls remain charged. Swapping axis order does not affect this
local proof, role-volume argument, or the total L; reversal23/25 still needs
its own actual common-basis/corner profile, which this audit does not supply.

Complex endpoint check
notes/copied-centers-complex.tex:34-55 has source frames(T,I), terminal(F,E),
E=F T^-1. Applying the scalar shear matrix(-y,x+y) therefore yields
A=-F y and B=F T^-2 x+E y. Retain A, apply T^-1 to a COPY and add it to B;
T^-1 A=-E y, leaving F T^-2 x. This costs one rank-one inverse child per
pair. It is not zero-cost and does not alter A. Since T^2=X_u and u has
weight nine, F T^-2 has C^-1 on those nine coordinates and C elsewhere.
The coordinate identity i Z C^-1 Z=C gives the paid scalar/diagonal wrappers
i^(9f) Z_u (F T^-2) Z_u=F on f columns. Negation restores A to F y, and
undoing the bank exchange yields the two required full-F outputs.
The translated auxiliary endpoint(C_I C_D0) C_D0^-1=C_I is unchanged.
General nondegenerate/alternating residual compilation remains inherited
from the cited Gauss normal-form theorem, not proved by these small tests.
