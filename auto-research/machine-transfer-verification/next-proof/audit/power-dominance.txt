Pinned power-moment comparison of PR67 with PR64, PR65, and PR68
================================================================

This note proves a finite profile statement, not an all-size integer
multiplication theorem. The identities below are ordinary mathematical
proofs; this new second-summation argument has not been checked in Lean.
Exact input arrays, every capped sum and every prefix, and source SHA-256
hashes are in power-dominance.json. The complete child lists, rather than
only the two local differences, were independently compared by
power_inputs.py. All unchanged exterior and data terms cancel exactly.

Let d_t be the NEW minus OLD multiplicity of width t. In the comparisons
here d_t=0 for t>25 and sum(t*d_t)=0. Set T=25 and

    C_k = sum_{t=1}^T d_t min(t,k),
    S_j = sum_{k=1}^j C_k.

For PR67 minus PR64, C_16=3542>0. PR67 therefore does not dominate PR64
for all concave functions: f(t)=min(t,16) is an explicit counterexample.
Likewise the capped sums can be positive against PR65 and PR68. The valid
stronger-than-a-single-exponent statement is POWER MOMENT dominance.

Finite summation identity
-------------------------
Take f(0)=0 and write b_k=2f(k)-f(k-1)-f(k+1). For 0<=t<=T,

    f(t) = (f(T)-f(T-1))*t + sum_{k=1}^{T-1} b_k min(t,k).

Proof: both sides vanish at 0. Their consecutive differences at t>=1
are equal because the right-hand difference is
f(T)-f(T-1)+sum_{k=t}^{T-1} b_k=f(t)-f(t-1), by telescoping.
Multiplying by d_t and summing cancels the linear term by equal rank mass.
Writing C_k=S_k-S_{k-1}, S_0=0, a second telescoping gives

    sum_t d_t f(t)
      = sum_{k=1}^{T-1} b_k C_k
      = b_{T-1} S_{T-1}
        + sum_{k=1}^{T-2} (b_k-b_{k+1}) S_k.

Consequently, S_k<=0 for 1<=k<T and S_{T-1}<0 imply a strict negative
sum whenever b_{T-1}>0 and b_k>=b_{k+1}. This is a decreasing-curvature
test; ordinary concavity alone would give only b_k>=0 and is insufficient.

Why power curvature is positive and decreasing, including k=1
-------------------------------------------------------------
Let f(t)=t^p for any real 0<p<1, with f(0)=0. For integer k>=1,

 b_k = p(1-p) integral_0^1 [
           u*(k-1+u)^(p-2) + (1-u)*(k+u)^(p-2)
       ] du.

For k>=2 this follows by two integrations by parts, or integrating the
second derivative against the triangular kernel on [k-1,k+1]. For k=1
the potentially singular first summand is exactly u^(p-1), integrable
because p>0. Perform the integrations from epsilon>0 and let epsilon
decrease to zero: the boundary term u*f'(u)=p*u^p tends to zero, and
f(u)=u^p also tends to zero. This proves the same identity at k=1 without
assuming bounded f'' at zero.

The prefactor p(1-p) is positive. Both summands are positive on 0<u<1,
so b_k>0. Since p-2<0, increasing k by one strictly decreases each
positive summand for every 0<u<1. The integrals are finite; integrating
their pointwise strict inequality gives b_k>b_{k+1} for every k>=1.
Thus the hypotheses of the second telescoping identity hold for all
power functions t^p with 0<p<1.

Exact finite evidence
----------------------
All 24 required prefixes are negative in each comparison. The values
below summarize them; the JSON includes every integer, including C_25=0.

    NEW - OLD       largest prefix S_k      S_24
    PR67 - PR64          -1765204           -7306318
    PR67 - PR65          -1470091           -3641245
    PR67 - PR68          -1446424           -3640348

Therefore sum_t d_t*t^p<0 for every real 0<p<1 in each of these three
comparisons. With p=1-a, the characteristic function is

    Phi(a) = (1/W) sum_t n_t (t/m)^(1-a).

All compared profiles have m=575, W=137151806 and total rank 78860441550.
The factor m^(a-1)/W is positive. Hence the full characteristic of PR67
is strictly smaller than the full characteristic of PR64, PR65, and
PR68 for EVERY 0<a<1. This concerns the pinned finite constructions.
It does not turn the assembly's conditional transfer theorem into a
proved physical multiplication algorithm or prove an optimal exponent.
