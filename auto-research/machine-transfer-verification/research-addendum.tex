\section{A literal-word dirty-scratch theorem}
Let $D$ be a set of auxiliary roles and let a binary state be an element of
$\F^D$. A gate replaces coordinate $d$ by $d+s$, with distinct source and
destination roles. Let $L$ be a finite chronological word of such gates.
Each gate is linear and involutive, hence the reverse word is $L^{-1}$.
Let $V:X\to\F^D$ be an injection rule, $J:\F^D\to\F^Z$ a linear
scatter, and $O:X\to\F^Z$ a specified output satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:terminal} J(L(Vx))=O(x)\quad(x\in X).\end{equation}
No linear structure on $X$ and no zero-scratch promise is needed.

\begin{theorem}[Dirty-scratch wrapper]
Starting from source $x$, target $z$, and arbitrary scratch $d$, execute
chronologically
\[
 L,\ J,\ L^{-1},\ V,\ L,\ J,\ L^{-1},\ V,
\]
where $J$ adds the scatter to the target and $V$ adds $Vx$ to scratch.
The result is $(x,d,z+O(x))$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The first scatter adds $JLd$. The first inverse restores $d$, and the
injection changes it to $d+Vx$. The second scatter adds
$JL(d+Vx)=JLd+JLVx$. The two dirty terms cancel in characteristic two.
The last inverse restores $d+Vx$, and the last injection restores $d$.
Equation~\eqref{eq:terminal} identifies the target increment. The source is
read-only throughout.
\end{proof}

The new file \texttt{lean/DirtyWrapper.lean} formalizes the literal gate
semantics, reverse-word inversion, linearity and the eight executed stages.
The terminal identity and scatter linearity are explicit hypotheses.
Uniform address reindexing commutes with a pointwise XOR gate, because both
sides evaluate the same two Boolean coordinates at the same reindexed address.
Induction extends this to a word. The Lean file also formalizes this fact.
This proves an equal-frame semantic interface; it does not prove that every
frame transition emitted by a particular compiler is legal or correctly charged.

The inherited geometric schedule also explains why four scalar mixer sweeps
do not require four copies of the varying-frame profile. In the notation of
\texttt{notes/dag-construction.tex}, the early $L,J,L^{-1}$ use a common
background $D_0$. Only the middle forward $L$ uses increasing frames
$D_U=D_0\perp(P\otimes U)$. The final inverse uses the common background
$D_1$; entry into $D_1$ is charged as terminal cleanup. Source injections
and scatters retain their separately charged source/target frames, and copied
centers use the explicit paid schedule of \texttt{notes/copied-centers-lemma.tex}.
For the reverse orientation, reverse the word and complement these frames.
This is a written geometric argument, not a new Lean proof of the full
frame-changing implementation. The local equal-frame and terminal contracts
remain necessary at every gate.

\section{Rank conservation and the correct optimization objective}
Set $h_1=23$, $h_2=25$, $m=575$, $v_h=\binom h3$, and
$N=v_{23}v_{25}=4073300$. Assume every auxiliary physical role has a
monotone frame path whose rank begins at zero and ends at $h$.
Every transition is charged by its rank increase. Assume also that the $h$
copied centers each incur the additional charged rank $h-1$.

\begin{lemma}[Local rank conservation]
With $R_h$ roles, the full local rank mass is
$hR_h+h(h-1)$, independently of how the intermediate monotone transitions
are grouped.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For each role the successive rank differences telescope to $h$.
Sum over roles and add the $h$ paid center contributions.
\end{proof}
The assertion requires the actual chronological frame path and complete
endpoint charges; a histogram alone does not establish its hypotheses.

Let $B_h=(N/v_h)R_h$, $W=2N+B_{23}+B_{25}$, and
$L=(N/v_{23})23\cdot22+(N/v_{25})25\cdot24=2226400$.
The retained data child list is two copies of
$9[1]+[21]+[17]+[481]$ per source pair. It contributes $1056N$.
The data-growth terms contribute $92N$, and paid endpoint copies contribute
$N$. Auxiliary interior and exterior terms together contribute
$m(B_{23}+B_{25})+L$. Consequently
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mass}
 s=mW-N+L,\qquad mW-s=N-L=1846900.
\end{equation}
Thus changes confined to this producer/compiler family preserve the deficit.
They improve the width and child distribution, not that deficit.

For child multiplicities $n_t$ define
\[
 F(u)=\frac{1}{mW}\sum_{0<t<m} n_t t (m/t)^u.
\]
At a fixed $u>0$, minimizing roles alone is not the right objective.
Let $\phi_u(t)=t(m/t)^u$ and $n_{h,t}$ be the local internal profile.
Then the sign of $F(u)-1$ is the sign of a fixed data contribution plus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:separable}
 \sum_{h\in\{23,25\}}\frac{N}{v_h}
 \left[\sum_t n_{h,t}\phi_u(t)
 +R_h\{\phi_u(h)+\phi_u(m-2h)-m\}\right].
\end{equation}
This follows by expanding all exterior and interior children and subtracting
$mW$. If axis choices are independently compatible with the unchanged common
basis and data geometry, each bracket can be minimized separately. Compatibility
is a hypothesis, not a consequence of the algebraic separation.

\begin{lemma}[Discrete concave profile comparison]\label{lem:concave}
Let $T\ge2$ and let two finite multiplicity profiles on $\{1,\ldots,T\}$ have signed
difference $d_t=\widetilde n_t-n_t$ and equal rank mass. Put
$C_k=\sum_t d_t\min(t,k)$. If $C_k\le0$ for every $k$ and $C_1<0$,
then for every function $f:\{0,\ldots,T\}\to\R$ with $f(0)=0$, nonnegative discrete curvatures
$b_k=2f(k)-f(k-1)-f(k+1)$ for $1\le k<T$, and $b_1>0$,
\[
 \sum_t\widetilde n_t f(t)<\sum_t n_t f(t).
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For integer $0\le t\le T$, telescoping successive slopes gives
\[
 f(t)=t\{f(T)-f(T-1)\}
       +\sum_{k=1}^{T-1}b_k\min(t,k).
\]
Multiply by $d_t$ and sum. The linear term vanishes by equal rank mass.
The remaining sum is $\sum b_kC_k<0$; its $k=1$ term is strictly negative
and all other terms are nonpositive.
\end{proof}
For the new and original profiles, the nonzero capped differences are
\[
\begin{split}

           &-431,-305,-202,-121,-62,-24;\\
h=25:\quad&-372,-669,-1005,-1206,-1288,-1264,-1182,\\
           &-1013,-843,-688,-516,-366,-263,-179,\\
           &-103,-76,-49,-32,-10.
\end{split}
\]
All later capped differences are zero. The exact integer script
\texttt{concave\_dominance.py} verifies these values and checks the
min-basis identity coefficient by coefficient.
The new \texttt{lean/CappedMass.lean} proves the finite expansion and
summation-by-parts comparison, verifies both concrete signed cap tables,
and uses real-power strict concavity to prove the all-exponent result.
The correspondence between those literal profile tables and the compiler's
words is independently checked outside Lean.

\begin{theorem}[Uniform local improvement]
For every $0<u<1$, the new ranked-pair characteristic is strictly smaller
than the original PR62 stacked characteristic.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}

strictly positive discrete curvature. Multiply by the positive replication

so they cancel. This proves strict improvement for the full characteristic.
\end{proof}

\section{Balanced assembly and a formal ceiling}
Let $a>0$ be the local bit saving, let $0<\epsilon<1$, and suppose a final
saving is constrained by $\kappa<1-\epsilon$ and
$\kappa<\epsilon q$, with $q\le a$.
\begin{theorem}[Two-margin ceiling]
Under these hypotheses, $\kappa<a/(1+a)$.
Moreover
$\min(1-\epsilon,\epsilon a)\le a/(1+a)$ for every real $\epsilon$;
equality holds at $\epsilon=1/(1+a)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
If $\epsilon\le1/(1+a)$, then $\epsilon a\le a/(1+a)$.
Otherwise $1-\epsilon<a/(1+a)$. Apply the two strict bounds for $\kappa$.
Substitution proves the equality assertion.
\end{proof}
This result is formalized in \texttt{lean/BalancedCeiling.lean}. Equality in
the relaxed two-margin problem does not satisfy all strict physical constraints.

The inherited assembly uses $b=717/10^7$, $\beta=1/20$ and a positive
backoff $\eta$, with
\[
 q=a(1-2\eta),\quad c=q+\eta/4,\quad
 \epsilon=\frac{1-\eta}{1+q},\quad G=\epsilon q,
 \quad r=(G+1-\epsilon)/2,\quad \delta=\eta/8.
\]
Its minimum of seven cost margins is $G$ and it checks 47 strict conditions.
The independent transcription \texttt{independent\_assembly.py} reproduces
all 47 published rational values before refining parameters. For the supplied
pair profile, $a=510195230290215848/10^{22}$, $\eta=10^{-18}$ and

       =5.10169201700880969\cdot10^{-5}
\end{equation}
pass the exact moment and assembly conditions. This is only a parameter

so that $\kappa=5.10275840622536575\cdot10^{-5}$.

\section{Rounded recurrences: the finite-to-asymptotic step}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:rounded}
Let $0<p\le1$, $c_i\ge0$, $0<r_i<1$ for a nonempty finite index set,
and $\gamma=\sum_i c_i r_i^p<1$. Suppose $f:\mathbb N\to[0,\infty)$
is finite-valued and, for all $n>N_0$,
\[
 f(n)\le C+\sum_i c_i f(\lceil r_i n\rceil)
\]
for a fixed $C\ge0$. Then $f(n)=O(n^p)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose an integer $N\ge\max(1,N_0)$ large enough that
$N>1/(1-\max_i r_i)$ and
$N^p\ge2\sum_i c_i/(1-\gamma)$.
Choose $A>0$ with $A\ge2C/(1-\gamma)$ and
$f(n)\le An^p$ for $1\le n\le N$.
For $n>N$ all children lie in $[1,n-1]$.
The inequality $(x+1)^p\le x^p+1$ holds for $x\ge0$;
for $0<p<1$ it follows by differentiating the decreasing increment
$(x+1)^p-x^p$, and $p=1$ is equality.
Strong induction now gives
\[
 f(n)\le C+A\gamma n^p+A\sum_i c_i\le An^p.
\]
The last two terms are at most $A(1-\gamma)n^p$, by the choices of
$A,N$ and $n^p\ge1$.
\end{proof}
For the desired bit recurrence take $p=1-a$, $c_t=n_t/W$ and $r_t=t/m$;
then $\gamma=F(a)$. This lemma concerns a scalar recurrence. Establishing
that one fixed finite-alphabet machine has this recurrence, uniformly over
all volumes, address lengths, row remainders and dirty workspaces, remains a
separate and essential obligation.
The retained \texttt{notes/batched-algorithms.tex} actually implements floor
children $t\lfloor n/m\rfloor$, handling the fewer-than-$m$ remainder digits
separately. The ceiling recurrence above is a sufficient upper envelope;
to obtain it from the floor recurrence one may use a monotone worst-case
normalized cost function. It is not a claim that the serialized machine
literally recurses at the ceiling widths.
The new \texttt{lean/RoundedRecurrence.lean} proves a quantitative form:
given an explicit threshold and the finite budget
$C+A\sum c_i\le A(1-\gamma)N^p$, it establishes the all-width bound using
actual upward-rounded natural children. It also proves the two half-budget
conditions used above imply that budget. Selection of machine-specific
$C,N,A$ and the physical recurrence are external application obligations.
The same Lean file proves the real exponential/power characteristic identity
and a network-specific specialization with weights $n_t/W$ and ratios $t/m$.
Its strict real characteristic bound is a named hypothesis. The subsequent
development described next discharges that hypothesis for the literal
selected profile by proving the real inequality inside Lean.

\begin{proposition}[Power comparison by integrated capped sums]
Let $T\ge2$ and let $d_t$ be a finitely supported signed multiplicity difference with
$\sum_{t=1}^T td_t=0$. Put
$C_k=\sum_t d_t\min(t,k)$ and $S_j=\sum_{k=1}^j C_k$.
If $S_j\le0$ for $1\le j<T$ and $S_{T-1}<0$, then
$\sum_t d_t t^p<0$ for every $0<p<1$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $f(t)=t^p$, $f(0)=0$, and
$b_k=2f(k)-f(k-1)-f(k+1)$. The capped-function representation and equal
rank mass give $\sum_t d_t f(t)=\sum_{k=1}^{T-1}b_kC_k$.
Summation by parts gives
\[
 \sum_{k=1}^{T-1}b_kC_k
 =b_{T-1}S_{T-1}+\sum_{k=1}^{T-2}(b_k-b_{k+1})S_k.
\]
The curvatures $b_k$ are positive and strictly decreasing. Indeed,
\[
 b_k=p(1-p)\int_0^1\!\left[
 u(k-1+u)^{p-2}+(1-u)(k+u)^{p-2}\right]du.
\]
Both summands strictly decrease with $k$ on $0<u<1$. At $k=1$ the
first is $u^{p-1}$, which is integrable; integration by parts is
justified by taking a positive lower cutoff and using
$u f'(u)=pu^p\to0$. The first term in the summation formula is negative
and all remaining terms are nonpositive.
\end{proof}
For those pinned complete profiles, the differences PR67 minus PR64,
PR65 and the older PR68 head are supported through $T=25$. All 24 required prefixes
are negative, with largest values respectively $-1765204$, $-1470091$
and $-1446424$. Thus PR67 has a strictly smaller full power
characteristic for every saving $0<a<1$. This new comparison is an
ordinary mathematical proof with exact integer certificates; it has not
been formalized in Lean. Ordinary concave dominance is false, since
PR67 minus PR64 has $C_{16}=3542>0$.

\paragraph{The literal floor recurrence.}
\texttt{FloorDepth.lean} defines the executable depth
\[
 D(e)=0\quad(e<m),\qquad D(e)=1+D(t_*\lfloor e/m\rfloor)\quad(e\ge m).
\]
It proves termination, monotonicity, and
$D(t\lfloor e/m\rfloor)+1\le D(e)$ whenever $t\le t_*<m$.
Consequently, $W^{D(e)}\mid R$ gives an exact split $W(R/W)=R$ and
$W^{D(t\lfloor e/m\rfloor)}\mid R/W$ for every child.
The companion file proves
\[
 D(e)\le1+\frac{\log e}{\log(m/t_*)}\quad(e\ge1, 0<t_*<m).
\]
For $p\ge0$ and $\gamma=W^{-1}\sum_t n_t(t/m)^p<1$, the formal
floor-recurrence theorem states that
\[
 f(e)\le C+\frac1W\sum_t n_t f(t\lfloor e/m\rfloor)\quad(e\ge m)
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad f(e)\le Ae^p\quad(e\ge1),
\]
where the finite-valued $f$ is arbitrary and
$A=\sum_{0\le u<m}|f(u)|+|C|/(1-\gamma)$.
No monotonicity of $f$ or ceiling envelope is assumed. Positive child
widths are strictly smaller; floors decrease their $p$-powers; strict
contraction pays $C$; and the finite sum covers the base cases.

for the literal selected profile, with $m=575$, $t_*=529$ and $p=1-a$.

\paragraph{Uniformity over invocation states.}
\texttt{PhysicalCostTransfer.lean} avoids assuming a finite normalized
supremum over volumes and spectator layouts. It quantifies over an arbitrary
type of admissible states, with width $e(s)$, volume $V(s)\ge0$ and cost
$T(s)$. Actual children $s_{t,j}$, $0\le j<n_t$, obey the named contracts
\[
 e(s_{t,j})=t\lfloor e(s)/m\rfloor,\qquad V(s_{t,j})=V(s)/W.
\]
Assume a uniform base bound $T(s)\le BV(s)$ for $1\le e(s)<m$, and
$T(s)\le CV(s)+\sum_{t,j}T(s_{t,j})$ internally. Then
\[
 T(s)\le\left(\max(0,B)+\frac{|C|}{1-\gamma}\right)V(s)e(s)^{1-a}
 \qquad(e(s)>0).
\]
Strong induction on the integer width proves this uniformly: each
child contributes at most its volume times its child-width power, and
the finite characteristic sums those contributions to
$\gamma V(s)e(s)^{1-a}$. The chosen constant covers both base and
overhead. Multiplicities remain symbolic finite sums, without expanding
billions of calls. The four modules contain 35 audited theorem declarations;
their 17 analytic dependencies were also rebuilt. The physical state,

\paragraph{Literal parking-tape operations.}
\texttt{TapeStack.lean} and \texttt{TapeStackPush.lean} use Mathlib's
bidirectional tape semantics. The alphabet has a blank, a separator and
two bit symbols. Pop has six control states and three tapes; push has
three states and two tapes. Instructions receive only the state and
scanned symbols, write at most one cell per tape and move each head by
at most one position. A refinement theorem equates these finite tables
with the step functions used in the runtime proofs.

For every bitstream of length $n$ and arbitrary finite ancestor suffix,
push parks the stream and delimiter, erases its source and restores the
source head within $2n+2$ transitions. Pop recovers the original stream
order, empties its temporary, restores the former parking-stack top and
returns the output head to its first payload cell within $3n+5$ transitions.
Push consists of $n+1$ copying/sealing steps and $n+1$ rewind steps.
Pop uses two entry steps, two reversing transfers of $n+1$ steps each,
and an output rewind of $n+1$ steps. Induction on the literal input list
proves each phase, including $n=0$. The round trip explicitly reuses
the erased source as output. No counter or seek-to-length primitive
is assumed. Boundary markers can be installed or removed in two local
actions each; removal on an empty work tape restores a genuinely blank tape.

All 36 tape theorem declarations are axiom-audited. The bounds concern
specified iterations, not first-halting or optimality. Ancestor contents
and the previous stack top are restored exactly, with no ancestor-size
term in the bound. The complete recursive scheduler remains to be
constructed: it must preserve the parked record and head across the child,
restore the reusable tape interfaces, and integrate active I/O, descriptor,
row and frame operations. The round trip composes two proved routines;
it does not encode the entire multiplier in one transition table.

\paragraph{An all-width choice of row digits.}
An additional bound on the executable depth is
\[
 (m-t_*)D(e)\le e.
\]
Indeed, every internal step decreases width by at least $m-t_*$:
writing $f=\lfloor e/m\rfloor\ge1$ gives
$(m-t_*)+t_*f\le mf\le e$. Strong induction proves the bound.
For $m=575$, $t_*=529$, choose
\[
 \rho=14D(e),\qquad N=q^{2\rho},\qquad P=W^{D(e)}.
\]
For every $q\ge2$ and $1\le W\le137151806<2^{28}$, one has
$P\le N$. Moreover, $2\rho<e$ for every positive integer $e$:
if $D(e)>0$, then $28D(e)<46D(e)\le e$; otherwise $\rho=0$.
Thus this explicit choice needs no unspecified finite exceptional set of
small widths. It need not be the least possible row-digit count, but the
proved logarithmic depth bound still gives $\rho=O(\log e)$.

The least multiple
\[
 R=\left(\left\lfloor\frac{N-1}{P}\right\rfloor+1\right)P
\]
satisfies $N\le R<N+P\le2N$ and $P\mid R$.
Depth monotonicity gives $W^{D(e-\rho)}\mid R$.
The exact identity $q^{2\rho}q^{2(e-\rho)}=q^{2e}$ shows that the rows
come from existing digits; rounding increases the full logical volume by
strictly less than two, including arbitrary positive spectator factors.
There is only one outer rounding. No internal recursive padding is needed.

For any $p>0$, the formal power estimate is
\[
 \rho\le14\left(1+\frac{1}{p\log(575/529)}\right)e^p.

It follows from the depth bound and $\log e\le e^p/p$.

\paragraph{Uniform descriptor absorption.}
Every descendant must retain the complete root main-address range
$X=q^{2(e-\rho)}$, even when some digits become spectators. At depth
$j\le D(e)$, with positive spectator factor $S$, put
\[
 V_0=RXS,\qquad V_j=(R/W^j)XS.

Exact row divisibility gives $V_0=W^jV_j$. Since $2\rho<e$,
\[
 W^j\le P\le N\le X\le V_j,
 \qquad\text{hence}\qquad V_0\le V_j^2.

The resulting explicit estimate, for every fixed integer $k\ge1$, is
\[
 \bigl(\log(2V_0)\bigr)^k\le2(2k)^kV_j.

Indeed, $\log(2V_0)\le2\log(2V_j)$ and
$\log(2V_j)\le k(2V_j)^{1/k}$.

\paragraph{A genuine localization interface.}

$\mathbb Z[1/D]$, with an injective map to $\Q$ when $D>0$.

of prescribed nonzero pivots or Gram determinants also includes their

reduction homomorphism. Rationally checked identities are first reflected
