Scalar payload versus address field: no F3 lift of the XOR word
================================================================

Reviewed at PR68 commit 85781f662b57521743cce07cdd5ea194e6f3879d, with
independent corroboration from the finite-frames audit group.

The current copied-fixed and pair/frame constructions deliberately use
binary payloads. research/copied-fixed/PROOF.md lines 99-109 distinguishes
the payload field F2 from rational address projectors, subsequently reduced
modulo an eligible odd prime. The scalar intersection identity and dirty
wrapper rely on characteristic two. The emitted compiler and replay use
literal XOR throughout. This is consistent with the original bit-network
transfer in upstream/build/sections/04-swap.tex: lines 107, 165-184 and 245
describe pointwise bit gates, common-address-permutation naturality, and
one-bit payloads. Rational or modular projector entries do not make those
payloads rational or odd-characteristic field elements.

notes/batched-algorithms.tex lines 9-18 instead starts from the prime-field
F3 five-subset circuit with h=28 and H0=784. Its F3 table at line 186 is a
specialization of that older construction. It must not silently be applied
to the later h23/h25 XOR program. A generic finite-alphabet transfer may
instantiate an F2 table; a proof or implementation consuming the older
text literally must change that instantiation and bind the binary circuit
and widths explicitly. This source mismatch is an interface obligation,
not evidence that the current checked binary word should work over F3.

An all-plus lift is false even for the minimal dirty wrapper with L=J=V=1.
Over F3 its successive two scatters and two injections send
  (x,y,z) -> (x, y+2z+x, z+2x),
instead of restoring z and adding only x to y. For example x=1,y=z=0
leaves scratch z=2. Reversing a literal XOR word is an inverse because each
XOR is an involution over F2; the same positive shear is not self-inverse
over F3. A signed arbitrary-field redesign is a different construction
and would require fresh scalar correctness and frame/charge binding.

The common-address-permutation argument itself is field-independent:
every fixed pointwise gate commutes with applying the same address
permutation to all of its operands. It therefore applies to the actual
binary payload alphabet without identifying payload and address fields.
This observation does not supply the omitted fixed-tape streaming,
padding, charging, recurrence or recovery proofs by itself.
