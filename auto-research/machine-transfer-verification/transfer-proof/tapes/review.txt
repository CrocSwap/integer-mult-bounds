Independent adversarial review of the fixed-tape stack primitives
=================================================================

Verdict: no correctness or specification blocker found in the final
sources below. They establish genuine finite-control, head-local
multitape primitives on the stated tape layouts. They do not establish
the entire recursive scheduler or multiplication machine.

Finite machine interpretation
------------------------------
Symbol has exactly four values: blank, separator, false bit, true bit.
The pop control has six states and three tapes; push has three states
and two tapes. Each instruction depends only on a finite mode and current
head symbols. Action permits one head write followed by at most one unit
move per tape. Both head_local_refinement theorems identify the proof's
step function with this literal table on EVERY configuration, not merely
on reachable ones. Neither transition inspects a list, length, ancestor,
address or recursion depth. The two-tape push can leave the third tape
idle when used with pop; no input-dependent number of tapes is needed.

Mathlib Tape is a bidirectional tape with finitely many nonblank cells.
Its left and right lists are nearest-first and are quotiented only by
trailing blanks. The definitions use that orientation correctly. Equality
restores physical contents relative to the head; it does not preserve the
irrelevant length of a list representation ending in blank symbols.

Layout, ordering and restoration
---------------------------------
push_correct accepts arbitrary finite Bool bits, source head at the first
payload cell, a separator immediately to its left, and blank continuation
after the payload. Parking starts at the blank cell immediately after its
old separator; ancestor is an arbitrary finite list of Symbols, including
embedded blanks, separators and bits.

Push erases source cells, appends bits to parking, seals with a separator,
and rewinds the source to its marker-defined origin. Its nearest-first
parking representation contains bits.reverse, exactly as pop requires.
Pop removes only the new closing separator and payload, stages through
temporary, then reverses the scan again into output. The final output is
bits in original left-to-right order with the head at its first cell; for
an empty payload this is the blank cell immediately after its marker.
Parking returns to its former top and preserves the entire ancestor tape.
Temporary returns to blank contents except for its stated origin marker.

The inductive stage invariants retain ancestor as an arbitrary untouched
suffix. Inspection of the actual transitions shows that parking stops at
the old separator and never moves into ancestor. The source currently
does not package that non-visitation claim as a separate trace theorem;
the formal final-state equality and uniform runtime theorem are stronger
than testing examples but should not be mislabeled as such a trace theorem.

Round-trip glue and boundary markers
-------------------------------------
The final park_pop_round_trip literally passes pushed.source as pop's
output, and its first conjunct proves that source meets the required
empty marked-tape condition. pushed.parking is the exact required parked
record. This fixes the earlier merely implicit physical tape reuse.
The temporary is explicitly supplied empty and marked. No arbitrary
intervening child is modeled. The updated documentation correctly requires
such a child to preserve/restore the parked parent record and parking head
and return the source and temporary in the specified empty forms.

setBoundary performs move-left followed by write-and-move-right, hence
two head-local actions. install_boundary and remove_boundary preserve
arbitrary right-side contents and the origin position. In particular,
remove_empty_boundary restores an actually all-blank work tape; boundary
removal after the round trip restores the original unmarked payload tape.
Installation assumes the unmarked Tape.mk1 layout, whose left side is
blank; it is not a permission to overwrite arbitrary live data to the left.
These lemmas remove a permanent-work-marker assumption. Boundary setup
and removal are additional constant work, not included in the push/pop
budgets below. setBoundary itself is a two-action composition, rather than
a separately instantiated finite-control run theorem.

Costs and limits
------------------
Push uses n+1 copy/seal steps and n+1 rewind steps: 2n+2.
Pop uses two entry steps and three stages of n+1 steps: 3n+5.
Both formulas also handle n=0 correctly (2 and 5 steps). They are uniform
in ancestor size. The theorem statements reach done at these budgets;
they suffice for upper bounds and do not separately assert minimal
halting time. A step is a standard simultaneous multitape transition,
not Lean evaluation time or the cost of a one-tape simulation.

The sum 5n+7 is the push/pop phase budget under these interfaces. No
single combined scheduler transition system, child execution, dispatch,
marker setup/cleanup budget, recursion charge or all-size multiplication
runtime is proved by that arithmetic sum. Keeping these limits explicit,
the claimed scheduler-primitive advance is supported by the checked code.
