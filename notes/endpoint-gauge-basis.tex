\subsection{The ordinary projector corner}
For a rank-$j$ projector in dimension $h$ with $j>h/2$, put $r=h-j$.
In a generic rational basis its first-$r$/last-$r$ corner is invertible:
one witness pairs these coordinates in rank-one blocks
$\frac12\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$
and is the identity on the middle $h-2r$ coordinates. Similarity of
rational projectors with the same rank proves that the relevant minor
is a nonzero rational function of the basis.

Write $I-P=XY$, $YX=I_r$. The corner is $-X_LY_R$, so $X_L,Y_R$ are
invertible. Eliminating it leaves on the middle coordinates
\[
 I-X_MY_M-(-X_MY_R)(-Y_R^{-1}X_L^{-1})(-X_LY_M)=I_{h-2r}.
\]
The resulting lower-lower profile is $r$ singleton pivots and one
contiguous block of width $2j-h$. For $j\le h/2$ retain $j$ singleton
pivots; for $j=h$ use one block of width $h$. The last is still smaller
than the full tensor dimension $m$. Nonzero scalings are absorbed in
the triangular factors. This argument is also the large-projector
identity used in the controlled corners below.

\subsection{The selected inner family}
Use physical index $i=b\alpha+\beta$, with $\beta$ fastest, and
\[
 K=T_2(I_a\otimes L_b),\quad
 T_2(v\otimes e_\beta)=J_\beta v\otimes e_\beta,\quad
 J_\beta=M_\beta L_a.
\]
The bases $L_a,L_b$ are free invertible rational matrices. For
$I=(0,\ldots,a-1)$ prescribe the first $2a$ row labels as
$[I,(1,\ldots,a-1,0)]$ and the last $2a$ inverse-column labels as
$[(a-1,0,\ldots,a-2),I]$.
A row label $c$ at $(\alpha,\beta)$ means
$e_\alpha^tM_\beta=e_c^t$; an inverse-column label means
$M_\beta^{-1}e_\alpha=e_c$, equivalently a prescribed column of $M_\beta$.
Their effective residues $\beta-c$ modulo $b$ are $\{0,1,3\}$ and
$\{25,27,28\}$, respectively. Within one side no coefficient coordinate
is repeated at fixed $\beta$; the two residue sets and the physical
boundary positions are disjoint. Each $M_\beta$ thus has a permutation
completion. These linear constraints define an affine space with a
nonempty irreducible invertible locus.

The first $a$ rows and last $a$ inverse columns match the coordinate
orders of $L_a,L_a^{-1}$, giving every ordinary axis-$a$ corner.
For axis $b$, the inactive first factor is rank one and its weights
separate, giving a diagonal rescaling of $L_bBL_b^{-1}$. These facts
apply to every internal producer projector and the data residuals.

\subsection{The A5 tree witnesses}
The inner projector $(I-P_a)\otimes(I-P_b)$ has nullity $a+b-1=61$.
After eliminating its first $b$ nullspace coordinates and rescaling
nonzero line entries, its forward restriction is the incidence matrix
on vertices $0,\ldots,b+1$ with edges
\[
(0,b),(1,b+1);\quad (j+2,j+1)\ (0\le j\le b-3);\quad (0,b-1).
\]
It is the path on $1,\ldots,b-1,0$ with $b,b+1$ attached at the ends.
The dual restriction has edges
\[
(b,0),(b+1,1),(b+1,2);\quad (j-2,j-1)\ (4\le j\le b+1),
\]
the path $0,b,b-1,\ldots,2,b+1,1$.
Each tree has $a-1$ edges; leaf elimination gives rank $a-1$.
Both nullspace restrictions are therefore nonsingular. The
large-projector identity gives 61 singleton pivots and one 838-block.

\subsection{Outer matching and the A1 affine witness}
Set
\[
 S=T(I_k\otimes K),\qquad T(u\otimes e_i)=G_i u\otimes e_i.
\]
For $i=0,\ldots,k-1$ prescribe the first row of $G_i$ as row $i$ of a
free $L_k$. At the last $k$ multiplicity indices prescribe the last
inverse columns in matching $L_k^{-1}$ order. The next $a$ first rows
and preceding $a$ inverse columns remain free. The boundary intervals
of length $a+k=72$ are disjoint. Ordinary axis-$k$ corners and the
full $k$-block for unmatched stage-three roles are retained.

The paired A1 null projector has the form
\[
 Q_1=I_k\otimes P_q+P_t\otimes P_U,\quad
 \operatorname{rank}P_q=1,\quad\operatorname{rank}P_U=a,\quad P_qP_U=0.
\]
Write $q_i$ for the transformed line coordinate and $U_i$ for the
transformed $U$ row, impose $q_i\ne0$, and put $z_i=U_i/q_i$.
Absorbing $L_k$ into outer coordinates, the first $k$ nullspace rows are
$[e_p^t,t_pz_p]$, with $t_p\ne0$. The first $a$ vectors $z_p$ are
nonzero multiples of the $L_a$ coordinate basis. Row $p=a$ repeats
coefficient 1 at $\beta=2$ instead of $\beta=1$; its multiplier is
generically different. Indeed the two distinct motif lines have
coordinate ratios not identically equal in these freely chosen
$L_b$ coordinates. Thus $z_0,\ldots,z_a$ affinely span $\mathbb Q^a$.
For each extra row with free first-row functional $g_i$, eliminating
the first $k$ coordinates leaves
\[
 \sum_{p<k}g_{i,p}t_p(z_i-z_p).
\]
Affine spanning makes this map onto $\mathbb Q^a$. Choose the $a$
extra rows to produce its coordinate basis. This witnesses a nonzero
first-72-row nullspace determinant. Dually the last $a$ columns give
the matching $L_a^{-1}$ basis and the preceding column repeats
coefficient $a-2$ at $\beta=b-3$ instead of $b-2$. The same affine
argument and the free additional inverse columns witness the dual
determinant. The disjoint outer intervals make these prescriptions
compatible. Hence A1 has 72 singleton pivots and a 38256-block.

\subsection{The gauged and A3 corners}
For the stage-two null projector $Q=P_k\otimes(P_a\otimes I_b)$,
the first-$b$/last-$b$ corner of $SQS^{-1}$ is diagonal. With
$i=(0,\beta)$ and $j=(a-1,\beta)$ its diagonal entry is
\[
(e_1^tG_it_k)(\phi_kG_j^{-1}e_k)
(e_1^tJ_\beta t_a)(\phi_aJ_\beta^{-1}e_a),
\]
where $P_h=t_h\phi_h$ and $\phi_h(t_h)=1$.
Each factor is generically nonzero in the prescribed family.
The corner of $I-Q$ is its negative; it gives one contiguous $b$-block,
and the Schur identity leaves $m-2b$. Thus its profile is $(30,38340)$.

The A3 null projector is
$P_t\otimes I_H+(I-P_t)\otimes P_q$, of rank $H+k-1$.
Its first $H$ rows restrict diagonally and nonsingularly to the first
summand. After eliminating them, each of the next $k-1$ rows is a
nonzero $q_i$ multiple of
\[
 \operatorname{row}_2(G_i)
 -\frac{\operatorname{row}_2(G_i)t}{\operatorname{row}_1(G_i)t}
       \operatorname{row}_1(G_i)
\]
on the complement of $t$. These second rows are free and can give
any basis of that complement; the dual witness uses free next-to-last
inverse columns. Both full nullspace restrictions are nonsingular.
Within the $H$-corner, the matched first-$k$/last-$k$ corner is a
diagonal rescaling of an ordinary rank-$(k-1)$ projector. Its one
singleton and $(k-2)$-block exhaust that perturbation, leaving the
matching interval of width $H-2k+2$. The remaining corner uses
$2(k-1)$ singleton pivots. The final profile is therefore 79 singletons
and blocks $38,882,36402$.

All required determinants, ordinary corner minors, nonzero coordinates,
and unequal ratios are nonzero rational functions on the same
irreducible family. The displayed witnesses prove each is nonzero;
after clearing denominators, their finite product is nonzero.
Rational enumeration therefore finds one basis satisfying them all,
independently of recursive width. Choose a sufficiently large odd
address prime avoiding its finitely many bad minors and denominators.

\]
For each axis-$h$ rank-$r$ histogram entry use its ordinary profile
and multiplicity $NH_{h,r}/v_h$. This gives 629339590854400 singleton
calls, with $\sum_tn_tt=s$ and every $0<t<m$. Set
\[
 \Psi_b(x)=\frac{\sum_tn_tt^x}{Wm^x},\qquad
 a_b=\frac{2993093}{250000000000},\quad \tau=1-a_b.
\]
For $1\le y\le2$ and $z=(y-1)/(y+1)$ the positive atanh series gives
\[
 \log y\le 2\sum_{j=0}^{23}\frac{z^{2j+1}}{2j+1}
                  +\frac{2z^{49}}{49(1-z^2)}.
\]
Extract powers of two and round this bound upward to $10^{-12}$,
obtaining $\ell_t\ge\log(m/t)$. For $0\le u<3$,
$e^u\le1+u+u^2/[2(1-u/3)]$. Exact rational arithmetic then gives
\[
 \Psi_b(\tau)\le\sum_t\frac{n_tt}{Wm}
 \left(1+a_b\ell_t+\frac{(a_b\ell_t)^2}{2(1-a_b\ell_t/3)}\right)
 <1-\frac{226731361147908}{10^{28}}.
\]
The strict characteristic and the integer-width proof below establish
the bit interface with exponent $\tau$.
